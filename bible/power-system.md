# Power System: Borrowed Gods

## Core rule

A Borrowed God is a future person’s practiced capacity crossing a causal seam into the present. It is temporary, relationship-based, and limited by the future person’s own boundary. The borrower is not possessing a deity or absorbing a soul. The lender remains a person with agency.

The supernatural effect is a temporary overlap between a present body and a possible future body. The gift can be a skill, a trained reflex, a sensory mode, a physical tolerance, or a piece of operational knowledge. It cannot be a universal solution.

## The Civic Spine interface

When the emergency network can carry a safe exchange, the borrower receives a short plain-language notice. The notice is assembled from the future person’s words and the seam’s physical conditions. It is not an intelligent assistant and does not know more than the lender.

**Panel format:**

```text
Loan offered: Mara Okafor, seventeen years ahead
Gift name: Still Hand
Gift: Keep one breath from failing while the body is repaired.
Boundary: Do not use my hand to kill. Do not use my hand to abandon a person.
Return: When the third wheel stops turning.
```

The fields mean:

- **Loan offered:** The future person is the source, not an item or title.
- **Gift name:** The conventional name the lending practice already uses for this capacity, when one exists. It is a label for the record, not a title, rank, or source of extra power. It may be absent.
- **Gift:** The exact capacity being lent. It never expands silently.
- **Boundary:** A present condition the future person sets. The current borrower must honor it.
- **Return:** A concrete event, action, or state the future person needs in order to remain a willing participant. It is not a timer written by a game system.

An interface can be incomplete, delayed, censored, or mistaken about a future person’s identity. A later message may contradict an earlier one because the branch has changed. The story treats a panel as evidence, not as an infallible prophecy.

## What makes a loan possible

A future self can be heard only when all of the following are true:

1. A physical seam is active.
2. The borrower has encountered or meaningfully affected the lender’s present-day self, or stands in a relationship to the place where that self lives.
3. The borrower is acting toward a concrete goal that the future person recognizes and values.
4. The future person chooses to answer.
5. A boundary can be stated, even if the borrower does not understand it yet.

A random god cannot be summoned by desire. A person cannot borrow a gift from a future enemy without first creating a relationship to that enemy’s present or place. This keeps the system connected to work, observation, and trust.

## Hard limits

- **No physical time travel:** Only a capacity, a perception, or a memory fragment crosses. A future person does not walk through the seam.
- **No fixed prophecy:** The future self knows a branch, not the whole future. It can be mistaken.
- **No permanent theft:** The gift fades when the loan ends. Ordinary practice may leave a real skill behind, but the supernatural capacity does not remain.
- **No forced consent:** A future self may withdraw at any time. A present person may physically force an anchor, but the resulting circuit is unstable and morally destructive; the system does not convert violence into consent.
- **No invulnerability:** A borrowed body can still be cut, crushed, burned, poisoned, tired, or killed. A future body’s resistance may protect the borrower only in the exact practice that produced the gift.
- **No generic power shopping:** A lender offers one bounded capacity, not a catalog. Requesting a different use, a longer duration, or a second unconnected gift breaks the exchange.
- **No guaranteed saves:** The gift can create a chance, a duration, a perception, or a transfer of strain. The borrower still has to build the solution.
- **No borrowed memories replacing identity:** Sensory and emotional afterimages may intrude, but a future self cannot overwrite the borrower’s choices. The borrower may be unable to tell whose fear is present for a while.
- **No automatic global access:** A stable loan is tied to a seam, an anchor, and a network. It cannot be stockpiled or carried across the world like a stored item.

## The five costs

Every meaningful loan produces at least two kinds of consequence.

### Immediate strain

The borrower’s body pays for the mismatch between present conditions and the future practice. Examples include burned hands after a heat-resistance gift, vertigo after a balance gift, loss of a recent memory after an overextended sensory loan, or bleeding from a sealed airway. The exact cost depends on the gift, the body, and the work; it is not a universal meter.

### Aftermath

The loan changes a future and leaves a concrete trace in the present. A door may appear where a wall was. A document may lose a name. A road may be open in one neighborhood and impossible in another. A person may remember a rescue that everyone else insists did not happen. Aftermath is not a symbolic mood. It changes what people can do next.

### Backwash

When a branch collides with the present or a boundary is broken, the cost can return to the borrower, an anchor, a tool, or the seam. Backwash may be physical, sensory, relational, or civic. A team can distribute it, but cannot pretend that sharing makes it free.

### Refusal

A future self can stop a loan. Refusal is not a malfunction. The gift may collapse before a task is complete, and the borrower must fall back to ordinary skills. Repeatedly ignoring a refusal damages trust and may make a future person permanently unwilling to answer.

### Irreversible choice

A changed future can close a possibility. A person may never exist in the present, a community may lose a route, or a loved one may become unreachable. The power cannot restore the original branch after the choice is made. This is the moral cost that makes future selves more than convenient allies.

## The stages of growth

These are changes in responsibility and reach, not a numeric experience bar. Elias moves through them only after practice, failure, relationships, and a changed decision.

| Stage | Name | What Elias can do | How it is earned | Cost and limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | **Unborrowed** | Rescue with ordinary equipment, training, observation, and local knowledge. | His existing work and competence. | He is vulnerable and cannot solve a seam with personal will alone. |
| 1 | **First Witness** | Receive one short offer and use one gift near an active seam. | Surviving the first collapse while listening to a future person rather than forcing them. | Immediate strain, brief sensory afterimages, and no control over the gift’s side effects. One loan at a time. |
| 2 | **Named Thread** | Recognize a lender by voice or name, keep a faint connection after a scene, and ask a future self to repeat a boundary. | Repeated drills, failed attempts, and honest conversations with present-day partners. | Aftermath becomes easier to identify but not easier to undo. A changed future may alter the thread before it can be used. |
| 3 | **Shared Load** | Stabilize a loan with consenting anchors and distribute backwash across a small crew. | Building trust after he lets others decide part of a rescue. | Every anchor carries a real risk. Withdrawing one can collapse the circuit. No command over the group. |
| 4 | **Crafted God** | Make repeatable tools—anchor lamps, seam wards, and safety frames—that hold the shape of a bounded gift for other people. | Craft, maintenance, teaching, and publishing failures. | Tools inherit their maker’s boundaries and can be captured or misused. A crafted gift is a shared practice, not an owned weapon. |
| 5 | **Branchread** | Perceive several nearby plausible outcomes and move through a short-lived route across a seam. | Surviving branching failures and learning to act without a single correct forecast. | He can be briefly split in attention and leaves Unwritten traces. He cannot choose a future by looking at it. |
| 6 | **Open Circuit** | Coordinate several independent lenders in a local network, each answering a separate need. | Public trust, delegation, and the willingness to lose control. | Slower decisions, visible conflict, and shared danger. A network cannot outrun a withdrawal. |
| 7 | **Cutline** | Sever a forced, stolen, or dangerously stabilizing loan and open a safe route for people trapped in it. | Practice, moral cost, and the ability to distinguish consent from desperation. | He carries part of the severed aftermath. A legitimate future may be damaged, and the cut leaves an Unwritten wound. |
| 8 | **Living Pantheon** | Act at the scale of a city or region by coordinating many anchors, gifts, and ordinary responders. | Building durable institutions that do not depend on him being present. | He has no private super-strength; the network can fragment under political or emotional pressure. |
| 9 | **Open Hand** | Stand at a final hinge and open a temporary, unowned relay in which any consenting future person may lend a bounded gift. | Refusing the permanent-anchor offer and building a network whose value does not depend on his obedience. | He cannot summon any god at will, cannot hear his own future, and cannot guarantee a single world afterward. |

Stage 9 is not a permanent godhood upgrade. It is a civic function that can be shared and that becomes dangerous if one person claims ownership of it.

### Advanced methods still obey the loan rules

- **Branchread** is not arbitrary portal travel. It requires an active seam, a named route-memory gift from a related future person, and a route that physically exists in the present and in that branch. Elias can follow a route for a short, unstable interval; he cannot open a door to any desired place.
- **Cutline** works only on a live loan circuit at an existing anchor. It severs a forced or exhausted connection, or opens the route that circuit is already holding. It cannot cut a branch from a distance, choose which people survive, or repair the Unwritten wound it creates.
- **The Open Hand** reconfigures already-established, consenting nodes. Future people remain the only source of borrowed gifts; present participants serve as anchors, decision-makers, and workers. The relay cannot bypass the five loan conditions, summon a stranger’s power, or make a refusing future answer.

### The permanent-anchor exception

The final offer associated with the Returning Hand is not a normal stage-nine loan. Once the Compact has begun the one-time First Choice activation, the offer is a **one-time transfer of operator control over that already-active lock** through captured nodes. It can force one approved sequence through those connected seams for a limited time, but it cannot restart the lock, create genuine consent, hear a future person who withdraws, or reach a seam that has not already been coerced into the network. Every forced node is unstable; as one withdraws or fails, the sequence loses part of its reach and produces severe backwash. The offer is powerful because it violates the rules of the system, not because it is a higher legitimate power.

## Failure and recovery

Failure must be legible. Examples include:

- using a future builder’s load-reading gift to force a structure to carry a load it was never meant to carry;
- accepting a future healer’s hands but ignoring the boundary that forbids using them to prolong a killing;
- asking a future self to lend power after the present relationship has been damaged;
- treating a refusal as a temporary technical problem and forcing an unwilling anchor;
- building a ward that works only because it assumes a neighborhood will evacuate in the same way;
- using a future memory of a route after the route has already been changed;
- sharing a gift with a team without telling them what the loan may cost.

Recovery always involves work. Elias has to identify what changed, apologize to the people who were exposed, redesign the practice, and accept that some losses cannot be repaired. A successful recovery can improve a relationship or a method; it does not erase the earlier failure.

## Standard future gifts used in the series

The following gifts are examples, not a complete list. Each is tied to a person, a branch, and a boundary.

- **Still Hand — Mara Okafor:** keeps one living body from failing while another person repairs it. It cannot resurrect the dead, cure disease, or make a person invulnerable.
- **Load Reader — Tomas Vale:** reads the path force will take through a damaged structure. It reveals direction and stress, not a magical repair.
- **Door Memory — Nell Rook:** remembers a route that will exist in a particular future. The route may be the only safe route in that branch and may be closed in another.
- **Quiet Hands — a future field medic:** keeps panic and noise from overwhelming a small group long enough for evacuation. It cannot erase a source of danger.
- **The Long Breath — a future tunnel diver:** works in smoke, floodwater, and confined air. The gift is useless against poison or a crushing ceiling.
- **The Name at the End — a future archivist:** retains one erased name or route long enough to report it. It does not restore the person or guarantee the story is true.
- **The Returning Hand — Elias Rook, Forty-Seven:** makes a network of emergency actions follow one approved sequence. It is a coercive tool and cannot be used by an unwilling network.

## How the power is earned in practice

Training scenes must show application rather than a skill tree. Elias learns to:

- identify a seam’s physical signs before opening a channel;
- state a goal and a boundary instead of demanding power;
- place anchors with consent and explain likely backwash;
- use ordinary equipment while a future gift buys time;
- distinguish a lender’s memory from his own perception;
- stop when a future voice refuses;
- repair a seam ward with the people who will live beside it;
- teach a method to someone else so the method survives his absence.

A training gain is only canon when it changes a choice or lets the team perform a task that was previously impossible for a specific reason. A larger magical effect is not progress by itself.

## Final system rule

At the climax, Elias can open a relay only when the present people around him understand the cost and can refuse to participate. The future voices can also refuse. A refusal is not a failure of the relay; it is proof that the system has not become the One-Future Choir in a new mask. Elias’s victory depends on partial, voluntary action rather than on everyone finally obeying him.
