# Terminology

This file is the continuity authority for names and rules. Use the preferred term in narration and dialogue. If a character uses a different word, make the difference intentional.

## Terms for the phenomenon

### The First Silence

The ninety-second event on 17 October 1998 when synchronized clocks, emergency channels, and public relays stopped reporting. It is the first documented public appearance of the seam phenomenon. It was not a worldwide blackout or a day that disappeared.

### The Long Emergency

The present era of recurring seam disasters, unstable rescue conditions, and emergency institutions built around the risk of contradictory histories. It is a political and social period, not a single event.

### Seam

A physical location where two or more plausible histories can affect the same place. A seam can be mild, localized, and harmless until pressure or machinery makes its branches collide. Do not use “portal,” “dimension,” or “dungeon” as automatic synonyms.

### Echo

A temporary manifestation of a future self’s voice, perception, memory, or practiced capacity through a seam. An echo is not a ghost, a soul, a prophecy, or a separate person in the present.

### Borrowed God

The common social name for a bounded capacity lent by a future self. The name is used by responders, media, and frightened civilians; it is not the supernatural being’s true nature. Keep the capital letters in formal references, but allow “god” in ordinary speech.

### Loan

A voluntary exchange that includes a gift, a boundary, and a return condition. A loan is not a purchase, a soul bond, or a permanent power-up.

### Vow

A concrete act of care or responsibility that opens the possibility of an exchange. “Vow” is not a religious oath and does not compel a future self. The present borrower must describe the act, not merely feel it.

### Boundary

A limit set by the future lender. A boundary can be about use, target, timing, relationship, or return. Violating it permits refusal and can cause backwash. Boundaries are not secret rules that the protagonist can ignore and still be “correct.”

### Return

The future lender’s requested present act or state that keeps the exchange honest. “Return” does not mean that the power is repaid with time, lifespan, or points. It can be a route completed, a person treated, a promise kept, or a choice not made on another person’s behalf.

### Anchor

A present-day person or place that stabilizes a seam and helps carry the physical and civic cost of a loan. A fully stable anchor consents, understands the likely cost, and can withdraw. Violence can create an unstable anchor, never genuine consent.

### Thread

A persistent but fragile connection to an identified future self. A thread can warn of a changed boundary or help Elias recognize a future voice. It is not a leash, a tracker, or a stored ability.

### Aftermath

The concrete consequence of a changed future in the present: a different route, missing record, altered relationship, or new physical hazard. Aftermath is always something people must live with.

### Backwash

The physical, sensory, relational, or civic cost that returns when a seam is stressed or a loan is broken. Backwash can be shared, but sharing does not make it free.

### Unwritten

A place, person, or history that has been excluded by a forced future and is losing the records and causal links that let it remain recognized. “Unwritten” is a condition, not a ghost realm.

### Hinge

A person or place at which several futures can negotiate their way into the present. Elias becomes a **self-made hinge** because of a particular rescue and a repeated choice to remain at consequential seams and accept future boundaries. This is a practice, not a bloodline status or innate power. Other self-made hinges appear throughout the series.

## Institutions and places

### Civic Spine

The global distributed emergency network built after the First Silence. It handles warnings, hospitals, transit, and seam records. It can display a future offer, but it does not choose which future is true. Its public infrastructure and its buried old relays are different systems with different loyalties.

### Continuity Office

The Havenport office that licenses borrowers, inspects tools, investigates unauthorized loans, and maintains the local registry. It is a bureaucratic institution, not a single secret order.

### Continuity Compact

The international treaty that created the shared emergency framework. It has no single world government and cannot command every national army.

### Mercy Project / Mercy Array

The pre-Silence experiment that tried to receive a warning from a future disaster. The Array opened a causal seam and produced the First Silence. It is the historical origin of the current system, not a god, a benevolent creator, or a final enemy.

### The Open Hand

A decentralized emergency and civic network built around consent, revocation, public records, and distributed anchors. It is a movement with internal disagreement, not a perfect institution.

### The One-Future Choir

The organized movement that believes a single protected future is the only way to prevent civilization’s collapse. It begins as a policy movement, then becomes the One-Future Compact.

### Bellwether Choir

The early name used for the One-Future Choir’s public pilot cells, each of which selected one safer district outcome. “Bellwether” is a political and bureaucratic label, not a separate faction. Later documents may use it to describe the coercive origin of the Choir.

### First Choice

A one-time coercive activation of the old Mercy Array lock through already-captured Civic Spine relays. It forces one approved branch through connected nodes and cannot convert refusal into consent. It is the culmination of the One-Future Compact’s policy, not a new entity.

### Permanent Hinge

The coercive operator role the Returning Hand offers Elias at the end of the series. A permanent hinge takes control of the already-active Mercy Array lock through captured nodes and can impose one approved sequence for a limited time, but cannot restart the First Choice, make a future self answer, or keep the network stable as nodes withdraw or fail.

### The One-Future Compact

The final political-military coalition that tries to activate the First Choice. It is composed of established Continuity Office officials, Choir members, military units, and the Returning Hand’s present-day agents. It is the culmination of the antagonist ladder, not a new faction appearing after the final conflict is established.

### The Returning Hand

A coalition from a future branch led by a possible Elias Rook. It is foreshadowed in Volume 01 and fully identified in Volumes 09–11. Its members influence the present through borrowers, coercion, and anchor networks; they are not external invaders.

### The Mercy Line

A dispersed technical and archival lineage connected to the original project. It can provide records, tools, or testimony without serving as a hidden master mind.

### Havenport, Salt Meridian, Kestrel Tunnel, Cinder Quarter, The Spine, Mercy Field, Ninefold Basin

The primary story locations. Keep their geography consistent: Havenport is an estuary city; the Salt Meridian runs through its rail and port; Kestrel is a tunnel and station; the Cinder Quarter is west of the city; Mercy Field is remote and desert-bound; the Ninefold Basin is inland and agricultural.

## Character and power titles

### Still Hand

A future Mara gift: keeping one living body from failing while another person repairs it. It is not healing, resurrection, or a general vitality boost.

### Load Reader

A future Tomas gift: seeing how force will travel through a damaged structure. It does not repair the structure or guarantee a safe outcome.

### Door Memory

A future Nell gift: remembering a route that exists in a particular branch. The route may be dangerous or impossible in another branch.

### The Long Breath

A future tunnel-diver gift for surviving smoke, floodwater, and confined air. It does not protect against poison, crushing force, or panic.

### The Name at the End

A future archivist gift that preserves one erased name or route long enough to report it. It does not restore a person or guarantee that the reported history will be accepted.

### The Returning Hand

A possible Elias gift that forces emergency actions across an anchor network to follow one approved sequence. It is coercive by design and must never be described as a neutral civic power. It can operate only through nodes already captured or coerced into the network; it cannot compel a future self’s consent.

## Interface language

The interface may use short headings such as:

- **Loan offered**
- **Gift**
- **Boundary**
- **Return**
- **Withdrawal**
- **Warning: the branch is changing**
- **No answer**
- **Aftermath ahead**

Messages should be sparse, concrete, and tied to the scene. Do not use “experience,” “level,” “stats,” “skill tree,” “cooldown,” “quest,” “shop,” “drop,” “buff,” “debuff,” “API,” “protocol,” “backend,” “runtime,” “token,” or “deployment.” “Protocol” is permitted only when a human institution names its own administrative or safety procedure, never as a substitute for a fictional world rule.

## Terms to avoid in narration

- “time travel” for a normal loan; use “crossing,” “seam,” or “borrow.”
- “chosen one” as a factual description of Elias; he is self-made as a hinge through specific choices.
- “god” as a claim that the future self is omnipotent.
- “future self” when the branch could be a possible person rather than a guaranteed self; use “future version” or “possible self” when precision matters.
- “the universe” or “the system” as a personality. The Civic Spine is an institution, and the apparent system is the combined physical and social machinery of seams.
- “simulation” or “game world.” The future branches are causal possibilities, not disposable simulations.
- “time bank,” “years owed,” “life debt,” or “interest” for the loan economy. The costs are embodied and civic, not a numerical lifespan account.
- “chosen prophecy” for future information. A lender knows one branch and can be wrong about the present.

## Continuity rule for future selves

Every future self introduced in the story must have, at minimum:

- a branch-specific want;
- a reason for answering Elias;
- a boundary that can be violated;
- one present consequence if the loan succeeds;
- a possibility that they will refuse or change their mind.

If a future self lacks these qualities, the character is functioning as a power-up rather than a person.
