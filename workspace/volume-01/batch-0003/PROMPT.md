## Batch goal

**A Ward Made by Neighbours:** the team and the Cinder Quarter build a real refuge around the Kestrel access corridors while a provisional classification, a ninety-six-hour clock, and a neighbourhood's own deadlines pull in three directions. The batch must end on the volume's midpoint reversal at **Chapter 25**: a safe exit the opening rescue made possible is absent from the physical world and from every current record, while the people who used it remember crossing it. That is the first physical-absence proof in the volume and it has to be harder evidence than the Chapter 12 page — a named user, a witness, a photograph, a measurement, a timestamp, and residue at a place that no longer has a place.

By the end of this batch:

- the first Still Hand loan remains complete and unavailable, and no new loan is accepted;
- Elias remains at Stage 1: First Witness, with persistent left-hand numbness and no summonable power;
- the service door is still structurally unusable; physical absence is now proven for a *different* exit and remains unproven for the service door itself, and the batch must not blur the two;
- the deeper signatures remain a live, unresolved risk; at most one of them is confirmed by voice, and neither is rescued or counted;
- Nell's present-day branch-crossed signal remains distinct from any future Nell echo, and the envelope to her is still unsent;
- the possible-Elias voice remains unidentified, appears only as preserved raw audio, and offers no power;
- the Bell family row is still missing from the digital response list, and the nine-day dispatch confirmation either arrives inconclusive or arrives and raises a worse question;
- `MNT-4C-17` and the 05:02 dispatch sheet are compared further without identifying the holder;
- Tomas's same-relay admission is public in the room, and the Mercy Array, the First Silence, the Returning Hand, the Choir, and the Compact remain unrevealed;
- the Office's safety argument is still credible, still coercive, and now has a partial institutional win and a partial institutional loss on the record;
- the Cinder Quarter refuge remains a physical and civic tool with resident consent, per-household cards, and withdrawal, not a shared-load circuit and not a stage.

## Pacing and scene requirements

Alternate pressure: survivor care, ward construction and cost, record investigation, local and Office politics, recovery, physical survey, and relationship consequence. Give the batch a midpoint reversal at Chapter 25 and let Chapters 26–30 be the cost of it. Do not make every chapter a fight or a System notice. Let the team disagree about evidence, timing, and acceptable risk, and let people outside the team be right more than once. Every chapter must change a practical or emotional situation and end with a decision, discovery, cost, or concrete next question rather than a manufactured interruption.

Aim for **1,800–2,400 words in an ordinary chapter**. Batch 0002 measured 30,204 words across ten chapters and overran its target in every chapter, so hold the line here: a ten-chapter batch should land nearer 20,000–24,000 words. Allow one longer chapter where a complete scene — the Chapter 25 reversal, most likely — cannot honestly be cut shorter. A finished scene governs length: never pad, never add a paragraph of restatement, and never truncate a beat to hit a number.

Use at most one short interface notice in a chapter, and only when it is necessary. Plain language is preferred. **No new loan is accepted in this batch.** A possible voice may warn, refuse, or refuse an extension. If a voice is heard, it must have a branch-specific want, a reason for answering, a boundary that can be violated, and a present consequence for refusing. Do not turn a refusal into a malfunction, and do not retry one as a workaround. Halla Dren in particular must not be asked a second question, because the team already refused her once and recorded the refusal.

## Chapter-by-chapter direction

### Chapter 21 — The Supervised Reading

Monday morning in the union hall, with Hollis Vane reading the originals where the survivors can watch. The hold was accepted on the Wednesday afternoon; this is the day after it expires, and the review board sits later the same week. Goal: complete the reading without surrendering custody of anything. Resistance: the archivist is thorough, unimpressed, and not a villain; a survivor withdraws consent mid-reading; the loan record is in the pile, and reading it aloud makes Tarin Voss a public figure whether he consented or not. Turn: Vane's written finding says the equipment-failure classification is not supported by the Office's own evidence, which weakens Sable's position and makes the review board dangerous instead of comfortable. End with two dates in the room that collide: the review board later that week and Perry Dunne's Friday.

### Chapter 22 — A Ward With a Name On It

Build the refuge. Inez, the cooperative, scavenged and bought materials, per-circuit consent cards, and a man trying to sell "seam-rated" cable. Goal: a physical place that keeps people, records, food, and decisions safer for a limited time. Resistance: cost, labour, no borrowed circuit, a profit motive arriving at the gate, and a neighbourhood that has been reorganised around a hole in the ground. Turn: the ward is smaller than anyone wanted and works anyway, and the team opens and physically marks a second surface access corridor because supplies cannot keep coming down the ramp. End on the ordinary, unremarkable fact that a second exit now exists, is on the survey, and has been used by a named person.

### Chapter 23 — The Listening Campaign

Run the passive campaign set up in Chapter 20, with no medic on the line and Ansel Duarte's condition in force. Goal: contact, not extraction. Resistance: silence, a shifting branch, Halla Dren's warning that warnings stop being free when the branch is changing, and the team's own temptation to write *almost* into a document. Turn: the team gets voice — not identity, not a count, not a condition — and the contact is not what anybody expected to be asked. End with what the person behind the wall wants, stated plainly, and with the question of whether the team is allowed to answer it.

### Chapter 24 — The Box at the Depot

The team tries, lawfully and awkwardly, to reach the 05:02 dispatch sheet original in the Spine depot. Goal: find out who held `MNT-4C-17` and who signed the second line. Resistance: a box, a key, a transfer hour, a supervisor's authority, and an access log whose own entry has a blank field. Turn: the log shows the box was opened once, on the day of the collapse, under a routine archive-review entry with a missing field. End with the institutional win from the review preparation landing in the room at the same moment as a worse fact about the archive.

### Chapter 25 — The Exit That Is Not There

The midpoint reversal, and the batch's spine. A second surface access corridor that was opened, marked, surveyed, photographed, measured, and used by a named person with a witness is gone: not the wall, not the ramp, not a blocked or unsafe version. The residue is still there — scuffed threshold, dust in a bolt hole, a handprint — at a place that has no place. The current route index says the corridor was never constructed. Goal: establish what has happened without forcing a single explanation. Resistance: the evidence is better than anything the team has had and it points at their own successful rescue; every instinct Elias has says this is a record problem he can fix with the right instrument. Turn: the team proves that the first loan's success closed or removed a route it depends on, and Elias cannot make the map agree, and does not try. End with the three-column page being torn down and replaced by something with only two columns and no comfort in it.

### Chapter 26 — The Slower Plan

Elias gives up the single perfect plan. Goal: build a distributed plan he does not own. Resistance: it is worse, slower, and depends on people who can leave; Tomas and Mara disagree about the medical shape of it; Inez's power budget cannot support it and says so. Turn: the plan only works if the ward gives up something it was built to protect. End with the cost named and accepted by the people paying it, not just by Elias.

### Chapter 27 — Friday

Perry Dunne's date arrives. Goal: reduce the crew without breaking the ward. Resistance: expertise leaves, someone competent is leaving for a reason that is not anger, and the residents discover what they can and cannot do alone. Turn: Anja Ro, Petar Lisk, Ru Sabar, and the cooperative carry a real shift, and the ward is briefly better run by people who are not trained for it. End with Elias being told, in plain words, that he is not the reason the ward works and that this is not a compliment.

### Chapter 28 — Adare Comes Back

The broker returns with a real offer and a real threat. Goal: keep the refused branch route out of the Quarter. Resistance: he is polite, useful, and can name a person whose family wants out; the offer is cheaper and safer than anything the team can offer; refusing costs somebody an option. Turn: the team refuses again under worse conditions, and the refusal is witnessed and signed a second time, and Nadi Bell's refusal from Chapter 16 becomes the precedent that holds. End with the question of what the Office does about a broker operating inside a provisional classification.

### Chapter 29 — What the Records Can Bear

Nell's carrier, the sealed Office data node, and the paper disagree about something that matters. Goal: decide what to do with an unsent promise and an incomplete signal. Resistance: the node is a copy the team can no longer fully control; a signal fragment arrives that is not a request for rescue; Elias wants to file the envelope and Joon and Mara both refuse. Turn: Elias does not send it, and the reason he gives is honest, and the envelope stays in the notebook. End with a live question about whether the maintenance route is a way to reach Nell or a way to close her branch.

### Chapter 30 — The Ward Holds a Shift

A working session, not a ceremony, and the batch's decision. Goal: decide what the team prepares next under distributed authority and what each domain will refuse. Resistance: the review board's survey requirement, the 2031 node, the missing second signature, Group D, and the fact that the Chapter 25 exit cannot be planned around because it is not there. Turn: the team prepares a distributed survey or explicitly holds the site, and Elias signs a promise another person is empowered to break. End with a concrete next action for Chapters 31–40 and a live question about the broker, the sealed node, and the possible-Elias voice.
