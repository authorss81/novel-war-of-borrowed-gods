## Chapter-by-chapter direction

### Chapter 21 — The Finding

**Friday, the hold's third day. The supervised reading is already over; do not repeat it.** Hollis Vane comes back to the union hall with the finding he wrote on Thursday morning, because the accepted term was that he writes his findings in the room and not in the Office. Goal: take delivery of a written institutional finding without letting it be used as a weapon by either side, and get Perry Dunne the clean week he asked for. Resistance: the archivist is thorough, unimpressed, and not a villain; the finding is **narrower than the team wants** — it says the equipment-failure classification is not supported by the Office's own evidence, and it also says the file cannot be revised while the holder field is empty, which makes the hole the only thing a board can act on; **Tarin Voss withdraws his own page** before it is read aloud, because the loan record names him as the body that was kept alive and nobody asked him, and Vane records the withdrawal *as* the finding, so the team's most probative line is now a gap; a survivor withdraws consent to one page in the same way. Turn: the finding goes on the hall wall with the Office's published answer posted underneath it, so the institution is on the record disagreeing with itself in the same building, and the team asks the board for the depot box rather than for exoneration — which is how the archive becomes reachable in Chapter 24. End with the conversation Chapter 19 promised and did not have: the hold's last day is tomorrow, so the crew cannot be gone today without breaking a signed term. Perry's week moves to the following Friday, and he says plainly that he did not move it to be helpful.

### Chapter 22 — A Ward With a Name On It

**Saturday, the hold's fourth day.** Morning is ordinary labor and ordinary money: Inez, the cooperative, scavenged and bought materials, per-circuit consent cards, and a man at the gate trying to sell "seam-rated" cable. Goal: a physical place that keeps people, records, food, and decisions safer for a limited time. Resistance: cost, labor, no borrowed circuit, a profit motive arriving at the gate, and a neighborhood that has been reorganized around a hole in the ground; every consignment still comes down one ramp. Turn: instead of cutting anything new, the team re-opens, re-measures, re-photographs, and re-names the **lower service corridor** — the run Groups A, B and C walked during the loan window — puts it back on the paper as a hazard with a keeper's name on it, and uses it that afternoon to move supplies. This is the corridor the first Still Hand loan made passable, which is exactly why Chapter 25 can take it away. In the afternoon the **review board sits** and does not seal and does not clear: the classification stays provisional, a survey requirement is imposed, and lawful access to the depot box is granted in exchange — a partial institutional win and a partial institutional loss. End on the ordinary, unremarkable fact that a route which has been off the paper for four days is now on it in three places, walked that day by a named person with a witness.

### Chapter 23 — The Listening Campaign

**Sunday, the day after the board.** Run the passive campaign set up in Chapter 20, with no medic on the line and Ansel Duarte's condition in force. Goal: contact, not extraction. Resistance: silence, a shifting branch, Halla Dren's warning that warnings stop being free when the branch is changing, and the team's own temptation to write *almost* into a document. Turn: the team gets voice — not identity, not a count, not a condition — and the contact is not what anybody expected to be asked. End with what the person behind the wall wants, stated plainly, and with the question of whether the team is allowed to answer it.

### Chapter 24 — The Box at the Depot

**Monday, the day after the review board.** The team uses the archive access the board granted on Saturday, lawfully and awkwardly, to reach the 05:02 dispatch sheet original in the Spine depot. Goal: find out who held `MNT-4C-17` and who signed the second line. Resistance: a box, a key, a transfer hour, a supervisor's authority, and an access log whose own entry has a blank field. Turn: the log shows the box was opened once, on the day of the collapse, under a routine archive-review entry with a missing field. On the way back the team uses the lower service corridor again to move a load, as they did on Saturday, and a named person walks it with a witness. End with the institutional win from the board landing in the room at the same moment as a worse fact about the archive — and with the corridor noted as used today, in writing, in three places.

### Chapter 25 — The Exit That Is Not There

**Tuesday, the day after the corridor was used.** The midpoint reversal, and the batch's spine. The **lower service corridor** — the run from the service landing past the changed door that Groups A, B and C walked while the first Still Hand loan held, re-opened, re-measured, re-photographed and re-used on Saturday with a keeper's name on it — **is gone**. Not blocked, not unsafe, not there. The residue is still there — scuffed threshold, dust in a bolt hole, a handprint — at a place that has no place, and the current route index says the corridor was never constructed. This evidence is categorically harder than Chapter 12's page: the corridor was measured and photographed on Saturday, three days before, with a laser and a named keeper, so the absence is a change over days rather than over years, twenty-three people remember crossing it, and the loan's return condition — the third maintenance wheel — sits in a medical ledger the Office has countersigned. Goal: establish what has happened without forcing a single explanation. Resistance: the evidence indicts the team's own successful rescue, and every instinct Elias has says this is a record problem he can fix with the right instrument. Turn: the team can prove the corridor's usable span and the loan's window coincide, and **cannot** prove cause — and the honest finding is worse than a cause, because there is no version of Saturday in which the opening rescue worked without the loan, so the route depended on a future that the return already altered. Elias cannot make the map agree and does not try. The three-column page comes down and something with two columns and no comfort in it goes up. The **service door's own three states stay unproven and separate**; the reversal must not settle the door by implication.

### Chapter 26 — The Slower Plan

**Wednesday into Thursday.** Elias gives up the single perfect plan. Goal: build a distributed plan he does not own. Resistance: it is worse, slower, and depends on people who can leave; Tomas and Mara disagree about the medical shape of it; Inez's power budget cannot support it and says so. Turn: the plan only works if the ward gives up something it was built to protect. End with the cost named and accepted by the people paying it, not just by Elias.

### Chapter 27 — Friday

**Friday.** Perry Dunne's date arrives — the one the Chapter 21 conversation produced, not the one he originally asked for, and he has had a week to think about what he got. Goal: reduce the crew without breaking the ward. Resistance: expertise leaves, someone competent is leaving for a reason that is not anger, and the residents discover what they can and cannot do alone. Turn: Anja Ro, Petar Lisk, Ru Sabar, and the cooperative carry a real shift, and the ward is briefly better run by people who are not trained for it. End with Elias being told, in plain words, that he is not the reason the ward works and that this is not a compliment.

### Chapter 28 — Adare Comes Back

**Friday night,** hours after the crew thinned. The broker returns with a real offer and a real threat. Goal: keep the refused branch route out of the Quarter. Resistance: he is polite, useful, and can name a person whose family wants out; the offer is cheaper and safer than anything the team can offer; refusing costs somebody an option. Turn: the team refuses again under worse conditions, and the refusal is witnessed and signed a second time, and Nadi Bell's refusal from Chapter 16 becomes the precedent that holds. End with the question of what the Office does about a broker operating inside a provisional classification.

### Chapter 29 — What the Records Can Bear

**Saturday.** Nell's carrier, the sealed Office data node, and the paper disagree about something that matters. Goal: decide what to do with an unsent promise and an incomplete signal. Resistance: the node is a copy the team can no longer fully control; a signal fragment arrives that is not a request for rescue; Elias wants to file the envelope and Joon and Mara both refuse. Turn: Elias does not send it, and the reason he gives is honest, and the envelope stays in the notebook. End with a live question about whether the maintenance route is a way to reach Nell or a way to close her branch.

### Chapter 30 — The Ward Holds a Shift

**Sunday.** A working session, not a ceremony, and the batch's decision. Goal: decide what the team prepares next under distributed authority and what each domain will refuse. Resistance: the review board's survey requirement, the 2031 node, the missing second signature, Group D, and the fact that the Chapter 25 exit cannot be planned around because it is not there. Turn: the team prepares a distributed survey or explicitly holds the site, and Elias signs a promise another person is empowered to break. End with a concrete next action for Chapters 31–40 and a live question about the broker, the sealed node, and the possible-Elias voice.
