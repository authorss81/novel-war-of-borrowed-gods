## Chapter-by-chapter direction

### Chapter 11 — What the Survivors Need

Begin the morning after the refuge move with ordinary care: antibiotics, meals, sleep, wound checks, oxygen, and the practical problem of moving twenty-three people without losing track of who chose what. Give Nadi, Tarin, Oren, Ansel, and at least one Group A survivor specific actions and speech rather than treating them as a count. Goal: stabilize the first refuge and establish what each person needs before a re-entry decision. Resistance: limited supplies, residents’ privacy, the Office’s demand for a consolidated list, and patients who do not want their names published. Turn: at least one survivor refuses public exposure while accepting care, proving that consent and rescue are separate. End with a bounded morning care plan and a request from Joon to compare the records in public.

### Chapter 12 — The Map Under Dispute

Use the refuge table to compare the paper map, measurements, survivor memories, raw audio, and the Civic Spine index. Goal: make the contradictions legible without forcing one explanation. Resistance: the terminal can produce a clean route only by deleting evidence; survivors remember different corridors; Elias’s hand makes fine drawing unreliable. Turn: the team agrees that the service door’s unusability is measurable, but cannot decide whether it shifted, closed, or became unsafe. End with a specific need to inspect the pre-collapse maintenance route entry before the Office seals the original logs.

### Chapter 13 — MNT-4C-17

Continue from Chapter 10, where Joon copied `MNT-4C-17` under two signatures and wrote the question **WHO ENTERED THE ROUTE BEFORE THE COLLAPSE?** The code has already been surfaced and preserved; this chapter investigates it and must not stage a second discovery scene or re-reveal the code. Inspect the 05:17 route entry and establish that `MNT-4C-17` is an internal maintenance credential rather than a Civic Spine author record. Tomas should recognize the format or its era without claiming to know the owner. Goal: learn what the second field could have authorized. Resistance: missing paper, copied credentials, a damaged relay, and Joon’s fear of turning suspicion into a public accusation. Turn: the code predates the collapse and points to someone or some system that touched the route before the public alarm. Do not identify Elias, Tomas, Sable, Nell, or a faction. End with the code copied into a duplicate ledger and a bounded question about who held the credential.

### Chapter 14 — The Form That Was Ready

Compare Sable’s 05:24 equipment-failure form with the 05:17 route entry, the 05:40 first alarm, and the 06:27 rescue report. Sable must give a real administrative explanation for the early classification while accepting that the timestamp is suspicious. Goal: obtain copies, witness statements, and a comparison that the Office cannot dismiss as panic. Resistance: Sable controls access to some logs; Elias’s refusal to surrender the original notebook may look like obstruction; Mara insists that the medical record include the loan’s exact boundary and return. Turn: the team proves the classification was prepared before the public event, but not who prepared it or why. End with Sable offering a controlled re-entry in exchange for access to the originals, which Elias and the team do not yet accept.

### Chapter 15 — The Local Vote

Bring Inez and the Cinder Quarter residents into the re-entry discussion. Goal: decide whether the refuge will support another entry and what local people receive, control, or refuse. Resistance: residents fear the Office, the tunnel, and being turned into infrastructure; some want the rescue team gone; Inez will not let Elias turn consent into a verbal command. Turn: residents approve only a limited survey or support role, with a withdrawal rule and no borrowed circuit, while a minority refuses. End with a split plan in which Mara controls medical triage, Tomas structural limits, Joon records, Inez local power, and Elias physical movement without sole command.

### Chapter 16 — The Bell Row

Continue from Chapter 9, where Nadi already gave Joon her sister’s contact and the pre-collapse west-apron shuttle number, and the paper manifest’s Bell row was found to be missing from the current digital response list. Both facts are established canon; do not stage the handover or the discovery again. This chapter is the consequence: the team tries to reach the relative through phone and transit systems that route through the Civic Spine, and an unlicensed broker offers to restore the row, sell a workaround, or move Nadi’s group through a branch route in exchange for using them as anchors. Goal: confirm a living relative remembers Nadi and the shuttle without exposing her group to the Office or to a broker. Resistance: routed calls, a relative who refuses to believe the official list, Nadi’s refusal to have her name made into proof of a larger theory, and a broker who is polite, useful, and dangerous. Turn: the relative confirms the family and the shuttle from memory; the broker is refused; the digital row stays missing and the discrepancy stays documented. End with Joon preserving both records and Elias recognizing that a missing record is a civic injury, not merely a puzzle.

### Chapter 17 — The Same Relay

Tomas admits, in a private but consequential conversation, that the 2034 Kestrel collapse involved the same buried relay or its paired maintenance system. Goal: understand what history can safely be disclosed before re-entry. Resistance: Tomas fears Elias will repeat Samuel’s decision, fears the Office will use the admission to seize the crew, and cannot yet admit that his safety language helped make one-way rescue plans look safer than consent-based ones. Turn: the team gains a practical reason to inspect the relay without learning the Mercy Array’s origin or the First Silence’s cause. End with Tomas offering a support plan that can be refused and a line he will not cross again.

### Chapter 18 — The Deeper Heat

Conduct a limited thermal and structural survey toward the two deeper heat signatures. Goal: locate enough evidence to decide whether people remain alive without reopening the altered service-door route. Resistance: smoke, water, unstable concrete, contradictory readings, the Office’s single-route instruction, and no active Still Hand. If a future voice answers, it must not be future Mara repeating the Chapter 9 one-body refusal; give any answering voice its own branch-specific want, reason for answering, boundary, and consequence for refusing. Do not accept a gift and do not let a refusal be retried as a workaround. Turn: the team finds evidence of possible human presence or a changed route but cannot safely reach it in this survey. End with a choice between preserving the refuge and preparing a controlled entry.

### Chapter 19 — The Seal at the Gate

Sable and the Continuity Office move to seal Kestrel, seize equipment, or force a controlled re-entry. Goal: keep the survivors, original records, and local refuge from being placed under a single authority without consent. Resistance: Sable’s mass-casualty argument is credible; the Office has legal and technical power; Elias’s hand and the team’s divided authority make a direct confrontation foolish. Turn: Elias publishes the disagreement and accepts a bounded temporary hold or supervised document exchange, while Mara, Joon, Inez, and Tomas retain separate control over their respective records and decisions. Do not identify the Returning Hand, One-Future Choir, or Compact.

### Chapter 20 — Who Entered the Route

Make the batch climax a decision, not a victory speech. The team must choose whether to seek one more limited re-entry/survey, what evidence it will preserve, and which people have the right to refuse. Resistance: the 05:17 route entry and `MNT-4C-17` do not identify a culprit; Sable’s 05:24 form still drives the official equipment-failure classification; the deeper signatures may be alive; Nell’s signal remains incomplete; the service door’s physical status remains ambiguous. Turn or consequence: Tomas’s same-relay admission changes how the team reads the route, the Bell family row confirms that civic deletion affects living relatives, and the Office’s single approved outcome becomes a concrete institutional pressure without revealing its larger source. End with a concrete next action—prepare a controlled, distributed survey or hold the site while seeking a safer method—and a question about who used the maintenance credential before the collapse. Do not resolve Nell’s disappearance, identify the possible-Elias voice, or turn the prototype refuge into a new stage.
