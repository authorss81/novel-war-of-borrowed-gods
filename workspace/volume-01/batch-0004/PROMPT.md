## Chapter-by-chapter direction

### Chapter 31 — What He Wants
**Monday, day sixteen, the day after the working shift.** The descent the Chapter 30 board promised for Monday, run as a conversation and not a rescue, with Ansel's condition in force, Mara at the top, and the stop page fresh. Goal: tell the man behind the wall the truth about his road and ask him what he wants to do about it. Resistance: he is not required to trust the team, the team is not entitled to an answer, and the honest answer is that nobody knows whether the run will keep shortening. Turn: **he states a want in his own terms, and it is not a rescue request and not a favor** — he wants the team to keep the eleven meters on the paper, to keep coming down on the same days, and to *not* treat the road's disappearance as news they owe him. End with a decision about what the team owes a man who cannot be thanked, and with the question of whether keeping a promise to him is the same as keeping the room's power conditions.

### Chapter 32 — Tuesday
**Tuesday. Three clocks, one day.** The written statement of refusal goes to the board with its reasons on the page. Brauer rescans the aisle ledger. The routine shuttle-six dispatch confirmation is due. Goal: file a refusal, get the fourth column back, and find out about a shuttle. Resistance: the rescan is a scan and not an event, and a scan cannot restore what was never looked at; the dispatch confirmation is a smaller question and is *allowed to answer with nothing*. Turn: **the confirmation answers, and raises a worse question rather than settling one** — shuttle six did run, on the incident morning, and the confirmation carries a passenger count that does not match the four in the building, so a fifth person moved that morning in a vehicle that is now in a record and not on any list. End with the Bell row harder to close than it was, and with the refusal filed and the joint-inspection condition lapsed.

### Chapter 33 — The License
**Tuesday night.** The Office answers the broker's application and answers the refusals, and the answer is more dangerous than a no. Goal: keep the refused branch route out of the Quarter. Resistance: **Sable can lawfully license a broker, and licensing is exactly what the offer always was**, and her reason is the credible one — she cannot stop a man from routing people through a bad ground, and licensing is the only instrument that creates a record. Turn: a license comes through, with conditions, and the conditions are better than the alternative and are still coercive. End with the institutional position stated without a cartoon and with the first genuinely ambiguous institutional offer of the volume sitting on the table.

### Chapter 34 — The Offer
**Wednesday.** Sable makes the volume's real offer: a controlled re-entry with Office instruments, Office chain, and a medical standard, in exchange for the originals — the notebook, the raw audio, and the sealed node's key. Goal: weigh an offer that is actually good. Resistance: it would get somebody out of the wall on a legal chain, and it would end the only custody arrangement the team has. Turn: **the team does not refuse it flatly and does not accept it**; it counteroffers, and the counteroffer costs something specific that is named. End with Sable's answer, and with the discovery that somebody has already used the licensed broker's route once, successfully, with nobody in it who was on a list.

### Chapter 35 — The Circuit
**Thursday.** The broker's operation is found: a coerced circuit, people standing in a tension who were not told what it was, and a route through a branch that a licensed broker is now allowed to offer. Goal: stop it without making the team the thing that shut the route. Resistance: the people in the tension include someone who wants out and someone who wants to stay, and the team has no standing over either. Turn: the circuit is interrupted without an anchor being broken by force, and the interruption is **cooperative, partial, and witnessed**, and it costs somebody their route. End with the reason the broker's circuit keeps working, which is that it is cheaper and safer than anything the team can offer, and which is the Chapter 28 offer again at a larger scale.

### Chapter 36 — The Cost of the Good Offer
**Friday.** The consequences of stopping the circuit and of the counteroffer, in money, in medical triage, and in one relationship. Goal: pay for Chapter 34 and Chapter 35 honestly. Resistance: Inez's power budget, Mara's triage, and Tomas's structural limits all fail at once, and one of them fails because the team chose it. Turn: a name goes into a record that somebody did not want in one, and it goes in because the team kept a promise, and nobody in the room is sure that was right. End with a new term on the wall and a new person holding a stop.

### Chapter 37 — Saturday
**Saturday.** Care, recovery, and the ordinary labor of a ward that has been running for a long time. Goal: keep the room a room. Resistance: the twenty-three are changing state — some are ready to move, some are not, and the Office wants a number; the dark hours; Ru Sabar's absent laundrette. Turn: **someone other than the team makes the decision about the twenty-three and is right**, and the team accepts it. End with the paper count moving for the first time since the collapse, and Joon's record updated in the same scene as the person moves.

### Chapter 38 — Saturday Night
**Saturday night, day twenty-one.** The possible-Elias audio is **played again** in a room. Goal: work out whether the 0.4 seconds means anything. Resistance: **Joon has already forbidden the room from meaning anything**, and the temptation is to overrule the person who forbade it. Turn: the team respects the refusal, and learns something anyway, from a place that is not the audio. **The file and the voice are not the same thing and this chapter may not collapse them: it stays raw audio — eleven seconds, the same warning as Chapters 6, 7 and 12, no identity, no faction, no gift, no new words.** Nothing here may render the possible-Elias voice as a person who has newly spoken, and the new fact learned must come from somewhere else in the room. End with the identity of the voice still unknown and one new, very small, very specific fact about the *copy* that nobody can yet use.

### Chapter 39 — The Old Relay
**Sunday.** Nell's signal points at the old relay rather than at a simple rescue. Goal: decide what to do with a direction instead of a place. Resistance: the direction is a temptation of exactly the kind the volume has been refusing for three weeks, and Elias is the person most likely to break. Turn: **a future Nell's Door Memory is heard**, through the broker's circuit, not through the wall man, and it is a route that exists in her branch and would cost the people already there. End with the want, the boundary, and the reason she answered, all three stated in her own terms.

### Chapter 40 — The Route That Is Not Home
**Monday, day twenty-three, the last chapter of the *Borrowed Roads* act.** Goal: decide what to do with a route. Resistance: the route would get somebody out; using it as a retrieval command would close the branch Nell is protecting; and the volume's own rule is that a present person may not be required to authorize a future self's capacity, in either direction. Turn: **Elias refuses to use it as a retrieval command, and the refusal is a refusal as a route and not an acceptance as a loan** — no capacity is taken, no boundary is stretched, and nothing is signed. The route remains a brief and incomplete clue, and the batch's decision is the one the volume has been earning. End with the volume's concrete next movement named plainly — Chapters 41–50, **The Sealed Choice** — so the next batch has its subject on the page, and with the three live questions the last forty chapters end on.
