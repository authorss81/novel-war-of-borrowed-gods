## What the batch is

**Nobody is lying, and that is the whole of it.** A regional pilot is clearing a district called Tidegate for a sea wall and is using a load reading that is accurate and that saved the district. The team goes there for five days to find a hand in a document, because for twelve weeks every document Elias Rook has met has had a hand in it, **and there is not one.** Instead there is a competent method officer who likes being asked, a competent program with a competent public face, an accurate measurement, a correct classification, and a footnote in italics that says the measurement is indicative and the list is the operative document.

**The volume's sentence is spoken exactly once, in Chapter 106, by Tomas Vale, on Ash Street, about method, with Joon Park writing it down as it is said, and nobody asks him a single question about Tidegate and Elias does not ask him anything at all: a reading tells you where force goes, a schedule tells you who is a household, they are not the same quantity, somebody has to convert one into the other, and that is where people disappear.** A woman at the back says *that's just* and nobody answers her, **and that was correct, because it is not just anything and it is also obvious and no sentence a room can produce will hold both of those at once.**

## The ten things that happened

1. **Chapter 101.** The board at seven; **the wall is full** and Petar Lisk says so — *not for a line and not for a name; when it is finished there will be a space the shape of a shoe and whoever comes in here after me is going to think somebody was tidy.* The three questions are read and are not answered. **The Rescue Service letter is one paragraph and puts a restoration and three revocations in the same paragraph in that order, and does not say the word career.** Elias takes the 11 at ten past eight on his own time with a week's inventory sheet he then files, and a man in a vest gives him a leaflet on **better paper than anything the Continuity Office has put on a table in eleven weeks**, with a Director's name at the bottom.
2. **Chapter 102.** **Winifred Alagoa, 63, at 22 Cutbank Row, shuts a door on him on a Sunday morning and it is not rudeness.** Four hours in a kitchen. The sheet: 1,163 addresses since the fourth of March 2031, against a board's 1,140. **A count of people is a thing that can be out by a person; a list of houses is a thing that can be out by a house; and a person cannot be inside the wrong number.** She lets him write down that the sheet exists and refuses the numbers. **Nobody has ever asked her about it in eleven years, and that is the answer that costs him.**
3. **Chapter 103.** Two rooms above a shop, four people, and a man who says *Rook. You have got one hand. — I have. — Good. Do not sign anything. It saves a step.* **Elias recognizes step four of the eight pages in one sentence and nobody in the room reacts**, because the resemblance is to a building two miles away. The Retention Schedule comes out of a drawer **unasked**, nineteen pages, four columns, **no column a person goes in**, 412 and 285, footnote in italics on page two. He reads the footnote on a bus and does not understand it, **and the reason is that on the first of December he does not know there is a second reading.**
4. **Chapter 104.** The depot at 06:30, his own Tuesday slot, the hose passes, **two figures in R. Dunleavy's own hand and nothing said**, and it is the first thing in thirteen weeks that has gone right in front of him and had not been him. **Tidegate is designated at a quarter to eight and the designation activates a line a man wrote about himself in November.** Inez Sato tells Tomas Vale in a corridor because nobody would telephone a man who cannot be asked, and comes back with one sentence and two more, and Elias does not go. **A Director's letter: the board has no procedure for a third category and does not intend to build one.** Mara hands him a chemist who runs out of money in January. Joon Park says nothing out loud about the shape of an observer clause, in daylight, and asks for the not-saying to be written down. **And in a corridor at ten past three he says out loud to a wall: nobody in that building is doing anything crooked and there is nothing for me to hold.**
5. **Chapter 105 (midpoint).** Six hours in a two-room flat on Cutbank Row with a woman of seventy-one who is not angry, does not want anything, and does not need him. **They are not putting me anywhere. They are putting my address somewhere, and I am at the address.** `PARTIAL` is correct and the correctness is the whole of it. He was four seconds from opening the folder and puts it on a chair behind him instead, and then **sits there for the rest of the afternoon and is no use at all.** At six in the evening he says *if there is anything you need* and stops, and she has already told him three people have said it since September.
6. **Chapter 106.** The card, dated on the back in pencil so she knows how old it is. The second reading, **29 November**, laid over the September roll on a trestle: **four inches apart along two streets. Forty-four addresses. Every one `TRANSITIONAL`.** Anum's operational reason, once, and he will not give it twice, and **Elias does not ask twice and nobody in the room sees it.** Hobbs says the one thing she will say in a corridor and **he lets her walk, on purpose.** The error out loud, to a man who does not care. **The footnote's last line is a field on a document dated three weeks later and there is no box on that page the twenty-ninth of November could have gone in, and he has the right question and not the sentence, and the sentence is somebody else's and is not his.** Then Ash Street at nine o'clock, nine people, and the sentence, and *that's just*, and no answer, and Joon Park writing it in the margin of the same sheet as the other twenty-two lines.
7. **Chapter 107.** A form with four boxes and a year box that is a menu that overrides the span. **Joon Park leaves it empty because he needs 2039 and 2042 and 2042 is the year the answer is *we did not do anything until December*, and it will be true and he will not be able to do one thing with it.** A counterwoman who tells him that four to six weeks is not the service refusing him but the service being able to tell him what it did. **He says both dates out loud to thirty people in the middle of a conversation about a light fitting and does not put them on the board, and Petar says put it on the board and is refused.** Then Tidegate, a church hall, forty people, and **the six weeks given out first, before anything about the schedule — step four, used on a promise instead of a plan for the first time** — and a man who asks whether six weeks is worth it, and the answer that is *I do not know.*
8. **Chapter 108.** Offices shut. **A woman reads a sheet of addresses out loud at a bollard on a Saturday morning for an hour, the way she has done since 2031, and about thirty-one people stop, and not one of them thinks it is remarkable, and Elias realizes on the far side of the street that the practice he has spent three volumes learning did not need him and did not need a wall.** Eleven carries with a trestle. **A woman he has never met: *you're the one that got off the eleven on Tuesday. — With the folder. — Yes. — Right.*** Then he opens the folder in a yard in front of nine people and **stops in the middle of a sentence**, because he has no version history, no forum and no standing, and nine strangers would believe him because he has a folder. **His hand goes on Nev Peart's shift book and comes off.**
9. **Chapter 109.** The clearance record is **three printed lines on the outside of a shut gate** and it has been there since the ninth of November and nobody has taken it down and nobody hid it. **Four names in a margin half an inch wide, in a different pencil, written with a kitchen pencil at eleven at night by a woman who was asked, politely, by a cell officer with a torch she did not need, whether anybody at 3 to 27 was elderly and living alone.** The works board says forty-one households; her sheet says thirty-nine doors; **the gap of two is never closed and the four are in neither list because neither list is a list of people.** She gives the four on her own terms — *you can have the four because the four are not on the sheet; the sheet is doors; doors do not die.* Joon Park takes them on the back of his own hand, on no schedule, with no number: **it is not a record, do not call it a record in this room, it is a pencil.** And Elias says out loud, once, that he will not use them for four days.
10. **Chapter 110.** Two hundred people, a leaflet on every chair, a microphone that works, and a man who asks the round and answers the round and then says **two hundred and eighty-five, twice, unprompted**, and four other things that are all true, including **I signed it, it is my signature and not the program's, I will sign it again in the spring, and if I am wrong about four hundred people then four hundred people find out on the morning they start, in front of everybody** — and the fourth, which is his: **the second reading came in on the twenty-ninth of November, the band moved two streets, forty-four addresses are inside September and outside November, I signed on the September reading, I have not rebuilt it, and I am not going to pretend I do not know what that sentence is.** Marged Prosser asks the best question of the night and is told the honest answer, which is an admission: **I do not have a third one.** Then Elias asks a question he knows is bad, a woman puts the microphone in front of him, **four minutes pass and nobody speaks and he does not fill them**, and Ferrand waits him out and then says: **you have been in this district four days, I have been in it nine years, you have a page in a notebook and I have four hundred and twelve addresses that are still standing, and not one of them is on any list of mine that I have had to explain to a coroner** — and offers the working folder, on a Monday, and it is not a favor, and Elias says *thank you* because there are two hundred people in the room and it was the only one available. **The back of his notebook for the week has one line on it and it is a date: 2 JANUARY.**
