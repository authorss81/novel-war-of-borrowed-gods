# Chapter Summaries

## Volume 01 — Chapter Summaries

### Chapter 1 — The Dead Track
Elias Rook begins an ordinary Kestrel signal-and-door inspection and finds a train moving on a decommissioned track, a service door with handles on both sides, a clock repeating the same minute, and Nell’s direct warning from inside the station. He establishes a physical retreat line, identifies the changing load path, and enters the service landing under ordinary procedures. The carriage appears behind an impossible wall, and a woman’s voice calls Elias from inside it.

### Chapter 2 — Keep One Breath
The collapse leaves fifteen Car 3 passengers trapped inside the buckled carriage, with Tarin Voss unresponsive and pinned beneath a steel handrail driven through his lower chest. Elias reads future Mara’s Still Hand offer aloud, records the present Mara’s limited emergency-treatment consent, and accepts the one-body loan for Tarin while Mara repairs the injury. The gift keeps Tarin alive but does not heal him; the operation creates an unmapped service door as aftermath, while Elias loses sensation in his left hand.

### Chapter 3 — The Door Everyone Used
With the Still Hand still active, Mara sets the movement order for the Car 3 survivors and Elias delegates rope work, movement decisions, and mechanical anchoring rather than trying to control everyone himself. The changed service door remains unstable and depends on ordinary braces, lines, and judgment; passengers remember incompatible versions of the corridor. The last survivors cross, the door narrows into a dark seam, and the terminal and paper map disagree about whether the route remains.

### Chapter 4 — The Record of a Rescue
Elias helps move Group A into surface triage and discovers that Joon’s manifest assigned sixteen Car 3 seats while the physical carriage count is fifteen. He records measurements, tool marks, conflicting memories, and raw audio, then admits that he did not disclose the branch-specific afterimage that may affect his judgment. Mara keeps her consent limited to present treatment, while Joon preserves copies. A live rail-engineer signal from Nell reaches them with a warning that the north stair is a branch, not an exit.

### Chapter 5 — The North Stair
Elias, Mara, Tomas, and Joon follow the branch-crossed signal through the still-active operation, refusing to ask future Mara to extend the one-body gift when Oren Pike is found alive in a maintenance pocket. Future Mara’s route memory is explicitly partial, and Elias chooses thermal imaging, rope, shoring, and a breadcrumb trail over a faster pursuit of Nell’s signal. Oren reaches the refuge, but the north stair and a second possible door disappear before they can be mapped.

### Chapter 6 — When the Third Wheel Stops
At the temporary refuge, Groups A and B bring the physical count to sixteen. The maintenance trolley’s third wheel stops, the Still Hand returns automatically, and Elias does not bargain for more time; the service door becomes structurally unusable and drops out of the immediate route index, although its physical absence is not proven. Joon and Tomas preserve measurements and paper records, and an unidentified male voice emerges from the altered wall and raw audio warning, “Do not make the map agree.”

### Chapter 7 — No Answer
In the surface triage yard, Elias tests whether the completed Still Hand exchange can be reopened and receives a deliberate `No answer`, not a relay failure. The raw relay carries a possible-Elias voice asking him to preserve contradictory paper and audio rather than force agreement; Elias refuses to treat the voice as proof of a faction. The team builds a battery-backed communications setup, duplicate maps, and a consent-recording anchor lamp while accepting that no supernatural capacity is available.

### Chapter 8 — The Office at the Door
Sable Arden arrives with a credible safety argument for sealing Kestrel but a form already classifying the event as equipment failure before the first rail alarm. Elias gives copies while keeping the original notebook and raw recording, while Mara and Joon retain control of the medical and communications records. Inez Sato offers local power and shelter only after residents consent, can withdraw, and keep local control; the team’s first refuge begins as a conditional civic partnership. A thermal survey finds two weak signatures behind a wall the terminal calls empty, and Nell’s signal repeats.

### Chapter 9 — The People Behind the Wall
With no active Still Hand, Elias, Mara, Tomas, and Joon reach Group C and extract seven people from the altered service alcove using ordinary braces, rope, thermal tools, and oxygen. Future Mara briefly answers Elias’s request for an extension but deliberately refuses to reopen the completed exchange or cover more than one body; Elias accepts the refusal and gives up the cleanest route toward Nell. Nadi Bell and Ansel reach the refuge, the paper count reaches twenty-three, and the Civic Spine reports zero Car 3 passengers despite the physical rescue.

### Chapter 10 — The Names on Paper
The team moves the twenty-three survivors into Inez’s Cinder Quarter refuge, where residents approve power circuits, record refusals, and retain withdrawal rights while a prototype uses batteries, lamps, seam glass, maps, and barriers. Sable presses for an equipment-failure report; Mara establishes a rule for disclosing every future offer and refusal, and Elias writes a reply to Nell, does not send it, and does not claim that the signal is a route home. Joon finds the pre-collapse route entry’s second internal maintenance code, `MNT-4C-17`, and the team decides to compare the records before deciding whether another entry is justified; two deeper heat signatures remain.

### Chapter 11 — What the Survivors Need
The morning after the move is spent on antibiotics, mats, one oxygen cylinder, and a demand for a consolidated list that nobody will fill. Elias works the care line rather than the plan, announces every time his left hand fails, and hands each job to someone else. Tarin Voss is told the whole truth about the loan and asks for his shoes. Niko Senn asks that his name not be published and dictates the wording. Oren Pike asks for a photograph of his own chart. Ansel Duarte refuses to be photographed or argued with. Nadi Bell asks whether she may make a call and is told that every line is logged. Mara makes the antibiotic decision without Elias in it. The twenty-three stop being a count, and Joon clears the long table so the records can be compared where they can be seen.

### Chapter 12 — The Map Under Dispute
The long table becomes a hearing room in front of the twenty-three. Elias cannot draw the door to measurement, says so out loud, and hands the pencil to Joon. Four survivors remember incompatible corridors and are told, without being disbelieved, that the conflict is recorded and not resolved. Anja Ro identifies herself as the woman in the orange coat. The Civic Spine offers a `Suggested correction` that would delete the 05:42–06:18 entries to make the index agree; it is refused, photographed, and signed. The room hears the raw possible-Elias audio. The page that results has three columns — unsafe, shifted, physically absent — only the first of which is proven. A 1600 site-archive notice makes the 05:17 paper route book the immediate clock.

### Chapter 13 — MNT-4C-17
At the west apron relay, the paper log has already been lifted from its felt slot by a hand that knew the clip, and the card reader can be read only once. The single read returns a route entry for sector 4C under `MNT-4C-17`, sequence four, at 05:17:11. Tomas explains the credential format and its pre-2035 class-three limits, which include an unindexed local route entry and no authority over alarms or passenger records. A mechanical fault tape shows two ticks before any alarm, 05:17.11 and 05:24.40, and the team takes a graphite impression rather than removing the tape. Joon argues the code off the public wall and gets a formulation that is true and accuses no one. Three copies go out, one of them into a residents’ lock-box, and the roster that could name a holder turns out to be in a building they cannot enter.

### Chapter 14 — The Form That Was Ready
The site’s paper records go into Office crates at 1600 with a clerk writing **LOG ABSENT PRIOR TO TRANSFER** on the transfer sheet. The next morning’s supervised exchange in the hall produces faithful copies and one refusal: the notebook stays with the team, and the Office is offered a supervised reading instead. Sable gives a real administrative explanation for the 05:24 timestamp — a standing classification pack for a designated seam site and a genuine 04:50 patrol fault — and then produces the dispatch sheet showing a Continuity team signed out for 4C at 05:02, with a filled second signature on the original and a holder field the 2035 renumbering never carried forward. Mara dictates the full loan record, including the borrower’s undisclosed afterimage and the refusal of an extension, and makes the Office sign it. Eleven witness statements are taken with their conditions attached. Sable offers a controlled re-entry for the originals; Elias declines, and Sable says her form is an indictment of everyone who came before her.

### Chapter 15 — The Local Vote
Inez calls a meeting and refuses to let Elias brief it. Residents speak first: an unasked laundrette power bank, a request to move the Office truck off a street, and a refrigeration tech who wants the crew gone by Friday because his whole life has been reorganized around a hole in the ground. Cards go round per person, including per crew member. Thirty-one consent, nine refusal, four abstain, one card filled twice. Every refusal is read aloud, including a father’s stated preference about his daughter sleeping. Seven conditions are agreed, including that a blackout means the team goes dark too. The authority split is written on the hall wall with Elias’s line — physical movement, and no sole command — added by him and tapped twice in chalk. Nadi Bell closes the chapter by pricing what a phone call about her sister will cost.

### Chapter 16 — The Bell Row
On Lena Mir’s loan of the clinic wall for the hour between nine and ten, with a two-minute warning given partway through, Yolanda Bell remembers signing the west apron run sheets and recites the series 9-1-7-4 from memory, and the carbon stub in Nadi’s hands carries the same four digits. Yolanda has done her own arithmetic: the official list says four people from that building and there were five. At the transit operations desk, clerk Bex Amadi confirms the stub physically and freezes any query that touches the incident, so a routine dispatch confirmation is pending in nine days. **R. Adare**, a polite broker, offers to restore the digital row, sell an unlogged line, and move seven people out of the Quarter that night using the seven of them as anchors. Nadi refuses and has the refusal written and signed; Elias does not offer her sister’s name and loses the cleaner argument for it. The file is copied into five buildings, and Elias records that a missing row is a civic injury rather than a puzzle.

### Chapter 17 — The Same Relay
Tomas asks for a conversation out of earshot and admits that the 2034 collapse began with the same east-leg draw and the same slab cycle, over a buried paired node under the west apron that was written off in 2035 with his signature second on the page. He was on the 2029 panel that wrote the one-voice safety rule, he signed the reroute that left Samuel Rook holding a frame, and he has been giving Elias the softer version of it for eight years. He admits the rule was written by frightened competent people to stop a real thing, that his caution may be part of the problem, and that he has not yet earned the right to say so. He offers a five-name, refusable, expiring stop plan and two lines he will not cross again. Elias asks for the expiry date so the plan cannot become a procedure with his name on it.

### Chapter 18 — The Deeper Heat
A six-hour, hard-cut survey of the west apron and lower junction finds a 2031 repair pour running eleven meters and terminating six meters short of the camera target, plus a brick lining at seven meters on the bore line, and a gas curve that reads like a sealed room rather than a fire. **Halla Dren**, a shaft and outfall foreman nineteen years ahead, answers with `No loan offered` and `Warning: the branch is changing`, lends nothing, gives the location of the pour, and asks that the shaft not be named in any consolidated list because a named location becomes one to be closed. Joon records the request and refuses it in the same hand on the same page. A person behind the wall answers three knocks in the code a school safety week taught Elias. At 12:40 the slab moves four millimeters on Tomas’s pre-written threshold and Elias, not Tomas, calls the stop. They return with a sounding map, a gas curve, a camera disagreement, a rebar coupon, and a recording of knocking.

### Chapter 19 — The Seal at the Gate
The Office arrives with a containment order, a seal crew, and a survey van, and Sable brings two things: a 2031 contractor survey putting a migrating void two hundred and ten meters from the clinic, and the 2019 Salt Crossing record in which an unlicensed crew opened a shaft and six people died. She also brings the crew sheet — a Continuity team signed out for 4C at 05:02, a first signature she can place, and a second signature the 2035 renumbering never carried forward. Mara, Tomas, Joon, and Inez each state their own terms before Elias speaks; his own part is the smallest, and he tells a **Quarter Sheet** reporter on the record that he does not know which account becomes the story. A formal objection with attached evidence is filed into the 1800 civic digest unedited. Sable offers ninety-six hours with no seal, no seizure, and a provisional classification, and it is accepted with separate custody in every domain. Inez writes down what the hold cost, including Perry Dunne’s unmoved Friday.

### Chapter 20 — Who Entered the Route
A working session starting at ten to six reads the Office’s published digest answer aloud, including *one approved outcome* backed by eleven real fatalities, and finds nobody in the room able to dismiss it. Joon reports that Nell’s signal carrier and the 05:17 route-entry carrier are the same physical path, and her latest fragment is four words: the east branch holds. The team decides against any entry, prepares a passive listening campaign with no medic on the line and a success condition of a conversation rather than an extraction, and forwards a citizens’ request for a joint structural inspection so that a refusal becomes a document. Halla Dren’s request, Joon’s refusal of it, and her warning go on the wall in that order. The envelope to Nell moves from Elias’s coat into the notebook marked not filed and not sent. A five-signature promise goes on the wall: no descent alone, four of five must agree in the same week, and the hall may stop it. The question at the end has three lines and still no name.

### Chapter 21 — The Finding
On the hold's third day **Hollis Vane** returns to the union hall with the four-page finding he wrote on Thursday, because the hold said he would write it in the room. **Finding 1**: the equipment-failure classification is not supported by the Office's own evidence, because the cause box arrives pre-ticked from the standing site pack and no field observation overwrote it before the 06:27 rescue report. **Finding 2**: the file cannot be revised in any respect depending on credential attribution, because the 4C roster has no holder field, which makes the hole the only thing a board can act on. **Finding 3** states that a named subject may withdraw the named field of a record without withdrawing the event — and **Tarin Voss** withdraws the field with his name on it, because *the body* is the word for a thing a loan gets used on. Vane records the withdrawal as a finding and not as an absence. **Niko Senn** withdraws a second page because its note says *declined publication* instead of the wording he dictated. The finding goes up on the wall with the Office's 1800 digest answer pinned directly underneath it, eleven inches apart, so the institution is on the record disagreeing with itself in one building. The team asks the board for the depot box rather than for exoneration and lodges it before the board sits. Then Inez settles the clock: the hold's last day is Saturday and the board sits Saturday afternoon, so Perry Dunne's clean week moves to the following Friday, and Perry says plainly that he did not move it to be helpful.

### Chapter 22 — A Ward With a Name On It
The hold's fourth day is ordinary labor and ordinary money. The Office truck is gone from Ash Street; **Denny Prokop** sells 240 m of good cable at 8,800 and admits in writing that *seam-rated* is his own marketing and not a rating for what the hall needs. The 1,100 dollars left in the money is a block wall, a door and a lock, and **Inez** names the wall as **a quarter** of a **4,400-dollar** requirement to make the building a building, with the other 3,300 on a list nobody would enjoy reading. **Ru Sabar** says the thing the room has been avoiding: the team has been treating the lower service run like a wound for six days, and it is the only way anything gets into the hall that will not fit through Ash Street. An Office access instrument signed by **Ilse Brauer** allows ninety minutes of non-invasive survey because Hollis Vane's Finding 2 has left the Office with no photograph of ground it is about to make a finding about. Elias wants to refuse the price of the data; **Tomas** overrules him, because a route with no paper is a route any institution can deny. The run is re-opened, laser-measured at **31.0 m**, photographed, and signed by **Emil Baros** as named keeper, resident, with **Petar Lisk** witnessing and Brauer observing; the keeper's name goes into the sealed node in free-text remarks because there is no field for it. Nine pallets of supplies go down it that afternoon. In the afternoon the **review board sits**: no seal, no clearance, the classification stays provisional, the citizens' request becomes a survey condition with an eleventh-day deadline, and the 05:02 dispatch original is ordered produced. Sable rejects Vane's Finding 4 by name as a request rather than a finding. The run is on the paper in three places, walked that day by a named person with a witness.

### Chapter 23 — The Listening Campaign
The day after the board, six hours on the bore line at the lower junction: no medic on the line, no door, no break, no opening, and Ansel Duarte's success condition read aloud by Petar Lisk. Four hours of nothing, during which Joon refuses to write *possible contact* for a relay click and records *acoustic event, unidentified, no contact* instead, and Elias is the first person to want to stop listening and the last to admit it. Nobody asks Halla Dren anything and the log says so. At 14:51 a **present-day voice** — not an echo, not a future, not a gift — says the team is the third group that has listened. It gives no name, no count and no condition, and asks for none. During contact the terminal displays a recommended re-entry and `Aftermath ahead: route will not survive another opening`, and nobody acts on it. The three wants: keep the run on the paper, send nobody in, and write nothing more than *voice at the wall*. The reason he answered is that the team published its own objection, so there was a record on his side of the concrete that could be argued with. A boundary with teeth: if one person goes into the run it shuts, because a run with a body in it is a run that frightened people close. He names **Emil Baros** and confirms he was in the alcove with the others, and says he can see the run's length, the dip at nine meters, and the door with two handles, and nothing else — he says so himself. He gives no first names, no count and no page reference, and the reason he answered at all is that the team published its own objection and gave him something to argue with.

### Chapter 24 — The Box at the Depot
The day after the board, the team uses the archive access it was granted, lawfully and awkwardly, to reach the 05:02 dispatch original in the Spine depot: box 214, out at 11:04 and back at 11:41 on two cameras, with **Hoyt** warning in front of a recorder that asking is not free and **Joon** refusing to make the request look small. The second signature on the sheet is **not a name — it is `MNT-4C-17`**, written in a box that asks for a person, and a class-three maintenance credential cannot lawfully close a two-person Continuity inspection. **Hollis Vane** draws the distinction himself: the same credential, not the same hand, and he will not let those two be confused. The **aisle log** shows box 214 opened on the day of the collapse at **14:12** under *Rout. arch. rev.* with the **subject field blank** — a field that exists, which Vane contrasts with a field that does not: a blank field is not a hole, it is a place where a name can later go without anybody lying. Vane hands over a copy because the board ordered production, and says plainly that he is not on anybody's side. While the room is still arguing about the blank subject field and before anybody leaves the archive room, **Brauer makes the Office's own copy of the aisle log page on the flatbed** — a bound single-sided ledger, one page at a time, the book never turned, the fourth column running past the edge of the glass — and initials the scan time without looking at the frame, because she is filing it and not reading it. Nine pallets of block then go down the run that afternoon at **15:00**, walked by Emil Baros and witnessed by **Anja Ro**, counted twice at 31.0 m, recorded in three places. Inez writes that the archive is now part of the problem and not only the answer.

### Chapter 25 — The Exit That Is Not There
The day after the corridor was used, the **keeper** finds it: the lower service run ends at **11.0 meters** and there is a wall. Continuous original concrete, a square-cut paint band, a length of emergency-strip conduit stopping in midair with a bright unweathered end, two meters of polished floor from a hundred and sixty boots, a scuff arc at ten meters, a floor anchor hole with a boot brace in the dust, and a right handprint at 1.40 m. The inspection niche at twelve is gone, with the survey sheet still clipped inside it. The terminal returns `no match — not in surveyed network` and a first recorded instance of 12:31 that week, because the team's own survey was the first system that ever held the run. **Tomas** stops Elias from re-measuring: you can measure the rest of it for a year and learn exactly how much is not there, which is not the same as finding out why. **Emil Baros** refuses to be a second column under the instrument, then tells the room more than the laser had, including a bar of soap he carried out of a niche in the winter of 'thirty-nine and a door he has never once been past in eleven years. **Mara** puts the honest finding on the ledger: the span that has ever carried anybody in a record coincides with the Still Hand window, the return is a wheel in a countersigned ledger, and there is no version of Saturday in which sixteen people come out without the loan — so we did not break it, we are being held to the same standard as everybody else, which is that it did not stay. The three-column page comes down into the file under a cover sheet saying it is still true about the door. **THE LOWER SERVICE RUN — WHAT IS TRUE** goes up: two columns, Saturday and Tuesday, four lines underneath, no cause, and an explicit statement that the service door is not in the table and its status is unchanged.

### Chapter 26 — The Slower Plan
Wednesday into Thursday. Elias writes a one-page plan in forty minutes with *ONE VOICE ON THE RADIO* at the bottom, and **Inez** kills it with one sentence: it is the same sentence with a different noun. She then cuts the power in front of the room and shows him that the medical bank and the lamp bank are on the same feeder, so twelve hours of "committed" load is not commitment but pressure wearing commitment's clothes. **Tomas and Mara** argue for the first time in a week — Mara will not put a medical protocol in a document because a paper read aloud above ground becomes an instruction; Tomas says a protocol you did not write is a reroute and he wrote the rule that made a man hold a frame. They settle on two refusals instead of a protocol: no medic on the line, and no predicted outcome anywhere in the document, which means the plan contains no answer to what happens if somebody is found. What comes out is four pages, none of them Elias's: four separate carded power draws a day, each revocable while running; a target list with no sequence; five domains and no coordinator; a standing refusal. **Ru Sabar** names it **THE STRAWB MAN PLAN** and withdraws her laundrette circuit permanently, and the cost is written in Inez's hand beside it — the hall dark from 0200 to 0400 every day. The count: forty-two cards in, twenty-two consent, fourteen refusal, six abstention, and every refusal read aloud.

### Chapter 27 — Friday
Perry Dunne's new Friday arrives and the crew thins before he does. The **Havenport Rescue Service recalls Rafi and Kellan** for a gas main, because the city has four certified confined-space rope technicians and three of them are in the hall, and because a west-bank pump station has been running unattended since the eleventh with two hundred people downstream — logistics, not anger, and Rafi says he should have raised it on Wednesday. **Perry Dunne** comes at eleven with a toolbox, refuses to be thanked, and says that what happened is that a hold ran to Saturday and five people signed a thing and Elias would not break it, which is not a favor and can be resented later. Joon puts two dates on the board: the survey-or-refusal deadline on the eleventh day, and the routine shuttle confirmation, due Tuesday, with the note that it is *allowed to be nothing*. The hall runs itself and runs it badly, and does it better than it used to: **Anja Ro** catches a two-minute error in Joon's custody log and makes him initial the fix, **Petar Lisk** finds the block wall four inches off square, **Ru Sabar** discovers the batteries are the wrong chemistry and have been run warm for a week, **Lena Mir** takes a full ward round with no physician in the building, and the residents rewrite the consent cards in plain sentences with no box ticked by default. Nobody wants a plan that day, and the reason is not forgetting. At nine, on the loading lip, **Ansel Duarte** tells Elias that he is not the reason the hall works, that this is not a compliment, and that the day everybody agrees with whatever Elias says is the day the building stops having a say.

### Chapter 28 — Adare Comes Back
Friday night, day thirteen, hours after the crew thinned. **R. Adare** returns with a **four**-anchor offer instead of seven — and opens by saying what the recall actually did, which is that three of the city's four rope tickets were in this hall on Thursday and that what is left is **one ticket and one safety instructor, and one ticket does not make a pair** — and admits himself that a smaller number in a tension is a worse tension and not a safer one, plus east-bank accommodation with three days of powered medical power for **Osei's wife**, whom **Lena Mir** has confirmed is on a non-powered clinic bed in a ward sharing a budget with a hall that is going dark two hours a night. The threat is not violence: a **broker license application** lodged inside the classification, and a form, three weeks out, in the hands of a neighbor, answered in a reasonable way by a reasonable person. Elias starts to refuse and **Nadi Bell** gets there first, and makes Adare write the whole offer down again in the same form as Tuesday, signs it, and explains why a man refused twice with two pieces of paper cannot tell a story about the refusal. Petar's scarecrow with a form on it goes up at the gate and stays up for a week. Six refusals, all written down by whoever made them, and none of them uses the word *anchor*. The chapter ends on the institutional question: brokers are licensed, not criminal, so this is a licensing question, and the record now shows that Sable Arden was told at ten past eleven on a Friday night, by name, in front of witnesses.

### Chapter 29 — What the Records Can Bear
Saturday. The three paper copies of Saturday's corridor survey — the original sheet, the residents' file copy, and the print out of the sealed node — disagree, and the one that disagrees is in a building the team cannot walk into. The Office's baseline form has **no keeper field**, so Emil Baros is present in free-text remarks and absent from every field query; and Brauer's single-pass scan of the bound aisle ledger **cut off the fourth column**, so the Office's copy of the log has no subject column at all and nobody in that building can see the absence. Joon writes the row: a field that does not exist, a field left blank, a field never designed for the thing in question — in all three the name is not erased, the name is not present. At 02:04 **Nell's fourth fragment arrives: *do not use the west run***, on the same present-day branch-crossed carrier, which runs through twenty meters that are no longer there; Joon records the carrier fact and refuses to interpret it. Elias wants to file the unsent envelope. **Joon refuses him out loud in front of witnesses** — filing makes it a record, and a record gets answered by a building. **Mara refuses him coldly** — filing a present voice makes it a search, and a search is a promise made to somebody behind a wall. Elias does not file, gives the honest reason (the only line that could carry a reply is the one she has just told him not to use), and adds that the real reason is that he is afraid of a survey. The envelope stays in the notebook, and the board carries the question nobody can answer: is the old maintenance route a way to reach her, or a way to close the branch she is keeping open?

### Chapter 30 — The Ward Holds a Shift
Sunday is a working shift, not a ceremony, and its structure is five refusals. Mara: no medic on the line, no predicted outcome, nobody moves a person for their own benefit. Tomas: no door, and his number is written before anybody goes and he does not argue with the instrument, and four other people may now say *enough*. Joon: no record in one building, nothing consolidated, nothing published that a person has not permitted. Inez: no load without a card, no unannounced blackouts, and the right to cut. Elias adds a clause nobody asked for: *I am not going to be the last person to speak on any of this, and the last word does not close the item.* **The site is held**, with the cost written in Inez's hand beside the gain: the joint-inspection condition lapses, the standing designation is unchallenged for a cycle, the 2031 node is not examined, the sealed node becomes the only version of that ground, and the man behind the wall has no way in. What they prepare instead is a method with no date on it. Elias signs a promise **Ansel Duarte is empowered to break** by saying *that is a rescue*, with no reason required and no requirement to be right, and Ansel insists the inconvenient clause stays in. The closing discovery: the copy of the possible-Elias audio in the sealed node is **0.4 seconds longer** than the team's, with a flat carrier gap in the middle, and Joon records the fact and refuses, out loud in the room, to put a name or a meaning under it. The board's last line is the next action — Tuesday's refusal, a rescanned ledger with all four columns, and a Monday descent — the board leads with Monday, not Sunday, because Chapter 30 *is* Sunday — to ask a man behind a wall what he wants to do about a road that is now eleven meters long, and then do what he says.


### Chapter 31 — What He Wants
Monday, day sixteen, the descent the Chapter 30 board promised. The argument is not about whether to go down but about who has signed: four of five, in the same week, and this week started yesterday, so Inez refuses a four o'clock departure and sends them at one. Mara signs under her two standing conditions and adds a third refusal for today — if he offers a route, a name, or a way out, Elias does not say yes in the next ten minutes. Tomas signs his number (0.4 mm at the lip) and notes that **Doss comes as far as the fence and does not go down, because one rope ticket and one safety instructor is not a pair and today is possible only because nobody is going in**. Joon signs and repeats his refusal of a meaning for the 0.4 seconds in advance, on the record, before they leave. At the loading lip, Emil Baros reports **eleven again, four times this week, not moving**, and writes it as an observer; the bar of soap from the missing niche is in his coat pocket. Elias kneels at the eleven-meter mark with a card Petar wrote, tells the man the truth in numbers rather than in weather, and is corrected twice on the wire — *in use*, not *road*; a line is live when he picks up a handset.
