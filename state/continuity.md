# Continuity State

## Opening canon

- The story opens in 2042 at Kestrel Station and the Salt Meridian. Elias Rook is 35, a Havenport Rescue Service field technician, and began Volume 01 at **Stage 0: Unborrowed**.
- Elias is competent with ordinary rigging, confined-space entry, thermal imaging, breathing gear, seam warning signs, and human retreat lines. His central movement is from solitary command toward disclosure, delegation, and bounded promises; he is not a passive vessel or an instant super-rescuer.
- Nell Rook is 27, a rail signal engineer inside Kestrel when the collapse begins. Her direct pre-collapse alarm is separate from the later clipped signal. Mara Okafor, Tomas Vale, Joon Park, Inez Sato, and Sable Arden enter with the roles fixed in the character bible. Elias and Mara are professional partners, not romantic partners at the opening or at the end of this batch.

## Timeline and physical evidence

- The first Kestrel rail alarm is recorded at **05:40**. The changed service door becomes visible at **05:42**. The pre-collapse route entry is timestamped **05:17**. Sable’s equipment-failure form is timestamped **05:24**, before the first alarm and before the door observation. The rescue report is filed at **06:27**. These times are evidence, not proof of who entered or altered the route.
- The service-door aftermath is physically evidenced by tool marks, measurements, wet footprints, a photographed/open-door record, survivor memories, and Joon’s raw audio. After the third wheel stops, the door is **structurally unusable** and drops out of the immediate Civic Spine route index. It has not been proven physically absent; shifted, unsafe, or otherwise branch-affected remains possible. Chapter 25 owns the stronger physical-absence proof.
- The route index’s report that Car 3 carried no passengers conflicts with the physical rescue. Do not treat the survivors or the route as imaginary.

## Rescue count and locations

- **Group A:** Fifteen people physically from Car 3, including Tarin Voss, Niko Senn (mobility-impaired), and Anja Ro. They move through the changed service door in Chapters 2–4.
- **Group B:** Oren Pike, one person on the Car 3 manifest who was not in the first physical count. He is found alive in the lower maintenance pocket in Chapter 5 and joins the refuge after the first loan ends.
- Groups A and B make a paper count of **sixteen** at the refuge. The first cohort is not the same as the later alcove group.
- **Group C:** Seven people in the service alcove behind the changed wall, including Nadi Bell and Ansel, who cannot walk. They are not in Car 3. The extraction in Chapter 9 raises the paper count to **twenty-three**. The older Group C passenger carries a child’s school coat and gives it to Nadi.
- **Group D:** Two deeper heat signatures remain farther inside Kestrel after Chapter 9. Their number, condition, and route are unknown. They are not rescued in this batch and must not be counted as part of Group C.

## Still Hand lock and aftermath

- The first loan is future Mara’s bounded **Still Hand**: keep one named living body, Tarin Voss, from failing while present Mara repairs him. It cannot heal, resurrect, protect Elias from crushing force, or solve the tunnel. The Chapter 2 offer names the gift in a **Gift name** field beside the lender, so later chapters may use the term **Still Hand** without reintroducing it.
- Elias disclosed the offer, target, boundary, and return before acceptance. Present Mara consented to ordinary emergency treatment and her own repair work under emergency rules, explicitly not on behalf of future Mara. Tarin was unresponsive at the time; his later waking comments occur in Chapter 4 and do not create retroactive consent.
- Future Mara’s branch-specific reason is a rescue in which a living person was left behind while a cleaner route was pursued. Her boundary forbids killing, forced treatment, and abandonment. The third maintenance wheel is the concrete automatic return trigger.
- Chapters 2–6 are one linked Kestrel operation tied to the active seam and the powered maintenance trolley. The Still Hand fades when the third wheel stops; Elias cannot voluntarily release, extend, or renew it. Chapter 6’s withdrawal is automatic, not a failure or a bargain.
- After the return, Elias has no active supernatural capacity. He has Stage 1: First Witness knowledge: he can recognize a future echo’s warning or refusal near an active seam, but cannot reopen this loan, summon Mara’s voice, or treat a future route memory as a command.
- Elias loses sensation and fine control in his left hand and experiences a brief branch-memory afterimage. This injury remains active in Chapter 10. He is competent but must use his right hand, observation, and team support.
- Chapter 9’s possible Still Hand extension is the same future Mara branch considering whether to reopen the completed exchange. She deliberately refuses to cover more than one body or stretch the returned promise. No second gift is accepted. This is a personal boundary for the completed exchange, not a permanent ban on every future Still Hand offer.

## Consent and voices

- The present Mara’s consent covers present treatment and repair, not future Mara’s capacity. Elias’s Chapter 4 incomplete disclosure is a failure to explain the branch-memory effect, not a secret acceptance. The team records the offer, boundary, return, refusal, target, and uncertain perceptual aftermath.
- The possible-Elias voice appears live from the altered wall at the end of Chapter 6 and in raw relay audio in Chapter 7. It warns Elias not to make the map agree and wants contradictory paper/audio evidence preserved. It offers no gift, route command, identity, or proof of a faction. It is not Elias Forty-Seven and is not identified as the Returning Hand in Volume 01. Elias tells it he will not use it as proof; the raw file remains preserved.
- Nell’s clipped transmission is a present-day, branch-crossed signal carrying her present voice and a rail engineer’s code. It is not a future Nell echo, not a pre-collapse recording, not a safe location, and not proof that she wants extraction. The pre-collapse analog route entry is a separate source and does not establish her exact location or intent. In Chapter 10 Elias writes a reply, tears out the page, and does not send it; the note is sealed in an envelope marked **KESTREL / DO NOT FILE WITHOUT RECEIVER CONFIRMATION** and has reached no one. Later batches may show him considering sending it, but must not treat Nell as having received or answered it.
- No one is used as an anchor without knowing the risk. Inez’s refuge records resident consent, refusal, and withdrawal; it is not a shared-load circuit or a new power stage.

- Nadi gives Joon a family contact and a pre-collapse shuttle number. The Bell family appears on a paper manifest, but the current digital response list omits the row. The erasure is seeded, not explained.
- Sable’s form contains the phrase **one approved outcome** and an equipment-failure code timestamped before the first rail alarm. Joon finds a second author code, **MNT-4C-17**, on the pre-collapse route entry. It is an internal maintenance credential, not a Civic Spine author record. Its owner, purpose, and connection to the failure code remain unknown.
- Joon preserves duplicate paper records, raw audio, route measurements, names, the 23-person count, and the Nadi family discrepancy. Elias keeps the original notebook; Mara controls the medical ledger; Inez controls local power and refuge records; the Office has copies only.
- The first temporary refuge is a physical and civic tool made from battery power, anchor lamps, seam glass, analog maps, and mechanical barriers. It can keep people, records, and decisions safer for a limited period but cannot stabilize Kestrel or open a seam.
