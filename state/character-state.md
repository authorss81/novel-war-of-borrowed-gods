# Character State After Chapter 30

## Elias Rook

- **External position:** Havenport Rescue Service field technician. **Stage 1: First Witness.** No active supernatural capacity and no summonable one. He holds the original notebook, an unsealed envelope he has now twice refused to file, and a public objection with his name at the bottom of it.
- **Physical condition:** Left hand has persistent numbness and unreliable fine control. He announces it — "Left hand's gone" — and hands the job over, and other people now say it for him. In Chapter 25 it failed on the case at the 11.0 m mark and Joon said it out loud before he did, and Elias said it anyway a beat later. The habit holds. The hand is a limit, not a symbol, and it has not improved.
- **Emotional position:** He has lost the thing he is best at, which is being the one who decides, and he has lost it in the two ways that actually cost: the Chapter 25 evidence points at the rescue that worked, and Ansel Duarte told him on a loading lip that he is not the reason the hall works. He is not consoled by either. He described the loss accurately when he accepted Tomas's judgement in Chapter 25 — *it was not that you were right about the measuring, it was that you were right that I already knew the answer and was looking for a job* — and he repeated the pattern in Chapter 27 by admitting he should have had the crew conversation on Wednesday and letting Tomas say it out loud.
- **Active want:** Understand why the lower service run lost twenty meters; learn who held `MNT-4C-17`; find out what happens to a corridor that was usable only inside a loan window; and reach Nell without making her a retrieval target.
- **Working change, and it is now visible on the wall rather than promised:** he has signed three promises this volume and the third one is the important one. The five-name promise is unchanged. Added in Chapter 30: *I am not going to be the last person to speak on any of this, and the last word does not close the item* — and, signed and initialled by Elias, by Ansel Duarte, and by Inez as holder of the copy: **Ansel may break any plan of Elias's by saying "that is a rescue," with no reason required and no requirement to be right.** Petar Lisk insisted on the inconvenient clause staying in the text.
- **He accepts other people's judgement over his own, repeatedly and in public.** Tarin Voss's withdrawal (Ch 21), Tomas on the node (Ch 22), Inez on the power and on Perry's date (Ch 21, 26), Tomas and then Emil Baros on the instrument (Ch 25), Joon and Mara on the envelope (Ch 29), Ansel on the last word (Ch 30).
- **He gave away the one voice on the radio** and the single perfect plan, and found that losing control of a plan is a different thing from losing the work.
- **Important limit:** he cannot summon a future voice, reopen the Still Hand, or make a branch memory prove a route. He has not asked Halla Dren anything and has said out loud, in a tunnel, that the team will not. He cannot be right about the corridor, and he has stopped reaching for the instrument.

## Mara Okafor

- **Professional position:** Emergency physician and triage lead. Sole holder of the medical ledger, which the Office reads and never holds. She holds medical triage and the stop on moving any patient, and she added a second refusal in Chapter 26.
- **Her two refusals, both standing:** **no medic on the line**, and **no predicted outcome anywhere in the document** — no triage categories, no expected numbers, no *if we find three* — and nothing at all about what happens if somebody is found, because they do not know and would not learn by writing it down in advance. She refused that against Tomas, in front of the room, and won it.
- **Emotional position:** She is the one who said the Chapter 25 finding out loud and made it bearable: *we did not break it. Every version of last Saturday that has twenty-three people in this building has the loan in it. We are being held to the same standard as everybody else, which is that it did not stay.* She refused Elias's envelope coldly in Chapter 29 and was right: filing a present voice makes it a search, and a search is a promise made to somebody behind a wall.
- **Working change:** she is running ninety-minute ward rounds with two nurse-assistants and no physician in the building, and a clinic that shares a generator budget with a hall that is going dark two hours a night, and she answered R. Adare's direct question about Osei's wife's bed honestly and in front of witnesses because he had asked her as a man who could find a bed and not as a man who wanted something out of her.
- **Relationship with Elias:** professional, slow, and built on conduct. She has refused him twice in public in this batch. Nothing romantic has happened and no future version has promised either of them anything.

## Nell Rook

- **Status:** Missing and not extracted. Her present-day branch-crossed signal has carried the north stair warning, the people-behind-the-wall warning, **the east branch holds**, and — at 02:04 on the second Saturday — **do not use the west run**, four words at the worst compression yet.
- **New technical fact:** her carrier and the 05:17 maintenance route entry share a physical path, and that path runs through twenty meters of the lower service run that are no longer there. Joon recorded it as a carrier fact and refused to interpret it. **The live question is whether that route is a way to reach her or a way to close the branch she is keeping open.**
- **Relationship pressure:** Elias wanted to file the envelope on the second Saturday and did not. Joon refused him out loud; Mara refused him coldly; and Elias gave the honest reason, which is that the only line that could carry a reply is the one she just told him not to use, and added the uglier one, which is that he is afraid of a survey. **The envelope is in the notebook. Nobody has received it.** She has not been made a retrieval target and may not be.

## Tomas Vale

- **Professional position:** Senior safety instructor and structural support lead. Holds structural limits and the stop threshold, written before anybody goes, and is no longer the only voice allowed to say the word. **The crew's confined-space rope competence left on the second Friday** when the Service recalled Rafi and Kellan, and he is now the only trained structural person in the building.
- **Disclosed history:** the 2034 collapse, the same east-leg draw, the same slab cycle, the buried paired node under the west apron written off in 2035 with his signature second on the page, the 2029 one-voice panel, the reroute that left Samuel Rook holding a frame. All of it is public in the room now.
- **Undisclosed:** the notebook of admissions, stored with the 2034 report. Nobody has read it. It has not moved in this batch.
- **Emotional position:** he is useful and has less authority than he has ever had. In Chapter 22 he overruled Elias on giving the survey to the sealed node, and it worked. In Chapter 25 he stopped Elias re-measuring and lost the argument he'd been winning for a week. In Chapter 26 he argued against Mara for a medical protocol, did not get it, and then said the rest of it out loud and asked for his name on the record as the man who said the thing he did not want to hear.
- **Active want:** keep the method available to people who are not him, and see the rule he wrote dismantled without anything worse replacing it.

## Joon Park

- **Professional position:** Dispatcher and record keeper. Sole holder of the raw audio, the manifest, the measurements, the witness statements, the dispatch copy and the Bell file. Holds records, custody, and what gets published, and is the only person authorised by Nadi Bell to ask anything of her sister.
- **What he did in this batch:** he put the Office's Finding 1 and the Office's own 1800 digest answer on the same board eleven inches apart; he made Tarin Voss's and Niko Senn's withdrawals legible as findings; he wrote the three-row table of institutional name-loss and initialled a sentence of his own about a field that is not in a photograph; he found the aisle ledger's missing fourth column before the Office did and said so out loud; he stopped Elias filing the envelope in front of twelve people; and he recorded the 0.4-second audio discrepancy and refused, out loud, to put a name or a meaning on it.
- **Emotional position:** he has become the person who says no on the record, and in Chapter 24 he discovered he did not much care whether the clerk liked him. He is more accurate than liked and he has made his peace with the trade.
- **Active want:** a record that can be checked by the people in it, a refusal as legible as an acceptance, and nobody reading a quarter-second of flat carrier and deciding what it meant.

## Inez Sato

- **Professional position:** Cinder Quarter Electrical Cooperative organizer and local power steward. Sole holder of the residents' lock-box, the power budget, the consent cards, and the right to cut the feeder. Holds local power and the right to cut it, and initialed the copy of the promise that gives Ansel Duarte a break-power over Elias.
- **Emotional position:** she is the one who names every cost and refuses to let Elias name one. She cut the power in the middle of the afternoon to make a point about a feeder, which is the most Inez thing in the batch. She negotiated Perry Dunne's date, kept both dates on the board with his name on them, and told him to complain about the not-telling on Tuesday.
- **What the ward costs, in her hand:** a block wall at 1,100 out of a 4,200 requirement — thirty percent — and then, from Chapter 26, **the hall dark from 0200 to 0400 every day**, with the seam-glass frame off and Petar walking it with a hand lamp. The hall is not a comfortable place any more and it is a room where decisions can be made.
- **Active want:** local control, resident consent and withdrawal that costs nothing to use, and a team that knows that a blackout means the team goes dark too.

## Sable Arden

- **Professional position:** Havenport Continuity Office director. The hold expired Saturday midnight and the site reverted to the standing designation, which puts the pre-ticked cause box back in her file. She sits on the review board she convened.
- **Partial win, partial loss, both on the minutes:** she kept the site, kept the classification provisional, and imposed a dated condition. She lost the joint inspection being turned into a survey requirement, lost the control of what the board saw first, lost Finding 4, and had to write to Vane's findings in a building where Finding 1 and the 1800 digest are eleven inches apart.
- **Emotional position:** she is a losing administrator with a procedure, which makes her more dangerous. She said in the room that she does not think Elias was wrong to stop and thinks he was wrong about three other things, and she is right about at least one of them. **She rejected Vane's Finding 4 by name** on the ground that a request is not a finding and that the minute it is, the institution takes instructions from a union hall. She is not identified with any faction and must not become cartoonish.
- **Open and pressing:** a broker has lodged a license application inside her classification, in a hall with forty-two people who have read the offer and the refusal, on a Friday night. She has to answer it into a room that already knows the shape of the question.

## Hollis Vane

- The Office archivist. About sixty, lean, quiet, thorough, unimpressed. Nineteen years as custodian of the file. He writes his findings in the room because the hold said so; he records a withdrawn page as a finding rather than as an absence; he drew the distinction between *the same credential* and *the same hand* and would not let it be blurred; and he made the observation the volume now runs on — **a field that does not exist cannot be blank, a field that can be blank can be filled later by somebody who was not there, and a blank field is not a hole, it is a place where a name goes without anybody lying.**
- He gave the team a copy of the dispatch original because the board ordered production, said in the same breath that they will be tempted to treat him as being on their side and that he is not, and wrote back to Sable to say she is entitled to reject his fourth finding and that he wrote it anyway. He is not a friend, an ally, or a convert, and his findings make the board more dangerous, not less.

## Nadi Bell

- The woman the Chapter 28 refusal is about. She stopped Elias answering Adare a second time and made him write the entire offer down again in the same form as the first, signed and dated, and explained why: *a man who has been refused once can tell a story about the refusal, and a man who has been refused twice with two pieces of paper cannot.* She prices a person who can put a name back on a list at what it costs the people who have to stand in a line to be found. She still holds the child's school coat and she still chooses her family over the case, and the file records that she did. Yolanda's name is still unpublished at her insistence.

## The man behind the wall

- **A present-day civilian.** Eleven days behind the altered wall, in a place the gas curve says has been shut long enough for the air to have opinions. He is not a future version, not an echo, and not a lender, and he must never become one.
- He has told the team three things in his own words: **keep the run on the paper in every building they can reach and in the one they cannot; send nobody in; write nothing more than *voice at the wall*.** He gave no name, no count and no condition, and asked for none. He said the run is in use by people who are not on any list and that a run which is only in a list gets closed by somebody who never had to walk it.
- **His boundary has teeth:** if one person goes into the run it shuts, because a run with a body in it is a run that frightened people close, and he has watched it happen to other people. **His reason for answering:** the team published its own objection, so there was a record on his side of the concrete that could be argued with.
- He said his own road is eleven meters shorter than it was on Saturday and asked nothing about it. The agreed next action is to ask him what he wants to do about it, and then to do what he says — **and not to go and get him, whatever he says.**
