# Character State After Chapter 50

**Volume 01 is complete.** This file records the state of the cast at the volume close, for Volume 02's writers.

## Elias Rook

- **External position:** Havenport Rescue Service field technician. **Stage 1: First Witness.** He has no summonable capacity, no reopenable loan, and no thread he can pull. **No loan has been accepted anywhere in Volume 01.** He holds the original notebook with an unsent envelope in the front of it, an unsealed refusal log, and eleven boxes of evidence in a garage on an ordinary street, **and a copy of the record that went out of the city on a bus on the Sunday morning of the twenty-ninth and is in a building he cannot enter. The count of boxes out of the city is unresolved in the record and no chapter may print one.**
- **Physical condition:** **The left hand has gone completely.** It was persistently numb for three weeks of this batch and by Chapter 50 it is gone, which is why he asks for the last thing the wall says to be repeated: he wants to be sure the words are for him and not for the room. It is a limit, not a symbol, and it is why he hands the job over.
- **What he refused in this batch, and none of it was argued out of him:** a route to a dead man he had never met, offered by a voice nineteen years ahead of his sister, on Chapter 40; a stretch of a future physician's one-body boundary, in Chapter 46, where **the asking was the extension and the extension ended the offer**; and **an offer that would have worked**, in Chapter 48 — a relay that takes one order and holds the corridor to fourteen meters and gets him his sister — refused because its boundary reads *the order is obeyed, not answered, nobody in your hall is asked*, and because it was the only thing anybody has offered him this month that gets his sister, and he said that out loud on the record in that order.
- **What he got wrong in this batch:** **he built a night operation around a card that expires at six and never once built a plan around the hall's own dark hours, and he lives there.** Inez Sato said it in seven words and was right, and the plan became a daylight plan. He also said the sentence in Chapter 48 that he has spent the volume unlearning — that there is no version of the week in which everybody comes out and nobody stays in it — and he has not been un-unlearning it.
- **His five things on the wall, none broken:** the five-signature promise; *I am not the last person to speak on any of this, and the last word does not close the item*; **Ansel Duarte may break any plan of Elias's by saying "that is a rescue," with no reason required and no requirement to be right**; **he is not the person who asks his own future for anything**; and, new in this batch, **he asked somebody else to write down the fact that he did not make a promise and did not say no.**
- **The claim he released, in his own hand, crossed out twice and not crossed out the third time:** *I am giving up the claim that a good rescue means I bring her back next.*
- **How the volume ends on him:** a voice that sounds like his asks for a promise about a man he is going to be, and **he declines to make it**, because making it would be forcing a future version of himself that had not been asked. That is the first time in the volume he has declined to make a promise at all, and it is the whole of his arc for this book.
- **The envelope:** still unsent. The reason has changed three times and is four words long: *the road is sealed.*

## Mara Okafor

- **Professional position:** Emergency physician and triage lead. **She triaged six people she had never seen, one at a time, in a corridor, out loud**, with no protocol and no order and no survival window, and wrote a decision with a time and her name on it and no number on it anywhere.
- **Her three refusals, all standing:** **no medic on the line**; **no predicted outcome, no number and no category, anywhere in any document about that wall**; and **she will not write a rule — only a decision.** She added a fourth in this batch: she filled the Office's *estimated survival window* box with a sentence rather than a technical term, because a technical term in a box reads as a finding.
- **What she did in this batch that the volume needed her to do:** **she argued for the approved sequence before she argued against it, and said the person in the room who most wants to say no is her and not Elias** — and then gave the reason it is worse than the Door Memory, which is that it does not use a future person to authorize a present decision, it **removes the question**. She challenged the word *retrieval* out loud. And when the Still Hand withdrew, she said the cost plainly and did not blame him: *the first time in this month a future person withdrew instead of giving, it was because of something we did, and I do not know what to do with us.*
- **Her relationship with Elias gains a disclosure rule and not a guarantee:** *any offer, any boundary, any refusal, and any condition goes to the people who pay for it, in the words it was said in, before it is acted on.* And her half, which is hers: **nobody inherits her.** A future Mara who lends a hand is a woman deciding about her own capacity, and the present Mara is not entitled to be angry at her later or grateful to her as though she had done it for her. **Nothing has happened between them and no future version of either of them has promised them anything**, and Mara asked for that to be said out loud in a room with witnesses in case somebody writes it down wrong later.
- **Her cost in this batch:** the only powered hour of the season, a generator margin given to a survey in an earlier chapter, a powered frame, and a man on a frame in her own corridor at ten to two in the morning.
