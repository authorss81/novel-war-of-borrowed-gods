# Volume 01 Batch 0001 Outline: The Ninety Seconds

- **Batch number:** 0001
- **Chapter range:** Chapters 1–10
- **Working title:** *The Ninety Seconds*
- **Batch goal:** Establish Elias Rook as a capable ordinary rescue technician before the supernatural promise changes his work. Put him and Mara Okafor inside the Kestrel collapse, make the first Still Hand loan a bounded human choice rather than a convenient power-up, rescue the living group they can reach, and end with a damaged service route and an official record that no longer agree. The batch must promise a larger search for Nell without resolving her disappearance.

## Batch shape

- **Beginning — Chapters 1–2:** Ordinary shift, immediate Kestrel collapse, first contact with Mara, and the first short loan offer.
- **Escalation — Chapters 3–5 and 7–8:** The team tries to make the changed route usable without pretending the gift created it, loses the record, protects the people who refuse to be moved, and meets institutional pressure.
- **Batch midpoint — Chapter 6:** The third maintenance wheel stops and the Still Hand returns automatically. The repair’s changed branch leaves the service door structurally unusable and omitted from the immediate route index; physical absence is not yet proven. Elias must choose a slower ordinary plan.
- **Batch climax — Chapter 9:** With no active supernatural capacity, the split crew extracts Group C, the first cohort behind the changed wall, through the changing tunnel, honors a refusal, and brings back a survivor the official system cannot name. Groups A and B are already at the refuge.
- **Aftermath — Chapter 10:** Triage, duplicate records, prototype refuge components, a partial Nell signal, and the decision to investigate the tunnel before the Office makes its version permanent.

## Starting condition

- **Date and place:** 2042, modern Havenport; Elias is beginning a field shift around Kestrel Station and the Salt Meridian.
- **Elias:** Stage 0, Unborrowed. He has seam-safety training but no experience accepting a future capacity. He still believes a rescuer should personally carry every dangerous decision.
- **Nell:** Inside Kestrel on rail-signal work when the collapse starts. Her last direct message is a personal alarm; the later clipped transmission is a present-day signal carried across a seam, not a future echo or a pre-collapse recording.
- **Mara:** Assigned to triage; she and Elias have professional respect but no established romantic history or settled authority between them.
- **Tomas and Joon:** Available as the senior safety voice and dispatcher, respectively. The Civic Spine and its translated interface are ordinary parts of emergency work until the first loan makes their limits visible.
- **No prior chapters:** This batch establishes the opening voice, the first future person, the first concrete cost, and the first surviving witness whose record will matter later.

## Starting relationships

- Elias trusts his own hands more than a plan that depends on other people’s judgment.
- Mara trusts Elias’s rigging but not his habit of promising more than the evidence allows.
- Tomas knows Kestrel’s history and expects Elias to obey a senior safety call; Elias is not yet willing to let anyone else own that authority.
- Joon trusts Elias as a dispatcher and will challenge him if a report erases a person rather than merely a route.
- Inez is not yet part of the tunnel crew, but the Cinder Quarter’s electrical cooperative becomes a possible source of local power and a condition of future partnership.

## Continuity bookkeeping for the batch

These groups and labels remain stable through Chapters 1–10. The numbers are working rescue counts, not a prophecy or an official classification.

- **Group A — opening carriage:** Fifteen people from Car 3, including **Tarin Voss**, the injured passenger who is the Still Hand’s single target, and one mobility-impaired passenger. They are moved toward the surface in Chapters 2–4.
- **Group B — missing manifest passenger:** One person listed on the Car 3 manifest but not present in the first physical count, found alive in Chapter 5. This person is not Nadi Bell and joins the refuge group after the loan ends.
- **Group C — Nadi’s group:** Seven people in the service alcove behind the changed wall, including **Nadi Bell** and one patient who cannot walk. They are reached in Chapter 9 and are not part of Car 3’s physical manifest.
- **Group D — deeper group:** Two unaccounted heat signatures deeper in Kestrel. The number of people and their condition remain unknown; they are not rescued in this batch.
- **First reachable cohort:** Groups A and B (sixteen people) reach the refuge before Chapter 9; Group C raises the paper count to twenty-three living people after Chapter 9. The Civic Spine’s later report that Car 3 carried no passengers therefore contradicts the paper count, even though Nadi’s group was not in Car 3.
- **Nadi’s missing family clue:** Nadi gives Joon a family contact and a pre-collapse shuttle number. Joon finds the Bell family on a paper manifest, but the current digital response list omits it. The erasure is seeded, not explained.
- **Equipment-failure clue:** Sable’s form already contains an equipment-failure code and a timestamp earlier than the rescue report. The next batch must compare that timestamp with the second author code on the pre-collapse route entry.

The **Nell signal** used in this batch is a present-day branch-crossed transmission captured after the collapse, not a future Nell echo or a pre-collapse recording. It carries Nell’s present voice, a rail engineer’s code, and warnings that are incomplete by the seam. The separate pre-collapse route entry is an analog record made before the collapse; neither source proves Nell’s exact location, safety, or desire to return.

## Future-voice ledger

- **Future Mara / Still Hand:** In her branch, Mara remembers a rescue in which a living person was left behind while the rescuers chased a cleaner route. She answers because Elias is attempting a concrete repair and the patient is already in immediate danger. Her boundary is that the hand may keep one living body from failing while Mara repairs it, but may not kill, force treatment, or be used to abandon anyone. If the repair succeeds, the patient is stabilized long enough to be moved and the altered work leaves physical and civic aftermath. The third wheel automatically ends the exchange; she can also refuse a later request for more than that one body. In Chapter 9 she considers an extension only to keep the first reachable group together, then refuses when asked to cover more than one body.
- **Unidentified possible-Elias voice:** This voice wants at least one honest record of the people who crossed Kestrel’s changed route to survive, rather than be forced into an approved account. It answers because Elias is preserving paper measurements and raw audio at an active seam, not because he has earned a private future connection. It offers no gift and no route command, refuses to identify its branch, and will stop answering if Elias treats it as proof that the Returning Hand exists. If it continues, the raw relay remains detectable and the branch that answered becomes easier for the Office to locate. It is not Elias Forty-Seven and is not identified as the Returning Hand in this volume.

## Interface and loan limits

- Chapter 2 uses one displayed loan notice. Chapters 3–6 continue the same active seam operation; they do not start a second loan or carry a gift into a new scene.
- Chapter 6 uses one automatic withdrawal notice when the third maintenance wheel stops. The service door is an aftermath of the repair, not a route directly powered or sustained by the gift; it becomes unusable and drops out of the immediate index, but physical absence is not established.
- Chapter 7 displays one `No answer` notice. The possible-Elias warning is heard in the raw relay audio afterward, not as a second interface panel.
- Chapter 9’s possible Still Hand extension is one short relay exchange and a deliberate refusal, not an accepted second gift. It cannot expand the first loan after the wheel has stopped.

## Batch midpoint

In Chapter 6, the third maintenance wheel stops and the Still Hand returns automatically; Elias does not bargain for more time or force the future Mara to remain. The loan ends cleanly. The repair’s changed branch leaves the service door structurally unusable, and the Civic Spine removes it from the immediate route index. Measurements, tool marks, and conflicting memories prove that the door existed, while the residue leaves open whether it has shifted into another branch or is unsafe rather than physically absent. Elias’s first miracle has created a new uncertainty, and his plan to keep every promise by keeping every route open is no longer credible.

## Batch climax

In Chapter 9, the team reaches Group C, the first cohort behind the changed wall, with no active Still Hand and no guaranteed route. Groups A and B are already at the refuge, so the paper count is sixteen before this rescue. Elias follows a split plan shaped by Mara’s triage, Tomas’s structural limits, Joon’s analog record, and the passengers’ own refusals. They bring the seven-person group out, including Nadi Bell, but the terminal reports that Car 3 was empty. The contradiction belongs to Group A’s physical rescue; Group C was behind the wall rather than in Car 3. Deeper thermal readings suggest that Group D may still be trapped. Elias chooses the living people in front of him over a clean chance to follow Nell’s signal, and the cost is not hidden.

## Aftermath

Chapter 10 completes an emotional and practical beat rather than the volume: the first reachable cohort receives care, Elias helps Inez’s people build prototype refuge components, Mara requires accurate records, and Sable’s Office begins pressing for an equipment-failure report. A thermal survey identifies two deeper heat signatures, while the present-day branch-crossed Nell signal and a pre-collapse relay entry give the team a next move without turning either source into a guaranteed rescue. Nadi’s paper family manifest and Sable’s pre-filled failure code give the next batch concrete record work to do.

## Required character changes

- **Elias:** Moves from solitary command toward disclosure and delegation. He still wants to carry the responsibility, but he learns to state a bounded promise, accept Mara’s and the team’s decisions, and let a refusal alter the plan.
- **Mara:** Moves from skeptical medical partner to a professional equal. She requires the team to separate her consent to present treatment from future Mara’s consent to lend a capacity, and she chooses continued collaboration based on conduct rather than destiny.
- **Tomas:** Stops treating Elias as either a promising rescuer or a repeat of Samuel. He challenges Elias to make safety shareable rather than heroic.
- **Joon:** Establishes the principle that a record can be evidence even when it is incomplete; he saves the contradictory audio and paper measurements.
- **Inez:** Enters as a conditional ally, offering local power and shelter only if residents share control of the refuge.
- **Sable:** Appears as a pragmatic regulator whose safety policy is partly earned and whose definition of a valid record is beginning to exclude living people.

## Required power changes

- Elias moves from **Stage 0: Unborrowed** to **Stage 1: First Witness** by accepting one short Still Hand loan during the single linked Kestrel extraction in Chapters 2–6.
- The loan is future Mara’s bounded gift: keep **one named living body, Tarin Voss**, from failing while present Mara repairs it. It cannot heal, resurrect, make Elias invulnerable, or solve the tunnel.
- The future Mara sets a boundary and a concrete return event. Elias must not use the hand to kill, force treatment, or abandon a person. The third maintenance wheel is the return trigger: when it stops, the loan ends automatically. Elias can honor the return by refusing to extend or force it, but he cannot voluntarily release or renew the gift.
- The cost is physical and perceptual: Elias loses sensation and fine control in one hand, experiences a brief afterimage of a life he has not lived, and cannot safely repeat the gift immediately.
- The loan’s end changes the present through aftermath, not through a directly powered door. The repair creates a changed service-door route; when the loan returns, that route becomes unusable and drops out of the index, while its physical absence or branch shift remains unconfirmed. A future voice can refuse or withdraw; no supernatural capacity remains with Elias after the scene.
- Do not add a second borrowed god, a stored ability, a persistent future connection, or a shared-load circuit in this batch.

## Required world reveals

- A seam is a physical condition in which more than one plausible history affects the same location; it is not a portal or a game instance.
- The ninety-second rhythm is real, but the interface does not explain it. Civic Spine language is a human translation and may be incomplete.
- A future voice knows one branch and one practiced capacity, not the whole future. The future Mara’s offer is a choice with a reason, not a prophecy.
- The Still Hand has a specific boundary and a concrete return condition; refusal is a valid outcome.
- The first loan is one continuous operation tied to the active Kestrel seam and the maintenance trolley. The service door is an aftermath of the repair, not a route directly created or sustained by the gift; after the wheel stops, it is unusable and omitted from the immediate index, but its physical absence is not yet proven.
- Loan aftermath can alter routes, records, memories, and relationships. A surviving person can be physically present while the official response list treats the person as absent.
- The first unfamiliar possible-Elias voice is a warning only. It has a branch-specific wish to preserve contradictory evidence, answers because Elias is protecting paper and audio records, offers no gift or route command, and can stop if Elias treats it as proof of the Returning Hand. It is not identified as the Returning Hand.
- Nell’s signal in this batch is present-day and branch-crossed, not a future Nell echo. The later pre-collapse route entry is a separate analog record; neither proves Nell’s exact location, safety, or intent.
- The public can learn that a loan is real, dangerous, and able to leave physical damage, while the rescue team’s private evidence suggests that each voice has a branch-specific motive and boundary. The public must not be given a definitive answer about whether a future self is a person yet.

## Open questions after the batch

- Why does Kestrel’s service door become unusable when the loan returns, and what later proves whether it has shifted, remains unsafe, or is physically absent? The answer is deliberately deferred beyond this batch; Chapter 25 owns the stronger physical-absence proof.
- Is Nell alive inside the seam, and is her present-day branch-crossed signal a request for rescue, a warning, or a responsibility she has chosen? It is not a future Nell echo and does not establish a retrievable location.
- Why does the Civic Spine log list the rescue as an equipment failure before the tunnel is sealed? Sable’s pre-filled code and timestamp must be compared with the second author code on the pre-collapse route entry.
- Who is Nadi Bell, and why has her family disappeared from the official response list while she is alive? Nadi’s paper manifest and the missing digital row are the handoff clues.
- What is the unidentified possible-Elias voice that sounds like Elias warning against making the map agree? It is not identified as the Returning Hand and offers no power.
- Will the Continuity Office seal the tunnel, seize the paper records, or force the team to re-enter under a controlled plan?

  - **Return:** When the third wheel stops turning.

Elias can sustain Tarin’s repair for a limited interval; his hand begins to lose sensation, and the future voice carries a branch-specific memory that is not his own. No second gift is available.

The return condition is the third wheel of the maintenance trolley, not an abstract timer. The future voice cannot command present Mara, and present Mara’s consent to treatment is not consent by future Mara.
- **Ending type:** Reversal / first cost.
- **Next-chapter pull:** The repair creates a service door as physical aftermath even though no map contains it; the rear route and one carriage coupling cease to be reliable reference points. The door’s changed load path, not the Still Hand itself, will govern what happens after the loan.
