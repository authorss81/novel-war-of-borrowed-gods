## Chapter-by-chapter direction

### Chapter 41 — Tuesday
**Day twenty-four, the day after the refusal.** Open the final movement without ceremony: the hall is a building that has been running for a month and the question on the table is what they are doing on Wednesday. Establish the three fronts — the ward, the Office's ninety-day clock, and the fact that a licensed circuit is still running on Calder Street with a licensed broker and a no-show observer. Goal: convert a refusal into an operation that is somebody else's decision as well as theirs. Resistance: the thermal survey needs a limit written before anybody goes, a rope pair the hall does not have, and a medical standard that cannot contain a predicted outcome. Turn: **the Office supplies the pair under a chain with conditions, and Inez accepts the conditions and writes one of her own into the chain in return** — a term the Office honors and is not bound by, as in Chapter 34. End with the survey limit on the page and a new question: what is the survey for, exactly, and who said it out loud first.

### Chapter 42 — The Survey
**Day twenty-five, the Wednesday already on the board.** The thermal survey at first light with a stated depth, a stated limit, a named observer, and Ruzicka and Ohno back from the regional pool. Resistance: the 2038 pour and its hairline crack are now on the survey sheet, and Tomas's number is that nothing touches it; the gas curve still reads like a sealed room; and the survey may find people, which changes every document in the building. Turn: **the survey finds the living group.** Not a triumph — an obligation, at depth, past the brick lining, in air that has opinions. End with a number nobody wanted and a triage decision that has to be made tonight.

### Chapter 43 — The Argument About the Number
**Day twenty-six.** The group is on paper and the room must decide what to do about that before anybody can act on it. Resistance: Nadi Bell's rule about counts applies to this building's own people and does not obviously apply to people nobody has ever met; Mara refuses a predicted outcome; the Office wants a number; and somebody outside the team says the true thing. Turn: **Nadi Bell's movement record is extended to people the hall has never met, on her own logic, and she is right about it before anybody agrees.** End with a plan that is a plan and not a rescue, and with the specific obstacle in it.

### Chapter 44 — Split Authority
**Day twenty-seven, the Friday.** The volume's structural chapter, and the one the outline names for 44–45. Goal: map the available routes and let five domains each own a piece. Resistance: Tomas's structural limit, Mara's triage, Inez's four carded draws and a hall that goes dark at two, Joon's rule that no record lives in one building, and Elias's own standing temptation to be the one who decides. Turn: **Elias accepts that no plan can promise every person a safe outcome, and says so in front of the five.** End with the operating plan on the wall with a fresh expiry and a fresh stop page, drawn by somebody who is not Elias.

### Chapter 45 — Saturday
**Day twenty-eight.** Care, recovery, and the ordinary labor of a month-old ward that is about to be the route somebody else is carried along. Goal: keep the room a room. Resistance: the twenty-two are changing state, Ansel is on the frame Mara gave away, Comfort Osei is on a dark frame at the clinic, Ru Sabar's card is still in the envelope, and the hall's own dark hours are two. Turn: **someone other than the team decides something about the twenty-two and is right**, and the team accepts it. End with a term, a refusal, or a person who has had enough.

### Chapter 46 — The Boundary Holds
**Day twenty-nine.** A bounded Still Hand offer from a changed future branch gives the team time to stabilize a wounded passenger, and **the future Mara withdraws when Elias tries to extend the gift beyond its one-body scope and agreed return condition.** Resistance: the temptation is enormous and the clock is real, and the refusal is a refusal of the same person who already refused once. Turn: **Elias does not interpret the refusal as a malfunction, says so out loud, and falls back to rope, shoring, thermal imaging, and a slower route that keeps the living group together.** End with the cost of the slower route in people, hours, and one relationship.

### Chapter 47 — Monday
**Day thirty.** The slow route and what it asks of ordinary bodies. This is a Monday, so the loading-lip appointment exists, and the question is what the team does with a schedule it cannot keep while a group is moving. Resistance: Doss is one ticket and no pair; the hall's power cannot carry four carded draws and a rescue; and the man at the wall is owed a Monday. Turn: **the team goes down on the Monday as promised, in the middle of the operation, because a promise that is kept only when it is convenient was never a promise** — and what Elias says down the wire is short and is not about the road. End with the man behind the wall having been told something true, and with the operation still moving.

### Chapter 48 — The Approved Sequence
**Day thirty-one.** A voice that sounds like a future Elias offers a way to make the relay follow one approved rescue sequence. The offer is sincere in its own branch and coercive in its effect: it would require the ward's people and the anchors to obey rather than answer. Goal: weigh an offer that would work. Resistance: it is the most attractive thing offered in the volume and it arrives while people are in the ground. Turn: **Elias refuses it**, and **Ansel's sentence is available and Elias does not need it this time**, and the refusal costs him the cleanest route to Nell and a temporary loss of access to the signal. **The voice is not identified as the Returning Hand and must not be.** End with the loss of the signal and with the choice to go slower anyway.

### Chapter 49 — The Living Route
**Day thirty-two.** Elias, Mara, Tomas, Joon, and the local crew move the remaining living passengers through the temporary ward. The rescue succeeds because it is incomplete, distributed, and honest about what it cannot reach. Resistance: the structural hold, the power, the air, the dark hours, and the fact that some of the group cannot be moved at all in the time available. Turn: **the group comes out, some of it, and a fragmented signal from Nell identifies a branch-side relay and carries her voice but not a location that can safely be treated as home.** End with the ward holding and the count moving, and with what the team will not do next.

### Chapter 50 — The Sealed Tunnel
**Day thirty-three. The volume close.** The Civic Spine seals Kestrel and classifies the collapse as an equipment failure; the Office signs and the team retains evidence that the classification is incomplete. The ward is established and maintained by residents. Duplicate paper records, patient names, route measurements, and the refusal log survive. Elias records Nell's partial signal and **releases his claim that a successful rescue means bringing her back immediately.** Elias and Mara set the working rule that any offer, boundary, refusal, or return condition must be shared with the people exposed to its cost, and **no future version of either of them has promised them anything.** Then the wall gives one last echo: a voice says Elias's name and asks him to promise that he will not force a future person to answer when that future person says no — **and he does not make the promise.** Final image: in his paper notebook, beneath Nell's partial signal, a rescued passenger's name remains in ink the Civic Spine terminal cannot find. End with the volume's next question, which is Volume 02's: **who edited the Kestrel response record so that a living passenger and Nell's route were treated as administrative errors, and what catastrophe does the Continuity Office believe a single approved outcome would prevent?** Do not close the volume tidily. It ends with a wound that is named and a promise that is not made.
