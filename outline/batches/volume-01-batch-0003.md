# Volume 01 Batch 0003 Outline: A Ward Made by Neighbors

- **Batch number:** 0003
- **Chapter range:** Chapters 21–30
- **Working title:** *A Ward Made by Neighbors*
- **House style:** US spelling, matching the bible and the prose — *neighborhood*, *neighbor*, *organize*, *recognize*, *center*, *color*.
- **Batch goal:** Build a real refuge around the Kestrel access corridors while a provisional classification, a ninety-six-hour clock, and a neighborhood's own deadlines pull in three directions, and deliver the volume's midpoint reversal: a safe exit the opening rescue made possible is absent from the physical world and from every current record, while the people who used it remember crossing it.
- **Expected length:** 10 chapters, roughly 1,800–2,400 words per ordinary chapter. Batch 0002 measured 28,159 words and overran its 1,700–2,400 target in nine of its ten chapters, so this batch should land nearer 20,000–24,000 words. One longer chapter is allowed where a complete scene — the Chapter 25 reversal, most likely — cannot honestly be cut shorter. A finished scene governs length; never pad and never cut a beat to reach a number.

This file is the canonical card record for Batch 0003. The same cards appear in narrative form in `workspace/volume-01/batch-0003/PROMPT.md`. If a card changes, change both.

## Batch shape

- **Beginning — Chapters 21–22:** The finding and the building of the ward. On Friday the archivist's finished finding goes on the hall wall above the Office's own published answer, and the neighborhood's clean week is negotiated instead of granted. On Saturday the ward is built out of scavenged material and ordinary money, the lower service corridor is put back on the paper, and the review board sits in the afternoon.
- **Escalation — Chapters 23–24:** The listening campaign produces contact rather than extraction, and the search for the 05:02 dispatch original runs into an archive log with a blank field of its own.
- **Batch midpoint reversal — Chapter 25:** The lower service corridor — the exit the opening rescue made possible, re-opened, re-measured, re-photographed and re-used days earlier — is gone, with residue still at a place that no longer has a place.
- **Aftermath — Chapters 26–30:** The cost of the reversal. A slower distributed plan, a crew that partly withdraws, a broker who comes back, an unsent promise that stays unsent, and a decision the team can actually keep.

### Chapter 0021 — The Finding

- **POV:** Elias Rook, third-person close.
- **Location/time:** The union hall, **Friday, the hold's third day.** The supervised reading already ran and closed on Thursday morning; do not stage it again.
- **Immediate goal:** Take delivery of Hollis Vane's written finding without letting it be used as a weapon by either side, and get Perry Dunne the clean week he asked for.
- **Resistance:** The archivist is thorough, unimpressed, and not a villain, and the finding is **narrower than the team wants** — the classification is not supported by the Office's own evidence, and the file also cannot be revised while the holder field is empty, which makes the hole the only thing a board can act on. **Tarin Voss withdraws his own page** before it is read aloud, because the loan record names him as the body that was kept alive and nobody asked him; a survivor withdraws consent to another page the same way.
- **Information revealed:** The institution's own archivist puts the classification's weakness and the team's failure in the same document, and the gap the withdrawal leaves is now in the Office's file too.
- **Action or decision:** The finding goes on the hall wall with the Office's published answer posted underneath it, so the institution is on the record disagreeing with itself in the same building. The team asks the board for the depot box, not for exoneration.
- **Emotional change:** Elias learns that being handed a document is not the same as being believed, and that the difference is a hole.
- **Power or resource change:** None. Custody is tested and holds; the archive becomes reachable.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** Tarin's consent governs his page, not the mission. The chapter-19 promise that Perry Dunne's date would be *a conversation and not a decision* is paid here.
- **Ending type:** Institutional finding, then a negotiated date. Perry's clean week moves to the following Friday because the hold's last day is tomorrow, and he says he did not move it to be helpful.
- **Next-chapter pull:** With the finding on the wall, the ward has to be built; the board sits tomorrow, and the depot box is now a thing they can ask for.

### Chapter 0022 — A Ward With a Name On It

- **POV:** Elias Rook, third-person close, with Inez Sato holding the practical work.
- **Location/time:** The union hall, the yard, and the surface approach to Kestrel, **Saturday, the hold's fourth day.** The review board sits in the afternoon.
- **Immediate goal:** Build a physical refuge that keeps people, records, food, and decisions safer for a limited time.
- **Resistance:** Cost, labor, per-circuit consent, a man selling "seam-rated" cable, and a neighborhood already reorganized around a hole in the ground. Every consignment still comes down one ramp.
- **Information revealed:** The ward is smaller than anyone wanted and works anyway, and the answer to the supply problem is not a new dig. It is the **lower service corridor** the first Still Hand loan made passable — re-opened, re-measured, re-photographed, re-named, and put back on the paper as a hazard with a keeper's name on it.
- **Action or decision:** The team uses the corridor that afternoon to move supplies, with a named Group C survivor walking it and a witness. In the afternoon the review board sits: no seal, no clearance, the classification stays provisional, a survey requirement is imposed, and lawful access to the depot box is granted in exchange.
- **Emotional change:** Elias is useful as a pair of hands and finds that satisfying rather than diminishing.
- **Power or resource change:** None. The ward is batteries, lamps, glass, maps, and barriers. The corridor is a hazard that has been put back on paper, not a route anyone is cleared to use again.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The corridor is real, freshly surveyed, freshly photographed, on the survey, and used by a named person with a witness — which is precisely what makes its loss in Chapter 25 unarguable.
- **Ending type:** Ordinary fact with enormous weight, then a board that gives and takes in the same afternoon.
- **Next-chapter pull:** The listening campaign is set for the following morning, and the archive is now reachable.

### Chapter 0025 — The Exit That Is Not There

- **POV:** Elias Rook, third-person close.
- **Location/time:** The surface approach to Kestrel, **Tuesday, the day after the corridor was used.**
- **Immediate goal:** Establish what has happened to the **lower service corridor** — the run from the service landing past the changed door that Groups A, B and C walked during the loan window, re-opened, re-measured, re-photographed and re-used on Saturday with a keeper's name on it, and used again on Monday.
- **Resistance:** The evidence is stronger than anything the team has had and it indicts their own successful rescue. Every instinct says this is a record problem he can solve with the right instrument.
- **Information revealed:** The corridor is not blocked, not unsafe, and not there. Scuffed threshold, dust in a bolt hole, and a handprint remain at a place with no place, and the index says it was never constructed. The evidence is harder than Chapter 12's page because the corridor was measured and photographed **on Saturday, three days earlier** with a laser and a named keeper, so the absence is a change over days rather than over years; twenty-three people remember crossing it; and the loan's return condition — the third maintenance wheel — sits in a medical ledger the Office has countersigned.
- **Action or decision:** The team proves the corridor's usable span and the loan's window coincide, and **cannot** prove cause. It does not make the map agree. The three-column page comes down and something with two columns goes up.
- **Emotional change:** Elias loses the argument he has been winning all week and recognizes the loss as the rule, not the failure — because there is no version of Saturday in which the opening rescue worked without the loan, so the route depended on a future that the return already altered. That is worse than a cause would have been.
- **Power or resource change:** None. The first loan's success is now a cost with a location, and that location is gone.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** This is absence for the **corridor**. The service door's own three states stay unproven and separate, and the reversal may not settle the door by implication. Group D is untouched.
- **Ending type:** Reversal, and the volume's rule becomes personal.
- **Next-chapter pull:** A slower plan is the only honest response, and it costs the ward something.
