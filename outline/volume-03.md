# Volume 03 Outline: The Bellwether Choice

- **Volume:** 03
- **Chapter range:** 101–150
- **Working title:** *The Bellwether Choice*
- **Volume promise:** Elias Rook goes and finds out whether a program that saves a district is lying about the people in it, and finds out that it is not, and that the place the people disappear is not in the program's method and not in anybody's hands — it is in the conversion of a measurement into a list of addresses, and the list has no history and therefore cannot show you when it stopped being true.

Volume 02 taught that a program with a name and a face is scarier than a villain and that the face must be competent. Volume 03 takes the lesson at its word and finds that a competent face is worse, because competence is what lets a good reading become a bad list without anybody lying.

## Central pressure

A regional pilot uses a future engineer's gift — the **Load Reader**, a bounded capacity already on the record in `bible/power-system.md` and `bible/terminology.md` — to read where force will travel through a district with a migrating void under it. **The reading is accurate. It saves the district.** On the strength of it, a statutory flood-defense alignment is re-cut through the neighborhood, the neighborhood is cleared, and four hundred and some people are moved out of houses that are, in fact, standing on ground that carries.

The pilot's method is this. The reading gives a **band**: a margin inside which the ground carries. The band is a measurement. The program then converts the band into **the Retention Schedule**: 697 addresses, each with an **occupancy class**, and the class is a building attribute, and the form has no field for a person. 412 addresses are `RETAINED`. 285 are `TRANSITIONAL`. **A `TRANSITIONAL` address is not counted in the retained district, and a district that is not counted carries no obligation to rehouse anybody.**

The band moves. The schedule does not. **A schedule records a state, and a state has no history, and a form with no field for when it changed cannot be asked whether it was right on the day it was written.** The schedule's own footnote, in italics, drafted by the cell's own method officer, says the band is indicative and the schedule is the operative document. **Forty-four addresses** were inside the September band and are outside the December one, every one of them is classified `TRANSITIONAL`, the program knows, and no individual chose anything.

And in November, on a block that the September reading said was carrying, the cell ran a clearance in the night. **Four people died. The clearance record is one line: `clearance complete, no outstanding`.** The four are recoverable only because a sixty-three-year-old woman in Tidegate has kept a sheet of addresses in her own hand since 2031.

**The gift is right. The conversion is wrong. That is the volume's whole argument, and it is not a restatement of anything Volume 02 paid for.**

## What is new in this volume, stated once

**A measurement and a list are not the same quantity.** A load reading tells you where force goes. A retention schedule tells you who is a household. Somebody has to convert one into the other, and the conversion is where people disappear, and it is done correctly, by an engineer, on a Tuesday, with a footnote.

That is the sentence the volume earns and states once and does not improve. It is Tomas Vale's professional judgment. **It is spoken once in the whole volume, in his own mouth, in Chapter 106, on Ash Street, in a room with Joon Park writing it down** — not in Tidegate, not about Tidegate, and not asked for by Elias, because Elias may not ask him and does not try. It is not a restatement of the Chapter 86 answer and may not be cut next to one. **Nobody else in Volume 03 states it, in any form, in any register, and nobody paraphrases it.** Anum gives the conversion and the operational reason in his own professional mouth and stops there; Hobbs declines to discuss the works in a corridor; Ferrand makes his own case in his own words in Chapter 110 and it is a different case. **A chapter that needs a second pass at the mechanism cuts a character's arrival instead.**

## The nine questions this outline has to answer

### 1. The district, the demolition, the body that orders it, the name of the program, the public face

- **The district: Tidegate**, on the west bank of the Tannery Cut, about two miles from the Cinder Quarter and eleven minutes on a bus. Four walk-up blocks, a strip of low workshops along the cut, a boatyard, a community health center, a school on Marden Row, a newsagent and post office on Cutbank Row. **1,140 addresses inside the alignment area, 2,900 residents on the works board's own 2039 model** — and the model has been adjusted twice and is wrong, and the board knows the model is a model.
- **The demolition: the Fourth Stage Tidal Defense**, a sea-wall alignment that has to be somewhere, because the board's flood model puts 1.4 m of water in Tidegate on a once-in-sixty-year surge and the ground along the proposed line is soft and cheap to build on. **The board did not choose Tidegate to be cruel. It chose Tidegate because Tidegate is where the ground is soft, and soft ground is cheap, and a flood model is a flood model.** This is the Volume 02 grammar applied to a public works department: nobody decided anything wrong; a line was drawn on a map by competent people who were shown a number.
- **The body that orders the demolition: the Havenport Tidal Board**, three commissioners, statutory, frightened, competent, and not the villain. It does not run the pilot and does not control the schedule and has been told the schedule is a schedule.
- **The body that runs the pilot: the Bellwether pilot cell**, part of a regional **Bellwether Pilot Program**, authorized by the Havenport Continuity Office on a pilot authorization signed by **Director Sable Arden**, who is right about the hazard and who has never read the schedule and who will say, in Chapter 147, in her own words, the sentence that is her volume: *I authorized a method. I have been reading a list as if it were the method for four months because the list was working.*
- **The program name: Bellwether.** This is canon from `bible/terminology.md` — the early name for the One-Future Choir's public pilot cells, each of which selected one safer district outcome. The word is a political and bureaucratic label and not a faction. **Volume 03 does not explain the Choir.** It puts the word on a leaflet, in a program heading, and in the mouth of one man in Chapter 148. **The paperwork shortens it and the shortening is itself a small institutional fact: `bible/terminology.md` gives the cells' name as the Bellwether Choir, and the authorization, the schedule's program reference, the verification log and every leaflet in this volume read `BELLWETHER` or `Bellwether Pilot Program` with no second word. Nobody in the volume remarks on it, Ferrand in Chapter 148 says the full name out loud in a hall for the first time in front of two hundred people, and the program does not correct him.**
- **The public face: Tobias Ferrand, 52**, Regional Director of the Bellwether Pilot Program. A duty flood officer in 2027, 2031 and 2036. **Competent, warm in a room, not a villain, and the volume's antagonist.** He says the number out loud. He is the first named political opponent of the series and he is a man with a program, not a voice.

### 2. The future engineer

**The reader has already met him. He is Tomas Vale, sixty-four, and the pilot's method is his gift.**

- **The gift: Load Reader.** *Reads the path force will take through a damaged structure. It reveals direction and stress, not a magical repair.* Already on the record in `bible/power-system.md` and `bible/terminology.md`. It cannot repair, cannot predict a collapse, cannot carry out of the room, and cannot tell you how many people are on a street.
- **Why this is devastating and not convenient:** on the twenty-seventh of November 2042, in a hearing room, a Deputy Director recorded a line in a form that says **Tomas Vale is not available as a structural authority on a designated site — not suspended, not under review, not available, in the past tense, in a document, forever** — with his own condition attached: *he is not consulted about any designated site by anybody, in writing or out of it.*
- **Tidegate is designated on day 87, Tuesday the second of December.** The designation is signed by a Director, it is correct, and **it activates his own condition.** The one man in the city who could read that ground honestly is barred by his own signature, and nobody did it to him, and he said so first.
- **His want, in his own words:** the works are moving and the reading is the only thing standing between a district and a hole in the ground, and he will not have it be said that the method failed because it did not fail. *Give it the season. I am not asking for the district.*
- **His boundary, stated in the offer and stated once:** **the reading is mine to give; the list of who is on it is not.** He will say where force goes. He will not draw a line with people behind it, and he will not know that he has, and the program will be careful to ask him in a way that does not require him to know.
- **His return:** *tell me, out loud, in a room, whether the line moved after I gave it to you.* Which is a request for a fact and not a favor, and which is the reason the volume's climax is a records question.
- **His cost to him:** in his branch the reading arrives eleven days later than it does here, and four people are in the November clearance who are not in it here, and he has been carrying that for twenty years and does not say so, and the volume finds it out from a schedule and not from him.

**And the thing the volume cannot do:** it may not make the present Tomas the villain, may not have him seek the pilot, and may not let Elias ask him. The whole difficulty of the loan is that **Elias cannot put the question to the man in front of him, because the man in front of him set a boundary in writing, and Elias has spent two volumes learning that a boundary is not an obstacle to be overcome.** Stage 2 is earned by a thread through a man who cannot be asked.

### 3. Which residents stay on the official map, and on whose authority

**A form. And the volume has to make a person feel it.**

- **The instrument: the Retention Schedule.** A printed schedule, a program reference, **a date — the second of December 2042 — and 697 rows.** Columns: address, structure, **occupancy class**, status. **`OCCUPANCY CLASS` is a building attribute.** A two-room flat is `PARTIAL` whether one person or five live in it. A workshop is `WHOLE` whether its owner sleeps there. **There is no column a person goes in, there never has been one, and nobody had to decide anything to make a person absent from it.** This is the **third** of the five institutional patterns the first two volumes established — a field never designed for the thing in question — and Volume 03 puts it in a building with a hundred people in it.
- **`TRANSITIONAL`, 285 addresses.** Not counted in the retained district. Not re-housed. Not compensated. Not in the school roll, the gas account, the water account, the electoral roll, or the count.
- **The authority: a person, and Ferrand signs it.** When Elias asks him, in Chapter 110 and again in Chapter 148, Ferrand does not hide behind the program. *I signed it. The form is mine. The box is not, and that is why the box is the thing I have to answer for, and I would like to talk about the box and not about the signature.* **The decision is a form; the signature is a man; the man wants to talk about the column.** Volume 02's whole argument, made to feel, by a man who agrees with it and cannot stop it.
- **The person who feels it: Ezinne Abioye, 71**, of 19 Cutbank Row, forty-four years in the same house. She is not a household. She is a **71-year-old woman in a two-room flat classified `PARTIAL`**, and the classification is correct, and the consequence is that when her building is not retained she is not re-housed, and she knows exactly what that means and has known since September, and she is not angry, and **the chapter's difficulty is that Elias's whole instinct is to be able to do something for her and there is nothing to do and she does not want anything.**
- **And she is not the only one and the number is the point:** 285. Ferrand says it in a church hall with a microphone and says it twice.

### 4. The future Mara's refusal

The volume midpoint, **Chapter 125, Tuesday the twenty-third of December, day 108.** It is a refusal, it is complete, it has a reason, and **it is not an outage.**

- **The crew's mistake is Elias's and it is the volume's cost.** To make the final clearances survivable the cell lends a future physician's hands to the people on the sheet, and the pilot's method is to ask, and **Elias asks the question anyway.** He asks a woman to put her hands on people who have been entered in a schedule. It is nine pages of him being the villain of Volume 03 and it is not softened.
- **The boundary, and it is one she has already stated in this manuscript:** her second refusal, from Volume 01 — *no predicted outcome, no number and no category, anywhere in any document about that wall* — and her third — *she will not write a rule, only a decision* — and her fourth, the *estimated survival window* box she filled with a sentence because a technical term in a box reads as a finding. **The Retention Schedule is a document with a category in it, about people, prepared before anybody was asked.**
- **The panel**, in the approved shape, with a return condition:

```text
Loan offered: Mara Okafor, twenty-one years ahead
Gift name: Still Hand
Gift: Keep one living body from failing while another person carries or repairs it.
Boundary: Do not use my hand on anybody who is on a list. Do not use my hand to make a schedule come out right.
Return: When the last one is out of the building, and you say out loud that it is the last one.
```

- **Her answer to the one question Elias is entitled to ask, in her own words, before the answer:** *You have written down what happens to them before you have asked me, and I am not going to put my hands into a plan that already knows. I have spent twenty-one years refusing to write a rule and only ever writing a decision, and a decision is about one person at a time, and there are two hundred and eighty-five addresses on that page and I have not met one of them.*
- **The program calls it `SECURED SUBJECT` and the field heading is `SUBJECT HELD` and the panel still says `Gift name: Still Hand`,** because a printer's face is what an institution wrote about one. That is Chapter 100's sentence arriving in a form, and it is the volume's smallest and best echo.
- **What it costs:** eleven people are hurt in the January clearance because the hands were not there, and **the refusal is not the villain of that, and Elias is.** And the refusal is in a room, in the words it was said in, before it was acted on, which is the practice Elias and Mara agreed in Chapter 122 — **so the refusal is the payoff of the promise and not a breach of it.**
- **The present Mara does not consent for her and does not defend her.** She refuses Elias, out loud, in front of people, on the ground that a future version of her is a woman deciding about her own capacity and that the present Mara is not entitled to be angry at her or grateful to her. *Nobody inherits me.* And that is the volume's relationship climax and it is not romance.

### 5. What "branch survivors" are in this volume's terms, and how they return

- **In Volume 03's terms they are not a mystery and not a category. They are three named people with two of everything.** The pilot has run four small clearances since September. When the Retention Schedule is withdrawn in Chapter 149, the margin reopens and **three people come back on Sunday the eleventh of January with no interface notice at all** — which is scarier than a notice, and is the aftermath of a program being stopped rather than a power being used.
  - **Emrys Vaughan, 41.** Legally, in this branch, married to **Ceri Vaughan**. Married, in the branch he came from, to a woman called **Bronwen**, and he uses the name every time he is tired. The register he is in names a woman who exists and whom he has never met. He wants to be told he is wrong.
  - **Marek Drzewicki, 19.** His father **Piotr Drzewicki, 54**, is alive in this branch, lives eleven minutes away, and is the man standing in the room, and Marek cannot make him and Piotr cannot explain it and both of them want it resolved in a room and it cannot be.
  - **Sabina Hough, 63.** Her other-branch son is, in this branch, her brother's son, **Declan Hough, 31**, who is in the room. She has a right to the relationship and there is no document in the world that can carry it.
- **The volume's lock, and it is the whole of the discipline:** **Volume 03 does not ask any of the three what they want.** Volume 03's only question to them is *what is your name and what is your address*, and it is refused to be anything more. Whether somebody wants to be brought into the present, wants to live in the branch, or wants continued contact is **Volume 06's question and may not be taken here.** What Volume 03 gives them is the civic thing: a name in a system, an address on a sheet, and a form filled in correctly.
- **The forms are the small horror.** Emrys has a marriage certificate and no wife. Marek has a father and no birth certificate that names him. Sabina has a son who is her nephew. **Every document is valid and none of them agrees with any other, and the men at the counter are not villains, they are correct, and the correct answer is that the system has no field for what has happened to these three.**

### 6. The Choir's first public leader, by name, with a real constituency

- **Tobias Ferrand, 52.** He is named on the program, on the leaflet, and on the retention schedule, and in Chapter 148 he says the word **Choir** for the first time in this manuscript, once, like a man saying a union's name, and **Volume 03 does not explain where it comes from, who is in it, or what it wants beyond his argument.**
- **His constituency, and it is not a lunatic one:** the bereaved of Tidegate and of the Salt Meridian; people who want one answer and a name on it; and a large number of tired, specific, competent, grateful ordinary people who do not want to be told that the safe answer is not a safe answer. **Marged Prosser, 55**, lost her son in the 2039 outfall, chairs a parents' group, is formidable, is not stupid, and is not a prop. She is the constituency, and she is right about most of it.
- **His argument, said once, in a hall, in Chapter 148, and not refuted because it is not wrong:**

> "You want me to hold uncertainty open. I was the duty flood officer in 2031 and I stood in a school gym at ten past three in the morning with two reports and forty-one people, and I could not tell them which one to stand in, and they stood in the one I was standing in. Forty-one of them are alive. In the other report that school is under nine feet of water and there is no list anywhere of who was in it. You have spent a year producing lists and asking people to sit in the uncertainty while somebody writes down that they chose to. My program produces a district that is still standing and a list of who was in it, and the cost of the list is two hundred and eighty-five addresses, and I say that number out loud every time and I do not apologize for it, and the people who would like me to apologize are mostly people who have not lost anybody this year."

- **And the question he puts to Elias, which Elias cannot answer, and does not pretend to:** *You have never once been the man in the room when the water was coming and the reports disagreed. Everything you have built is a way of never having to be that man.*
- **Elias's answer, which is true and is not enough:** *No. I have never had to. And that is not because I am better at it. It is because there were twenty-eight people in a hall and the hall had its own lights.* **It is the volume's growth stated in one sentence and Ferrand's constituency does not accept it, because it takes eleven days and a shed and a woman with a sheet, and that is the whole of the argument the volume hands to Volume 04.**

## Midpoint reversal — Chapter 125

**A future Mara refuses to lend her hands when the present crew tries to force the pilot's method.**

The reversal is not a power event. **It is a woman declining, in a room, in a sentence that uses three of her own standing refusals, for a reason that any reader of Volume 01 can check against the record.** It is prepared by four things: the program calling the gift `SECURED SUBJECT`; the sheet of `TRANSITIONAL` addresses sitting on a table with nobody's name on it; Elias's four-minute promise made three days earlier; and a present Mara who says, in front of people, that she is not entitled to be angry at a future version of herself and is not going to be grateful to her either.

**Its cost is that it works and it costs eleven people.** And Elias asked, and the asking is his, and the volume does not let anybody take it off him.

## Volume climax — Chapters 141–149

1. **Chapter 141, Thursday the eighth of January** — the final clearance fails. A stair, a scaffold, a lorry on a soft road, and injuries. **The failure is in the cell's own log, in the cell's own hand, contemporaneously, and it is not the log the team asked for.** The works foreman, **Nev Peart, 51**, has kept a shift book in his own hand for nineteen years, and the reason he keeps it is not that the terminal refuses corrections — the terminal takes corrections — **it is that the terminal cannot show what it said before.** The team's records request was for the wrong book.
2. **Chapter 142** — the four in the November clearance are named, from Winifred Alagoa's 2031 sheet. The team's own fact about the request and the sheet is put in one exchange and is the volume's method: a person, a document, a date, a refusal, and no speech.

## Concrete resolution — Chapter 150

- **The district survives**, and it survives on the reading and not on the list, and the people who evacuate it are Tidegate people, and Elias is a pair of hands in a corridor.

## Volume continuity locks

27. **No narrator reference to the book** — *the volume, the batch, this chapter, the novel, manuscript, the reader, this story, this series* — anywhere in Chapters 101–150.
