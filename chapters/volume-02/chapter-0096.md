# Chapter 96 — Eight Pages

He came up the ramp at ten past nine on Monday morning, the twenty-fourth of November, the seventy-ninth day, with a cardboard records box under his right arm. He had carried it on the eighty bus because there was nobody in that building he was willing to ask to carry it.

Cliff Marner was forty-nine days out of the ground and in the chair by the window. Della Marner was seventy-nine days at a desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was seventy-nine days in, and nobody had gone to look at him in eleven weeks.

The front room had the chairs out of it and the board left as it was, and there were four people in it.

---

Tomas Vale put the box on the table, took the lid off, took out eight sheets with a printed heading on the top one, and squared them up against the edge of the table with his fingertips.

"Eight pages. It is not what you think it is, and I am saying that first, because if I do not, somebody spends ten minutes looking for the wrong thing.

"**It is not a confession. It does not explain anything. It is a way of doing a thing, I wrote it in nineteen ninety-eight, my name is on it four times, and I am not going to read the four times out, because you will find them and counting them is not a use of the thing.**"

He turned the first sheet round, pushed it toward Mara Okafor and Joon Park, took his hands off it and put them flat on the table. Elias Rook was standing by the wall because he had been told to stand by the wall, having said *I am going to be useless in this room and I would like that minuted*, and he had just watched a man do a version of a thing he had watched him do in a records room six days ago.

"It is a method," Tomas Vale said. "**Put a held plan in front of a room in such a way that somebody who is not the author of the plan can stop it.** That is all of it, and there is no ninth page because there is no ninth page."

---

Joon Park read it out, because Mr. Vale had put it on the table and not given it to anybody, and reading it aloud was the only way it stopped being one man's thing.

He read the first two steps and then stopped and asked whether he was to read all of it.

"All of it, in order. And read the fourth one twice, because the fourth one is the whole method and the other six are how a person gets to a place where he can do the fourth one."

Joon Park read the first six in a flat voice and read the fourth one twice.

> **4. Say out loud, before you say anything at all about the plan, that you have not told this room what you are about to do, and that you are telling them that now and not afterwards. Then stop talking.**

Mara Okafor said, quietly, "That is a disclosure rule."

"It is a disclosure rule with the thing it is *about* taken out," Tomas Vale said. "**Dr. Okafor's rule goes to the people who pay for it, in the words it was said in, before it is acted on. Mine says *before you say anything about the plan*. I wrote mine in ninety-eight because I had watched a man say a true thing in a room two hours after he had decided to do the thing the true thing was about, and be thanked for the true thing, and the two hours were the whole of the damage.**"

"And step five," Joon Park said.

> **5. Stop talking and let them stop you. If nobody stops you, you have not done step five, and you go back to step two and take it to somebody else.**

"That is the one nobody believes," Tomas Vale said. "**Every man I have shown that page to has said *I would stop him*, and it is not about whether you would. It is that a room told in advance is a room and a room not told in advance is an audience, and the same eleven people in the same chairs do completely different things to each other.** I have watched it go wrong twice. Once was a scaffold. Once was a woman, and the woman is alive, and I am not going to talk about the woman."

---

Mara Okafor read the last page, and then backwards, and then put her finger on the head of page one. "Your name is here."

"That page is about a night in a year and not about a date," Joon Park said, out loud, to the room, in the voice he used for things a room could hear. "I am not going to put the two together and I would like it written down with the rest of it, and Mr. Vale, if there is a month on that page anywhere, nobody in this building is going to read it out."

"Four times. **Once at the top, which is a habit and not a boast. Once where I say the method is mine and not the industry's and not this office's, and I have had two people in thirty-one years offer to make it the industry's and I have said no twice. Once in the fourth paragraph, and that is the sentence that matters and I would rather say it than have you find it.**"

He did not read it. He said it.

"**I wrote it down because a man ought to be able to write down how to be stopped before he needs to be, and I did not write it down, and that is the whole of the confession and it is about a man and not about a plan and it is not a thing anybody can use.**"

He stopped.

"You have been looking at eight pages for a thing that says where any of it started," Elias Rook said from the wall, before he could stop himself, and then went on with the rest of it, because stopping halfway was worse. "It is not in there."

"No."

"What is in there."

"A method, a man's name four times, a page about a night in a year, with no month on it that anybody in this room is going to say out loud, and about a hundred and forty words on step five." Tomas Vale looked at him. "**I have been asked since the sixth of September by four people whether I know where any of this started, and I have said no four times, and it is true four times, and I would rather you had it from me than worked out that I had it.**"

---

"I did not know you had that," Elias Rook said, and then heard himself, and heard what the sentence was.

He had been in the inquiry room nineteen days ago and watched a man of sixty-four hear himself told that a belief he had held for a year was the most dangerous belief a man can hold in an inquiry, say thank you, and be told not to thank him. He had watched him give the only true thing he had given anybody in ten weeks on the Friday night, to three people, and then go and stand in a corridor, and then write up stairs refusing to have his name on it.

And on every one of those days Elias Rook had been quietly protecting him from it.

**He had been standing between that man and a document the man had put in his own hands in nineteen ninety-eight, and the document was not about the inquiry and was never going to be found by the inquiry, and it was a set of instructions for how to be stopped by somebody who was not you — and it had been protecting all of them for eleven weeks, including Elias Rook, and the only person in the world who could use it was the man holding it, and he had not used it once.**

"Mr. Vale," Joon Park said. "Step two. It says take the plan to a room with at least one person in it who was not you and who was in the room where you were going to do it. **You have been in this building for eleven weeks. There have been at least four rooms in this building where you were going to do something. Have you taken this page into one of them.**"

"No," Tomas Vale said.

"Why not."

"**Because I do not think this building is the room where I was going to do it, and I have not once written down why, and if I had written down why it would be a page about me, and I have had enough of those.**"

Nobody said anything for a while, and the building did its ordinary evening thing on the other side of the wall, and Petar Lisk's board was four feet away.

---

"I am not giving you that to use," Tomas Vale said. "I want to be exact, because I have been asked twice in a room with a recorder in it whether I will make myself available, and I answered yes, and I am not going to arrive in a hearing in December with a document and have it read out as though I have been holding it back since September, and I am not going to be a man who produced something on the day it was convenient either.

"**It is not evidence. It is not about what anybody did on the sixth of September, and it is not about a form, and it is not about the twenty-third of October. I am not putting it in front of anybody's inquiry and I would ask you to leave it off anything that goes to the Meridian.**

"**I am putting it on this table because a method is only worth anything if somebody else uses it, and I am not going to ask you to use it, and if I asked you to use it that would be me using it on you, which is the entire thing the fourth step is against.** So it is on the table and it is four lines of my handwriting and it is Monday, and Wednesday is Wednesday, and that is all I am going to say about it."

Joon Park wrote in the schedule on his knee with his right hand, because his left held the book open, and Elias Rook understood about eleven seconds late that the line was a new one.

> **22. Eight pages, one sheet of hall paper with a printed heading, the writer's own hand, the writer present. Three named: T. Vale, M. Okafor, J. Park. Not a record of any event. No holder required, no building required, no address: a man can produce a thing in a room and then the thing is his again. Not to be recalled. Not to be produced on request by anybody, including us. Not a record. The only line in this schedule that is a person keeping a thing for himself, and the reason it is in here is that three people saw it happen.**

He read it back, and the twentieth and twenty-first lines above it were untouched, and nothing was renumbered.

---

Mara Okafor stood up first, and did not say thank you, because she had heard somebody else be told not to thank him in an inquiry room nineteen days ago and had worked out in about a second what he would do with it.

"You have never been caught," she said. "That is what I want to say and it is not a compliment. In September you told this office, unprompted, that there was a 2034 write-off and a box with a number on it, and the disclosure they hold names the write-off, and it does not name a second thing in the same box, and there is nothing in eleven weeks of their questions that would take them to a second thing. **You have been carrying this since the third week of September and nobody has come for you and that is not because you were careful. It is because you gave them the true answer to the question they asked, and the true answer was the whole of the box as far as they were concerned, and a man who tells the truth about one thing and not another is not a man hiding anything. He is a man with a door in him.**"

"Yes," Tomas Vale said.

"When did you decide."

"Third week of September. About the Thursday."

"That is a month of deciding."

"It is a month of deciding and eleven weeks of not doing it. Those are different lengths, and I have spent thirty-one years being the man who does not confuse the two, and nobody in this room is going to write it down, and that is correct."

Emil Baros was not in the room. Nobody in the room's function was to write it down, and Elias Rook found he did not want anybody else to be, and understood about four seconds later that this was step four, that he had not been told it and had worked it out, and that a method which worked on somebody who had not read it was either nothing at all or the only thing in this building that worked.

At ten past ten the man of sixty-four put the eight pages back on top of the 2034 report, put the lid on the box, and said he was going to go and stand in a corridor, which is what he does.

Nobody in the room promised anybody anything. That is on the page, because Joon Park wrote it there, and because on Wednesday morning at seven, in the front room, somebody was going to have to say out loud that they had not told the room what they were about to do, and that they were telling it now and not afterwards.
