# Chapter 73 — The Shelf Nobody Had a Reason To Look At

On Saturday morning, the fifty-sixth day, Joon Park put a sheet of the hall's own paper on the table in the records room and did not sit down, and Elias Rook understood before anybody said anything that this was going to take four hours.

"You cannot ask for a book by describing what is in it," Joon said.

"You can ask for a series."

"You can ask for a series if the series is a thing that exists without you." He wrote four headings across the top of the sheet. "A subject, a shelf range, a span, and a request for a certified copy at the end if one turns out to exist. That is a real request. Any clerk in that building could walk to a shelf with those four things on a piece of paper and not ask one question on the way.

"And it is honest, and I want it on the record that it is honest, and I am writing down that it is honest before anybody can say afterwards that I narrowed it later. **There is no blade in it. There is no field in it. There is no signature in it. There is no year in it. There is a subject and a shelf range and a span and that is the whole of what we are asking for, and if what comes back is nothing then we will have spent a bus fare finding out that there is nothing, and that is also worth having.**"

The subject line read: *regional signal maintenance series, Civic Spine relay, Meridian — paper working volumes, 1990–2005.*

"A span of fifteen years on a request form," Elias said. "That is fifteen years of somebody's handwriting in a store room and you are asking a stranger to walk a trolley for it."

"Yes."

"Because we cannot say the year."

"Because we cannot say the year." Joon did not look up. "And because a request has to name a thing, and the only thing we are allowed to name out loud in that building is a subject and a shelf range, and if we name a year then we have told them what we are looking for, and then they are helping us look for something, and a helper is a person who is in it."

Nobody in the front room had said a year out loud in nine days and nobody had made a rule about it, and the rule of not saying it had been made in the last week by twenty-eight people who had never discussed it, and Joon Park was not one of the twenty-eight and had understood it in about four seconds on the Wednesday and had not asked.

---

The second problem was the man in the building.

Rufus Aitken had said on the Wednesday that he would not be told what a box was for, and had done it in a way that cost him nothing, and Elias Rook had spent two days in a kitchen on Ash Street deciding that he was going to be able to walk in there on Saturday and ask a question and be answered.

He could not.

"I am going to ask you to bring out a run of volumes," Elias said, "on a shelf range, on a request, and I am not going to tell you why, and you are not going to ask, and both of those are the correct shape."

"That is the correct shape," Aitken said, "and it is not the shape I said no to on Wednesday."

"You said no to being told what a box was for."

"I said no to carrying something for somebody who had not asked in a form." Aitken had the request on the counter between them and he was not touching it. "Do you know what the last such thing was? Nineteen years ago. A man came in at a counter in front of me at about four in the afternoon and he had a question about his wife and there was one page, and there was no form because he was a member of the public and members of the public do not have forms, and I carried it out to him myself and I read it to him in the interview room, because I thought a man who has been told *there is no record* and a man who has *heard it read* are two different men. I was right about that. It changed nothing. He went away with the page and the file did not change and I have never found out whether he read it, and if I had known I would have been told it was not my business.

"So I do not carry things for people who have not asked in a form, and I am not telling you which of you is the man who is going to be disappointed, because you are both of you going to be disappointed, and I would rather be disappointed by two men who asked properly than helpful to one of them at a counter on a Saturday morning with the fog coming in."

Elias Rook stood at that counter for a second and understood that he had been about to ask a question, and that the question was not a question, it was a way of getting the thing done, and that the man in front of him had known that for nineteen years and had stopped doing it on purpose.

"That was your reason for the page," he said.

"That is my reason for the page," Aitken said. "The reason for the request is on the counter and it is in my hand at ten past nine on Monday, and the eleven days start then, and the eleven days are the same for a man with a holding number and a man with a piece of paper, and I will say that out loud in this building once so that nobody afterwards tells you it was special."

"Please say it where Mr. Park can hear it."

Aitken said it again in the same voice at the same temperature.

---

What came back on Saturday was a clipboard, and on the clipboard was a shelf range, and next to the shelf range was a line in Aitken's own writing that said *run, 5 vols, paper, 1996–2001, Civic Spine relay maintenance, Meridian, no index card.*

Then Aitken did a thing that Elias Rook did not expect and wrote down afterwards because he could not think of it for nine minutes.

He went out to the middle of the floor and came back with a second clipboard and put it flat on the counter and did not say anything about it.

It was a shelf list for the whole of that store. Nine hundred and some lines of typing. He put his thumb on one line and left it there.

"That," he said. "Five volumes, paper, bound, working logs, a relay in the west district, maintained by somebody in a unit and not by a person, from ninety-six to two thousand and one. They came in on a cart in the twenty-thirty-nine consolidation because the twenty-thirty-nine consolidation moved a shelf of Civic Spine papers and this was standing next to it and nobody has ever asked for them and there is no card on the run, and I have been in this store eleven years and I did not know they were on that shelf until this morning, and I found them because you wrote a subject down that happened to be the subject of a run of paper."

He took his thumb off the line.

"So now you know what is on it, and I want to be careful here, because I am about to say the only thing I have got and I am not going to say it in a way that makes it more than it is. **I am not saying this to you. I am saying it because in this building the answer to somebody not asking a thing and an answer to somebody asking it are the same document, and if nobody ever asked, then in nineteen years somebody will go through this file and find that nobody asked, and that is a different fact and it is a worse one to have in a building than a line in a day book.**"

"What day book."

"The store's. It records movements and it is the only book in here that anybody outside my building has ever been asked to write in, and that was you, ten days ago, and it is still there, and it is not a very good answer and it is the one I have got."

---

The third lever was the only one they could pull without a form.

The store room's public hour from ten to twelve on a Sunday exists because a preservation levy pays for it, and the levy comes out of a county budget that Bettina Hoyle's desk sits inside, and a preservation levy buys a public hour in a store room and does not buy anybody's goodwill. There is no form for it. There is a key on a hook and a woman under the key who has been on that desk for six years and has had four visitors in six years, two of them lost, and she is not sorry about the two.

Joon Park took the bus out on Saturday and filed the request at the counter between nine and one, and came back on the four o'clock, and the fare is two dollars sixty each way and the hall agreed to one, so he was out one fare and did not ask anybody for it.

He came back with a page torn off a day book, with a date and two sets of initials on it, and nothing else, and he said on the step of the hall that the Sunday hour was not his to spend and that he was not going to spend it, and that if the Sunday hour was going to be used then the man who used it had to be somebody a woman in that building could ask a question of and get an answer from.

Elias Rook said no to the second trip. He had said no on Friday and he said no again on Saturday, and the second no was easier, and he noticed that the second no was easier, and wrote nothing about it.

---

He was left in a building that was running without him for the second time in ten days, and he found that he could do the work.

He did the lamp count. He checked the four carded draws for the evening and three were in a hand he had not seen before and one was Ru Sabar's with her time on it. He walked the corridor at ten past one in the morning with a hand lamp because it was his turn on the sheet and because Petar Lisk is nine and the sheet has no clause about that. He stood in the records room with the tin on the table and nineteen lines in nineteen hands on the wall in front of him and nothing new under any of them.

At about two o'clock he understood that there was nothing in the building that needed him, and that the reason there was nothing in the building that needed him was not that it had failed, and that the only thing in the room he could have done with the morning was the thing he had been told not to do on Friday.

He did not enjoy finding that out. He noticed, at about two, that he had been looking at the front door every twenty minutes since ten, and that the bus from Cordell came in at ten past two and forty minutes, and that he was glad, and that the gladness was the exact shape of the thing he had said out loud in the front room on Friday about enjoying being the right one. He wrote the date under it and shut the notebook.

Five bound volumes of paper were standing on a shelf in Cordell that nobody had ever asked for, and the thumb that had found them had come off the line, and the next bus out of this city was on Sunday morning, and he had looked that up twice and told nobody either time.
