# Chapter 94 — What The Office Cannot Say

It was ten past nine on Saturday morning, the twenty-second of November, the seventy-seventh day, and Elias Rook had been up since five and had done the only thing in his week that anybody had thanked him for, which was not asking a man about a box under a chair.

Cliff Marner was forty-seven days out and in a chair by a window. Della Marner was seventy-seven days at a desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was seventy-seven days in. Nobody had gone near any of it.

He had a page in his own notebook with nine days written on it, being the things he did not know, and the line at the top of it said *they have never withheld anything from us*, six words, his own, written on a bus coming home from a building forty minutes and an ordinary road away, and there was not one more sentence after it. **That was the shape of the whole business. Every true thing anybody has got hold of in eleven weeks is a line with no more sentence after it, and the reason nobody has got a sentence is that a sentence would need somebody to be able to be asked what it meant.**

---

The problem in the records room at eleven o'clock was this. The team had eleven weeks of things the Office had said, and every one of them had been said by two men.

"That is not a complaint about them," Elias Rook said. "**It is a description of what we have got.** Everything true this office has said in ten weeks came out of one man's mouth in a hearing room and one woman's mouth in a corridor, and they are the only two people in this building who are allowed to say it, and if we build a page that carries their names, then that page is a document about Nathan Rusk and Renata Solano, and in about four years somebody reads it and says *those two were the ones who found it*, and the page stops being anything about a design and becomes two careers, and we will have handed a man's ordinary job to the man it is about."

"Then leave the names off," Nadi Bell said.

"**Then there is no way on the page of knowing who said it, and a page with no way of knowing who said it is a page about a rumor.**"

Emil Baros got up, got the roll of hall paper out of the cupboard, and came back without saying anything.

"Emil."

"**I have got a pencil and it is Saturday and the thing this sheet is for is Tuesday, and if it is not on this table on Tuesday morning it does not exist on Wednesday, and I have been the man with the pencil in this building for six weeks and this is the first job anybody has ever given me that is actually the job.** Let me get it wrong in front of you instead of in front of a room."

---

He built it on the top of the records table over about an hour and a half, and he built it the way he built everything, which was by refusing a heading four times before he wrote one.

"Not four columns," he said, on the second refusal. "Four columns is a finding. A finding has a subject and a predicate and a date and a place, and the subject is a thing and the predicate is a decision, and a sheet with a subject and a predicate is a sheet that decides something."

"It is a table," Joon Park said. "Tables decide nothing."

"**A table with a heading on it is a table somebody will read as a finding in about two years, and nobody will be able to prove they are wrong, because they will not be able to prove they are right either, and I have been on the wrong side of exactly that twice.**"

He wrote, at the top, in capitals, and underlined it once:

> **THIS IS NOT A FINDING. IT IS A QUOTATION.**

And then he ruled two columns and headed the first one *the words as spoken* and the second one *where, when, and by whom*, and he ruled a third space down the side for nothing, which he explained when somebody asked: "So that if in about four years somebody has to add a line, they add it in the margin and not in the middle, and the middle stays the way we said it."

He got to the bottom of the page and stopped with the pencil about a quarter of an inch off the paper.

"There is no signature block," he said.

"No," Elias Rook said.

"**If four of us sign that then it is a document about the four of us, and the four of us are the people who went and got it, and in about four years it will be a document about a team that went and got it, and that is the last thing on earth that page should be.** So there is no signature and no printed name and no capacity line, and at the bottom I am going to write the only sentence that is mine on it."

He wrote:

> **Every line on this sheet was said in a room with a recorder in it, or typed by a clerk who types what is said. There is no line on it that anybody in this building remembers better than the person who said it.**

"That is the fourth refusal," Nadi Bell said, "and you did not refuse a heading that time, you refused a signature, and it is the same argument and it took you an hour and a half longer."

"It took me eleven years at a print shop before," Emil Baros said. "You get faster."

---

They filled it in from the fourth of September to the twentieth of November, and it took until about half past two, and it came to nineteen lines.

It is not going to be read out here. It is four columns of words on a Saturday afternoon and the room can have it.

What the room got instead, at about three o'clock, was Lena Mir reading the top of it aloud with the part of her voice she used for things she had decided were true.

"**Put that on the board and none of you are quoting the Office. You are quoting two people, and one of them is a Deputy Director and one of them is a lawyer, and a page with those two names on it is a page about the men and not about the machine.**"

"And if we take the names off," Tarin Voss said, from the door, "then somebody who did not hear them can put words in their mouths, and in about four years the Office can say the sheet was made up, and they will be able to say it, because the sheet will have no author and no date and nobody in it who can be called to account for it."

"**Then say it, and say the whole of it, and say it in the first line.**" Emil Baros had the pencil up. "*This page quotes the Office of Continuity and two officers of it and nobody else. The officers are named. If the Office says any line on this page is not what was said, the sheet goes to a hearing and the recorder's transcript settles it, and the recorder's transcript is a thing we do not have.* **A claim that a document is a fake is only dangerous to the document if somebody can check it. We cannot check the transcript. So the claim is free, and we say so before the claim is made, and then we are not defending anything.**"

Nobody argued with it, which is not the same as agreeing with it, and Emil Baros went red to the ears in the way that had become a thing in that building and that nobody mentioned.

---

And then Emil Baros did the thing nobody in the room had asked him to do, which was to follow a difference all of them had heard him name twice back to the place it came from.

He had the hall's copy of the four lines on the table. Joon Park had the typed copy, the one that went into the inquiry's file on the seventeenth of November with a date and a name on it, and it had come back from the Meridian that morning by hand.

"**Emil.**"

"I am not doing anything. I am holding two pieces of paper up to a light like a man who has been asked to hold a pencil and does not have to do anything else."

He held them up to the window.

"**You all know the letter,**" he said. "Ours says *items, 2*. Theirs says *item, 2*. One letter and no comma on theirs, and I have said it twice and Mrs. Mir read it out on Tuesday, and it is in the record. That is not what I am looking at."

Nadi Bell said, "Then say what you are looking at."

"Where it came from. Both of them." Emil Baros put the two sheets down very carefully on the table, in the right order, and put his own finger on one and then on the other, and had to stop and start again. "**Our copy is a copy of the clerk's chalk. Somebody in this room copied it off that wall in September. The Office's copy is a copy of the same chalk. And the one-letter difference is the letter that is on our copy, and ours is the one that is right, because I have held the wall's version in my hand about forty times.**

"So we have got no original anywhere. There is no original. There is a stranger's chalk on a wall, and two copies of it, and one of them is ours and one of them is theirs, and the difference between them is *items* and *item*, and the one that is correct is the one this hall made."

Lena Mir said, quietly: "Emil, that is the third time that function of yours has been worth something."

"I know. I am not going to say anything about it, because I have been refused a line twice and I have stopped expecting it, and today the function was worth something and that is a Saturday and not a pedestal."

---

The argument that followed went on until about five and nobody won it, which was the correct outcome and which both of the people who cared most about it treated as the point.

The case, in the end, was eleven words long and none of them were the team's, and Elias Rook said them out loud in the records room at about ten past five because he wanted to hear whether they were true in a room.

"**'An irregularity in a record is answered by a process, and the step is written down, and the person who takes it is on a rota, and the rota does not have my name on it and is not supposed to.'** That is a Deputy Director, on the nineteenth of November, in a room, on the record. And our whole argument is one sentence long: *that is the correct answer to an irregularity in a record, and we would like to know what happens when the rota does not do the step, and so would the man who is on it.*"

"That is insulting him," Inez Sato said.

"**It is the only sentence I have got that is not a complaint, and it is made entirely out of his own words, and if it is insulting then he wrote the insult.**" Elias Rook put his right hand flat on the table, which he had started doing and had not noticed. "And that is the whole of the case. There is no other case. We have got a quotation of our own opponent and it is the only strong thing in this building, and it is humiliating, and it is correct, and I would like somebody to write *humiliating* next to it so that in about four years whoever reads it knows we knew."

"Somebody write it," Nadi Bell said.

Emil Baros wrote it in the margin, in pencil, under the line, in his own hand, and then he did not underline it, and he put the pencil down on top of the sheet, which was not part of the function and which everybody in the room noticed and none of them mentioned, and which was, on the whole, the most that anybody in the building had given anybody in a week.

On Monday at nine, in the front room with the chairs out of it and the board left alone, a man of sixty-four was going to come and put eight pages on a table, and nobody in the building was going to ask him what was on them.
