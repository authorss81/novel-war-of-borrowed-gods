# Chapter 82 — The Night It Was Invented

The clerk at the forms and returns unit of the Havenport Continuity Office read the revision letter out on the telephone at ten past nine on Monday morning, the sixty-fifth day, and she read it the way a person reads a thing she has read before, in the order it is printed, twice, in case anybody on the telephone had not written it down the first time.

> **OFFICE OF CONTINUITY — FORMS AND RETURNS.**
> **DS-4, DESIGNATED-SITE INCIDENT RETURN. REVISION C. Issued 14 January 2003. Supersedes Revision B.**
> **Change: one line added, being a consent line for consent entered on behalf of a subject unable to give it. No other field altered. No field removed.**
> **Reason, as printed: NO FIELD WAS AVAILABLE ON THIS RETURN FOR CONSENT ENTERED ON BEHALF OF A SUBJECT UNABLE TO GIVE IT.**
> **Requested by: District 9.**
> **Signed: Forms and Returns, Continuity Office. (A block. No name.)**

"That is the whole of it," the clerk said. "Eighty-odd words with the headings, and it is the only revision letter in the set for that form. I have read you the page and not the folder, because you did not ask me for the folder."

"Was there a covering slip."

"There is, and it says *per District 9, night of 13 January*, and it is initialled, and I am not reading an initial out to two men on a telephone at ten past nine in the morning, because an initial is a person, and if I read one out you will both write it down, and in a year somebody will say this office released a name."

"That is the correct answer and I would like it in my notebook as such," Joon Park said.

"Deputy Director's standing instruction," she said. "Questions get answered. Requests get queued. It is not written down anywhere and I have never seen it in a procedure."

---

Joon Park had the notebook open on the records room table and wrote all of it out in longhand and read it back to the telephone, every word, in the order it was printed, and then said the thing he says.

"I am not writing what anybody thinks it means. **That is the seventh time in ten weeks,** and this is the first one that is about a piece of paper nobody owns, so I am going to say why I say it, because in a fortnight somebody is going to read that line and think I am hiding behind it.

"I am using it. I have four questions I ask at a counter and the answers decide what I am allowed to write: what is it, where is it, who holds it, and who put the name on it. The habit underneath the four questions is not mine. It is Finding 3, and Finding 3 assumes a document has a holder and a subject and somebody who can be shown to have made an error about one of them."

He put the pen down.

"**This has no holder.** A file set does not have a custodian. A form store does not have a custodian. A revision that adds a line has no author and no requester and no victim, and a document like that cannot be impounded, cannot be produced, cannot be corrected, and cannot be handed to a person in a room and pointed at, and I have been doing this for ten weeks and I have never once had a document I did not know what to do with."

"So that is the first thing in nine weeks that frightened you," Elias Rook said.

"That is not what I said and you are going to let it stand because it is easier." Joon Park turned a page. "On Saturday I told nineteen people in that front room that I wanted to stop, and that a refusal which has stopped costing anything is a habit, and that I had written the wanting-to-stop down with a date on it so somebody could hold me to it in a year. I have been waiting since Saturday to find out what I would do when it mattered.

"And what I did this morning was ask a fifth question. **I made one up. On the spot, in a corridor, to a clerk who had already been more generous than her office requires.** I have never in nine weeks made up a question. I did not stop, and I did not want to, and what I did instead was build one, and I would like that in a book with a date on it, because a document with nobody in it was the reason and I am going to want proof of that in a year."

---

They went out to the district at twenty past two in the afternoon, forty minutes on a bus and eleven minutes on foot, and there is no Continuity Office building in the Ninth Ward and there has never been one.

**District 9 Service Office, 118 Ninth Street** is a municipal counter with a plastic queue rail, a fan heater, and a wall of forms in plastic sleeves, all of them, all current. Behind the counter was a woman called Mavis Toomey who had been there twenty-two years, and the nameplate had not been replaced since before the collapse.

"I am not going to pretend to know what you want," she said. "You are the third pair this month and the first two wanted a pothole."

"We want the night book," Elias Rook said, "and I am going to tell you what for before you decide and not after. To read one line of it and put the line down and nothing else. Joon Park is going to write down what I have just said while you are deciding, because that is the way he works and you should know it now."

Joon Park wrote it.

"A duty officer's night book is not a public record," Mavis Toomey said. "I have just done one of the three things in that sentence that could get me fired and I am going to do the other two. **You are not going to see the book.** I am going to open the cupboard, find the volume, read you the one line, and shut the cupboard. If anybody in this office ever asks me I am going to say I did not know what you were doing, and I will not be lying, because I genuinely will not."

"Because you told me the whole of it before I decided, and nobody in twenty-two years has done that at my counter, and because you have not asked me for a name." She opened a cupboard. "Which night."

"The night of Sunday the twelfth into Monday the thirteenth of January, two thousand and three. Any hour after midnight."

She found the volume. It is a bound book with a canvas spine, one of about forty on a shelf, one for each year, and none has been off that shelf since the year after it was filled.

She turned to the page and her hand went down the ruled columns the way a hand goes down a page it has read before, and she stopped about a third of the way down and read one line out loud, and her voice did not change at all.

> **13 JAN 2003 · MON — OFFICER ON DUTY:** *(a signature)*
> **02:41 — SITE 4, GATE STREET — INJURED MALE, UNCONSCIOUS ON ARRIVAL, AMBULANCE 22:10. NO SUBJECT SIGNATURE OBTAINED. RETURN COMPLETED BY DUTY OFFICER AND SENT TO DISTRICT OFFICE 06:50. RELIEF ON SITE 04:15.**

"That is the whole of that night," Mavis Toomey said. "There is a signature at the bottom of the page for the officer who took over and the district clerk signs the month off in the first week of the next one, and neither of them tells you anything. A night book records what was dealt with and who dealt with it. It is not a story."

Joon Park wrote the signature out in full and did not say it and did not read it back, because reading it back at a counter puts a name in a room, and then he closed the notebook, and Elias Rook read the two words off the page instead because there is no other hand he can read with.

"Was he good at it," Elias Rook said, before he knew he was going to ask.

"I have no idea," Mavis Toomey said. "I was front of house then. He came in on nights for eleven years and he was never anything to me."

"Then I am not going to ask the second question."

She looked at him. "What second question."

"Nothing," he said. "I have got a rule and I am not explaining it at your counter."

She shut the cupboard and turned round and said a second thing, which she had not been asked for either.

"He would not have needed to write that if the form had a line on it." She said it the way you say a thing that has been in a drawer for a long time. "I have had that page in my head for nineteen years and I have never said it out loud, and I am not saying it because you asked me anything. **Take it or leave it. It is yours either way and I have already forgotten that I said it.**"

Joon Park wrote it down, and then read it back to her, and she said it was not what she would have written.

"You are not going to use it," she said, not as a question.

"No, ma'am."

"Good," she said. "It is not a document and it does not want to be one, and the day somebody makes a document out of a thing a woman said at a counter in nineteen ninety-nine is the day this counter stops being a counter."

---

They stood outside on the pavement at ten past three with the fan heater still in their ears, and Joon Park turned round and said it to the door.

"I am not going to write down what anybody thinks that means, and I want you to know that it took me about a second and a half, which is faster than the other six put together, and I would like it on the page that it was fast.

"I am not going to write that he was competent. I have been tempted by that sentence twice since Monday morning and both times it was me doing the tempting, and in about forty years somebody is going to read a phrase I made in a municipal building and believe I found it in an institution's file. I did not. **I found it in a night book in a cupboard on Ninth Street because a woman opened it for me and had to be careful about it.**

"I asked for one to be written down in a store room in another city on the eighth of November and it is in a letter in a bookbinder's in this county and it cost me nothing at all. **This is the first one that has cost me anything, and it is the first one I have had to do in a building that is not mine, in front of a person who did not ask me to be careful.** That is not an accident. Somebody always has to do that one somewhere it costs them."



---

He got the bus back and walked the last eleven minutes because of the leg, and the whole of what he had found out was this.

There had been a man on a night in January 2003 who had a person on the floor and a form that wanted a signature and a witness who could not find anywhere on the paper to put his own name, and about nine minutes of shift in him, and he had done a small defensible thing that nobody had given him the power to do, and then he had gone to the next hour, which is on the same page in a different column.

**And there was nobody to be angry at.**

That was the thing he had not been ready for, and it arrived on a Monday afternoon on a pavement in a neighborhood with no Office building in it, and it was worse than a villain by a distance he could not have described in October. The reason was arithmetic. A villain is an address. Nine weeks of weight — a corridor, a board, a blade, a rectangle two inches by one, a woman in a school, a form that does its job, six people whose lives he had carried since the sixth of September — had all been on a frame with a man at one end, so that when the frame broke there would be somebody to hand it to.

There was no man at the end of the frame. There was a signature in a column in a book in a cupboard and a line of his own on the return saying he had sent it to the district office at twenty to seven in the morning, and a Tuesday-morning revision letter with a form number on it, and that was the whole of him in the world. It was complete. It had a date on it. There was no room in it left over for a person to be angry at.

And there was nowhere to put it, either. A night book on a municipal shelf is not a thing this business knows how to be responsible for. It cannot be given to a county library, because it is not theirs. It cannot go in a report, because it would put a name in a proceeding. It cannot go on a wall, because a name on a wall is a name on a wall forever, and the hall does not have a line for a resident to refuse on.

Mavis Toomey's line went in a notebook and stayed there, and nobody on Ash Street ever heard it, and it is not a document and does not want to be one.

Cliff Marner was coming home from Cordell on the Tuesday on a hospital car at ten past six in the morning, the ward had a card ready for him, and there was no designation for him anywhere in the world and the team was not going to invent one.

Both of those men had done a small defensible thing in a hurry, thirty-nine years apart, and neither of them had anybody to hand it to, and that is the whole of what a signature is.
