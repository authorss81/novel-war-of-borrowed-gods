# Chapter 65 — Two Words

Rosalind Teague was on the second floor in a room with eleven desks in it and she was thirty-nine and she had been doing it for twelve years, and she was the one who had opened the file, because Joon Park had telephoned her on Thursday afternoon and said four sentences and then stopped.

"You asked me for the fourteenth of April in two thousand and thirty-five," she said, when he came in. "I looked at the sheet when you called and I knew before you said the year. I want to say that first, because I spent about a second and a half being pleased that somebody had asked, and then about a second and a half being frightened, and both of those are mine and neither of them is yours."

Elias Rook sat down in a chair that was not hers.

"I am going to be here for an hour," Elias said, "and I am going to ask you about a form, and I am going to tell you before we start that I am not going to ask you about anything else and I am not going to ask you twice."

"You are going to ask me about the box," Teague said. "I put something in a box on the fourteenth of April in two thousand and thirty-five and I have known for nine days that it was a box, because somebody from an inquiry in another city telephoned a records clerk and asked me whether I remembered a Saturday in April, and no one has ever telephoned me before."

---

She did not want a lawyer and nobody suggested one, and she put a hand flat on the sheet in front of her and did not let go of it for most of the hour.

"It was not one job. That is the first thing you are going to get wrong if you go away with the wrong shape. **The renumbering was eleven weeks and I was on it every day of them and I was the lowest person at the table every day of them, and what I did was the part nobody wants.** I sorted the sheets. I matched the old sector numbers to the new ones. When a sector came in with a field on it that was not going anywhere — and there were more than you would think, and it was not a scandal, it was Tuesday — the box wanted a reason and I gave it the reason that was true."

"What was true," Joon said.

"That it did not apply." Teague said it without heat. "Not that the person did not exist. Not that the credential was never issued. **Not applicable.** The field was a holder field and the question printed over the second box was *state the reason a field is not carried forward*, and the reason was that the field could not be carried, and the correct answer to a question about a field is about the field. If the box had asked me who, I would have written who, and it did not, and I would like that written down before anybody decides I was being careful."

Elias Rook wrote it down.

"That is the whole of what I did," she said. "I put two words in a printed box in the correct space on the correct day, and I did it accurately, and I have not got a single one of them wrong, and I would like that on your page as well as the other thing."

---

"Tell them about the nine," Elias said.

"I do not know about nine."

"You know about nine," Joon Park said. "That is why we telephoned you. Somebody in this building has an index with a number in it and the number is nine, and it is in a file, and the file is a hundred and six pages, and you have not asked what the nine is, and I would like to be able to say that in a room in November."

Rosalind Teague put her hand further onto the sheet.

"Then here is what I know and it is the whole of what I know, and I am going to say it in the order it happened and not in the order it sounds best. **There were nine sectors in that run whose holder fields were not carried, and I was told by a man whose name I am not going to give you, because he is not part of this and I am not spending him, that the fields were not going to be carried and that it was a consolidation and that the names were on a succession list in the regional office, and I believed him, because I was twenty-six and it was not my department and because he was right that it was a consolidation.** I did not ask. Not because I was told not to. Because nobody made a thing of it, and I have spent nine days since somebody telephoned me finding out how much of my working life was that.

"I have gone back and looked. I want that said too. I went back on Friday and I went back again on Monday, and there is nothing to go back to, because the fields were not carried, so there is no field, and the file is a hundred and six pages of sheets with a ruled box on each one, and the box is empty, and there is a second box, and the second box has two words in it, and the two words are mine, and they are correct."

"Did you change them," Elias said.

"No."

"Would you?"

"I would go back tomorrow if the box had asked me who," Teague said. "It did not. And I am not going to stand in front of a Deputy Director in November and say that I was brave in a corridor, because I was not brave in a corridor, I was **competent at a form**, and there is a difference, and I have thought about it for nine days and I have got quite good at it."

---

Joon Park asked the last question in the wrong order on purpose, and asked it twice.

"Two questions, and you can refuse both. One: is there any other document anywhere, in any of the four buildings you have worked in, that carries a holder field for sector four C for that sequence. Two: did any of the nine names ever get written down in any building, by any person, for any reason."

"No," Teague said. "And no."

"Are you certain."

"I am certain of the second one and I am a clerk and not an archivist about the first," Teague said, "and if you want me to say *no, none that I have ever seen* instead of *no*, you will have to write down that I asked for the difference, and I would rather you did not."

"You get to say *no, none that I have ever seen*," Elias said, "and we write down that you asked for the difference, and we write it down in your words, which is the only way it is any use."

Joon Park wrote it in her words. He read the whole page back before he closed it — the date, the eleven weeks, the box, the two words, the succession list, the two no's, the requested qualification, and the six times she had said that nobody was owed an apology by her and once that she was sorry.

He added nothing to it. Elias Rook watched him not add anything to it, and understood for the first time in six days what that had cost the man in the corridor on Tuesday.

---

"Before you go," Teague said. "I am allowed to ask one thing and I want to ask it properly, because I have thought about it since Wednesday and I have decided that not asking would be the coward's version of not asking.

"What did the site produce."

She said it as a clerk says it, meaning a figure, meaning a return.

Elias Rook looked at the table.

"No," he said.

"I have a file in front of me that says it was a structural failure, and I am not asking you to tell me what broke—"

"I am not refusing you a number because of the file. I am refusing you a number and I am refusing you a name, and I would like to say why in one go, and then I would like never to say it again.

"There is a person under a street in my city. **Perpetua Oyelaran**, thirty-four, and she has been under Ash Street since the sixth of September, and she is on a wall in chalk in a building I work in, and I am not going to say that name to you, and I am not going to say a count, and neither of those is because of your file.

"It is because the only sentence in the whole of this that has ever been true about her is a sentence with a proper name in it and a tense — *was recovered, was recorded, was not* — written in advance by a woman who is alive and who was asked, and there is no version of this in which a man hands a name to a stranger in a corridor. **You did not ask me a cruel question. You asked the ordinary one, and the ordinary one has a person in it, and I am the man who has to keep her out of your ordinary one.** If anybody ever asks me in October what that site produced I am going to say that the answer is not a number, and you are going to hear the same sentence, and if you are the person who has to write it down then you are going to write that no figure was given and you are not going to fill the space in."

The room was very quiet and there were eleven desks in it.

"All right," Teague said, and it was not a small thing for her to say, and it was not a large thing either, and she said it in the ordinary voice of a woman who has a queue downstairs. "Then I will write *no figure was given*. I would like it written that I did not push."

"I will write that you did not push," Elias said, "in your words, and I will not underline it."

---

They stood in the second-floor corridor for eleven minutes after that, outside a fire door, and neither of them said anything for the first four.

Then Wendell Pryce came round the corner with an empty cardboard box, and stopped, and read both their faces the way a man reads a signal hut.

"You did not get a name," he said.

"No."

"Good." He shifted the box. "I am going to say one thing to you and then I am going to go up the stairs, and I am not being clever and I am not being kind, and I am not doing it for you.

"You have just left the strongest thing you had out of the room on purpose. The day you could have walked in there with a number in your hand, and a witness, and a form that said *and the person who answered this was* — and there is such a form, I have seen it — today you went in with two men and a holding number and you walked out with a page and a woman who was right, and the page is worth about half of what it was worth, and you will never be able to put the other half back. That is what it costs and you paid it in about nine minutes.

"And I will tell you the part that is not mine to give you, which is that I do not know how anybody does that twice. I have watched a man be honest for a living. I have never watched one be honest and then come out of a room poorer on purpose and I am not sure the trick is a trick and I am not sure it is one that survives being written about."

"You have written about it," Elias said. "On a pavement, in May. You told us you wanted that written down."

"I did," Pryce said, "and you wrote it down, and I have checked, and that is the only reason I am saying this to you in a corridor instead of not saying it."

He went up the stairs.

Elias Rook stood in a fire door corridor in another city with a page in a folder and a form with a box on it in a building ninety meters from a woman who was going to spend forty-eight more days at a desk, and he understood, in the way he understands rigging and gas and a room full of people who are frightened, that he had spent the whole morning refusing to help anybody, correctly, on purpose, at a cost, in a language he was making up as he went along, and that it was the first thing he had done in six weeks that he would still be able to describe in eleven years.

Joon Park put a hand on his shoulder for about a second and a half on the way to the stairs and did not say anything at all, which was the correct thing, and which Elias noticed and wrote down, with the date, in the car, with the tin in the boot.
