# Chapter 63 — The Form With a Box For It

Shelf 14C is not where anybody keeps a thing. It is the fourth bay along the east wall of a regional store room at half past ten on Wednesday morning, the forty-sixth day, a steel unit with a printed label and no index card, and Rufus Aitken read the holding number off it twice before letting them near it.

"This is the shelf," he said. "As of yesterday afternoon it was the third box on the bottom of a cart. As of this morning it is this, and the cart is a different problem and is not yours, and if you had come tomorrow you would have wanted a box and there is no box and you would have been quite right about that."

The 4C renumbering file was eleven centimeters thick and came out of a gray box onto a gray table under a bulb.

"That is the whole of it," Aitken said, from the doorway. "I have to be in the store for the public hour on Sunday and I have to be at the counter at one, and you have until one, and I would like it said that I have not read it and I am not going to."

---

The form was on top, and it was a single sheet, and it was regional.

That was the first thing Joon Park said, and he said it in a low voice with his hand a foot above the page, not touching it.

"This is not theirs."

"It is on a regional letterhead with a regional typeface," Elias said. "The Office has never had this form. We have never seen it. It is not in the eleven-page demand, it is not in the Spine's own file set, and there is no finding about it, because the Office's own practice does not reach across a regional boundary."

"So what happens if somebody has been careless on it," Joon said.

"Nothing automatic," Elias said. "That is what I am trying to say and I have been trying to say it since Monday. **In Havenport, the form writes back.** The moment a person has signed a wrong thing, a clerk enters a correction, a withdrawal is a finding, a copy goes into a file, and there is a shape for it. In this room the shape does not exist. Whatever this form was, nobody was ever required to do anything about it, and nobody could have been disciplined for getting it wrong, and nobody has been protected either."

Joon Park wrote one line and looked at it.

"I have been standing next to a fill-in since the fifth of October," he said, "in four buildings, and I have never once asked whether the paper was the Office's."

---

The form had eleven printed fields. Nine were about the sector, the block, the sequence, the date of the renumbering, the sheet count and the store.

The tenth was a box with **HOLDER** printed over it, and it was empty.

Not blank in a way a person leaves a thing. Empty in the way a printed box is empty — squared off, ruled, the same size as the other eight, with a hairline under it where a name would sit if a name had ever been the design.

**It has been empty since the spring of two thousand and thirty-five**, and the file is eleven centimeters thick because nine of those centimeters are sheet after sheet after sheet, and the holder box is on every one of them, empty on every one.

The eleventh field was on the right-hand side of the form, below the signature block, and it was a box, and it was printed.

It said:

**IF A FIELD IS NOT CARRIED FORWARD — STATE THE REASON.**

Elias Rook read it four times.

"That is a box," he said.

"It is a box," Aitken said from the doorway, without coming in. "They printed those on every regional mapping form from nineteen eighty-odd until about two thousand and twelve. I used to fill them in. It is not a secret and it is not a scandal. Half of them said *superseded* and a third said *not applicable* and the rest said *not held at consolidation* and every one of those was a true answer to the question that box asks."

"And what question does that box ask," Joon said.

"It asks what happened to the field," Aitken said. "That is all it has ever asked. It does not ask who it belonged to, because the design of this form is that the answer is about the field and not about the person, and that is deliberate, and it is good practice, and I would fight anybody in this building who told you it was a cover."

He looked at the doorframe.

"One of them filled one in in this building once, in front of me, and I would like to be left out of the rest of this."

---

The box had something in it.

Two words, in a small neat hand, in the middle of a ruled space about two inches wide, in a pen that had been the ordinary issue in a room like this one in the spring of two thousand and thirty-five:

**not applicable**

Joon Park took one breath through his nose and then put the pen down.

"I need to say something out loud and I am going to say it badly," he said, "because in a hall in Havenport I can say a thing like this for free. Everybody in that room is on my side, the room is ours, and a refusal costs nothing and is a performance the room lets you do. **This is not that.** You have done nothing wrong. You filled in a box on a form in your own hand, in your own building, and you were not hiding a person, and I am going to say out loud that I do not know what it means, in a room where I am the one with the notebook and you are the one who has to live here."

Somewhere down the aisle a trolley went past with four boxes on it.

"I want it written down that that cost me something and that I did it anyway," Joon said. "It goes in the custody log, because if it does not, then in a year somebody will say we never came."

"Write it," Elias said.

"That is the whole of it," Aitken said. "You have until one."

---

Joon recorded what was on the page. He recorded the printed heading, the field order, the fact that the holder box was ruled and empty on every sheet in the file, the two words, the pen, and the date the work was done, which was the fourteenth of April, and the season, which was the spring.

He recorded who had signed the form: a unit, in a block, in the way a unit signs a task and not the way a person signs a name.

He did not record what the two words meant. He wrote a line under it instead, in his own hand, and read it back to the room before he wrote it, which is a habit he has and does not explain.

"*We do not know what this says. We know who wrote it, and we know when, and we know the question it answers, and the question it answers is not the question we came with.*"

---

At five past eleven Elias Rook wanted to ask a second question.

He wanted to ask whether four returned sheets from the west apron in nineteen eighty-eight were in this file, and he wanted to ask it in the voice he uses when a person is about to be helpful to him, which is a voice that works.

He had used that voice at the depot on the far bank in the third week and it had got him two thousand four hundred meters of a corridor, and it had cost them the ground, and a month of his life is made of that.

Joon Park did not look up. He was writing.

Elias Rook shut his mouth, and what came out of it instead was a question about the appointment on Sunday, and the day went on.

---

They filed the request at the counter at four minutes past twelve, which is what they came for, and the woman who took it read him the interval off a wall chart without looking up.

"Four to six weeks from the twenty-second of October. That is the nineteenth of November at the near end and the third of December at the far end, and the near end is the one that arrives, and I have never once seen the far end arrive."

She looked up.

"You came in on a holding number. That is not a request and it does not start a clock, and I want to be clear that the two of you have had two days in a building on a number printed on a schedule in two thousand and thirty-nine, and nobody here is going to pretend that was the same as a request."

"We understand." Elias said.

"You are the first people in a year who have said that sentence in this room," she said, "and the ones who said it before you said it to get something."

---

In the lobby at half past twelve, by a bench and a coffee machine nobody had used since March, Elias Rook said the thing he had been not saying since half past ten.

"It is not a box for hiding a person."

Joon waited.

"All the way up I thought it was that," Elias said. "A field designed to be left empty so that somebody could be somewhere without being found. And it is not that at all, and the difference is worse. **It is a box for not carrying one.** Somebody in two thousand and thirty-five sat at a desk and was handed nine names on nine cards and a form with a box on it that said *state the reason*, and the form was not hiding anything and the person filling it in was not hiding anything, and the correct answer was two words long, and the two words were *not applicable*, and the field did not have to be carried, and nobody had to decide anything, and everybody went home at four o'clock."

"That is worse," Joon said.

"It is worse and it is correct," Elias said, "and there is not one thing anybody in that building could have done differently, and that is the part I will be carrying on Friday."

---

He telephoned the hall from the bench at ten past one, and Inez Sato answered, and did not say hello.

"Twelve for the function, eleven against, five not voting," she said. "It went at about eleven. I told you it would not be quick and I told you both costs and neither of the costs was the number."

"How is he."

"He is not here," Inez said. "He is at the clinic with Osei's wife and he does not know yet, and I am not ringing a man at a clinic about a vote he asked for a week ago and got nothing for nine days, so somebody has to telephone him, and I have not got a telephone and there is a handset in that building that only works in a corridor."

"You have me," Elias said.

"You have got until Tuesday," Inez said. "And when you ring him, do not tell him it was close. He will find out that it was close in about four minutes from the shape of your voice and I am not having him told a thing he can hear."

Elias Rook put his hand over the mouthpiece and said to Joon Park, who was writing, "How do I not let him hear it."

"Ask him a question," Joon said. "Ask him whether the ramp is fixed at the clinic end, because it is not, and then you will have to listen to it for four minutes, and by the time he has finished telling you about the ramp you will not have to do any of this."

Elias Rook asked Emil Baros about the ramp at the clinic end for four minutes, and then told him.

He got the count out in the wrong order twice and Baros stopped him on the second one and said, "Say it in the order it happened, not the order you want it to go in," and Elias said it again, and at the end of it Baros was quiet for about four seconds and then said a thing that Elias Rook wrote down in the notebook afterwards because it turned out to matter:

"Nine days I asked for it and nobody asked me a question. Twelve people voted for it this morning and not one of them asked me whether I wanted it. I would like somebody in that room to understand that I noticed."

---

At ten past three the box was back on the second cart, because the store room closes at one to the public and does not close to the work, and the conservation run had come back in, and shelf 14C was empty again for four days.

The last page of the file had a routing slip on it, and the routing slip had a signature block, and the signature block was a unit in a block.

And under the signature block, on the same page, in the same hand, two words in a box that somebody had printed in nineteen eighty-odd because they had thought about it carefully:

**not applicable**

There was a name in the center's own current index for the block, because the block is still in use, and it had a person in it this morning.

Joon Park read the name out loud twice, in the lobby, in the failing light, to a man with one hand, forty minutes and an ordinary road from the only place the paper existed.

Her name was Rosalind Teague. She was thirty-nine, and she was a records clerk on the second floor of a building he was standing in.
