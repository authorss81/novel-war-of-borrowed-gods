# Chapter 59 — What the Word Is For

The second sitting was in the same room on the third floor of an Office building on the Meridian, the file was an inch thicker, and the microphone had been moved from the middle of the table to the end of it, which is a small thing that changes where a person looks while he is saying something true.

It was twenty-nine minutes past nine on Saturday morning, the forty-second day, and Nathan Rusk sat down at the head of the table and said he was going to read for a while and that nobody would be asked anything until he had finished.

"I have had this since the seventeenth," he said, "and I have been getting it wrong in front of you on purpose for twenty-five days, because a theory you can argue with is worth more to me than one you have to take on trust, and because the person who benefits most from this document being read aloud is the person who wrote it against me. I am that person."

He read for two hours and eleven minutes.

---

The Office's case was not a monster and it was not stupid, and the part of it that made Elias Rook put his hand flat on the table was how old it was.

The **2019 Salt Crossing** outfall: a crew with no designation, a shaft on no drawing, six people dead inside eleven days, and a finding that said the word *unlicensed* in every paragraph and the word *cause* in none of them. The **2029 Meridian** review: eleven people dead behind a single approved outcome, and what came out of it was not rope access and not judgment. It was **one instruction, one route, no contradiction in the running order**, with a unilateral senior-safety-officer power to reroute, written by frightened competent people who wanted the number of decisions in a corridor to go down.

"Sir Peter Loman sat on that panel," Rusk said. "So did the man on your left. His name is in the file, I am not going to use it as a weapon at ten past eleven in the morning, and I am telling you it is there because in November somebody will find it there."

Tomas Vale said nothing and did not move.

"Here is the shape of it, as a shape and not as a finding, because it is not one. **A borrowed capacity was offered to you on a Saturday morning and a decision was taken with it inside a run of concrete that had already changed twice that month.** Your choices did not create the seam and did not close the route that had already closed. The Office's position — and I have never had the evidence to make it a conclusion — is that **an unlicensed, unrecorded, self-governed practice is a cause of these events, because the events recur wherever that practice is tolerated.** Not the only cause. A cause. Take it apart. I would rather you did that here than in a document I have to write in October."

---

They took it apart for an hour and it held, and the holding was the part that mattered.

Elias asked whether the Office would have issued a designation that permitted entry into the lower service run on the twenty-ninth day. Rusk read for a while and then said, "No."

"That is the answer to the whole thing," Elias said.

"That is one half of it and I have been waiting two hours for you to notice the other half," Rusk said. "A designation would have permitted entry, in the morning, into a corridor that terminates at eleven meters with the far twenty not there — which puts a crew past the boundary of the thing they were told about. Your practice found that boundary by walking it. That is the strongest thing in your case and it is also why I can write what I am going to write, because a licensed entry would have gone past it and been blamed for it."

Nobody had a better answer, and Elias sat with a hand that does nothing and understood that the sentence was not a trap. It was arithmetic.

At twenty past twelve, Counsel Solano asked Mara Okafor to confirm a page that was in her handwriting and described a thing that had worked: a disclosure given to the people who paid for it, in the words it was said in, before it was acted on.

"A document in an exhibit file is the only place in the world where a thing that worked is read by strangers," Mara said. "Somebody is looking. It is the wrong somebody, and I would like it read anyway — and if a page of mine is ever used as an example of a process that failed, I will be back in this room in a week."

---

Then, at twenty past one, Rusk asked for the counter-case, and Joon Park produced it, in order, with the times on it.

The blank subject field in an aisle ledger, visible in a printed document since the seventeenth, filled in on Saturday at 11:12 by a woman who was in the room and had not been in the ground — accurate about her own afternoon, silent about somebody else's. The alignment the Office's own terminal had offered, *close the line, the discrepancy resolves, no further action required*, and the refusal written on the page under it in a hand, with a reason in one sentence, initialed, read out twice, in a file four people signed.

Then the receipt. Two clerks and a handcart, ninety minutes, and a **fourth column in a stranger's chalk on a wall**, because the last clerk ran out of forms at twenty past two and the wall was the only surface in the room with a column on it. One of the twenty-eight lines was wrong, and a nurse made the clerk write a name out in full, strike the number through with one line, and initial the correction in her own hand. It took four minutes, and it is the only place in that ward's history where an Office clerk and a ward nurse stand in the same column in the same ink.

Rusk had the receipt. He read it twice, turned it round, and read the fourth column aloud, all twenty-eight lines, at twenty-five past one in the afternoon.

"It is in your file already as a receipt," he said. "That is not the same as evidence, and the difference is the whole of what I can do for you this month. I was at your building on Friday night, on my own, and stood in front of that wall for about four minutes. I am trained to look for an author — it is the first thing you do with a document, and I have done it for nineteen years — and I did it in a hallway, and what came back was nothing. Nine-year-old capitals, chalk, a woman who organizes a cooperative, a nurse, a clerk who ran out of paper. If I had to write a paragraph about that wall for a court I could not say who wrote any of it. Neither could you. That is not a gap in your evidence. That is its shape, and I have spent my morning reading a theory that says the shape is the danger."

"So the counter-case is not persuasive," Elias said.

"The counter-case is **good**," Rusk said. "It is not persuasive. I am telling you both in one sentence so that you cannot have one without the other."

---

The four words came at half past two and the room had gone completely still by the third of them. He had one sheet, and he said he had made it himself on Wednesday because he did not trust anybody else to make it correctly.

"**Not used.**" He read where it came from and who wrote it. "An aisle ledger, a subject field, a fortnight in which nobody entered anything, and the entry was accurate because nobody had been told the field was empty. That is the honest word of a system that had not been asked a question.

"**Withdrawn.** A nineteen-year-old, in his own words, on the sixteenth, through a finding this office adopted at the cost of five years of forms and a third of its clerks. The strongest document any of you hold has no name on it, the withdrawal is what took it off, it was correct, and it is the only reason the page is still valid.

"**Declined.** A man on a bus, in a form, with a precedent of his own, and a rule in this city that says *say declined, not not used*, and he said it correctly and he was right to.

"**Administrative error.**" Rusk put a finger on it. "My office, in a letter on this case this month. Nobody in that sentence hid anything and nobody decided anything. An error is a thing that happens and then gets put right in the ordinary course.

"And **unresolved**, which is not ours. A family-reception desk in Cordell, on a case open since the sixth of September, in a clerk's hand, because she did not know. She sent me a copy on Friday without asking one question, which is the most professional thing that has happened to this inquiry."

He put the sheet down.

"That is the whole of it and I want to be exact, because this is going in my report. **Not one of those five was written by a person hiding anything.** Every one is a system's honest word, produced by a form, a workflow, or a hurry — and every one of them takes a person out of a sentence and puts nothing in the space where the person was."

Then he said the thing he had said he could not do.

"You have been asking me since Friday what an institution does when it is asked to write a living person's name down, and I have been giving you procedure, and the procedure is correct, and the procedure is what does it. There is nobody to subpoena. It is a set of forms and the forms are good and my office wrote them. And here is the thing I cannot do, and I would like it written down that I said it: **I cannot tell you what a system gains by having fewer contradictions in it. My office is not built to hold that question.** I have asked it twice this week and twice been told it was a fair question, which is not an answer."

---

He accepted every item in the counter-case into the file and ruled on none of them, and he said why in one sentence.

"An inquiry that rules on contested evidence becomes a trial. I am not running a trial. I am running the part somebody has to write in October, and on the day I start deciding which witness is telling the truth this stops being a record and starts being a verdict, and I am not putting a Deputy Director's stamp on that."

At twenty past three he produced the other file. It was thin: a schedule dated the eleventh of May 2039, with a unit number on the top line in a block that was not a person's block.

"This office does not hold the sector 4C renumbering papers. I have known that since the seventeenth, because my Director answered Finding 2 in a room with five people in it and said the mapping was consolidated in two thousand and thirty-nine into a regional archive in Cordell that does not answer to this office. I have had her minute for twenty-five days. What I have never had is the schedule itself."

Elias Rook said nothing.

He sat at that table and heard a sentence he had been hearing since a Tuesday in the third week, out loud in a hall, from a woman who had not known she was saying the useful thing, and he understood that he had carried it for twenty-five days in a pocket without ever writing it down while Rusk had carried it in a file. He noticed it once, he did not say it out loud, and nobody in that room ever learned that the two of them had met over it like two men reading the same street name from opposite sides of a road.

"What is on the schedule," Rusk said, turning the page round, "is a store location, a volume, a box, a date, a unit number, and a **holding number.**"

He put his finger on it.

"That last one is the whole reason I have been sitting in this room for two days. Everything else about that box you would have to be in the room for. This you can read out loud on a telephone. A custodian in another city with a queue and no favor to me can be given a holding number, a unit number and a date and can find it. It is the only thing in this business that crosses a regional boundary in a spoken sentence."

Elias understood, about four seconds late, that the letter he had been handed on Thursday had been worth almost nothing.

"Without that number," he said, "the letter sits in a queue and somebody answers it with a form that asks for the box number."

"With a form that asks for the box number, eleven days in a queue, and a letter back to me stamped *insufficient identifier*," Rusk said, "which I would file, and in five years you would be right and the file would be empty."

He would have the letter typed and signed and sent by Monday. And then he said what the letter was worth, flatly, with no performance in it at all.

"I am giving it to you because I am required to. It will be read by somebody whose job is to read things. That person is not me. I will not know what they do with it, I will not be told, and I am not permitted to ask, and in nineteen years I have never once found out whether a letter I signed was read or filed."

---

One question was left in the room, and Elias asked it at ten to five.

"If I show you the field, will your report say the field was cut out and reviewed, and not altered by us."

"Your report is a document and not a finding, and I told Mr. Park on Thursday that a private record is not an alteration of a public one. It is the correct sentence, it is the one you have to live behind for eleven months, and I would have said it to your face in a room with no recorder in it."

"I can live behind it," Elias said.

"You can live behind it because it is true," Rusk said. "That is the difficulty. If it were false it would cost you nothing. Being right is not the same as being safe, and nobody has ever said that to you in a room where it mattered, so I am saying it three months before I write it down."

Then he said, "One more thing, and I am not going to be thanked for it."

Nobody in the room thanked him.

"You have won the part I can give you," Nathan Rusk said. "And I want you to understand that the part I can give you is small and I chose it."

The recorder's red light was still on. Elias Rook put the schedule into his own folder with his right hand, and outside on the third floor a Saturday afternoon was happening to the city with no idea that any of it had been decided in a room with the microphone at the end of the table.
