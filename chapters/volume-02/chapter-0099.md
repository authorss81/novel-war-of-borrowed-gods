# Chapter 99 — What It Costs

The folder on the table on Thursday the twenty-seventh of November, the eighty-second day, was thinner than the folder on the Wednesday, and that was the first thing Elias Rook noticed and the thing he spent the morning trying not to be glad about.

Cliff Marner was fifty-two days out of the ground and had not been told there was a sitting. Della Marner was eighty-two days at a desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was eighty-two days in, and the seal on the tunnel was nine words and a date, and nothing in the room that morning touched either.

---

"Counsel, put the charge as you have put it three times and then I will dispose of it."

Counsel Solano put it. The crew of the Kestrel response, on the face of a notice served on the thirty-fourth day, entered a consent on behalf of a person unable to give it, on a resident's card, at a designated site, without a designation, without a physician's order, in a circuit not on any register the Office holds. A particular act, a particular month, a particular signature in a particular capacity box.

"Mrs. Mir," said Rusk, "you are in the room because your name is in the second particular and not because anybody asked you to be."

"I am aware of what my name is in," Lena Mir said. "One thing, and then I am sitting down, and I would like it in the words I say it in. **I put my hand in that box in October because a man on a frame could not hold a pen and hold his shoulders at the same time, and I would have put it there again that afternoon, and if you take the charge off I would like it recorded that the only reason I am sitting here relieved is that you took it off for a reason about a form and not because anybody in this room thinks I should not have done it.**"

"Recorded," said Bernadette Locke.

"The charge falls," Rusk said, flatly. "**It falls because this office cannot bring it without explaining its own form set, and the form set has carried a printed row since the fourteenth of January two thousand and three for entering a consent on behalf of a subject unable to give it, and the inquiry cannot produce the form, and an institution that prosecutes an act it cannot show is a thing a court throws out and a citizen should be able to throw out.** That is the whole of it, and it is a bad reason, and it is the true one and there is not a better one hiding behind it."

"I have an objection," said Counsel Solano.

"I know you have. Make it."

"**The charge should fall because the act was not a coercion and the practice was disclosed and the circuit was run on consent cards that any household can withdraw from with no notice.** Your reason is that the paper is old. Mine is that the paper is the only thing in this building that anybody has got wrong about, and I would like the finding to say so, and if it does not I am going to write a dissent of about four hundred words and it is going to be read by somebody in about four years."

"Then write it."

"I intend to."

"In October," said Elias Rook, from the chair, "you told me a Deputy Director's office is not built to hold that question. It is not built to hold a second one either, and you have just said so in a room with a recorder in it, and that is twice."

"I am aware of the count," Rusk said, and did not improve it, and did not put a number on it.

---

"Findings, then, read in full and fair, and fair because this office will have to defend this file in about four years to somebody who was not in the room.

"One. **A records management finding.** An irregularity in a record is answered by a process, and the step is written down, and the person who takes it is on a rota. The form revision of the fourteenth of January two thousand and three is referred to that process. The finding is that the form cannot be produced, and that is a fact with a date and not a finding about anybody.

"Two. **Three endorsements are revoked.** The Office-endorsed medical advisership of Dr. M. Okafor. The designated-site advisor's endorsement of Mr. E. Rook. The designated-site advisor's endorsement of Mr. T. Vale. Effective today.

"That is the cheap thing and I am presenting it as exactly that. **I have known since the fourth of October that I could do it and I have held it for seven weeks, and the reason I did it is that it costs me nothing, because an endorsement is not a license and a license I could not have touched.** It is neither cruel nor generous. I am doing it because it is available, and I am saying so in a room with a recorder in it, so that in four years nobody can describe this office as punishing anybody."

"Mine first," said Mara Okafor. "**I want the exhibit read into the record. The disclosure page that is in my hand, which is going into a file as the example of how this crew does things properly, and I want it read out, and I want it called what it is: a process that worked.** I said in October that I would say that and I am saying it, and I am not a traitor to a team for saying it, and Mr. Rook can put that in his notebook with a date on it and I would prefer it in somebody else's notebook than his."

"Elias," said Rusk. "Your endorsement, and then a second thing that is not mine and that I am reading because the department asked me to."

He read it off the file.

> **HAVENPORT RESCUE SERVICE. Re: E. Rook, field technician. Suspension from seam assignment of the forty-first day is EXTENDED, case reference HC-42-0417 remaining open. The department's position is that the individual is not a field technician this month. Hose certification and inventory, depot, far bank, Tuesdays and Fridays, 06:30. These slots will not be held twice.**

"I asked for that to be read aloud," Rusk said, "and I am going to say why, and the reason is that in about four years this file will be read by somebody who was not in the room and it will look as though a Deputy Director ended a man's career in four paragraphs. **A Deputy Director wrote one paragraph. A man in a depot wrote the rest, and the man in the depot is right, and the department has a form for this and the form says the individual is not a field technician this month, which is a sentence about a month and not about a career, and it is the most accurate sentence anybody has written about you in eleven weeks.**"

He let it sit.

"That is the best thing that has happened to me in nine weeks," Elias Rook said, "and the slots went before this morning and I want that in the record too, and it is a loss, and I am not going to explain it in a room."

"Nobody asked you to."

"No. And that is why I said it out loud, and I would like the recorder to have it, because **I have spent eleven weeks in rooms where nobody asks me what a thing cost, and today a man read a form out loud that says the correct thing about me for the first time since the sixth of September, and I cannot pay it back and I cannot use it and I am not going to be the man who says thank you for it.**"

---

"Mr. Vale." Rusk closed the folder and did not open another. "Your endorsement as a designated-site advisor is revoked. The second part is not in my gift and I want it said as a fact and not as a favor.

"**The file will record that Mr. T. Vale is not available as a structural authority on a designated site.** Not suspended. Not under review. Not available, in the past tense, in a document, forever, for any site that carries a designation. It is one line and it goes on the form in a box that already exists, and the box is the only place in this building where a thing like that can be said, and **it is the only identity you have left and I have just taken it, and I am telling you that I know what it is, and I am not going to pretend it is a smaller thing than it is.**"

Tomas Vale looked at the table for about four seconds.

"Then it is in a box with a form in it," he said, "which is where I have been putting things since the fourth of September, and I have no objection and I have one condition."

"State it."

"That I am not consulted about any designated site by anybody, in writing or out of it, and that if anybody does consult me they are doing it because they have to, and I will not be able to help them, and that is the correct outcome and I would like it in a box with a form in it too."

"It will be in the file."

---

Then Rusk did the thing nobody in the room was expecting.

"Mr. Vane. The designation line for a person recovered from a designated site. **I am going to make one now, in a room with a recorder running, and I am going to say out loud first that a form does not do this and will in a year, and a Deputy Director can, in a room, and that if it is done wrongly it is done wrongly in a document that exists, which is the whole argument of the last eleven weeks in one sentence.**

"**Not a passenger. A person recovered from a designated site under an inquiry reference.** Case reference HC-42-0417, nineteenth of November 2042. The name goes on it, spelled as it is on a chalk wall in four hands on Ash Street and as it is on a case extract produced by a transit operator in another city, and I am saying where the spelling comes from, because a designation with a misspelled name in it is a thing that gets used against the man it names."

He wrote it out himself, which nobody had expected, and signed it, and gave it to Bernadette Locke.

"Is there an objection."

"There is one," Elias Rook said. "**It should have been done on the nineteenth of November and it was not, because this office's covering letter of the thirty-seventh day promised the omission in the equipment list would be corrected as an administrative error, and it has not been corrected, and I would like the word *error* to stay out of this entirely.**"

"It stays out. Counsel, note that the designation is a field created on the twenty-seventh of November and that the covering letter of the thirty-seventh day is unfulfilled and will be fulfilled in about a year, when the form can do it."

"I will note it," said Counsel Solano, "and I will note that the form cannot do it, and I have been saying that since the seventeenth of November."

---

"**Mr. Park, run a search.**"

Joon Park looked up. "On what."

"On the patient named at line six of the Kestrel return, in this inquiry's own system." Elias Rook heard himself say it and heard the shape of it a second too late. **Mr. Voss is not in this room. He has not been in this room since September, and his name is not in that system, and it is not in there because this office's own practice took it out, and nobody is going to fix it, and I would like the room to see it working.**"

Bernadette Locke typed for about nine seconds, in a search box on a screen that is a filing system and not an instrument, and read out what came back.

"*No result.*"

"That is correct," Hollis Vane said from the wall, where he had stood for the whole morning without being asked. "It is the practice working. **In about four years somebody is going to ask why the patient on line six is not a name, and the answer is a form this office adopted and this inquiry's own findings section carried, and used twice, and that is the correct answer and it is the worst answer anybody in this building has ever given.**"

Nobody in the room treated it as a problem. Elias Rook wrote that down afterwards, because it was true and because it was the whole of the business in one exchange.

---

Rusk let the room empty by about ten past one, then said one more thing with the door open and four people still in it, in the way he said everything, as though it were a paragraph.

"**The team is not prosecuted. Three endorsements are revoked, one line is written into a form forever, and one field is created that did not exist in this city in 2042. I have presented every one of those as what it is, which is cheap, and available, and correct, and I would like it minuted that in about four years this will read as a victory, and it is a victory, and it cost less than the alternative, and every single thing in it was available to me on the fourth of October and I held it for seven weeks because holding it was worth more to this office than spending it.** That is the finding too. Put it in the file. Put my name on it, because I am the one who held it."

Then he went out, and it was Friday tomorrow, and Elias Rook had nothing in his week for the first time in eleven weeks and did not know what to do with a Friday.
