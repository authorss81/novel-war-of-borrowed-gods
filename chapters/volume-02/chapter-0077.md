# Chapter 77 — What I Have To Answer For

The Office put eight chairs in the inquiry room on Wednesday morning and eight people sat in them, and Recorder Bernadette Locke typed the first four minutes and then stopped typing and just wrote, which she has done four times in two months.

Rusk had asked for the inquiry room and not a conference room, and nobody said thank you for it, and Elias understood about forty minutes later that it was a kindness.

He came in with a tin and a piece of paper with a date and a block on it and nothing else, and put it on the table face up.

"The date of an annotation and the block of a unit," he said. "Nothing else. You said on Monday that you would send it on Tuesday and it is Wednesday, and I am going to assume you spent Tuesday deciding whether to send it, and I am not going to ask which way it went."

"It went," Elias said.

"Good. I am not running a trial. I accept the whole of it into the file and I rule on none of it, and I have said that to you three times now and I am not going to say it a fourth time in a nicer voice."

---

The first hour was Counsel Renata Solano, and she was not hostile and she was not on their side, and she said so in the first minute, because in October she has to say what she did and did not do in a records center forty minutes away.

"Give me the date of the annotation and the block of the unit," she said. "Nothing else, and do not offer it."

"There is nothing else," Elias Rook said. "There is a page, a field cut out of it with a blade, the index, the annotation, and a signature at four twelve. I have not brought a name and I am not going to, and that is not a courtesy to this office. It is the reason the thing is worth anything to you."

Solano wrote for a while.

"I am not going to ask for a name," she said. "There is not one in what you have handed me, and if there were one I would have to ask, and if I asked you would not give it, and then I would have to write down that I asked a witness for the name of a person in a regional record and was refused, and that finding would be about me." She looked up. "So I am going to write *an alteration in a regional maintenance record, in a store in another region, in a year not stated in this file, by a method not established, with a review annotation of a later date signed by a unit.* That is the whole of what I can write, and it is going to look thin, and it is going to look thin on purpose."

"It is thin," Rusk said. "Write it anyway."

"I will write it in three sentences, in the Office's words and not yours, and if either of you has a better sentence I do not want it."

---

And then Rusk did the thing that took the rest of the morning, and he did it slowly, and he did it in the voice he uses when he is explaining a thing to a room that has to go and repeat it to eleven other people.

"This is what happens," he said. "I want you to hear it in the right order, because the order is the entire answer and I have watched two of you go at it backwards in the last ten minutes.

"There is a regional maintenance log in a store in Cordell, a field was cut out of a page in it, and nobody knows when or by whom. That is not a finding. **That is an irregularity in a record, and the correct and only response to an irregularity in a record is a process.** So: a review, under a reference, with a date; a finding; and if the finding is that a record has been altered, it is a *records management* finding, and that is about the record. **It is not about a person. There is no version of this that is about a person, and the reason is that no individual had a hand in it, and if one had, it would be a different act, and I would refer it.**"

"That is correct," Tomas Vale said.

"It is correct and it is worthless, and both are true at once, and that is the part that took me two years." Rusk turned the paper round so the room could see the date. "So. Somebody, in about four years, gets a file that says a regional record was altered in an unstated year by an unestablished method and a review confirmed a field was not applicable. And what is in that file about anybody? Nothing. No name, because there is no name. No unit that can be given a meaning, because a unit is a block. **There is no author.** And you have both of you already written the sentence in your own heads, and I am going to say it out loud so that nobody can pretend later that nobody said it."

Nobody said anything.

"It is going to say the record was amended *in accordance with review*, and that is the entire sentence, and it is going to be true, and it is going to be the end of it, and in eleven years a person is going to hold that page up and ask who amended it, and there is going to be nothing in the file to put in their hand, and they will not even be able to be angry at anybody in particular, and that is not a scandal. **That is what an institution is for.**"

He said it without heat and without apology, and Elias Rook, who had spent nine weeks learning the difference between a person hiding something and a system being correct, heard a person being correct, and it was the worst sound in that room.

""Because you have to understand the shape of it," Rusk said. "It is the same shape as a printed box. Somebody, in about nineteen eighty-odd, thought carefully about a field and printed a box that asked what happened to it, on a form that a clerk in a regional office would fill in correctly and go home from. **It is the same shape as a box, and neither has a person in it, and the reason neither has a person in it is that the design is good.** That is the thing you have been unable to say in this room for two months, and I am saying it because you have handed me a unit's block and not a person, and I owe you that sentence as much as you owe me the date."

Elias Rook put his hands flat on the table on either side of the tin and said nothing, and Joon Park wrote *PROCESS, NO AUTHOR* in his notebook, and the recorder typed it.

---

"You believed the truth was better for you," Rusk said, at about half past eleven, to Tomas Vale, who had not spoken for ninety minutes.

Tomas Vale looked up.

"You wrote nine lines before you went into a hole," Rusk said, "and you told the hall about the collapse for eight days and did not tell it that you signed off the node the collapse is about, and you did that because you were told the truth was better for you. I am the man who has to write about you in October and I would rather say this in a room with a recorder in it than in a report you will read once.

"**That is the most dangerous belief a man can hold in an inquiry.** Not because it is false. Because it is true, and because it is true it will feel like a correction every time you do it, and never once like a cost, and every time it feels like a correction the file gets a little more complete and a little less useful, because a man who is only ever right by being more complete is a man who will eventually be handed a question he cannot answer without inventing something, and he will not notice, because inventing it will feel like the truth catching up."

The room was extremely quiet.

"That is not advice," Rusk said. "I have no standing and no clause gives me one. It is a Deputy Director telling a named man what his belief looks like from about eleven weeks away, and I would do it at the table if I had to, and I would rather do it where somebody writes it down."

"Thank you," Tomas Vale said.

"Do not thank me. Write down that I said it and that you said thank you, and in October somebody will read them next to each other and they will make a very good paragraph." He looked at the recorder. "Type both."

---

At twenty past one he offered the one thing, and he said that he was choosing it, and he said it was small before he said what it was, which is the only way anybody in that building ever says anything.

"I am going to tell you two things about a report I have not written yet, and one of them costs me something and the other does not, and I want you to know which is which before I start.

"The report is the same report either way. I have said that on a telephone and I am saying it in a room. Nothing that happened on Monday changes a word of it. I have to write a paragraph about what your crew did in a corridor that ends at eleven meters, and somebody cutting a rectangle out of a book in nineteen ninety-eight is not a paragraph about what your crew did."

"That is fair," Elias Rook said.

"**And here is the thing I am choosing.** I am not going to put the annotation in as a finding. I am going to put it in as a fact with a date on it, and a fact with a date can be shown to a person, and a finding decides something, and I have no authority to decide anything and I am not going looking for a way to get it."

"Sir," Solano said, "that is the entire distinction the findings section exists to make, made in advance, on the record."

"I know what it is, Counsel, and I am making it on the record, which is why I am saying it out loud instead of doing it quietly in a draft."

He looked at Elias Rook.

"That is the seventh true thing I have said to you, and it is worth less than the other six, and I am telling you that because I have watched you people take a procedural kindness for a concession four times in two months and I am not going to be one of those four."

---

Then he fixed the third sitting, and it was the last twenty minutes of the meeting and it took four sentences.

"The third sitting was going to be in October. It is not going to be in October. **It is Thursday the nineteenth of November, at ten in the morning, in this room.**"

Nobody said anything for a second.

"That is the same day your request is due at the near end," Elias said.

"It is," Rusk said. "It is a coincidence of calendars and I chose it, and I would like it written down that I chose it, because in a month somebody is going to read a transcript and decide for himself whether that was a courtesy or a tactic, and it is neither. It is the closest thing to fair I have been able to arrange in nine weeks, because you have spent nine weeks being told by my office that your request went into a queue, and the queue has a date on it, and it is the same date. **I am not going to hold your answer hostage to a calendar and I am not going to pretend a calendar is the only thing I have.**"

"Would you have taken it in October if we had asked for the date and the block?"

"No," Rusk said. "And I would like that minuted too, because it is the honest answer and it is the reason the offer is worth what it is worth."

Then Inez Sato asked him the only question she had, which was whether his office had ever in nine weeks produced a document the team could hold, and he said no, and she said *thank you, that is the correct answer, and I am not going to say it again*, and he said that was also the correct answer.

At half past three a clerk put his head in and said that a woman in Cordell had declined the offer of a machine and had asked for a table, and Rusk said he would take it himself at five, and then said, to nobody, "She is the second person this week asked for something in a form who has said no, and I am going to stop using the word coincidence in this building."

---

Elias got what he asked for on Wednesday afternoon. It was a procedural kindness, it was worth exactly what Rusk said it was worth, and it was not the thing the hall needs.

He understood on the bus back that those two facts are not in tension, that he would have to hold both for about two more years, and that there was nobody in Havenport he could say either of them to.

A school on Sycamore Row is three streets from a store room where a book is still lying open on a trolley under a bulb, and on Friday a man who cannot write in his left hand is going to stand in that corridor and not ask a second question.
