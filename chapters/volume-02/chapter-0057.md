# Chapter 57 — Five Questions

The inquiry room was on the third floor of an Office building on the Meridian with a table, six chairs, a microphone on a stand, a recorder with a red light, and a paper file that was four inches thick and had a tab on it for each of the five questions.

Counsel Renata Solano, forty-seven, sat with the file. Recorder Bernadette Locke typed what was said, including, as she said once at the start of the day and did not say again, the sentences that would be quoted back at the people who said them. There were no forms on the table, which Elias had asked for on Friday and got.

"There is no signature line on this transcript and there is no attestation," Solano said. "You will be asked to confirm accuracy at some point in a proceeding and you will be able to refuse, and the transcript is a record of what was said and not a finding about it. The Deputy Director insisted on that in writing on Friday, which is the first time in this office's history that a Deputy Director has insisted on anything in writing that made his own position weaker, and I would like that on the record as a thing that happened."

---

They went in the order the Notice listed them, and Solano read each question out in full before the answer, and nobody answered a question that had not been read.

**One.** Elias gave the collapse Saturday in the order it happened, in eleven sentences, and did not editorialise. When the recorder read it back one of the sentences had *the corridor behaved* in it, and he asked for that to be struck, and it was struck, because it was an opinion in the mouth of a witness and he had spent four weeks telling a nine-year-old that a plan has a time on it and a rescue has a person on it.

**Two.** Tomas Vale took the twenty-ninth day. He said that a plan had been drawn by a man who was not him, that it had two triggers, and that it expired at ten minutes to six on the morning of that Sunday, ten minutes before the wall was opened, and that nobody noticed for seven hours, and that the reason no second plan was written is that he would not draw it and none of the rest of them would draw it over him.

"Is that your judgment or the fact," Solano said.

"That is the fact," Tomas said. "The judgment is a separate document, it is nine lines long, it is on your wall in chalk, and I would rather you had the nine lines than my summary of them."

**Three.** Mara Okafor said what was offered in the first week, by whom, with what boundary, and what was done with it, in eleven minutes, without softening anything.

"State your standing conditions for a medical offer," Solano said, "and the disclosure rule that was adopted in this room on the thirty-third day."

"Any offer, boundary, refusal, and any condition somebody is asked to perform before they give anything goes to the people who pay for it, in the words it was said in, before it is acted on. And the half that is mine: nobody inherits her. A future version of me that lends a hand is a woman deciding about her own capacity, and I do not get to be angry at her later, and I am not going to be a person who says a future version of me agrees with me about this."

"Was that rule in force on the twenty-ninth day?"

"No," Mara said. "It was written on the thirtieth. On the twenty-ninth there was a rule about medical decisions and there was a man who would not ask a question on the radio, and I am telling you that the disclosure rule did not exist when the question was asked, because the answer is no and I would rather you had it from me than found it in November."

"That is not in your favour," Solano said.

"It is in the file," Mara said, "and I would like the file to be the kind of thing somebody can check."

**Four.** Joon Park gave a custody schedule. It is eleven items long and it names a building for every copy and a holder for every copy, and it says *not in this building* four times, and one of the four is a garage on an ordinary street and one of the four is a bus on a Sunday morning going out of Havenport. It is the only document in that room anybody will be able to use in five years.

Solano asked the fourth question in a different order, four times, and the four times got closer together.

"Did the team alter the Kestrel response record, Mr. Park."

"No."

"Did any person in that hall alter any part of the Kestrel response record."

"No."

"Is there a second record."

"There is a parallel record kept by the residents' body," Joon said. "It is not an alteration of the Office's record. It was made at the same time, out of the same measurements, by different hands, in a different building, and it is on paper and it is on a wall, and neither of those two things touched the Office's document at any point, and I have the schedule and I will read you the schedule."

He read the schedule. It took nine minutes and it is in the file.

Then Solano said it flatly, and she was not trying to win anything with it: "Then be clear about what that is, because I am going to write it up in a way somebody can misread. **A private record is not an alteration of a public one.** That is the answer, and the answer is correct, and I am aware that it is the sentence the rest of your case has to live behind, and I am aware that I am the person who has just said it to you and not to a jury."

Nobody in that room had a better sentence.

**Five.** Inez Sato answered for the residents in one sentence she had written down on Friday night in her own hand, and Joon Park read it out, and nobody in that room knew that it had been written before it was asked for and they would not have found out from her.

"The residents' body declines to provide its file, provides a power summary, and asks that its refusal and the four votes against it be recorded together and in full."

Solano said, "I am obliged to ask you whether the refusal was the decision of the body."

"The refusal was mine and it is signed by me," Inez said. "The body's decision was taken the way it takes every decision, one name at a time, out loud, and it was not unanimous, and the four are recorded, and if the Office's form has a box for the refusal I would like somebody to look at it, because on Wednesday evening there was not one, and I have been in this building five weeks and I have never seen a form with a box in it for what a person said."

---

The fifth question came back at half past three, and Elias Rook had been the subject of it since the twenty-ninth day and had not been asked it once.

He said four sentences and made no promise and signed nothing.

"Question five. Nobody in this building edited anything." He put his right hand flat on the table, and the recorder's red light was on, and that was the part he could not stop noticing. "A field was cut out of a page with a blade in a book, in a room, by somebody, and somebody else reviewed the page eleven years later and wrote a word across the hole in a block that is not a person's block, and both of those are in another city, and we can show you the field and we cannot show you the hand. Nobody in this building edited anything, and I am not going to give you a name in a room with a microphone in it, because if I give you a name this afternoon then in October you will have a name and no document, and in eleven years the name will be in a file with a question mark on it, and I have watched that happen to four other things this month and I have decided I am not doing it to a fifth."

Rusk, who had been sitting at the end of the table for the whole day without speaking, said, "That is a refusal to name a person in exchange for a document. I accept it. I am also going to record what you did not say, which is that you know there is a field and you have not seen it. Do you want to correct that."

"No," Elias said. "I want you to leave it exactly where it is."

He wrote it down, because he had said on Friday that he would, and read it back, and then said the sentence he had been carrying since Friday, and said out loud that it was the same sentence.

"I said something in a hall on Friday and I am going to say it again into a transcript, and I want it noted that it is the same sentence twice and not a new one. **I did not write those nine words, my office did, and I signed the seal, and if you can show me that the classification is not the whole cause, I will put the whole cause in my own report, and it is the only thing I have got.**" He looked at Rusk. "You said that on Friday where nobody could quote you."

"I said it on Friday in front of nine people and a nine-year-old," Rusk said, "and I meant it, and I have thought about it every day since, and I would like it in the transcript that I said it twice."

Then he put the file down and gave them the rest of it, and it was not in the file yet, and it was the only part of the day that Elias Rook afterwards described as the worst thing anybody said to him.

"Here is what the whole cause looks like from where I sit," Rusk said. "On the morning of the collapse, at five minutes past five, somebody opened a cabinet in sector 4C. The credential that opened it is a class three maintenance credential. That credential is not a name, it is a string, and the string cannot be named because the sector's holder fields do not exist — nine of them, they were not carried forward, and the mapping is in another city. On that same morning, at five minutes past five, two people were also in that cabinet, and they are the two people whose names are on a piece of paper in a garage in this city, and I have read it, and one of them is on the twenty-ninth day of this inquiry's schedule."

He stopped, and he did not point at anybody.

"That is the theory," he said. "It is the only theory this office has and it is not a good one. It is a category and a time and a gap where a name should be. I am not accusing anybody in this room of anything. But if the theory holds then your capacity in that corridor is the reason a building came down, and if it does not hold then I need a better one before October, because I have to write a report either way and I would like to write the true one. I need it to hold or I need it replaced. I have no third position and I am not going to pretend to one in front of a recorder."

Elias said nothing for eleven seconds, which is the longest anybody in that room had gone without speaking, and Rusk waited, and then Rusk said, "Now the part I actually need."

"The predecessor index for a sector renumbered in two thousand and thirty-five is not in this network and has not been since. Ask Cordell. Cordell keeps a queue and wants a box number and a unit number before anything else, and they are right to."

Rusk asked Elias where the field was.

"It is in another city," Elias said, on the record, with Bernadette Locke's hands on the keys. "Forty minutes by an ordinary road. It keeps a queue. The answer is a records request with a box number on it and they quote four to six weeks, and they quoted four to six weeks to a woman on a telephone on Tuesday who has been at a desk since the sixth of September."

Solano said, "So the answer to the fifth question is in Cordell."

"It is in Cordell," Elias said, "and the answer to the second half of the question on our wall is in a building this office cannot enter, and I have been hearing the first half of that sentence since the seventeenth day, and I did not know until this afternoon that I was the only one in the room still hearing it."

---

They ended at four o'clock because the recorder's tape ran out, and there was a spare, and the spare was there because a Deputy Director had insisted on a rule about it in 2036.

Nobody signed. Elias did not sign, and Mara did not sign, and Tomas did not sign, and when Joon asked whether a witness statement would be offered separately, Solano said it would be a document, and it would be theirs, and they could put in it anything they wanted, including this afternoon, including the parts of it that were against them.

"Including the corridor behaving," Elias said.

"Especially that," Solano said.

And in the lift, on the way down, Elias Rook was holding a piece of paper with his own words on it, typed by somebody else, in a building he had been in eleven times in five weeks, and the sentence he had said to make himself feel better — *nobody in this building edited anything* — was now in a record held by the people who had written nine words about a tunnel. The most frightening four minutes of the day had not been an accusation.

It had been Rusk writing down a thing Elias had said, in front of a recorder, because he had promised on Friday that he would.
