# Chapter 97 — Ninety Seconds

Ansel Duarte, Lena Mir and Ru Sabar went out of that building at about half past twelve on Tuesday the twenty-fifth of November, the eightieth day, for a clinic appointment at ten past two, two hours in a car, a wheelchair on a ramp that is not at that building, and a form that Lena Mir was going to sign with a pen in her right hand because he could not hold a pen and hold his shoulders at the same time. Ru Sabar took it as a swap against a night and not as a favor, and nobody asked Ansel Duarte anything, which was twice in six weeks.

That is why Elias Rook was in the building. He had gone over the bridge at half past six for the Tuesday slot, which was his and had been for three weeks, and come back at about ten, and there was nothing on his Tuesday except forty-four minutes of inventory and a depot sheet with two figures on it in R. Dunleavy's own hand, and he had not gone up the ramp on Friday until half past eight.

Petar Lisk read the board at seven, out loud, twice, and said the wall was the same. Cliff Marner was fifty days out and in the chair by the window. Della Marner was eighty days at a desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was eighty days in, nobody had gone near him since the last Thursday on the schedule, and nobody was going to, and the eleven meters was a length with a day on it and nothing else.

---

It happened at about twenty past two in the afternoon, in the records room, and it did not begin.

That is the part Elias Rook has never been able to write down properly afterwards. There was no sound and no cold and no light. At about twenty past two the records room was a records room with the custody schedule on the table and the tin on the shelf and a box going into a cupboard, and at about the same time there was a doorway in the north wall, and the north wall had been a wall for the whole eleven weeks with a picture frame on it that Petar Lisk's mother had brought on the second day.

Somebody said the time out loud. It was Tarin Voss, who was by the door because that is where he stands.

Twenty past two. Nine people in the building and eleven more asleep upstairs.

---

The doorway was a door's width and about seven feet high and it was in a wall that has never had an opening in it, and beyond it there was a street.

Elias Rook has been asked about that four times since and has given the same four sentences, and none of them is the one he would have given if nobody had asked him.

**It was a street with buildings on both sides and no people on it and rain falling on it, and the rain made a sound like rain.** There was a smell of rain on a road with grit in the surface, and under that a smell of hot brakes, and there was no engine making that smell, and the far end of the street did not get smaller.

He did not step toward it. He has been asked about that too.

What happened next took about eleven seconds and it happened without him.

---

Somebody said a number out loud. **Nine people in the building** had been said by Tarin Voss at the start and **nine in the records room and the front room and the corridor** was said by Nadi Bell from about four feet inside the doorway, and then a hand went to the card rail in the front room and came off it with a card on it, and the name on the card was read out and written on the round sheet with the word *out* against it, and the card went into a pocket and not back on the rail.

"Mr. Lisk is in the dry end," Nadi Bell said, to nobody, to the sheet. "Card's in my pocket. **Not on the rail, so in about four minutes somebody has to decide whether to look for him or not, and the October answer is not to, and I am not going to change the October answer in a doorway.**"

"There is a rule about that," Joon Park said, from the terminal, in a voice that was not steady. "There is a rule and I did not make it up and I have wanted to make up one every day for eleven weeks. **If a card is on the rail its holder is in the building and accounted for. If the card is not on the rail the name goes in the round sheet as out and that is the end of it, and the round sheet is not a record of where anybody is and never has been.** Nineteen of us agreed that in October and nobody has to be asked about it now and I would like that written down in whatever anybody ends up writing down this afternoon."

Nobody wrote it down then. It is on nineteen pieces of paper.

---

The terminal in the records room printed one line at about twenty-three past two, and the corridor repeater printed the same line at the same second, and Elias Rook read it out loud, because that is the practice, and because he was the only person in the room who had ever read one of these out loud for anybody.

That was all of it. There was nothing under the line.

"I want to say that out loud," Elias Rook said, "because in eleven weeks every one of these has had something under the heading, and this one has not, and the nothing under it is the first new thing anybody in this building has seen since the fourth of September. **It is not an offer. It is not a loan. There is nobody in it to ask and nothing in it to refuse.**"

He did not say *I hear you.* He had said that once, in September, into a relay, to a man nineteen years older than his sister, and he had got a rule back instead of a gift and had been right to, and there was no version of this in which he answered.

And nobody went through the door.

Tarin Voss was standing in the frame. He did not go through it and he did not go near it, and he said afterwards that he had wanted to, and that wanting to was not an argument.

"**The reason nobody went through that door is not that we are brave,**" he said. "It is that nineteen people agreed something in October without being asked and I was one of them, and being in a frame for ninety seconds is not courage, it is a procedure, and I would like that said before anybody in this building gets proud about Tuesday."

At about twenty-one and a half past two the doorway was not there. The picture frame was on the north wall and the wall was the wall.

Ninety seconds. Nobody had gone through it, and nothing had been asked of anybody, and the whole of the price was that nine people in a building on Ash Street had spent ninety seconds of an ordinary Tuesday not doing something they would have thought about every day for the rest of their lives.

---

Petar Lisk came out of the dry end four minutes later with a book in each hand and did not ask, and looked at the rail, and Nadi Bell showed him the card, and he said *right*, and asked whether anybody had been frightened, and nineteen people said yes in four different ways over the following hour and a half, and he wrote nothing on the wall about it, and the wall had room for about two more lines and the closing one was going to be his.

"You were in the doorway," Nadi Bell said to Elias Rook, about an hour later, in the front room, with about fourteen people in it. "You were standing in the doorway and you did not do anything."

"There was nothing for me to do."

"That is not what I am asking and you know it is not what I am asking." She had the round sheet in her hand. "**For eleven weeks I have been waiting to find out whether that building stands up because you are in it. It does not. It stood up tonight because nineteen people agreed something in October without being asked, and none of them are you, and one of them is a nine-year-old who was in the dry end with a book in each hand, and he was not even in the room.**"

Elias Rook said nothing for about a minute, and what came was not a sentence.

The cost he had been paying since October arrived, and it arrived in a records room at twenty past two on a Tuesday afternoon, and it was that he was not needed, and it was not a loss.

That was the part he had no idea what to do with. In ten weeks he had been given a great many things to carry and every one of them had a weight, and the thing that had no weight was the one that was true. **He had spent his whole career learning to be the person in the doorway. On Tuesday afternoon the doorway opened and the doorway was a room where nine people were already doing it, and he stood in it, and there was nothing for him to add, and nobody looked at him, and nobody was wrong.**

He went up the ramp at four o'clock and he did not tell anybody that it was the best hour of his week, and that was the first time in eleven weeks he had not told somebody something.

---

The door is on no drawing.

Joon Park said that at about five o'clock, standing up, with the round sheet in one hand and the custody schedule in the other, and said it in the tone he used for things he had checked twice. There is no plan of that building in four places. The wall with the frame on it is a solid wall on every one of them, and it was a solid wall on the day it was measured, and the picture frame is on a nail that is in a stud.

"Nobody photographs a doorway that they are not allowed to photograph and nobody goes out on the street to see whether the street is there," he said. "What I am going to do is write it down, in as many hands as will write it, and then I am going to take four copies and I am going to put one in Cordell and one at the depot on the far bank and one in the county library's local-history room and one in my own notebook, and **none of the four is the record and the record does not exist and the four of them are four people being frightened in four different buildings, and that is the whole of what I can do and I am going to do it anyway.**"

In the end it was nineteen hands, in chalk, on the wall, in the space under the clerk's fourth column, and it took until about eleven that night, and Petar Lisk read the board at seven the next morning out loud, twice, and there were three questions on it and one date, and by Sunday the nineteen hands were in four buildings.
