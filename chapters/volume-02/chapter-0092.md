# Chapter 92 — The Charge

The sitting on Thursday, the twentieth of November, the seventy-fifth day, was not a sitting. It was twenty minutes with one item on it, and the item had a heading on the sheet in Counsel Solano's own typing, and the heading was the whole of the morning.

**PARTICULAR OF THE CHARGE, STATED IN ADVANCE OF THE DISPOSITION.**

Elias Rook had known since the sixth of September what was under the heading, and he had not said it in a room, and he said it in a room on the twentieth of November because somebody was going to make somebody else say it first.

---

The room was the same and the jugs had been filled again and the microphone was at the end of the table. Six name cards this time, and Elias read his from where he sat, because he still could not reach them, and because he had begun to like being a man who reads name cards from two feet away.

Deputy Director Rusk said, before anything else, that nothing was going to be taken as evidence and that Mr. Rook was not being examined, and that if Mr. Rook wished to correct counsel he could do so from his chair and would be thanked for it and would not be asked anything else.

"Ms. Solano."

"Two particulars," Counsel Solano said. "**First: that on a resident's consent card in a premises on Ash Street, in the month of October of this year, a consent was entered in a capacity box on behalf of a subject who was unable to give it, by a person who was not a physician, at what is described in the notice as a designated site, without a designation, without a physician's order, and in a circuit that is not on any register this office holds.** Second: that the same premises has operated a per-circuit consent and carded-draw system since the fourth of September without designation and without registration, and that the first particular is an instance of the second."

Nobody said anything. Elias Rook looked at the middle of the table, because the only name in that room that the first particular fitted was a name he had written into a column of his own in October, and he was the only person in the room who had done that.

"Mr. Rook," Counsel Solano said. "Correct me if I have got a date wrong."

"The date is right."

"Thank you. That is all I needed and I am not going to ask you anything else about it."

---

The trouble, when it came, came from the second half, and it came from the Deputy Director, and it was not an argument.

"The inquiry has to be able to say that the act it is charging is not an act," Rusk said. "**Not that it is lawful. Whether it is lawful is before nobody in this room this morning. That the act exists — that there is a printed authority for a person to do it, in this office's own form set, on this office's own current form, with a date on it — is the only thing that stops this charge being an invention.** I have had that document since the sixth of September and I have not been able to lay my hand on it in ten weeks and I am going to say why out loud, and then I am going to say the rest of it, and I would like the recorder to have both."

The recorder had both.

"**The authority is the Designated-Site Incident Return, form number DS-4, Revision C, issued the fourteenth of January two thousand and three, requested by District 9, signed by a unit in a block, with a printed reason that says no field was available. It added one row: consent entered on behalf of a subject unable to give it, with the signature of the person entering it, the printed name, and the capacity in which it is entered. It is on the current set. It is the reason the first particular is a particular and not an invention.**

"And I cannot produce the form. A form in a drawer in a form store is not a document. There is no instrument in this building or in this city that compels anybody to open a drawer, and I have looked twice, once on my own and once with the clerk who went and looked on her own, and I have minuted that the name was not withheld from me."

He put his hands flat on the table, and Elias Rook, who had watched a man of sixty-four do that exact thing in a records room six days ago, had to look at the wall.

"So there is a defect in this office's own form set, and it is a defect in a set and not in a person, and I have found it seven days before the hearing that is going to decide whether this team is prosecuted, and I am telling a room full of the people it is going to be told about by counsel in a report in about three weeks. **I am not withdrawing the charge in this room. That is the twenty-seventh and it is mine and I will take it there.** But nobody in this room is going to be able to say afterward that I did not say it."

"That is going in your report," Solano said.

"That is going in my report in the words I have just used, and I am telling you that as counsel so that you can object to it now rather than in writing in December."

"I am not objecting," Counsel Solano said. "**I would like it read into the record that the second particular is the one I am worried about and I have not said so this morning.**"

---

Nobody improved on that either, and the twenty minutes were over by twenty past eleven.

Outside on the second-floor landing, Elias Rook said the thing he had said on the steps on the Monday and had hoped was not true.

He had hoped it. That was the whole of the difference between Monday and Thursday, and it took him four seconds to work out and about four minutes to sit with. On Monday it had been a thing he hoped was not the case. **On Thursday it was a thing he knew, and what he knew was that a nurse put her hand in a box in October because a man on a frame could not hold a pen and hold his shoulders at the same time, and the box is the right box, and the box is printed, and the printing is the reason the box is the right box, and a Deputy Director has just told a room that the box is his office's.**

The Office's paperwork being badly designed in one small place had never been a defense of anything. He had said that on the Monday. What he had hoped, and had not said, was that the paperwork being *right* in one small place might be.

It was not. **Being right about the Office's paperwork is not a defense of anything he has done, and it has stopped being a thing he is waiting to find out.**

"Stop," Joon Park said, on the stairs. "I know that face. That is a man doing arithmetic about how much it would be worth if the paperwork were on their side, and the arithmetic does not exist, and I have watched you do it three times since Monday."

"Three times."

"Three." Joon Park turned round on the landing. "**Write it down. Not for me. Write it down somewhere that is not your own notebook, because your own notebook is the only record in this business that is in your handwriting and nobody can check.**"

---

Mara Okafor said it in the front room on Ash Street at about ten past seven that evening, standing up, in the order her rule requires, to the twenty-two people who pay for it, and she refused the vote.

"Four things and I am going to say all four before I say any of them.

"**One.** In October a page of mine went into this inquiry's file in my own hand. It is a disclosure record. It is a record of a decision I made about a person in a bed and a driver and a form I was not going to sign with a pen I could not hold, and it went in the way my rule requires, which is that anybody who has to pay for a decision is told about it in the words it was said in, before it is acted on.

"**Two.** That page is going to be read by strangers. It is going to be read as the example of how this crew does things properly, and it is going to be read by somebody who is looking for an example of how this crew does things properly, because that is what makes a page an example.

"**Three.** And here is the cost, and I am naming it before it happens and not in a corridor afterwards. **The moment that page is an example, it stops being a record of a decision and becomes evidence that we had a procedure. And if we had a procedure, then we knew what a procedure was for, and we did not register one, and that is not a question anybody at the Meridian has to ask out loud because it is in the page already.** I have written the thing that does this. My handwriting is the instrument. It is going to be a public exhibit of a process that worked, and it is going to work against me, and I would like it read anyway, and I said in October that I would say that and I am saying it.

"**Four.** Nobody inherits me, and I am not taking a vote on this, because a vote would be a way of pretending the decision is a thing this room owns. It is mine and it is about a page of mine and I have made it."

The front room was quiet for the length of a breath.

Then Inez Sato said, from the wall, without turning round: "That is not a meeting about a form and nothing is going on the pad, and I am saying both of those out loud so that nobody in this room can use either of them against her later. She has told the people who pay for it. That is the rule. It has been the rule since the thirtieth of September and it is the only thing in this building that has never needed anybody to be a villain."

Nobody wrote it down. Elias Rook understood, about four seconds later, that it was not going to be written down anywhere, and that this was the first time in eleven weeks he had been in a room where the most important thing said in it was going to exist only in the people who heard it, and that he could not do anything about that either, and that this was exactly the same problem as Friday's, and that it was not going to have a column and it was not going to be a record and it was not going to be item twenty-two.

He went up the ramp at half past eight and stopped on the step, and put his right hand flat on the rail, and did not make anything of it.
