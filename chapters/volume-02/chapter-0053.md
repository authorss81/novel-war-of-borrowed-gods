# Chapter 53 — Cliff Marner's Name

The line came out of the terminal in the records room at nine minutes past eight on Sunday morning, the thirty-sixth day, and it was not an offer and it was not a warning, and it had the flat helpfulness of a thing that has done this before.

```text
Suggested correction: KESTREL 2042 return, line 6 (persons recovered
from site) has no incident to attach to. Close the line so the
discrepancy resolves. No further action will be required.
```

Joon Park read it twice, and then said the thing that has been his job for four weeks.

"I am going to say this out loud in a room instead of just not typing, because in this building silence looks exactly like a decision." He put the pen down. "The line is not a discrepancy. The line is reporting something true, and the only reason it has nothing to attach to is that the thing it is reporting has been given a name that does not hold people, and the machinery has noticed the shape of the hole and not the man in it."

He wrote a refusal on the sheet under the message, in his own hand, and the sheet went into the residents' file and was read out twice.

**DECLINED. A LINE IS NOT A DISCREPANCY IF THE THING IT IS REPORTING IS TRUE. LINE 6 STAYS OPEN. J. PARK, AND THE HALL KNOWS WHY.**

Nobody argued with him, and the reason nobody argued is that in the first week he refused a thing everybody wanted and it cost them a line, and everybody in that room has a different opinion about that and none of them think he is wrong.

---

The man it was about was in the front room with a blanket over his knees and a cup of something Ru Sabar had not been asked to make.

He had come up a corridor on a board on the thirtieth day at twenty past eight in the evening, at fifty-one pounds, and said his own name twice — once into Lena Mir's ear and once to nobody in particular — and he had been in the ward six days and was fifty-six pounds now, which Lena Mir wrote on the page and did not make a sentence out of, because a sentence out of it would be a rate and a rate is a kind of promise.

"I want my name entered," Cliff Marner said.

He said it the way a man says a thing he has been composing on a bus.

"Not in your book. I can see your book, I have looked at it, Lena has let me look at it and I have not touched it, and I am not asking for your book." He turned the cup around. "I want it entered in the system. I have a service number. I have had one for twenty-two years. It opens four doors in Cordell and it is a card and a number and the number is on a form at a desk in a building, and on Saturday morning that desk had a piece of paper on it with my name typed at the top of it, because I am a person who was owed an inquiry, and they had my name and a case number and no event."

"Who is the desk," Elias said.

"A transit authority. Regional. The end of the line is mine." He put the cup down. "That is the whole of it, and I have been working on the wording for two days, which you can tell because it is short."

Mara Okafor said, "You have gained five pounds in six days and you have a lesion from a shored face and a cough that I can hear from here, and it is four hours on a train to that desk, and I am not going to be the person who sends you. Not this week. I will write to them if you want a letter and I will not write to them if you do not, and that is my whole contribution to this."

"Then what am I," Cliff Marner said. "I am what, in a document."

"You are the fifth person out of that chamber," Elias said, "and here is what the hall has and what it does not have, and I am going to say the second half first because you will not believe the first half until you have heard it." He put his right hand flat on the table. "There is no response list. There has never been a response list. The classification is nine words that mean *the equipment failed*, and an equipment failure does not carry passengers, and a man who came out of a chamber under a station is not a passenger on a vehicle and there is no form in this city that will hold him, and there is not one in the region either, because the same nine words are on the same template everywhere."

Cliff Marner sat with that for about half a minute.

"Then I am an error," he said.

"That is the word that is going to be used," Elias said, "and I would rather it was somebody else's choice than mine."

---

Joon had the return in front of him on the table by nine twenty, because Elias had asked for it and it had been in the residents' file in four places since the fourth week.

The Office's return for a designated site had grown a new line in the middle overnight, with a revision letter, and Joon had written *revision in the middle of the form* in his custody log without interpreting it. The line was line six and it was called **persons recovered from site** and it was a real box in a real form and it had been printed since the fourth week and nobody had ever been able to put anything in it.

"Because there is no event to hang it on," Mara said. "Not — because they decided not to. The designation is an equipment failure. There is no incident, and a recovered-persons line attaches to an incident, and so it is a box with nowhere to attach, and the man is standing in my front room with a service number."

"That is the second one," Joon said.

"The second one of what," Elias said.

"There is a field that does not exist, and there is a field that can be left blank, and there is a field that was never designed for the thing in the question." Joon put his finger on line six without touching it. "And now there is a field that exists, that anybody can see, that every clerk in this city can see, that was printed for exactly this, and that a man has to stand in a room and be told by a person he trusts that it cannot hold him. That one is worse than the other three. In the other three nobody has to decide anything. In that one somebody has to look at a box that fits and turn around and go and get somebody else."

Nobody had an answer to that, and the sentence went up on the board at seven the next morning in a nine-year-old's capitals, which is where everything in that building goes eventually.

**A BOX THAT FITS IS WORSE THAN A BOX THAT DOES NOT.**

---

Elias Rook got up at ten past two in the afternoon and did the only thing available to him.

The chalk was on the shelf by the dry end where it had been since the second week. He wrote on the wall, in the same capitals the wall already had nine names in, four inches under the last one, and it took him eleven seconds, and he did not have to think about where to put it because Inez Sato had left a hand's width for it on the Thursday night without saying so to anybody, including him.

**CLIFF MARNER**

Then he stopped, because Cliff Marner was standing behind him, and had read it, and had not said anything for long enough that Elias turned around.

"It is wrong," Cliff Marner said.

"I know."

"You have written *Mar-ner*."

"Yes."

"That is right and I want you to know that it is not the only right one." He put one finger on the chalk, without touching it. "My mother's people spelled it without the second r and my father spelled it with, and I have spelled it with since I was nine, and the only people on earth who have ever got it wrong in writing are a booking system and a doctor in 2011, and both of those were corrected, and one of them was corrected by me at the desk in person with my service number, which I have still got." He took his hand off the wall. "I am not being precious. I am telling you that the whole of what I have been asking for for six days is that it is spelled the way I spell it, and that a man in a records room in another city is going to type it however he likes, and that if anybody ever reads my name off a page of this hall it has to be the one I use."

Elias Rook looked at the four letters in his own handwriting and then he went and got the notebook out of the records room and copied the name out of it into his own list of things that were true, and the list is a list of things that were true and not a list of people, and that is the only reason it is allowed to exist.

He did not make anything of it. He did not tell the room. Six weeks later a woman in Cordell would ask a clerk to spell a name a particular way, and the clerk would ask her, and she would say no.

---

The covering letter came in with Ru Sabar's card at half past four, in an envelope with a case number on it, and Ru Sabar brought it to the table and put it down and said, "It has a number on it and it is not from a courier, and the man on Ash Street is not the postman."

Inside was one page and the first line of it was the sentence that the rest of the volume is standing on.

*Where a person is evidenced as having been recovered from a designated site and the response list for that site contains no corresponding entry, the omission is being treated as an administrative error in the return, and correction of the return will follow in the ordinary course.*

Nobody said anything about it for about a minute and a half.

"There is a word in that," Mara said at last, "and it is the third time this month I have watched an institution choose a word instead of a fact, and the word is *error*, and an error is a thing that happens by accident."

"It is a word that closes a box," Elias said. "An error gets corrected. An error is nobody's decision. If the omission is an error then nobody has to say who looked, and what the omission was, and why there is no list to be on, and it will be corrected by a person typing a name into line six — which is the only box in the country that could hold him — and if it is corrected there, then the thing that was wrong was the form, and the form was not wrong, and the name will be in a system in a way that nobody chose and everybody signed."

"And if nobody corrects it," Joon said, "then it is a small clerical matter in a large file, and in four years it is an old thing, and there is no name in it at all."

Cliff Marner, from the chair, with the blanket over his knees, said the sentence that the wall was going to be about for the next fifty days.

"I have been in this room six days," he said, "and I have not once been angry with any of you, and I am not going to start, and I would like it written down that I have thought about it and I have decided not to be. I am angry with a piece of paper. I am angry with a piece of paper in a building I have never been inside, and I would like somebody to write that down, because in about a month I am going to be too tired to say it and it is going to look like somebody else made me do it."

Petar Lisk wrote it down.

**CLIFF MARNER IS NOT ANGRY WITH THE HALL. HE IS ANGRY WITH A PIECE OF PAPER. HE ASKED US TO WRITE IT DOWN BEFORE HE GOT TOO TIRED TO SAY IT.**

And then, in a different hand, at the bottom, in Inez Sato's chalk, which nobody asked for and which nobody in that building would have refused:

**AND THE HALL IS NOT ABOVE IT, AND WE ARE NOT GOING TO LET IT LOOK LIKE WE ARE.**
