# Chapter 66 — Nine Is Nine

The center opens on a Saturday from nine till one, and the only thing open is the counter, and the counter is a woman called Wren Okonjo who has worked there for six years and who can tell you a credential count for any sector in about four seconds and cannot tell you anything else at all.

"Aisle list, sector, year, count," she said. "That is the whole form. Four things on it, one of them a number and three of them words telling you which number it is."

"And there is no holder field," Joon said.

"There has never been a holder field on a count," Okonjo said. "You do not put a person's name on a piece of paper that says *there are nine of these*, because then it is a different piece of paper, and a different piece of paper is a different return, and the other return has a name on it somewhere, and that is where you go." She looked up. "You have come down here for a name."

"Yes," Elias Rook said.

"Then let me tell you what you are going to be told at the counter, and I would rather you heard it from a person than from a wall. Sector four C, that sequence, that year: **nine.** Two thousand and thirty-five. Nine credentials issued, nine retained, nine in the count. That is four seconds. That is also everything."

"Can you tell me the nine numbers."

"No. The count form does not carry them. The sequence list does, and it is a different document in a different drawer, and it is nine numbers with nothing next to them, and somebody in this building would have to sign for it, and today is not a day anybody signs for nine numbers with nothing next to them."

---

Rufus Aitken came in at ten past ten with a piece of paper in a plastic sleeve and put it on the counter face down.

"You should know where this came from before I let you have it," he said. "Your Wednesday request went into a queue. In the same week this center received a letter with a case reference on it from the Havenport Continuity Office, addressed to nobody in particular, which is not unusual, and a booking for a public visit under a holding number, which is not unusual, and the two arrived within four days of each other. That is a coincidence. We told the other institution so in writing on the Thursday, and the answer came back on Friday saying the booking is not connected to the letter.

"That is a true answer. It is also an answer the person signing it did not have to check anything to give, and I am accusing nobody, because I have been on the other side of that and I did not check either.

"And this morning a Counsel from that office arrived at the door and asked for you by the name you gave, and I have told her where you are and told her the rule about four of you in one room, and I would like it on your page that I did both."

He turned the paper round.

"It is a certified count. Sector, year, sequence, count, and a certificate in my hand and Wren's that the figure is the figure in the regional sequence register on the date of issue. **Nine.** Signed this morning at nine forty. It is not a photocopy. It is a document the Cordell Regional Records Center has put its hand to, and in five years somebody can walk in here and ask whether it is still true and Wren can look it up."

"Thank you," Joon said.

"Read it before you leave the counter," Aitken said, "because people take these out of buildings without reading them and are surprised later in a hearing room."

---

Joon Park read it twice and then asked the question that made the morning.

"Will you certify that the nine are unassigned."

Okonjo looked at him for a second. "Say that again slowly, because I want to be sure I said no to the right one."

"I want a document from this center that says, officially, in your hand, that nine sector four C maintenance credentials from the two thousand and thirty-five sequence have no holder of record. **Not that we cannot find them. That there is nobody.** Signed, dated, and usable in an inquiry."

"No," Okonjo said. "And it is not because I do not believe you. It is because there is no such return. An unassigned credential is a thing this center records when somebody hands us a form saying somebody died or resigned or was never issued, and nobody has handed us a form, and if we printed a certificate saying there was nobody we would be making a finding, and this center does not make findings. It holds them. Somebody else makes them. That is not modesty, that is the whole design."

Elias Rook put his hand flat on the counter.

"Then let me put it another way and I will be the one who has to live with having asked. **We write it ourselves.** Not here, not on your paper. We print nine lines on a sheet — number, sector, sequence, year, and a column with nothing in it — and we write at the top that this was compiled by a residents' body from a certified count, and we sign it with two names, ours, and we hand it to a Deputy Director, and it is a document about nine people that we made, and it will be the only shape those nine ever have, and it will be in our hand and not in anybody else's."

He stopped, because Joon Park had stopped writing.

"No," Joon said.

The counter had four people in it and none of them moved.

"I am going to say the rule and I am not going to make a speech about it, because we have been in this building three days and I have already made one speech in it and it cost me. **The rule is that a document we make is a document we own.**" He turned the notebook so that Elias could see it, which he never did. "From the day that sheet exists, it is ours, and ours is the problem, because ours can be demanded, and ours can be impounded, and ours can be read out in a room by people who are not us. And in October somebody will describe it in one sentence, and the sentence will be *a team produced a record of nine unnamed maintenance credentials*, and that sentence will be true, and it will be the only true sentence anybody has ever written about those nine, and it will be true because we were in a building in another city with a certified count in our hand and it was easy."

"That sheet would say what happened," Elias said.

"That sheet would say what we decided to write down," Joon said. "Not what happened. **We would be the first people in the whole of this to have an opinion about those nine, and we would be putting it on a page in a column with a heading on it.** That is how it starts. Not with a conspiracy. With a competent person filling in a box they designed themselves for a question nobody asked them."

Elias Rook took his hand off the counter.

"That is the plainest thing you have ever refused to me," he said.

"It is the plainest thing I have ever refused to anybody," Joon said. "Write it down and do not make it more than it is."

---

At half past eleven a woman in a gray coat came into the counter with a case portfolio under her arm and asked for the man and the woman who had filed on Wednesday.

Counsel Renata Solano is forty-seven and she writes the findings that hurt people, and she said why she was there in one sentence, standing up, with her coat still on.

"In October I have to be able to say in a document what I did and did not do in a regional records center in the week of the twenty-first of October, and if nobody from this office was ever in this building, that is also a thing I have to say, and I would rather say the other one."

"You are not on their side," Joon said, before she had sat down.

"I have never been on anybody's side, and I have been on this office's side for eleven years, and I am telling you what I am here for and you may check it with the custodian, who has already told me he does not know what I am here for either."

She did not look at the certified count, and did not touch it.

"Three questions, all mine, none of them about the count. One. Have you asked this archive to hold, reserve, embargo or withhold anything."

"No," Joon said. "We asked for a count, it was certified, we signed for it. There is nothing anywhere with a hold on it."

"That is the correct answer and I would like it on a form with a date on it, and I will sign the request for the form.

"Two. Does your residents' body hold a document you cannot produce on demand."

Elias Rook heard himself answer before he decided to.

"Yes."

"Am I going to find out what it is."

"No."

"That is also correct," Solano said, "and I want to say why, because you will think it is kindness and it is not. In eleven years somebody will say in a room *the residents held a record they refused to produce*, and if I have spent five minutes in this building today I can say you were asked and declined, and that is the difference between a refusal and a disappearance. **I would rather you refused in front of me than vanish and be talked about later.** That is my whole trade and nobody thanks me for it."

Joon Park wrote both answers down and gave them to her, and she read them standing up, and said, "Third. Do you have a person in your building who has been asked a question by this office and has not answered it."

Elias Rook said nothing at all, and Lena Mir's sentence came up in him like water, and Joon said, "There is no outstanding question to this office from the hall."

"Thank you," said Solano, put the two answers in the portfolio, and left at ten to twelve, because the counter shuts at one and she said she was not going to be the woman who made them stay past one.

---

Wendell Pryce was in the lobby at half past twelve with the same empty box, and he had been in the building twice that week and had not been asked to be.

"You are going to be told on Friday that there is a store of older sheets," he said. "I am telling you on Saturday, and I am telling you the way to get into it, and I am not asking you for anything and I am not being a favor, and I have thought about whether I am being a favor and I have decided that I am not, because I was in a ring in this city in two thousand and thirty-six and the only reason anybody knows anything about it is that a shopkeeper wrote it down, and I would like there to be one more thing written down that is not about what I paid."

"Wendell—"

"It is the Sunday hour. Ten to twelve, store room, a preservation levy, and a key on a hook, and there is a woman on the desk on Sunday who has been doing it for six years and has had nobody come. **Ask her for the store log of the two thousand and thirty-five mapping pull and not for the sheets.** The sheets are what you want and the log is what is in the building. I know that because I asked her in the spring and she showed me the shelf and I have thought about it since and I would rather somebody who needs it had it than me."

Elias Rook stood in a lobby and did not say thank you, and Pryce said, "Good," and went out into the rain.

---

He was tired in a way he had not been tired before, and it was not a bad tired.

He had spent two days being unable to help a woman who wanted a piece of paper and two days being unable to help a man who wanted to be named, and on Friday morning he had watched a competent woman refuse to be brave in a corridor, and on Saturday morning he had watched a clerk say no to a certificate on the grounds that a center holds findings and does not make them, and the two of them had between them made a thing he could not make.

And the number was nine, and it was the same nine, and they had it certified in a hand that was not theirs with a date on it, and there was no person in it, and that was not a failure of the archive.

That was the correct behavior of a well-run archive. It was the only thing anybody had wanted all week, and it was exactly the thing nobody in Havenport would ever be able to explain to a Deputy Director, and Elias Rook stood in a lobby forty minutes and an ordinary road from a building with twenty-eight people in it and understood that he had spent six weeks being angry at a system for being correct, and had come here to prove that a system that was not correct existed, and had found a number.

Joon Park put the certified count into the tin, and the rubber band went round twice, and neither of them said anything on the way to the car.
