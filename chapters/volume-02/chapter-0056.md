# Chapter 56 — The File Is Not the Office's

The demand came into the building on Monday afternoon in a plastic wallet and it was one page, and it had eleven lines on it, and one of the eleven was the whole of it.

*Item 11. The file of the residents' body, including all consent records, power consent cards, and the minutes of any decision of that body.*

There is no line on that page for a resident to say no on.

Elias Rook read it four times in the records room and then put it on the table in the front room and stood next to it and said the only two sentences he had, and then he went out of the building and walked to the end of the street and back, twice, for about fifty minutes, while twenty-eight people inside it worked out how to refuse something that the form had no category for.

He did not have a way to help. He went looking for one for fifty minutes. That is on the record because Petar Lisk asked him about it on Thursday and Elias told him, and Petar Lisk wrote it down in his own hand under the date, and nobody in that building has ever taken it off the wall.

---

They started at half past six because Lena Mir's round finished at six and a meeting that starts at half past six is a meeting that twenty-eight people can get to.

Inez Sato stood up at seven and did what she has done in this hall since the second week, which is to name the cost out loud before she pays it.

"I am going to say the cost first and I am going to say all three parts of it, and then I am going to do the thing, and I would like the three parts written up exactly the way I say them." She had the sheet in her hand and she was not reading it. "One: after tonight this hall is the obstruction in a public document with a case number on it. Not a person. The obstruction. It will be the word, and it will be in the file, and it will be read by somebody in October who has never been in this room.

"Two: the power arrangement for this building is now visible to somebody from that office. They have asked twice and I have said no twice and the reason I gave was that the cards are the cards. The next time they ask, the answer that works is not *they are the cards* any more. The answer that works is going to have to be a fact about four households on Bell Row, and the old man who has walked down here twice is going to have a fact about himself in a file in a building on the Meridian, and I did not tell him that was coming, and I am not going to be able to un-tell it.

"Three: two households on Bell Row are going to hear about this before I get to their doors, because it is on a bench at a transit desk and because Lena's page is on a nail and because people talk. I would like to know which of you are willing to have that be true, and I would like to know it now, from you, before we vote, because I am not going to do it to you afterwards."

Nobody answered for about a minute, and then about nine people said variations of yes, and one person said, "I do not care," and Inez said, "That is a yes and I am writing it as a yes, because you meant it as one and I would rather you said the true one."

Then she gave them the thing she had spent an hour building, which was that the form has no box for a refusal, and that she was not going to let a room invent one in a hurry.

"So it goes like this," she said. "There is no line for *refused*. There is a line for *provided* and a line for *not applicable* and a line for *withdrawn*, and I have watched a form writer pick *not applicable* when a person says no, because it is the nearest box and it is the shortest, and it is a lie and it is a lie that a stranger will read as a decision by a person, and the person is never in the room. So we are not going to use their box. We are going to write our own, on a separate sheet, in my words, and I am going to sign it, and I am going to ask for the whole of it read into the instrument and not a summary of it. If somebody in that office does not like a sheet of paper, that is a real thing and I would like it in the transcript that I said it first."

---

The vote was not unanimous and nobody pretended it was going to be.

Nineteen said no. Four said yes. Five did not vote, which is a thing twenty-eight people can do in a hall if somebody has said in advance that not voting is a thing you are allowed to do, and Inez had said it, at seven, in the third minute.

Osei Kwarteng was one of the four.

He stood up, and he said the reason before anybody asked him for it, because he had been thinking about it since Sunday and he had it in the order he thought in it.

"I want it to go," he said. "My wife is on a bed at the clinic and the clinic has asked four times for a record of her and there is no record of her, and there is a record of her in the residents' file because Lena has written one, and the record is better than what the clinic has got, and I am not going to stand in a hall and be proud this week. That is a reason. I would like somebody to write down that I gave a reason."

"It is written down," Nadi Bell said, from the table, and she wrote it down.

"Then say the other part," Inez said. "Say that you know what it costs."

"I know what it costs. I was in the room. I am saying yes anyway and I am saying it with my name on it and not behind somebody else."

And Inez Sato, who has never in five weeks let a thing in this hall be granted quietly, said the thing that cost her.

"I am not going to overrule you," she said. "You are one of the twenty-two and you have been since the second week and the paper does not say that the hall agrees. I am not going to do the thing I do, which is make a room into one voice, and I am not going to do it *at* you as a punishment for saying a true thing. So here is what we have. Nineteen no, four yes, five not voting, and a refusal that is mine and signed by me and in my words, and underneath it, in the same ink on the same sheet, the fact that four people in this room said hand it over and one of them gave a reason about a bed at a clinic, and it stays there as long as my refusal stays there. It does not go under. It is not a footnote. It is on the sheet, in order, at the same size, and if the sheet is copied then it is copied whole, and I will be checking, and Petar will read it at seven in the morning whether it is on the wall or not."

Osei Kwarteng said, "That is fair."

"It is not fair," Inez said. "It is careful. Fair is not what I am able to do this week."

---

The Deputy Director of the Havenport Continuity Office arrived at a quarter to nine on Wednesday evening, on his own time, in a car, having come forty minutes and given up an evening to a hearing room with nobody in it, and Elias Rook noted that it cost him and did not say so, because the man had said on Friday that he would not be proud of being bound by Finding 3 and this was him keeping to that.

He stood at the front of twenty-eight people and said four things and they took eleven minutes.

"The file is the residents'." He said it first and he said it plainly and he did not sit down. "The inquiry will not take it. I have looked for the box and there is no box and I have told you that on Friday and I am telling you again in a room with a recorder in it, because I would like the transcript to have it twice.

"It is not taken because of a finding your archivist made in the second week and I adopted at the cost of five years of forms and a third of my clerks, and it is the same finding that leaves a nineteen-year-old's name off the only strong page in your file while the page stays valid, and I am bound by it and I did not enjoy being bound by it and I am not going to pretend to you that I did.

"The Office will take a power summary — circuits, drawn load, hours, and the person responsible — and it will not take the cards. The cards are the cards. I have read the third of your clerk's letters and I agree with them and it is not my decision to make and I have made it.

"And the fourth thing is the one I have to say, and I would like to say it to the person who wrote the sheet, and not to the man who was on the telephone on Friday." He looked at Inez. "**Then you have declined, and I am required to record that you declined, and it will be read by people who will not read the rest of it.**"

The room was very quiet.

"I am not being cruel and I am not warning you about a mood," Rusk said. "It is a document, and the document will say *the residents' body declined to provide its file*, and it will say that in a sentence with a full stop at the end of it, and the summary of the inquiry will say that a residents' body declined to provide its file, and nobody who reads the summary is going to know that you offered a power summary, or that nineteen of you said no and four said yes and five did not vote, or that one of the four stood up and gave a reason about his wife's bed. They will read the sentence. I have been on the reading end of that sentence for nineteen years. I am telling you what you are going to be on the reading end of, and I am telling you in advance, and I am telling you at eight forty-five in the evening in a hall and not in a letter afterwards, because in a letter afterwards it would only be a warning and this way it is a witness."

Inez Sato said, "Then read it in and I will sign that you read it in."

"Read the whole sheet in," Rusk said. "Including the four, including the one, and including the reason about the clinic."

"Read the reason about the clinic," Inez said, "and say the date on it out loud, and say that the reason was given by Osei Kwarteng, who is in this room, and who did not hide behind anybody."

"It will say that," Rusk said. "Write it in a way that makes me say it."

She wrote it in a way that made him say it. It took nine minutes. It is in the file. It is the only page in the Kestrel inquiry of 2042 that names a resident's reason for something, and it is there because a woman who organizes a cooperative stood up in a room and made the man with the pen say it out loud, and Elias Rook was standing eight feet away and was not part of it and did not try to be.

---

He went outside at ten past nine, and Nadi Bell came out too, and stood on the step in the cold with the movement record in her hand, and did not look at him.

"Twenty-eight," she said. "Nineteen no, four yes, five not voting. One asked for the file to go and gave a reason about a bed at a clinic and asked for the reason to be written down, and it is written down, and it will not be read. Twenty-nine people have now been through that door. I wrote the twenty-nine in a past tense at ten past nine at night on Wednesday, which is a tense I invented in week four for people I have not met, and tonight I used it on a thing that has not happened yet, and I am going to have to live with having done that."

Elias Rook took out the notebook and wrote it down, and the writing down was the whole of what he had, and he wrote it with his right hand, and the left one stayed on the table all night where he had left it.

Nobody in that building has ever been able to say the day that the Cinder Quarter Electrical Cooperative became a party. Petar Lisk wrote a date under it on Thursday morning and nobody has been able to prove him wrong.
