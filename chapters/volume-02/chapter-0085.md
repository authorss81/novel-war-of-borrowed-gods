# Chapter 85 — The Hall, Told First

There is no depot on a Thursday, and he had learned in the first week that this was not a gift, it was just the shape of a man's week when a man has had a job taken off him.

Mara Okafor was in the front room at ten past seven on Thursday the thirteenth of November, the sixty-eighth day, with a sheet in her hand, and there were nine people in there, and she said the decision in ninety seconds in the order the rule requires and did not explain the rule.

"Mr. Duarte has a clinic appointment on the twenty-fifth of November at ten past two. It is two hours in a car, a wheelchair on a ramp that is not at this building, and an examination that has been outstanding since the first week of October.

"What I need is a driver for two hours, and fuel, and a card. I have said what it costs before anybody asked, and the fuel is not the money. **Lena is going to sign a form at the clinic, because he cannot hold a pen and hold his shoulders at the same time, and I am telling the room that before it happens and not at the counter with a pen in her hand.**

"He can say no to any of it, including the examination, and if he says no to the examination I am going to write *patient declined examination* in his own words on his own chart and I will not ask him why, and I have said all of that in this order to the people who are going to carry it, and I am not taking a vote, and anybody who wants to discuss the rule can discuss the rule afterwards."

Ansel Duarte said, from the corridor, where he had been listening the whole time on his frame: "Say the last part again."

"The last part is that I am not taking a vote," Mara Okafor said.

"No. The part before it. I am not voting either and I am not being asked, and if you had asked I would have said no to the vote before I said no to the driver, and you knew that, and you did not ask, and that is twice in four weeks." He did not turn round. "Do the appointment."

---

Then Elias Rook put the tin on the table, which is what the tin is for, and everybody in that room knows it, and there was the DS-4 revision letter in it in a plastic sleeve and a copy of the letter with the clerk's hand on it and nothing else, because there was nothing else.

"I want to say the finding of Wednesday in this room, in the words it was said in, to the people who are going to be affected by it, before it is acted on," he said. "And I am going to say the thing that is hard, which is that you did not write that rule, Inez. **A doctor wrote it. And it says *any offer, any boundary, any refusal, and any condition somebody is asked to perform before they give anything.* A document is not any of those four things and I have been trying since Sunday to work out how to use a rule on a thing it was not written for, and I cannot do it, and I have decided to say that out loud in this room instead of working round it.

"So I am telling you the finding of Wednesday. The Office's own incident form has had a line on it since two thousand and three for a person signing on behalf of somebody who cannot sign, and the line was added after a night, and there is no name in it, and there is a man in a hardware shop on Ninth Street who was on that night, and he is seventy-one—"

"That is enough of him."

Inez Sato had not moved. "**Mr. Park wrote a rule about that man on Tuesday and read it back with you in the room. You have just said a street, a trade and an age into a room of nine people, and a street and a trade and an age is an address, and this building does not get to be the thing that writes him down again.**"

"Then the finding without him," Elias Rook said. "**I do not have a document about him and the reason I do not is that there is no form in the world that asks a man of seventy-one for his eleven minutes. I said the first half of that before anybody in this room had decided whether I was allowed to, and I would like it written down that I said it, and not on the wall.**"

"It goes in the pad and not on the wall," Inez Sato said, "and I am writing it now."

"And that is the whole of it and nobody has to do anything about any of it, which is what I am asking, and I would like to know whether that is allowed in this room."

---

Emil Baros put his pencil down on the table, which is a thing he does.

"I would like to say that this hall now knows more about how a form works than any hall in this city," he said, "and that I said that sentence before I decided whether it was a good idea, and that I have been putting my name to a line for six weeks about a column with twenty-eight lines on it. **I would like to put a standing item on the pad. Forms. Once a fortnight. Not to do anything. Just to know.**"

"In a form," Nadi Bell said from the back, "with a heading on it, and a date, and a line for what was found, and a line for what we do about it, and a signature block."

"Baros said yes," Nadi Bell said, "and here is what a yes turns into."

"Which is a process," Ru Sabar said from the door, with her card in her hand, "and I have kept a laundrette for nine weeks on a condition, and a condition is not a safe place, it is a thing you can break, and I break it myself every night at ten past six, and that is the only power I have got."

---

"Emil," Inez Sato said.

"I am not asking for it to be mine," Baros said. "I am asking for it to be on the pad."

"**The hall is not going to hold a meeting about a form.**" She said it flat and she did not raise her voice, and there were nine people in the room and the laundry started up two floors up, and nobody spoke. "The reason is one sentence and I am not going to improve on it in the next ten minutes: **a hall that convenes itself about a form is a hall that has started a process.**

"And I am going to say the whole of that before I have done it, because I put the rule on this wall in the third week and I have not once used it on myself, and that is the day this building stops being a hall and becomes a committee.

"**What I am deciding is what this building's mouth is allowed to do. That is all it is, and it is the only thing I am actually good at, and after this morning I will be the only person on this street who knows a finding that other people know, and nobody will be able to check it, and that is a bad way to live and I am choosing it and I am not going to be argued out of it.**

"And two more things, and the second one is going to be unpleasant. The hall has not been asked to agree with Wednesday's finding. The hall has not been asked to refuse it. Nobody is going to be asked. **And the hall does not get to have not known, and the hall does not get to have not asked, and I am putting both of those on the wall tonight, and one of them is going to cost somebody a feeling, and I have thought about that for a day and a half and I do not care.**"

Ansel Duarte said, from the corridor, "That is three times somebody has said *the hall does not get to have not asked*, and every one of the three times it was in a room where about nine people could hear it, and that is a fact about this building and not about me, and I would like somebody to write it down."

"Somebody will write it down," Inez Sato said. "Mr. Lisk will write it down."

---

The rest of it was about the driver, and Osei Kwarteng said he would take the Thursday and not the twenty-fifth because the twenty-fifth was a Tuesday and he was on the feeder rota, and Ru Sabar said she would take the twenty-fifth and would take it as a swap against a night and not as a favor, and both of those were done in about four minutes and Inez Sato wrote them down and read them back.

And then, at about ten to eight, Ansel Duarte said the thing that was in the room and that nobody was going to be able to put back.

"Here is what I have been thinking about since yesterday," he said. "You have all got very quiet about the word *hall*.

"**A hall that knows how forms work is a hall that can be described by people who do not like us, in one sentence, accurately.** Not a lie. Not a villain. A sentence. *The hall on Ash Street understands the Office's own paperwork.* And that sentence will have been written by somebody who was not being cruel to us, and it will be true, and it will be in a file somewhere in nine years, and when somebody reads it they are not going to know that what we actually did was find a page in a cupboard in another city because a woman of sixty-eight would not let us use her name.

"I am not frightened of the Deputy Director," he said. "**I am frightened of the sentence, and the sentence is fair, and nobody in this room is going to be able to stop it, and I would like it minuted that a man on a frame who cannot walk to a hospital has noticed it before any of you did.**"

Nobody managed it. Lena Mir said, "Write it down," and Petar Lisk wrote it down, and then nobody said anything else for about eleven minutes, and a sentence on a wall had been true before it was said.

---

Tarin Voss was still by the door at half past eight with his coat on, which is where he stands, and he stopped Elias Rook on the step outside.

"Two things," he said. "One. I am not putting my name to a form watch, and I want it written down that I was asked and I said no and nobody is going to explain to me why I was wrong, because I know why I said no. If there is a line on a pad with a heading and a date, then in a year this hall has a *record of having considered* something, and everything this building does in a file can be served on it. I do not care what it is called. **A pad is a file that has not been given a stamp yet, and I am not going to be on it.**"

"Written down," Elias Rook said, "with the date."

"Two. You said in there that nobody has to do anything about any of it, and you were right, and that is not a thing you get to be right about for free." Tarin Voss pulled his coat closed. "**The hall does not get to have not asked. You lot have had that on a wall since Tuesday. So if the hall is not allowed to ask about this, then somebody in this building is going to do the asking, and it is not going to be you, and it is going to be me.**

"I am not asking you to stop me. A refusal with a sentence on a wall under it is a *place*, and a place gets used, and in three weeks somebody in this hall is going to say the word *form* in this room and mean the two thousand and three line, and not the Deputy Director. **I would like somebody to have written that down on the day it was still only a warning.**"

---

Elias Rook went back up the ramp at twenty to nine and stopped on the half-landing, because there was a thing in his chest he wanted to do and had not done.

He wanted to ask Inez Sato for the finding. Not to publish it, not to hold a meeting on it. Just to have it, the way he had twenty lines of a custody schedule and two certificates and four frames, so that if Wednesday went badly there would be something in his hand.

She would have given it to him. That was the thing. She had taken a refusal on herself for the sake of nineteen people and it had cost her the only power she has, which is deciding things in the room she is standing in.

And that was the third time in a month he had ended up on the wrong side of a wall, and it was the hardest, because the other two had doors in them and this one did not.

**A wall is not a wall because it is safe. It is a wall because somebody decided where it goes.** She had put this one exactly where it belonged, and he was not going to argue with it or write round it or ask her, and it was the correct outcome and it cost him a thing he wanted, and there was no form on earth on which he could complain about it.
