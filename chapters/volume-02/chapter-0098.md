# Chapter 98 — The Release

It was Wednesday, the twenty-sixth of November, the eighty-first day. The round was done before seven, the chairs were in the front room, and the board was left exactly as Petar Lisk had read it at seven, out loud, twice. Twenty-one people in the room were on the sheet, a twenty-second name was in the room without its body, and a twenty-third person was in the chair by the window and not on the sheet at all.

Cliff Marner was fifty-one days out of the ground. Della Marner was eighty-one days at a family-reception desk in Cordell and was not in the room and did not know there was one. The man behind the wall was eighty-one days in.

"I am going to do a thing on Monday that Mr. Vale wrote down in nineteen ninety-eight," Inez Sato said, "and I am going to do it before I say one word about the release, and nobody is going to have to be told that this room is a room and not an audience, and that is the whole of why I am telling you.

"**I have not told this room what I am about to do, and I am telling you that now and not afterwards. Mr. Vale wrote it on page four of eight pages he put on this table on Monday, and he told me on Monday not to ask you to use it and I have not asked and I am not going to, and if you want to know whether it worked you can decide that yourself and not tell me.**"

Then she said: "Right. The vote is on the release and it is not on the finding and there is nothing on the pad. Mr. Park has the sheet. Mrs. Mir reads the no's out. Twenty-two names, one at a time, in the order they are on the rail, and if a person is not in the room their card is not counted and I will say so when it happens."

---

It took about an hour.

Petar Lisk read the board at seven and then sat on the floor at the back with a book, which is where he has sat since October. Ru Sabar's card had gone down at ten past six the night before, as it does, and she was in the room, and she had told nobody she was going to be, and nobody asked.

"Mr. Lisk's card is on the rail, so he is in the building. That is the October rule, and it is the only rule in this building that has never once been wrong, and I am not making an exception for a boy of nine at seven in the morning." She said it to the room and not to him. "He is not voting on a document nobody is going to ask him about. **Joon, read his name when you get to it.**"

The vote was twenty in favor and two against, and nobody abstained, and Joon Park wrote down after the fourth name that the answer was in the sheet and not in the count.

Four of the twenty-two gave a reason out loud and the other eighteen said nothing, and the instrument records the four and the count and not the eighteen, and Lena Mir read all four out loud at the end in the same voice, without comment, the way she reads a round.

**Ansel Duarte**, who had been at a clinic on the Tuesday at ten past two and had got back about five: *"I said in November that the arguing is the appointment and that the hazard is my legs. I am voting yes and I want it written down that my reason is that on Tuesday afternoon a thing came through a wall in this building and I was forty minutes away at a clinic, and this house did not need me, and I have been waiting eleven weeks to be able to say that out loud."*

**Osei Kwarteng**, who was on the feeder rota: *"My wife is asleep in the next room on a battery the clinic charged forty-one dollars for, and nobody in this room knows where that money came from, and it is not her money and it is not mine and it came out of a tin on Wednesday night. I am voting yes and I want it written down that I know what it cost and that I am not going to be thanked for it and that if somebody thanks me I am going to hand it back."*

**Nadi Bell**, who was the person who has spent ten weeks writing records for other people: *"No. And I have been quiet about this for ten weeks and the reason is that nobody asked me, and I would have said exactly this on the ninth day.

"Dr. Okafor's rule goes to the people who pay for it, in the words it was said in, before it is acted on. **She is right about the rule and I have watched her apply it four times and it is the best thing anybody has done in this building since the sixth of September. And I am voting no because a release is not a decision about a person, it is a document, and the rule was never written for a document, and Elias Rook said that out loud in a room in November and was right, and what we are about to do is ask twenty-two people to hand a refusal of a document to a room that is going to write it down, and the first of those is me, and I am not going to pretend to a room that I have been comfortable with that for ten weeks.** Nobody is to soften it and nobody is to ask me twice."*

**Ru Sabar**, who had not spoken: *"No. I am not going to explain it and I am not going to be asked.

"A condition is not a safe place. A condition is a thing you can break, and I break mine every night at ten past six, and that is the only power I have got, and it is not even a good power. **And the day there is a copy of a condition is the day somebody can come and ask me whether I agreed to it, and I will not be able to prove that I never agreed to anything because agreeing is not on any of my cards, and I would like that written down.**"*

Mara Okafor was standing by the wall. Elias Rook watched her hear it, and watched what she did with it, and what she did with it was nothing at all, for about eleven seconds, and then she said:

"Thank you, Miss Bell. That is a rule of mine being right and not being enough and I am not going to say a word about it in this room, because a word from me in this room would be a word from the person the rule came from, and then it would be the rule arguing and not a person voting." She looked at Ru Sabar. "I am not going to thank you either and you are not to hand anything back to me, because there is nothing to hand back, you have not given me anything. **Nobody inherits me. That includes the people who vote no.**"

---

"Then it goes forward," Inez Sato said, "with two no's in the same ink and at the same size, and the instrument is on the shelf in the tin's place with the band round it twice, and it is not in the residents' file, and it is not going to the Meridian today, and anybody who asks me for a copy can have it in this room and cannot have it out of this room."

Four people stood up in the next four minutes and put a page on the table in front of twenty-one people who had voted on it.

Elias Rook put down the four institutional fields: a field that does not exist, a field that can be blank and gets filled in by somebody who was not there, a field nobody designed for the thing in question, and a field that exists and has nowhere to attach. He put down the blank field, which is the fourteenth row of a weekday in October in a depot's aisle log, in a floor supervisor's own hand, eighteen days after her own hand restored the column it lives in. Joon Park put down the two words, *not applicable*, filled in on the fourteenth of April 2035 in a printed box that asked about a field and not about a person, by a clerk nobody has put in anything.

Tomas Vale put down the revision letter of the fourteenth of January 2003 and said out loud what it was: a form number, a revision, a date of issue, a printed reason, a district, and a block with no name in it, and a line that says a person can be entered on behalf of a subject unable to give it, and a form in a drawer in a form store that cannot be produced by anybody, including the Deputy Director who convened the inquiry.

And then he put down eight sheets of paper with a printed heading. "Those are mine, I am not offering them as evidence, and I have said that once already this week. They are on the table because a method is worth nothing if only one person has it."

Nobody in the room read them. That had been arranged on Monday and Elias Rook had made himself stay by the wall, and it held.

---

The one interruption was Emil Baros, and it was about a letter.

"I have to say a thing," he said, "and I am not a person in this room and have never claimed to be, and I have got a pencil, and if I do not say it now it goes in wrong in a document about this building.

"**Our figures and theirs are not the same document.** Our copy of the clerk's fourth column says *items, 2*. The Office's says *item, 2*. One letter, no comma, twenty-eight lines, and I found it with a pencil and a Tuesday six weeks ago.

"And I have traced where that letter comes from and it comes from the wall. Our copy is a copy of a stranger's chalk, and theirs is a copy of the same chalk, and **there is no original anywhere on earth, and the version that is correct is ours, and the reason it is ours is that Mr. Baros has held the wall's version in his hand about forty times.**"

He put the pencil down on the table, which was not part of the function and which nobody mentioned.

"So the page has to say which copy it is using," he said, "and it must not say it is quoting the Office. **It is quoting a chalk wall that a man in an orange jacket ran out of forms to write on, and a file, and this hall — and I have told you the file and this hall are not the same object as the wall.** That is the whole of what I have and I am sitting down."

The instrument's fourth page carries that sentence now. It was read out, it was agreed, nobody voted on it because it is not a decision, and Lena Mir wrote it out longhand because a typed page would be a page in a file.

---

There was one more thing and it was in a plain envelope and it was handled by nobody.

Nadi Bell put it on the table herself. "It is a movement record, six pages, written in a past tense on my own authority in October about people I have never met, about a thing that has not happened. The tense was mine and I chose it and I said at the time that I would be judged on it.

"**Enter it as testimony. Do not enter it as a finding, and I am not going to explain why. A finding is a thing somebody decided and a movement record is a thing somebody wrote. In four years there is going to be a file with a finding in it saying the record was not evidence, and I would like the record in that file first and called a record.**"

"Testimony, and not a finding," Joon Park said, and wrote it, and read it back. "The record is entered as testimony. It is not a finding. It is a movement record in a past tense, it is on the shelf with the band round it twice, and nobody in this building is going to be asked to read it out."

---

It was twenty past nine when the room emptied and the instrument went onto the shelf, and Elias Rook stood by the wall where he had been standing for two hours and a quarter and did not pick anything up.

On the Monday of the previous week, on a landing in an Office building on the Meridian, Joon Park had told him to write something down somewhere that was not his own notebook, because his own notebook was the only record in this business that was in his handwriting and nobody could check it.

**There was not one line of the instrument in his handwriting.** There were four names and two reasons and two refusals and a sentence about a chalk wall, and nineteen hands on the wall underneath the clerk's column, and four refusals read out loud in Lena Mir's voice, and his own hand had been on a chair and then on a rail and then in his pocket.

He had asked nineteen people to vote on a thing that was true, and he had stood in the room while they did it, and he had been the least important person in it, and that had been arranged on Sunday by a woman who had told him in a corridor that asking was the expensive part, and she had been right, and it had cost about nine seconds per person, and he was the man who had asked.
