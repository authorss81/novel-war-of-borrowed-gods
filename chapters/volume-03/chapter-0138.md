# Chapter 138 — Two Documents On One Wall And Neither One Taken Down

Cliff Marner was ninety-one days out of the ground. Della Marner was one hundred and twenty-one days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was one hundred and twenty-one days in, and Elias Rook was on the yard at half past eight on Monday morning, the fifth of January, which was the first working day of a booking of twenty-eight and the last day of the week that the counter at number six and the board would both be open before the weekend.

The yard had three lorries in and a fourth waiting and the office was warm and there were eleven men in it who had not all been in it on the same morning since the middle of November.

Nerys Hobbs had the terminal working and the first sheet of the booking on the trestle with her name at the foot of it, and the booking ran to the fifth of February, and after the fifth of February the board had to come back for money.

---

The program had sent the operational list on Friday and the works had it, and Nev Peart had pinned it up on the wall next to his own sequence, one above the other, about eight inches apart, and neither of them covered the other.

"You have left the old one up," Elias Rook said.

"Of course I have left the old one up," Peart said. "That is my sequence and it is the only record of what this yard is actually going to do on four nights in January, and it was on that wall before any of you arrived in this district, and if your man in the office takes his list away in February and comes back in March with a different plan then I am going to want to be able to stand in front of somebody and say that this is what I was told and this is the day I was told it."

"That is not obedience. That is an argument you have already had with a woman from communications."

"It is an argument I am going to keep having with a woman from communications until she retires or I do," Peart said. "And I will tell you what two pieces of paper eight inches apart actually is, because you are about to go and think something about it. It is the correct state of the world on the fifth of January. One of those pieces of paper came from an office and one of them came from a bench, and they agree with each other tonight, and the day they stop agreeing is a Tuesday in February and I will not be the one who decides which one is right. That is not my job. My job is the four men and the ladder and the door."

"Which one will you work from tonight."

"From mine," Peart said, "and I have told my crew that, and they have told their families that, and if the office comes out here on Tuesday and asks them to work from theirs then there is going to be a conversation on a road at seven o'clock that I cannot control, and I have not slept properly about that either, and you did not ask, and I have told you anyway because you will be standing on it."

He unhooked the program sheet and held it out flat so that both of them could see the bottom of it.

"There is a column on theirs and it is called doors and it says five for your street and there is no column on mine and mine is a card for every door, and if you put the two together tonight you will find they do not agree and they have never once agreed, and I am not going to reconcile them for you on a Monday morning." He put the sheet back. "A range of numbers is a street and a card is a door, and a flat has a door and does not have a number on the street, and I have had that argument with three offices in nineteen years and I have lost it twice and won it once and I do not remember which. Work from my cards. That is the whole of the instruction and I am not giving you another one."

---

The board's surveyor came at about eleven with a hard hat in his hand and did not put it on, and this was his third visit to the district since the middle of December.

He had a query in a folder. It had gone up to the Meridian on the sixteenth of December in an envelope with a copy of the schedule in it, and it was one question, and it had been asked because a competent clerk in a public works department had noticed that a document about a district was dated after the occasion on which the ground had last been looked at, and nobody in three offices had been able to put the two together.

Nerys Hobbs gave him all three in about ninety seconds without being asked twice.

"Eleventh of September, twenty to seven in the morning, occasion one. Twenty-ninth of November, quarter past six, occasion two. First of January, quarter past seven, occasion four, and that is this month's, taken on the alignment with the street shut and a foreman's slip issued the same morning." She put her finger on the sheet on the trestle. "Every one of them has a date and an hour and a name on it and the name is a foreman's, and he is fifty-one and has been in that yard nineteen years and I would put my sheet in front of anybody on the strength of that."

The surveyor wrote all three down in a small hardback notebook with a rubber band round it, and he wrote them in a column, and then he stopped and looked at the column for a second.

"That is three occasions," he said, "and my query is one date."

"Yes."

"Then I have three and I wanted one." He closed the notebook and did not say anything else about it, and he did not say anything else about the schedule either, and he went out to the alignment with a hard hat on at the gate and walked to the end of it and back, which took him an hour and ten minutes.

On his way back through the survey office he looked at Elias Rook, who was standing at the trestle with a tube under his arm, and he said, "Are you the district's something?"

"I am on this road on Tuesday evening," Elias Rook said. "I am not anything. I am a fifth man on a road."

The surveyor took a pen out and wrote one word in the notebook and turned the notebook round so it faced him, which is what a man does when he wants somebody to read a thing he has written.

*Present.*

"That is in our vocabulary," the surveyor said. "It is on a hundred thousand forms in Civic buildings and it has never once meant that anybody is owed anything, and I have never met a form that could tell you what to do about a man who is *present*, and I have stopped trying to find one. I am going to write the word down because it is true and I would like there to be a piece of paper somewhere that says a man was on that road, and I would like it to be the only thing on the page."

---

He asked Peart at about half past two what a man on a road is for, on a night, and got an answer he had not expected, which was a piece of work.

"You count the door numbers," Peart said. "Not the people. The people are the crew's and the clinic's and mine, and if you count the people you will be doing a foreman's job badly and asking a woman questions she has not offered you. You count the doors. Five on that street. You write the five on the back of your hand with a biro when it is done, and you do not tell anybody, because nobody is going to ask you for it, and if somebody does ask you for it you tell them five and you tell them the number came off a door number on a printed list and not off anybody's head."

"That is a job that takes about four minutes."

"It is a job," Peart said, "and it takes four minutes, and it is the only thing on that road I would let a man in a coat do without asking myself first, and you have just spent an hour telling me you have a lamp and a rule and a schedule with no addresses on it, and I have listened to all three of those and I do not want any of them. I want five on the back of your hand."

He thought about it for the rest of the afternoon, on a bench, in a yard, in a coat, and the reason it stayed with him was not the four minutes. It was that a foreman of fifty-one with nineteen years in a yard had looked at a man who had come four miles to be useful and had given him the only job he could hand over that could not be got wrong, and had not once pretended it was worth more than that.

---

"There is a form for a person present," Nerys Hobbs said, at about four o'clock, "and I am not filling it in, and I have already made that decision and I am going to name the cost of it, which is that if something goes wrong on Tuesday then there is nobody in the works' paperwork who was there, and it will be a foreman's word and a fifth man's word against a form, and the form is worth more, and it is worth more because it is signed by somebody whose job it is."

"Who would sign it," Elias Rook said.

"That is not the part I am refusing," Hobbs said. "If I filled it in you would be on a works' document and in February somebody with a clipboard would be able to say that a member of the public was present at a night clearance, and in about four years that would be a line in a report about engagement, and there would be a number of people like you on a night clearance and the number would be the achievement. You can be on that road. You just cannot be in my paperwork, and I have not got a good reason and I have got an old one."

"What is the old one."

"That a man who is not on the sheet is a man who can be sent away," Nerys Hobbs said, "and on Tuesday night there are going to be four households on that block that will tell a stranger with a lamp in his coat to go to hell, and I would like them able to do it without a form in their front room afterwards."

---

They shut at four on the Monday and the office cleared out and the barrier came down, and the two pieces of paper were eight inches apart on a wall with nothing over them, and the first sheet of the booking was on the trestle with a name at the foot of it.

"Two things and then go," Hobbs said, with her coat on. "One. We are going to empty Rennard on the tenth and Cutbank Row on the seventeenth, and the sixth and the nineteenth are the other two, and four nights with four crews and twenty-eight working days, and I am not going to be able to say any of that to a room in February because I will be doing it."

"And two."

"Two. There is a page in a file upstairs with forty-four addresses on it that were retained on the second of December and are not on the first of January, and thirty of them are on Cutbank Row and the rest are on Rennard, and I have not counted that out loud and I am not going to, and the crews are going to empty both those streets in the next three weeks, and the program has said in writing that the schedule it holds is the operative document and that it is not issuing another one." She put her helmet under her arm. "I am going to do it anyway. I am not going to do it well. Both of those are true and I would like both of them written down by somebody, and there is nobody in this building I would like to write it down more than I would like a lamp on a table."

Elias Rook took the 11 at ten past five and sat on the far side of the cut, and the thread was on under the yard and had been on for two hours, and the second document was on a wall in a room he was not in, and he had been told he could stand on a road on Tuesday and could not be in a form, and the price of that was a foreman's word against a signature, and he had understood the price and had not argued about it, and there were a day and a night to go and a lamp in his coat.
