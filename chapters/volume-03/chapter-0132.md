# Chapter 132 — Three Minutes, And Then She Was Not Going To Be In It

Cliff Marner was eighty-five days out of the ground. Della Marner was one hundred and fifteen days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was one hundred and fifteen days in, and Elias Rook was on the far bank at ten to six on Tuesday morning, the thirtieth of December, because the depot ran Tuesdays and Fridays at half past six and the Tuesday half had been his since the end of October and it cost him an hour of sleep a week for the rest of his life and he had done the arithmetic in October and got the right answer.

R. Dunleavy did not do the talking part of it. The needle went up and stayed up and he wrote two figures in a book in his own hand and turned the book round so the page faced the man who had brought the sheet. Twenty minutes. Elias Rook pinned the sheet flat with his right forearm, because the left hand was gone and would not have held it against a table for twenty minutes in a depot in December.

He got the 11 at ten past eight with the tube under his right arm and nothing in the notebook, which on the first night of a new year is a thing a man notices.

---

He telephoned Mara Okafor at eleven from the corridor outside the survey office, where the heating had been off since two o'clock the day before and had now been on again, and where the door shut and the stair shook.

"I am going to ask on Thursday morning," he said. "I am telling you I am going to ask, and then I am going to stop, and I am not going to say what."

Then he stopped and counted, and he counted the way he had counted in a room above a shop on the twenty-third of December, which was badly, because he had a hand he could not use on the wall.

He got to two minutes and forty and thought she was not going to answer at all, and then he got to three minutes and she said, "Yes, and you should have asked me on Friday, and I want to say the next part and then I am going back to work."

"Say it."

"You are going to ask her for the boundary again. That is all it is. I heard what you said in that room in December and I have thought about the shape of it for eight days, and a woman drew a line in a form about nineteen years ahead of a man in this city and wrote down what the line was for, and there is exactly one question available to you about that, and it is whether the line is still where she put it, and everything else you could ask is you asking her for something." She did not soften it. "And when you have asked it and she has answered it, I am not going to be the person you come and tell what it means. That is not a rule and I am not writing you one. That is me not doing it."

"That is fair."

"It is fair and it is also the whole of what I have," Mara Okafor said. "I am at that clinic until four and there are sixty people on a round that is going to stop in the middle of next month, and I have got eleven of them inside a thing I can invoice and forty-nine outside it, and I have got a committee in the second week of January that has not yet read a letter. Do not come and tell me what the woman said on Thursday. Bring me a time."

---

She had been at the health center on the corner of Marden Row since half past eight and he had not known that until the corridor, and he said something about the forty-nine that he had been carrying in his mouth since Saturday, and she stopped him in the middle of the second word.

"No," Mara Okafor said. "Do not do that."

"I have not said anything yet."

"You were about to. You were about to make the forty-nine into a thing you are right about, and then I would have to send that letter again in different words, and the day I send it in words a man from the Cinder Quarter has chosen, it stops being a letter from a doctor about a district and it becomes a document somebody can wave, and the first person it will be waved at will be a committee that has not read the first one yet." She put the telephone down on the desk in front of her and said it into it, which was the way she said things when she was working. "I can get eleven of them invoiced to this center because this center has already seen eleven of them. The other forty-nine are not patients of a district that does not count them. That is a fact about a form and it is the whole of what it is, and I am not going to stand in a corridor and make it into anything, and the moral point of that letter belongs to somebody with a microphone and it is not going to be me in the second week of January."

"Who is it going to be."

"That is not mine either," Mara Okafor said. "I have got a round to keep going until the fifteenth and a form to fill in for the eleven and a woman of eighty-four who lives alone, and one of those three is harder than the other two and it is not the letter."

---

Nev Peart was at the bench with the kettle on when he came in at half past one, and the wallet of cards was on its nail and the worked pile was on the second nail where it belonged, squared up.

Elias Rook did not touch either nail.

"Two of them are on your block," Peart said, without being asked.

"Who."

"That is the answer you get and it is all of the answer you get," Peart said. "I have eleven cards in a wallet in a drawer in a room off a canteen and eleven names on them in my own hand, and a clerk of Hobbs's wrote them down off a list that a woman at that clinic gave me and not to anybody else on this earth. Two of the eleven are between fourteen and twenty-two on Marlow Street. I am not telling you which doors and I am not telling you which stairs and you did not ask me for either of those and if you had asked me I would have told you to go and ask the foreman, and the foreman is me, and that is the arrangement, and it has been the arrangement since the twenty-second of December."

"That is the right answer."

"It is the only answer there is," Peart said. "You want to know whether those two are going to be all right on Tuesday and I have told you they are on the block and that I have a card for each of them with a floor and a stair written on it in my own hand, and I have signed both, and I have put a ladder on the lorry for the sixth. That is everything. If I gave you the doors you would be a man with eleven names and no list, and a man with eleven names and no list is the most dangerous thing in this district, and there is no form anywhere that says he is not."

He picked the kettle up.

"You will be on that road on Tuesday," he said. "I have not asked you to be and I have not told you not to be. If you are, stand on the east side, because the west side is where they bring the ladder out and I do not want a man who cannot use his left hand in the way of a ladder."

---

At four o'clock the yard was full in a way it had not been since before Christmas, and a clerk from the regional office was in the survey office with a laptop open and forty-one sheets of something on the terminal, and the counter at number six was open because it was a Tuesday and it was a working day and it would be open again on Wednesday and then it would shut all weekend.

Joon Park telephoned at four twenty from a payphone outside a building two miles away, which he did twice a month and which cost him money he did not have.

"I am not asking about the log," he said. "I have told two people in a room the two dates and I am not going to say them to you on a telephone because you would then be a person who knows the dates, and in three days' time somebody in a room is going to ask you whether it has come, and I want you to be a person who says that the second of January is the near end and stops there."

"That is fair."

"It is entirely fair and it is also me not wanting to be in a room on Friday telling somebody I told you," Joon Park said. "The far end is the sixteenth. I have told two people. I am not telling a third until somebody asks me in a room, and if nobody asks me in a room then the sixteenth is the sixteenth and I will find out on the seventeenth the same as everybody else, and I have thought about that and I am not going to change it."

"You could ask on the sixteenth."

"I could and I am not going to, and you know why I am not going to, and it is the same reason you are not going to telephone a man you have got a number for," Joon Park said, and then he hung up, which he did.

Elias Rook stood in a warm office in a yard that had been shut for four days and had come back with three lorries and a terminal and a man from a regional office, and the thread was live under the floor he was standing on and had been live since ten past eight, and he had not used it and was not going to use it until ten past six on Thursday morning, and he had told a woman in a corridor what he was going to do, and she had said yes, and she had also said she was not going to be in it, and both of those were true and the second one had cost her the whole of what she had, and the first one had cost him a night's sleep on Wednesday.
