# Chapter 112 — A Room, As She Asked

Cliff Marner was sixty-five days out of the ground. Della Marner was ninety-five days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was ninety-five days in, and on the Wednesday morning of the tenth of December the rule Elias Rook had said out loud in a kitchen in Cutbank Row on the Sunday had one day left to run on it, and he did not tell anybody that, and the day was a Wednesday because everything since the sixth of September had been days that were not chosen.

The room was off the canteen at the works yard and it had a table and four chairs and a window that looks at a wall, and it was on a board by the door with the hours written on it and a line for the name of the person who had booked it, and it was free at half past nine on a Wednesday, and Elias Rook put his own name in the line, which he had not done in three weeks and which cost him nothing and which is the whole of what this chapter is about.

The woman from the canteen brought two teas without being asked and did not stay.

---

Nerys Hobbs was forty-seven and had a hard hat under her arm and drawings under the other one, and she shut the door behind her, which nobody had asked her to do and which was the first thing that told Elias Rook he had come to the right room.

"Right," she said. "Say the thing you are supposed to say first, and then ask me your question, and then I will decide how much of it I am answering."

Elias Rook stood up, which he did not need to do.

"Before I say anything else, I am going to tell you that I have not told this room what I am about to do, and that I am telling you now and not afterwards. Then I am going to stop talking for about a minute and then I am going to ask."

She looked at him for a second.

"That is a real one," she said. "Nobody has said one of those to me in this building since I came here. Fourteen months. The consultants do not do it and the contractor does not do it and my own clerk does not do it because I trained her out of it in October and never told her why." She put the hard hat on the chair. "Ask."

---

He asked for the setting-out.

"I am not going to give you the setting-out."

He had known that in about eleven seconds and had asked anyway, and the reason he had asked anyway was that he had come two miles and eleven minutes for it.

"Sit down," Hobbs said. "I will tell you what I would have said if you had not asked and then I will tell you the actual reason, and the actual reason is not that you are a stranger and I have got a rule about strangers."

She took a drawing out from under the other arm and did not open it.

"I build to a bearing. That is the job. The bearing comes off our setting-out and the setting-out comes off a survey in 2039 that the board has adjusted twice and has put a printed slip on the gate about, and the slip is correct, and the slip is the best thing anybody in that program has done for public confidence and I mean that as somebody who has spent fourteen months being asked whether I trust it."

"And the pilot's sheet."

"I have never seen it. Not once. It has never been in my hands and it has never been in this room, and nobody has ever sent it to me, and I have never asked for it, because I did not know it existed until you were in this district, and if you want to know why I did not ask for it it is that nobody tells me things before the Monday." She put the drawings down. "So there are two documents in this district that decide where a wall goes. One of them is mine and it is a line on a board and it comes off a survey. The other one is a band drawn by a man I have never met, and it lives in a filing system, and I am not going to be able to tell you which one of us is right and I am not going to be able to tell you which one of us is wrong, and I am also not going to be able to tell you whether they are talking about the same street, because nobody has ever put the two of us in a room, and the reason nobody has is that there is no form for it, and the absence of the form is not anybody's fault, and I have stopped being able to say that sentence out loud in about November and now I only say it in rooms."

Elias Rook did not write any of it down.

"That is about my instrument," Hobbs said, looking at him. "I want to be exact about that, because you are a man who writes things down. I know where the wall is. I do not know where the ground is. Those are two different pieces of knowledge and I have got one of them and there is no version of the next four months where I get the other one, and I have stopped apologizing for it. Do not take that any further than that, because if you take it into a room as a thing about this district then I will have given you a sentence I do not know the meaning of."

"Understood."

"Good. That is the actual reason in the first half." She took her tea. "The other half of the actual reason is a yard on Saturday morning with about thirty men in it and a man with a folder who opened it and then stopped in the middle of a sentence and put it away, and told nine people he had no forum and that they did not have to answer him. You were right. You were more right than you knew, because if you had said the number in that yard it would have gone into about forty kitchens by Monday, and there is not one person in this district with any obligation to answer you, and if you had been wrong about the number then you would have been the only man in the country who could take it back."

"I was not wrong about the number."

"I did not say you were wrong. I said you had no forum, and no forum is not a thing a number can carry." She put the cup down. "Give me a room, a date, a piece of paper with a reference on it and somebody to write it down, and I will give you the setting-out and every chainage on it, and I will put my name on it, and I will not enjoy it."

"Then give me the room."

"You do not have a room," Hobbs said. "That is not an insult, it is the reason I can do it. You are a man standing in a district with a standing request for an observer at a counter and no forum and no document with a reference on it that anybody has to answer, and the day you have one of those three things I will hand you the setting-out myself and I will not send it by anybody."

---

The clock on the canteen wall said twenty past ten and Elias Rook said the thing he had come for, and he said it badly, and it was the second worst question of his three weeks and he knew it was the second worst one while it was in his mouth.

"Miss Hobbs. The clearance at three to twenty-seven Sallow Court ran on the nights of the eighth and ninth of November. I have read the notice on the gate and it says the clearance was complete and had nothing outstanding, and it is dated the ninth, and it is the only record of it in the world that anybody has shown me."

"I know there was a clearance."

"Your yard ran it."

"My yard moved the furniture. That is not the same and you know it is not the same, and I am not going to have that in a conversation where I do not know who else is in it." She looked at the door. "This room has a door and a corridor on the other side of it and about forty people in that building, so I will say one thing and I will say it once. My clerk signed off the clearance against the reading. She signed it because the reading said the block was inside and the reading was the thing we worked to. I did not sign it and I have not signed anything since 2036 and there is a form about that."

"You could see the reading."

"I could see the roll. Not the schedule. **The schedule is dated the second of December.**"

Elias Rook pressed his right palm to his own knee, below the table edge, and felt the whole week turn over.

"Which means," she said, "that on the eighth of November the works was clearing a block to something that did not exist yet."

---

She let that sit for about four seconds, and she was not doing it for effect, and then she did the thing that made her the second most useful person in the district.

"So I am going to ask you for one thing and I am going to be exact about the shape of it, because I have been carrying this since November and I have not got anybody to ask."

"Ask."

"I do not want a document. Documents are no good to me, I have got the setting-out and it is the only thing I am allowed to build to. I want a sentence, said out loud, in a room, by somebody who is allowed to say it, about what this works was working to on the eighth of November, and whether the line moved after it was given to us. Not off. I am not asking to be told we were wrong. I am asking to be told whether the ground changed under us between the day we cleared a block and the day the district was told which addresses were in it, and if it did, I want it said in a room with the door shut and written down in words that whoever heard it will not be embarrassed by in three years."

Elias Rook did not say anything at all for long enough that she looked at him.

"Say something," Hobbs said, "because I have just asked for something and it is on the table."

"You have asked for a fact."

"I have asked for a fact out loud in a room."

"That is the same thing."

"It is not remotely the same thing and you know exactly why it is not, and you have known since about a week ago, and I have watched you get there over a fortnight and it has been the most interesting thing that has happened in this yard since the twenty-ninth of November." She picked up her drawings. "There. That is the whole of what I have got. There is a form for a document and there is no form for that, and I have asked four people since November and three of them sent me a form."

---

He walked her out to the gate, which was the opposite direction from the bus, and it cost him eleven minutes.

At the corner of the yard approach he said the thing that had been in him since the Sunday, because the rule had a day left on it and the room had been the right room and she had not asked once.

"There is something in a margin in a kitchen in Cutbank Row and I have told nobody in this district and I am not going to use it for four days from Sunday and there is one day left on it, and I have just told you that I have it, and I am telling you because you have earned it and not because I have decided to."

"I did not ask," said Nerys Hobbs.

"That is correct," said Elias Rook, and got the 11 at twenty past eleven with a day left on a rule he had set for himself in somebody else's kitchen, and a request in his own notebook that was not a document and had a reference on it that nobody was obliged to answer.

He wanted somebody in this city to say out loud, in a room, the true thing about a line and a list of addresses. There is a man who could say it in four sentences and he is sixty-four and he is not available and he is not going to be asked. The woman who had just shut a door in a room on purpose had told him the truest thing she had, and it was not that, and the reason it was not that is that she had a job on Monday and he had a notebook and neither of them was the thing he needed.

There is no version of a Tuesday that produces a sentence in a room. He had now spent eleven days finding that out and he had spent about nine pounds of fares.
