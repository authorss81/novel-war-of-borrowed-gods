# Chapter 104 — A Designation

The depot on the far bank runs on Tuesdays and Fridays at half past six and the Tuesday slot is his and has been his since the end of October, and there is a hose on the far wall with a pressure gauge on it and a board with two columns, and R. Dunleavy is fifty-nine and does not do the talking part of it.

Cliff Marner was fifty-seven days out of the ground. Della Marner was eighty-seven days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was eighty-seven days in, and the designation that was signed at a quarter to eight that morning was about to make that last figure permanent in a way nobody in the building had intended, and nobody was going to be told about it in a corridor, and a corridor is where it got told.

Elias Rook was there at ten to six, which is what he does, and the hose passed at nineteen hundred and forty and the gauge settled at one hundred and six, and Dunleavy wrote two figures in a book in his own hand and turned the book round so the page faced the man who had brought the sheet, and said nothing at all.

Twenty minutes. It had been twenty minutes every Tuesday since the end of October, and the Tuesday last week had been on it, and this was not a return and not a first and there was nobody there to be impressed by it.

It was the first thing in thirteen weeks that had gone right in front of him and had not been him, and he did not make anything of it, and he rode back over the bridge in the dark and was at Ash Street for ten to eight with the week's inventory sheet in a satchel and a bus change in his coat.

---

Inez Sato was in the front room at ten to nine and she had her coat on.

"Sit down," she said.

"I have got until eleven before—"

"Sit down. Tidegate was designated at about a quarter to eight this morning. It is in a notice on the board at the Meridian and it is on the fence at the yard and the woman in the vest gave a man a leaflet about it at about nine yesterday and did not think twice." She took the leaflet out of her bag and put it on the table face down. "It is signed by a Director. It is correct. It is the thing that makes the pilot lawful, and without it the whole of what is happening in that district is a man with a clipboard."

"And the designation is what makes you legal," Elias Rook said, "and it is also the thing that puts your own line into effect."

"Yes."

"How long have you known."

"Since a quarter to eight. I have been in since seven. I did not telephone you because you were asleep and because you would have come in a car and I would rather you came on a bus with everybody else." She put her hands in her coat pockets. "I am going to say the cost of you being over there out loud before either of us pays it, and then I am going to stop saying it, and I am not going to be soft about it and I do not want a thank you for it.

"Eleven minutes. Twice a day, and you will go four days and you will not see anybody in this building for four days, and you are not the only man who has anything else to be doing, and the two of you are taking a day each out of a building that has twenty-eight people in it and none of them are paid for this. Say the number out loud."

"Eight days of travel between the two of us," Elias Rook said, "and about twenty-two pounds."

"Say it like that to Joon Park and not to me, because he will work it out and he will not ask, and if I hear it worked out I will have to look after him about it."

She picked up her bag.

"What are you doing."

"I am going to tell him."

"Tomas Vale is not available as a structural authority on a designated site, in the past tense, forever, in a box with a form in it, and that box got a job at a quarter to eight this morning, and nobody in this building was going to telephone a man who cannot be asked, and I have been standing in this corridor for twenty minutes deciding not to be the person who did not." She got the strap off her shoulder. "I am not going to take a message. I am going to stand here with him for eleven minutes and then I am going to come back and tell you one sentence, and if you want to know what he said you are going to have to ask him, and you are not going to, and that is the correct arrangement and it is his, not mine."

She went down the corridor. Elias Rook sat at the table in the front room and did not follow her, and could hear the two of them through the door because the door does not close properly and neither of them raised their voice at any point.

He could not hear a word.

---

She was gone eleven minutes.

"He says the designation is correct," Inez Sato said, "and he says he put himself in that box in November on purpose and the box has now got a job and he is not going to be the man who takes it back out because it is inconvenient. And he says two more things and I am going to give you both of them because I said one sentence and I am not going to cheat.

"He says you are not to come and see him about it. And he says that if you come and see him about it, it will be the one time in this whole business that he is rude to you, and he would rather have that on a Tuesday than have you turn up on a Friday."

Elias Rook said thank you and meant it and did not say anything else, and Inez Sato said, "Do not," and went out.

---

The letter was on the table when he came back in at about five to eleven, and it was one side of a sheet, and it had been typed, and it had come in a hand, and there was no name on it beyond a clerk's.

> **HAVENPORT TIDAL BOARD — Fourth Stage Tidal Defense, Tidegate.**
> **Enquiries regarding the alignment area should be addressed to the board's surveyor, whose office is at Civic buildings, third floor. Requests to attend site, to observe works, or to be consulted on structural matters are not accepted and are referred. The board has no procedure for a third category and does not intend to build one during an active alignment.**

Elias Rook read it three times and could not find a sentence in it that was unkind and could not find a sentence in it that was anything other than blunt.

There is no procedure for a third category. He put his thumb on that line and thought about a man he had met once in a records room in November who had said a thing almost exactly like it and had been right, and he thought about a wall on Ash Street with a column on it headed *items* where it should say *people*, and he thought about a box with a form in it, and he understood that he was in a building where the entire technique was a field that did not exist, and that the entire difference between that building and a form with a third category on it was who was standing in the room when the category was decided.

That was the whole of what he learned about Tidegate on Monday the first of December and it had not taken him into the district.

---

Mara Okafor was outside the health center at twenty to one with her coat open and a look on her face that said she had eleven minutes.

"You are not to tell me what you have found," she said, "and I am not going to tell you what I have found, and I have found one thing and it is not a moral thing so I will tell you that one.

"There is a chemist on Marden Row who does a weekly delivery round to about forty of the older residents in this district out of his own van, and he has been doing it since March, and last Thursday he was told that a transitional address cannot be billed against the district, and he has not stopped, and he is doing it out of his own money, and he is going to stop in about the middle of January because he is fifty-eight and there is an amount of his own money that ends. **That is my question and it is a real question and I have asked it of four people this morning and got three different answers and one of them was yours is the only one that was honest.** I am telling you because you are going to be in a room with people who can make it go away, and I am not, and I am not going to pretend to you that this is a small thing, because in January it is going to be a very large thing for about sixty people and none of them will know why it happened."

"Mara—"

"Four o'clock is what I said and I mean it. Go and do your day."

---

The counter on the ground floor of number six was open on a Tuesday afternoon and the woman behind it had a name card and a card that said what her role was, and her role was *enquiries*, and her role was a thing you could ask a question of.

"Is there anybody we are allowed to speak to," Elias Rook said, "about the schedule."

"There is a named observer clause." She said it the way she would have said *there is a bus stop*. "Any visiting party in a district with a designated pilot gets a named observer with a hand on the log, and it is the same for everybody, and it is not a favor. Do you want me to put you on for this afternoon or for a standing request?"

"For a standing request."

"I'll need a name and an organization and a reason you are asking."

He gave her all three and she typed them and did not ask him to prove any of them.

Then she said, "Mr. Rook, the observer clause came in under the pilot authorization in August and it is a regional form, and it is in every cell that has one of these running, and I am not being difficult, I am telling you what kind of door you are standing in."

Joon Park, who had been standing four feet away looking at the leaflet rack, said nothing at all.

He said nothing in daylight, at half past two on a Tuesday, in a voice that anybody in that shop could hear, for about four full seconds, and then he said:

"Mr. Park has noticed something about the shape of a clause and is not going to say what it is, and I would like it written down that he noticed it and did not say it, and I am not going to explain the not-saying to a room that has not asked me for an explanation."

The woman at the counter said, "Certainly," and typed it, and did not ask, and that was the correct professional behavior and it was the single most generous thing anybody in that building did for him all day.

---

The corridor outside the survey office at the works yard is eleven meters long and has a door at each end and a window that looks at a wall.

Elias Rook stood in the middle of it at ten past three on Tuesday the second of December, the eighty-seventh day, with a folder under his right arm, and waited for Joon Park to finish being legible, and then said one sentence to a wall, out loud, because there was nobody else in the corridor and he had been wrong since Sunday and had not been told.

"Nobody in that building is doing anything crooked and there is nothing for me to hold."

Then he stood there for another four minutes, because the office at the end of the corridor was where a determination would eventually be made about a district, and he was not going to be let into it, and nobody had done a single thing to stop him, and the whole of it was a form that had been correct on the day it was written and had never had a field for the day it was written on.
