# Chapter 117 — Sent Back Twice

Cliff Marner was seventy days out of the ground. Della Marner was one hundred days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was one hundred days in, and Elias Rook got the 11 at ten past eight on Monday morning, the fifteenth of December, with a promise made in a church hall a week before waiting on the day.

The next Monday after the eighth was the fifteenth. The fourteenth was a Sunday. He had checked it twice, because a hundred that fell on a Monday wanted checking, and day one hundred fell here and not on any earlier page. The inside back cover of his notebook had two dates on it in his own hand. 2 January. 16 January. The back pages of the notebook had one line written on them in all and it was the near end. None of that was today. Today was the working folder with the drafts in it and the September overlay and the thing Ferrand had sent back twice, offered in public with two hundred people listening, unanswered, with Elias saying thank you because it was the only sentence available.

The left hand was gone and it made Mondays slow. He buttoned his coat with the right hand and teeth, carried the folder he already had under the right arm, and kept the tube against his side on the bus. The right knuckles had loosened overnight and could hold the rail if he was careful. The plaster on the second knuckle was new.

---

Number six was a shop that sold door furniture with the cell's two rooms above it and a public counter at the back, and the counter opened at nine, Monday to Friday, nine to half past four. The woman at the counter had a name card and a role card and the role was enquiries.

"You are on the list," she said. "For an observer. Standing request of the first of December. There is not one free today."

"I am here for Monday," Elias Rook said. "Mr. Ferrand said Monday in a hall."

"I know he did," she said. "Everybody in this district knows he did. It is upstairs."

Selwyn Anum was forty-one and had the laptop closed and the kettle cold and a cardboard folder on the trestle that had not been there on Friday. It was thicker than the nineteen-page schedule, with a rubber band round it, and a transparent overlay rolled beside it with a date on the corner in a clerk's hand.

"Before you ask," Anum said, "I am going to tell you what it is and what it is not, because a man who was promised a thing in front of two hundred people deserves to be told which thing he is getting."

He took the rubber band off with two hands.

"The drafts," he said, laying out three sheets with crossings-out in two inks. "The September overlay, which is the eleventh of September drawn so it can go over the schedule. And the thing he sent back twice."

The thing was one page, from the regional office to the cell's method officer, dated the first of December 2042, listing the forty-four addresses with a proposed status change from one word to another and a box for the method officer's check. It had been stamped and returned twice, once dated the first and once dated the second, with the same annotation in Anum's own hand in the check box both times. A schedule is drawn once. Re-issue on the next occasion. At the foot, printed by the regional office in the same body type as the letterhead, a tally line.

Elias read the two numbers and did the subtraction in his head without writing it down. The schedule on his knee said two hundred and eighty-five of one word and four hundred and twelve of the other, out of six hundred and ninety-seven. The printed line at the foot of the single page said two hundred and forty-one and four hundred and fifty-six, out of the same six hundred and ninety-seven. The difference was forty-four. Nobody in that room said either number out loud, because the numbers were printed and did not need saying, and anybody going through any page about this district was left to do the subtraction, which was the district's own method.

"The next occasion is the fourth," Anum said, "and it is January, date not yet fixed, because the date has to be inside the contractor's window and the contractor has not given it. That is the reason it is not fixed and it is the only reason."

Elias put his right forearm on the overlay to stop it curling and looked at the drafts. They were a measurement being reduced to an operational document, correctly, with a footnote in italics on the second page of the schedule. The band is indicative. The schedule is the operative document. Where a reading and a schedule differ, the schedule governs.

"You sent it back twice," Elias Rook said.

"I sent it back twice," Anum said. "Once on the first and once on the second. The same box both times. A schedule is drawn once. If you draw it every time the band moves you are rehoming four hundred people in an afternoon and you do it badly, because there is no version of an afternoon that is not bad. I have not slept properly since the twenty-ninth of November. That is not a defense and it is not an excuse. It is the reason the folder is what it is."

He put the rubber band back on the folder that was not the verification log and did not shorten the six weeks, and pushed it across the trestle with one hand.

Elias laid the September overlay over the schedule with his right forearm holding the edge, because the left could not hold anything and the overlay curled if it was not held. The two lines were four inches apart along two streets and on top of each other everywhere else. He counted the difference the way Anum had taught him on the Thursday, with a finger, one at a time, without putting the schedule on the same table as the rolls. Forty-four addresses along two streets and not a blob. Every one of them said the same word on the schedule of the second of December. None said the other word. The drafts showed the conversion done correctly, band to occupancy class to status rule, with the date the day it was done and not the day the ground moved.

"You counted it yourself on Thursday," Anum said. "Count it again today if you want. Everybody gets it the wrong way round the first time."

"I counted forty-four on Thursday," Elias Rook said. "I am not going to count it again today to make it a different number."

"Good," Anum said. "That is the only thing in this room that is not going to change between now and January. The band will move again on the next occasion and the schedule will still say what it says, because a schedule records a state and a state has no history. Do not take that any further than that, because if you take it into a room as a thing about this district then I will have given you a sentence I do not know the meaning of."

"I am telling you what I am about to do before I do it," Elias Rook said, because it was Monday and the room was Anum's. "I am going to take this folder to Ash Street and show it to the man who keeps our custody schedule, and I am going to tell him what is in it in the words it was said in before I act on it."

"You do tell people," Anum said. "Take it. A man on a bus is the reason things get damaged, so keep it in the tube."

---

He carried it in the tube on the 11 with the folder he already had under the right arm, because a man of forty-one had told him on a Monday that the reason things get damaged was somebody carrying them onto a bus, and the characterization was not a quote and did not need to be one. The thread went quiet over the water and live again on the far side, faint and exact, the way it had been live on Sallow Court and in the room above number six and on the gate pier. It was live when he was standing on ground he was being asked about and off everywhere else. It did not tell him where anybody was.

At Ash Street Joon Park laid the drafts on the table end to end with the two rolls Elias already carried, and did not touch the single page with the two returns on it except to read the printed line at the foot.

"Twenty-two items," Joon Park said, with the custody schedule in front of him. "This does not become a twenty-third, because I do not hold the folder and I do not hold the rolls. I am writing down that I saw it, with the date in the margin, in my own hand."

He wrote it down as it was said, on the same sheet as the other twenty-two lines, in the margin where he put dates. The four names on the back of his hand were fainter now, eleven days after the Sunday he had written them, and he put nothing on the schedule and gave them no number.

Petar Lisk stood at the board with a pencil behind his ear and said there was still no room, not for a line and not for a name, and that a man who put one up there anyway would be doing it for himself and not for the three questions. The three questions were untouched, the second half of the oldest still a bracket with nothing under it, the nineteenth of November still the only date. Elias stood in the doorway with the tube against his leg and the left hanging empty, and listened, because the folder he had waited a week for changed nothing about a wall that was full.

That night the district learned nothing new, because a folder with drafts in it was not a finding and a tally line with two numbers on it was not a sentence. The working folder had arrived, with a scene, on the Monday it was promised for, and it did not shorten the six weeks by a day.

He rode home on the 11 at ten past six with eleven minutes each way and two pounds seventy-five a fare, and the tube against his leg and the folder under his right arm, and the left hanging empty. Inez Sato met him at the door and asked what it had cost and he said sixteen days of travel between two men and about eighty-eight pounds in fares, and she said say it like that to Joon Park and not to her, because he would work it out and not ask. The hall held twenty-eight people and twenty-two cards, and the four dark hours came at midnight, and nothing in the folder added a household to Ash Street.
