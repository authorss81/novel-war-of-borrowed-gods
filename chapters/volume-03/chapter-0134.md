# Chapter 134 — Two Times On A Page And Nothing Between Them

Cliff Marner was eighty-seven days out of the ground. Della Marner was one hundred and seventeen days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was one hundred and seventeen days in, and Elias Rook was on the north corner of Marlow Street at five past six on the morning of Thursday the first of January, and the yard was open, and the counter at number six was open at nine, and the board was open, and nobody in that district had shut anything for the day, and nobody in that district was going to.

He got there on the first bus that ran and his palm was on the brick at ten past six, at the low wall on the corner where the alignment came up out of the ground and turned toward the Tannery Cut, and the left hand was in his pocket doing nothing, which was its job.

The street shut at twenty past six. The barriers went up at six twenty from both ends and a man in a high-visibility vest walked the length of the alignment with a board under his arm, and the reader came out of the depot at ten past seven and went down Marlow Street at four miles an hour with its mast up, and nobody was allowed on that street until it had been through.

Nobody had asked him to be there, and it was going to make no difference to a single number that came off that vehicle, and Selwyn Anum had told him so on Monday so that he would not spend the morning doing arithmetic about what he was for.

Three of the fields were empty. There was no gift in it and nothing in the return, and the boundary was the whole of what came back, in a shape a printer would have put on a form if a printer had known how.

He had asked for that, and had told a woman in a corridor on Tuesday that he was going to ask and had stopped and counted, and she had said yes at three minutes and had also said she was not going to be in it.

He took his hand off the wall. There was nothing in the air in front of him and nothing in his hands and nothing to carry up the street, and the barriers came up at six twenty, and he was on the pavement on the public side with his glove back on, and he did not write anything down, because there was nothing to write down that a man in a room could use, and he had been told that in a room in December and it had been a kindness and it had also been true.

---

The room above number six was free from eight. Nerys Hobbs came at eight with her coat on. A clerk from the regional office came at ten to nine with a laptop shut and a folder. Hollis Vane came at a quarter past nine, and he was about sixty and had been the custodian of exhibits to a board of inquiry for nineteen years, and he had a train from the Meridian at half past six and had come on purpose, and he said so in the first thirty seconds.

"The copy of the schedule went into my file on the seventeenth of December in an envelope with a board query on it," Vane said, "and a copy that goes into my file is an exhibit, and an exhibit is superseded by a document that says which document it supersedes and on what date it takes effect. That is the whole of the rule and I have written it out myself and I have never had to use it." He put his hat on the table. "I am not here about Tidegate. I am here about a folder in the Meridian with a copy of a document in it that says the fourth of March in a year and a page count and a program reference and no date on the copy at all, because a copy of a document carries the date it went into a file and not the date it was made, and the date this one went into my file is the seventeenth of December, and the seventeenth of December is the day a clerk opened an envelope."

He looked around the room.

"So before anybody in this room does anything with anything, I would like to know what the document in my folder is a picture of."

---

At about a quarter to eleven Selwyn Anum came in with a roll of printout and did not sit down, and stood at the end of the trestle and unrolled about two feet of it and held the end of it down with his coffee cup.

"The reader was on the alignment at a quarter past seven and off it at ten to eight," he said. "The check box is signed. Occasion four. This is a copy of the output and I am going to read three things off it and I am not going to talk about the other two hundred lines."

Nobody said anything.

"The securing band is where it was on the twenty-ninth of November plus a difference," Anum said. "It has come off Rennard and off Cutbank Row and gone two streets back the way it came in September, and the edge on both of those streets is now clear of every address on the sheet."

Hobbs had been holding her clipboard against her chest with both hands and put it down on the chair behind her.

"Write that down," she said, "and write it in your own hand, because in February the only sentence in this district anybody is going to believe will come out of the hand of the man whose name is on the slip. I have signed a great many sheets in my life and nobody has ever believed one of them on its own."

"Forty-four," Elias Rook said.

"Forty-four," Anum said. "Every one of them a `RETAINED` on the second of December, every one of them on Rennard or on Cutbank Row, and not one of them anywhere else. I want that in the room before anybody says the word problem, because I have been up since four and I am not having a word put on this in front of a clerk until somebody has said what the word is."

---

It took until about half past twelve to find out what to do with it, and what they found out was that there was nothing to do with it.

The sheet Anum had been sent in December came out of the folder, one page, forty-four addresses with a proposed change and a check box, returned twice and annotated twice in his own hand, *A schedule is drawn once. Re-issue on the next occasion.* The next occasion had a date on it. He filled the check box in at ten past one and dated the reissue the first of January, and the reissue was one page with a program reference and a signature block, and it did not withdraw anything.

At the foot of the December sheet, in the type of the regional office, was a printed line.

`IF ACTIONED — TRANSITIONAL 241 · RETAINED 456`

Nobody said either number out loud. Nobody crossed the line out, because it was printed by an office a long way away and it had been true on the day it was printed for a movement that had not happened, and there was no hand in the room with the standing to do that to another office's printed figure.

"That line is going to be in that file for forty years," Anum said, "and in about thirty of them it is going to be the only thing on the page anybody reads, and I am not going to be the man who scratches it out, and I would like that said in a room while I am in it."

---

Hollis Vane had not said anything since half past nine. He was sitting with his hat on his knee, and when the clerk asked him whether the reissue page was enough to supersede the copy in his folder, he took about ten seconds.

"No," he said. "It is not, and I want to be exact about why, because the why is not about Tidegate and I have come a long way and I am not going to go back with something vague."

He put the hat on the table.

"I have kept documents for nineteen years," he said. "That is the whole of my trade. And every one of them is a picture of a day. A photograph of a street with a date on the back is worth having and I could not do my job without them, and I have never once been asked to do the other thing, which is tell somebody what a picture was a picture of on a day that was not the day it was taken. There is no field for that anywhere in this Office. Not on this, not on mine, not on anything a form has ever asked me to keep. And I want to be careful, because I have watched four good people be blamed for that and it was never their doing. The man who drew the picture did not have to know what the light was going to do. A picture of a day is not supposed to want to know about any other day. Nobody designed a form to be asked that, because nobody ever thought anybody would come and ask."

"And who is that," Nerys Hobbs said. "Who is the one who does not have it."

"The cabinet," Hollis Vane said. "I am the cabinet. I have got a folder in the Meridian with a copy of a document in it that says what the light was on the second of December, and the copy does not know the day the light was taken, because a copy of a document gets the day it was put in a file, and that is the seventeenth of December, and the seventeenth of December is not a day anybody measured anything on. So the folder has a thing in it that is not wrong and cannot be checked, and the copy upstairs on your wall is a different thing that is right, and there is no version history on either of them, and that is nobody's fault and it is the whole of what is going to happen to a district that keeps asking which document governs."

There was a second thing in that room which had been there since November, and it was on page two of a document in a folder two miles away, and it was not what Hollis Vane had just said, and Elias Rook did not say it and was glad afterwards that he had not.

---

The clerk wanted to know what to type. Hobbs said the works would build to the schedule until somebody told her otherwise, because the schedule was the operative document and the only one with a date nobody had argued with. Anum said he was not going to withdraw the reissue and was not going to leave it out of the file, and that a schedule is a document and he did not know what else it was. And nobody in that room said one word about how any of it had happened, and neither did Elias Rook, and he noticed that twice and did nothing about it.

At ten past four he asked the only question he had asked all day, and he asked it because a panel with three empty fields in it had come back with no return condition in it and there was a return condition somewhere else with a date on it in December.

"What time was the reader on the alignment," he said.

"A quarter past seven," Anum said. "Off it at ten to eight. It is on the slip."

"And what time was I on the ground."

Anum looked at him for a second.

"You were on the ground before the street shut," he said. "The barrier went up at six twenty and you were on the pavement on the north side with your hand off the wall before the reader came out of the depot. There is a foreman's man on that gate from six twenty and he writes down everything, and it is in his book, and I am not going to read his book out in a room because it is his."

Elias Rook got the 11 at ten past four and sat on the far side of the cut with the roll of printout folded in the tube under his right arm, and on the inside back cover of the notebook, under two dates that were still the second and the sixteenth of January, he wrote two times.

He looked at them for a while on the bus and then put the notebook away, and he did not write one word between the two of them and was not going to, and there was nobody in that building he could have said it to, and he had told a woman on Tuesday that he was going to ask and had been told yes and had also been told that she was not going to be in it, and both of those had been true and one of them had cost her everything she had and the other had cost him a page with nothing in the middle of it.
