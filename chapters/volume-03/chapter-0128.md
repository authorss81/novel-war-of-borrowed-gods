# Chapter 128 — The Two Dates And A Sheet With Four Rows On It

Cliff Marner was eighty-one days out of the ground. Della Marner was one hundred and eleven days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was one hundred and eleven days in, and Elias Rook was on Ash Street on Friday morning, the twenty-sixth of December, because it was Boxing Day and the yard was shut and the counter was shut and the board was shut, and all three of them had said so on paper rather than to anybody.

The cell's notice was a printed slip in a plastic holder screwed to the inside of the door at number six and had been put there some time in December.

`CLOSED 25, 26, 27, 28 DEC · REOPENS MON 29 DEC 09:00`

He read it standing in the cold on a doorstep for about as long as it took to be sure of it, and there was nothing behind it, no exception, no arrangement, and the person who had printed it had not thought about him at all, which was correct.

The depot's notice was still on the fridge at number six in the plastic sleeve. The Tuesday column had his name in it. The Friday column had a tick in it, and the tick was a depot clerk's tick and not a cancellation, and there was no name in it and there had not been one since October. Friday the twenty-sixth was a depot Friday and it was the inventory half and it was empty in his week, and it was empty in his week on Boxing Day the same as it was empty in his week on any other Friday, and that was a fact about a rota and not about a holiday, and the notice said so in the sense that a notice never says anything at all about him.

---

The post came at about half past ten and the woman who brought it up the stairs had four things for the building and one of them was for him, and it was one side, and it was on the regional letterhead of the Bellwether Pilot Program, and it was typed, and it had a signature block at the foot with a name printed under it.

He read it standing up in the corridor. Then he took it into the front room and read it out loud, because that is the practice now, and because a man who reads a document from a long way away to himself in a building of twenty-eight people is a man who has decided what it means before anybody has heard it.

> **BELLWETHER PILOT PROGRAM · REGIONAL OFFICE**
>
> The pilot authorization carries a date in August and it stands. The method it authorizes is not withdrawn and nothing in this letter is a criticism of the method.
>
> The Retention Schedule of 2 December is to be reduced to an operational list. A schedule is a document about what is where on a particular day. An operational list is a document about what a crew does next. The program is going to hold the first and stop producing the second.
>
> The headings `SECURED SUBJECT` and `SUBJECT HELD` are program headings. A gift name is a gift name. Neither is a finding about anybody and neither will appear in any document produced under this authorization.
>
> The verification log for the cell's district was requested at a regional counter on 5 December. The service quotes a near end of 2 January and a far end of 16 January. Nothing will be produced before those dates and no expedite is available on a request of this series.
>
> The log is the only document held by this office that can answer the question the Havenport Tidal Board's surveyor asked on 16 December. I am not able to tell you today whether the answer will be in it.
>
> **Tobias Ferrand, Regional Director**

Joon Park listened to the whole of it with his hands in his coat sleeves.

"Say the dates again," he said, when Elias Rook had finished.

"Two January and the sixteenth of January," Elias Rook said.

"Thank you," Joon Park said. "I have now been told in a room, on a public holiday, by a man from a long way away, and I have written the two dates on the inside of my left forearm, and I want it on the record that the request stands and that nothing has come back and that nobody in this building has chased it and that nobody in this building is going to."

"Agreed," Elias Rook said.

"You are not going to reduce it either," Joon Park said. "I want to say that out loud while there are two other people here. You have been in this business for thirteen weeks and every single thing you have come home with has been a piece of paper that somebody else had already drawn, and now a program has told you in writing that it is going to take a document about where people are and turn it into a document about what a crew does, and my honest expectation is that you will go quiet and thoughtful for a fortnight and come back with a page in your notebook that is very good."

"That is what I would have done in November," Elias Rook said.

"I know it is. I am not accusing you of anything. I am asking you to remember in January that a man in a regional office said it in a letter on Boxing Day, and that it is in this building, and that we did not have to find it out."

Ru Sabar said, from the doorway, that the heat in that room had been off since two o'clock and that somebody was going to have to do something about it before four, and then she went and did something about it, which is how that building has worked since September.

---

Petar Lisk came in from the hall with a book under his arm and Ru Sabar behind him, and the book was the file of working logs from the records room, which had been upstairs in a box on a top shelf since before any of them had been in that building, and he put it on the table and did not open it.

"Thursday you said nobody was going to do anything about a box on a shelf," Ru Sabar said.

"Thursday I said it," Petar Lisk said. "And on Friday morning a man in an office on the far side of this city put it in writing, on his own letterhead, that a program is going to stop producing one kind of document and start producing another, and that is a man in an office and not me, and I have never in my life been able to leave a thing alone once a person has written down that they are doing it."

He opened the box. There were eleven working logs in it, and the 1998 one was at the bottom with a spine label that had gone the color of tea, and Petar put his hand on it and left it there for about a second and then took his hand off, and did not take the book out.

"That is it," he said. "And I am not opening it at a table with nine people in the room, and nobody is opening it in this building who does not have a reason to, and I would like it written down that the reason it is on this table is that a letter came, and not that a man of thirty-five came home and looked interested."

"Somebody will have a reason by the middle of January," Joon Park said. "That is how it goes. A box comes upstairs and then somebody upstairs has a bad month, or a case, or a question from an office, and a reason turns up, and the reason is always better than the man who wanted to open it, and the file goes back on the shelf with one page out of order in it, and forty years later there is a woman writing a book who finds the page and cannot tell which way round the pages went."

"That is a very good argument for never opening it," Petar Lisk said, "and it is also an argument for being extremely careful about who it is that opens it, and I intend to keep the list of both, and neither list is going in the custody schedule."

Elias Rook laid two fingers on the cover of the box for about two seconds and took them off, and the cold of the cardboard went into his palm and nothing at all came up through it, because the ground under Ash Street is not the ground anybody is being asked about, and the thread had been off in that building since the fourteenth of December and it was off now, and there was nothing in the box and there was nothing in the wood and there was nothing in the room.

He had wanted, for about four seconds, to know whether the 1998 line in that log had been cut out with a blade. He was thirty-five and he had thirteen weeks of it in him and he had a rule about the four minutes and a book on a shelf with four minutes in it, and the four minutes were about a person and not about a document, and there was nobody else in the building to tell.

"I am not going to look at it," he said.

"Noted," Joon Park said, and did not write it down, because a man not looking at a thing is not a custody item and had never been one.

---

The rest of the day was the rest of a day. Inez Sato did the day's list and the day's list had the yard's four nights in it in the works' own words with a crew number against each and no addresses anywhere on it, and she read it out because she read everything out, and then she said the thing that was going to be said in this building and said it once.

"They are going to do the fourth one on a Monday," she said, "which is the nineteenth, and the two before it are on Saturdays, and I do not know why and neither do they, and I have asked, and the answer was that the booking is a booking."

"Nobody is to take a Tidegate address out of this building," Elias Rook said, "and nobody is to take that sheet out of this building, and I will read it out in this room on Sunday and I will say what I think it is and I will not take it any further than this room, and that is the arrangement and it is yours and not mine."

Inez looked up from the lock-box for the first time that day.

"It is a good arrangement," she said, "and you have arrived at it three days late, and you arrived at it because a woman said no to you, and I want that noted in whatever you are keeping, because in a month somebody in a good position is going to offer you the whole of that document, and your own arrangement is going to look like a small thing you gave away."
