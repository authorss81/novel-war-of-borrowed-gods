# Chapter 149 — The Far End Is A Friday

Cliff Marner was one hundred and two days out of the ground. Della Marner was one hundred and thirty-two days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was one hundred and thirty-two days in, and Elias Rook got the 11 at ten past eight on Friday morning, the sixteenth of January, and the counter at number six opened at nine, and the board was open, and the yard was open, and it was the last business day of this winter in this district and everybody in it knew that without being told.

The envelope came at eleven minutes past nine and Wendy Tallow put it on the counter on top of her own book, face up, with the corner of the request form sticking out of it, and there were three dates on the outside of it in three different hands, and the last one was the sixteenth of January, and Joon Park was standing at the end of the counter at about two o'clock in the afternoon with the custody schedule in his coat and did not look at it and was not asked about it by anybody, including Elias Rook.

"There is no signature to check it against," Tallow said. "It is not a witness statement and it is not sworn. It came out of a regional office in a padded envelope and it is the book they said it would be."

---

It is a hundred and ninety-four pages and it is a register of readings and instruments and it is a very good book. Occasion one, the eleventh of September, twenty to seven in the morning, three instruments, a named checker. Occasion two, the twenty-ninth of November, quarter past six, three instruments, a named checker. Occasion three does not exist and there is a line in the book that says so, ruled, with nothing in it.

Occasion four, the first of January, quarter past seven, four instruments, a named checker, output on a trestle at a quarter to eleven.

And in the margin against occasion two, in the same hand as the rest of it, in ink, four words long.

`Schedule raised 02.12 against occ. 1.`

Nobody in the room above number six said anything for about twenty seconds.

"That is the whole of it," Selwyn Anum said. "It is a book about instruments and it has a margin on it, and the margin says a schedule went up on the second of December against the first occasion, and the second occasion was the twenty-ninth of November, and there is no line in that book anywhere that says anybody looked at the second occasion and then looked at the schedule, and I have read one hundred and forty pages of it since eleven o'clock and I have found no such line, and I have wanted one for four months and I have now got a different thing and I do not yet know what it is."

"It does not say anybody was wrong," Elias Rook said.

"No," Anum said. "It does not say that either. It is four words in a margin in a book about instruments, and I wrote a footnote on page two of a document in December that said the band is indicative and the schedule is the operative document, and if I put those two things side by side in front of a board in the next fortnight the board will do with them whatever the law requires, and I am not going to do it, and I want it said in a room while I am in it that I have thought about doing it for about nine seconds and then stopped, and the reason I stopped is that I do not have a second occasion in writing that anybody signed, and the fact that the band moved is a thing I know and not a thing that book says."

---

The hearing room on the third floor of Civic buildings holds about thirty chairs and there were about forty-five people in it and it is smaller than it needs to be.

**Rosalind Treloar** is sixty-one and chairs the Havenport Tidal Board and had signed the order that put the alignment through this district on a day in October without ever once standing on the ground, and she said so in the first thirty seconds, in the room, unprompted, before anybody had asked her anything.

"I approved an alignment on a flood model that has been adjusted twice," Treloar said. "I have never walked the whole of it. I have driven two-thirds of it in a car. The board has three commissioners and two of them are in this room and neither of us is a hydrologist, and there is no form in this building that says a commissioner has to stand on the ground, and there was no such form in October either, and I would like that in the minutes in my own words."

Then they did it, and it took three hours and ten minutes, and the two things that decided it were a form and an instrument.

**Aurelio Bexley**, fifty-four, put the Retention Schedule of the second of December in front of the board and put the page of the verification log with the four words on it next to it, and said one sentence. "This document records one occasion. That record has been applied to three others. There is no field on it for the second, third or fourth, and there is no field on it for a person, and I am not going to say anything else about it because the board does not need me to and the method officer is sitting there with his hands flat."

The board's surveyor, who is still unnamed and has been since the sixteenth of December, read out four dates in about ninety seconds and closed the notebook and said that he had been given four occasions and his query had been for one, and that the difference between one and four was a Tuesday in February in a yard and a document with a date on it, and that the board's own query of the sixteenth of December had been written on the strength of a document dated after the occasion on which the ground had last been looked at, and that he had wanted to know about that in December and had not been able to find anybody who could tell him.

Then he turned the notebook round on the table, which is what a person does when they want somebody in a room to read a thing they have written, and there were two words on that page and one of them was the word he had written in a yard on the fifth of January with a bent drawing pin in his pocket.

*Present. Confirmed.*

---

The determination was read out at four minutes to two and it is on one side of a sheet and the side is the reason the room did not argue about it.

```
HAVENPORT TIDAL BOARD · DETERMINATION
Ref HTB/TD/4SD/DET/1 · 16 January 2043

1. The securing band of 1 January 2043 (occasion 4) is confirmed as the
   current survey of the securing margin and remains in force.

2. The Retention Schedule dated 2 December 2042 (ref BWP/TG/4SD/2042) is
   withdrawn from the date of this determination.

3. Grounds. The Schedule was drawn once, on the reading of 11 September 2042,
   and it records that occasion and no other. Three further occasions are on
   file, and one of them is a night in November and two are in January. The
   footnote at page two directs, in italics, that the band is indicative and
   the Schedule is the operative document. That direction is withdrawn. A
   record of one occasion cannot be applied to another, and an office that
   prefers its record to its instrument preferred it once already, on
   2 December, with forty-four addresses on the wrong side of it.

4. The pilot authorization of August 2042 is not withdrawn. The reading
   continues. No instrument, occasion or register in this district is
   superseded by this determination, and nothing in it affects any other
   district, any other alignment, or any person's occupancy of any address.
```

Nobody in that room said one word about forty-four addresses, and nobody said a second number out loud, and the man who had written the footnote in December did not scratch anything out and did not ask for it to be scratched out, and sat down at ten past two and put his hands in his pockets and let the minute go by.

"The securing band stands," Treloar said, "which means on Monday morning a crew goes onto fourteen to twenty-two Cutbank Row and thirty-one Cutbank Row and empties them, because that is what the band says and the band is the only instrument in this district that has never once been wrong about the ground. Everything else that has happened in this room today is paperwork. I have a piece of paper in my bag that says this district is being defended and it is the size of a postcard, and I have now read the four corners of every document in this city that claims to speak for the ground under it, and the four corners of that piece of paper are the only ones that have ever been about the ground."

Nobody clapped. About four people did, in the ordinary way, and one of them was a man in a works jacket who had been in a hall the night before and had believed a different man, and he clapped because a board had admitted in a room that it had been wrong, and that was a thing nobody in this district had seen before, and it did not fix the four dead people at Sallow Court or the eleven on Rennard Street or the four names in a margin in a kitchen on Cutbank Row, and it was not going to.

---

Outside, on the steps of Civic buildings, at ten past two, in the cold, about nine of them: Selwyn Anum, who had not slept properly since the twenty-ninth of November; Nerys Hobbs, who had a sheet of her own with three of four lines signed and one for Tuesday; Nev Peart, whose booking closed on Thursday the fifth of February and who had been to a board four times in nineteen years and had never once been thanked; Joon Park, twenty-four, with twenty-two items on a sheet; and nobody else, and a surveyor who had gone out of the front door at two o'clock with a hardback notebook under his arm and had not waited on the steps, because in nine years nobody has ever once asked a surveyor to stand on steps with anybody.

"So we won the paper," Elias Rook said, to nobody in particular, on the steps, in the cold.

"We won the paper," Anum said, "and I have been awake for forty-six days and it has taken a regional office a hundred and ninety-four pages and a margin with four words in it, and the four words were written in November by a clerk who was not asked to write a sentence in a margin, and it will be in a file for forty years, and I would like all of you to notice that nobody in that room was cruel and it still cost us four months and two hundred people a night in a hall."

"It is Friday," Joon Park said. "It is half past two on a Friday, and I have not said the date once today and I am not going to, and I want to say one thing about that and then I am going to go and post a letter. Everybody in this city has been waiting all week for that log, and not one of them asked me, including you, and on the sixteenth of December I said it in a room to a man at a counter, and I have said it three times in rooms since and I am not going to say it a fourth time to anybody unless somebody asks me in a room, and today nobody asked me and the thing arrived, and I would like it minuted in a book that a man of thirty-four was standing at a counter on Friday afternoon with his hands in his pockets while it did."

Elias Rook got the 11 at ten past three and there were two people on Cutbank Row with a length of paper and a pencil, four feet from the newsagent at number two, and the fourth column had four names in it in four different hands, and the fifth name on it was in a different pencil and was not the same as the other four, and neither he nor anybody else on that pavement was going to be the one who asked who had put it there.
