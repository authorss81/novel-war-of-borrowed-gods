# Chapter 141 — It Failed In Front Of Everybody

Cliff Marner was ninety-four days out of the ground. Della Marner was one hundred and twenty-four days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was one hundred and twenty-four days in, and Elias Rook got the 11 at ten past seven on Thursday morning, the eighth of January, and the day was a working day inside a booking of twenty-eight and nothing in this district was shut for it.

Twenty-two to thirty-two Rennard is the low side of Rennard, where the ground comes off the bank of the Tannery Cut and goes flat. A tower scaffold went up on the verge at the low end at about a quarter past eight, because a crew had been told to put one up, and a low-loader came in over the soft road at ten to nine, and the whole street took the lorry going past. The thread was on under that block all day and gave him nothing, which was what it did.

---

Nerys Hobbs had the sheet for the Saturday on the trestle and a second sheet on top of it, and the second sheet was not hers.

"Somebody handed this to Peart at five to nine this morning," she said. "It came out of a car. I have read it and I have said out loud to the yard that I have read it, which I have never had to do."

Elias Rook read it over her shoulder. One side, program letterhead, a reference ending in the same six characters as everything else, and two typed lines and no time on either of them.

```
WORKS TASK SHEET · Fourth Stage Tidal Defense · Tidegate
Ref BWP/TG/4SD/2042/OP/1 · block as per operational list

22–32 RENNARD — day block, make safe. Internal stair, first-floor access.
```

"That is the tenth of January's row copied out of a column headed NIGHT," Hobbs said. "Not the words. The column. Somebody took the block off the list and the night off with it and left the rest, and there is no field on this for a morning because there has never been a morning on a paper in this district."

---

The tower was up at about eleven. Eight feet of scaffold on two outriggers, one of them on the verge at the low end where the Cut bank has been shedding clay since the flood model was last adjusted, and a man of about thirty put his boot on one plate, watched the ground go round, and said so out loud, and two other men heard him and carried on. Thirty people were on Rennard at eleven in the morning because it was a street. A woman of about eighty-two was on the second step of number twenty-six with her door open behind her, because the internal stair at twenty-six was being taken out on the make safe and three men were on it at the time.

At twenty past eleven the outrigger on the low side went into the ground. It went about a foot. The tower lifted, and eight feet of scaffold came over across a pavement and into the front garden wall of number twenty-four, and it took about two seconds and it was not loud, and eleven people were on it or under it or holding it. The man of about sixty-one holding the ladder and the woman of about forty-three holding the door frame at twenty-six were the two who were not on the clinic's list. The other nine were.

Mara Okafor came out at five past twelve with a bag and did the thing she is for, and was the most competent person on that street the whole of the time. The works' accident form went in at ten past two with eleven names on it in a foreman's capitals, because a foreman filling in a form at two o'clock on a Thursday has one pen and no time. At about half past two Rhoda Mistry came down from the health center, read the eleven names off the form the yard had taped to the barrier, and went back up the hill.

The yard shut the daylight operation at twenty-six minutes past eleven, nobody contradicted her, and she wrote the reason on the works' own stop sheet in her own hand, which she has not done since 2036. The scaffold came down at half past one. The low-loader went out over the soft road at twenty past two with the skip still on it, because the tip was on soft ground and the driver was not going to be told anything about soft ground on the same day.

Elias Rook had been on Rennard from half past nine and not before ten to nine, and in between he had held a gate and carried a coil of line to a man who wanted it, and at about twenty past eleven he had been nine feet from a scaffold that went over in a street where thirty people were standing, and had caught a child and been hit on the shoulder by somebody's elbow on the way down. He had no gift. There was no hand. There was a woman of eighty-two on a stair and a man of about sixty-one holding a ladder, and he had one right hand and it was holding a child. That is the whole of it and nobody has been allowed to soften it since, including him.

---

The room above number six was free from three. Selwyn Anum came at a quarter past three and did not unroll anything, which was the first thing anybody in that building noticed. Wendy Tallow came at half past three and said in the first four seconds that Joon Park had asked her to be here, and that the reason was that she had a hand on a book and he did not.

"The day book," Anum said, and got it off the shelf behind the counter, under a stack of binders, and did not open it on the table. "It is ours. It is not the verification log and it is not the counter log and it is not a works record, and I have been in this cell nine weeks and had to be told by a man of thirty-four in a corridor that we keep one. It is not on an index, it does not go to the region, and I have never been asked to write in it, which is how I know it has not been kept since August."

It went to four o'clock, it was read out in the room, and the method officer's August went with it. It is a hardback book with one ruled line a day, written by whoever is on duty, in that person's own hand, at the end of the shift, and it is not a form and nobody signs it. The line for the eighth of January is in a woman's small upright hand:

```
8 JAN — 08:05 task sheet to works, Rennard day block,
per OP/1 row 2, no time on it, T. Ayodele. 09:12 counter open, Tallow,
nothing said. 11:20 incident works crew Rennard,
11 persons hurt, works form in. 14:30 Rook in the room. Nothing sent anywhere.
```

"That is the failure," Elias Rook said. "In their own hand, in their own book, written at half past three this afternoon, and it will still be in that book in four years, and nobody in that office is going to remember writing it. And it is not the book you asked for."

"No. You asked a regional office on the fifth of December for the verification log," Anum said. "It is a register of readings and instruments and it is a very good book and it will come on the sixteenth, and it will tell you the ground was measured on three occasions and by whom, and it will not tell you one single thing about a man in a car at five to nine this morning handing a piece of paper to a foreman, because a reading has no field for the morning." He shut the book. "You have been asking the right office the right question since December about the wrong book, and the wrong book is four inches thick on a shelf behind me and has never left this room, and I am not sending it anywhere, and I would like that said in a room while I am in it."

---

Peart had the book open on the bench by the kettle when Elias Rook came back at ten past five, on the bench it had been on since the twenty-ninth of November with a rubber band round the corner of it, and for nineteen years it had been the one object in that yard nobody had ever asked about.

"You have had your hand on that for three weeks and taken it off," Peart said. "Ask me about it or do not, and I would rather you asked."

"The terminal takes a correction." He said it without any weight, the way a man says the price of milk. "I have argued with that machine for nineteen years. Change a number and it holds the old one and the new one and the man who changed it and the hour. It is very good. It will hold a wrong figure for as long as you like and it will never once tell you what it said this morning at ten past six, and that is not a fault in it, because nobody built it to be asked what it said yesterday, because nobody has ever needed to know what it said yesterday."

He turned the book round and put one finger on the top line of the page.

"That is what I was told at five to nine this morning, in my own hand, at the end of the shift, and nobody has altered one word of it, and it is a rubbish book and it is the only thing in this yard that can tell anybody what this crew was standing in front of. That is the whole reason I keep it. It is not because they will not let me keep the other one."

He shut it and put the band back over the corner.

"There is a woman at the health center with eleven names on the back of a discharge list and I have got eleven cards in a wallet in a drawer in the canteen," Peart said. "They are the same eleven. I have known that since the twenty-eighth of December, which is eleven days ago, and in eleven days I have not put the two things on one table once, and here is the reason and it is not a good one. My eleven cards have got a name, a night, a floor and a stair, and no street, because there is no field on the card for a street. So this morning, when a woman in a good coat handed a foreman a sheet that said RENNARD, there was not one thing in this building I could have done with the eleven except the two I already knew were on Marlow Street and had told nobody." He looked at his own hands. "Nine of them were on that street this morning and I did not know and I could not have known, and that is not a defense and I am not going to stand in this yard and make it into one. It is a line on a card."

---

At half past six there were nineteen people in the front room on Ash Street and Elias Rook stood up, because that is what he does in that room now, and told them what had happened on Rennard Street in the words it had happened in, including the nine feet and the child.

Then he said the other part, and he said it without stopping.

"I asked a woman in a room on the twenty-third of December to put her hands on eleven people, out loud, in front of five of you, on this ground, on a Tuesday. I had four minutes and I used eleven seconds of them. She said no and she gave me a reason I have thought about every day since, and she was right, and if anybody in this room wants to be angry with somebody about those eleven then they will have to be angry with me, because I am the only person who has done anything wrong in this."

Mara Okafor, at the back, on her feet, said, "That is the correct sentence and I am not going to add to it."

Joon Park asked no question. He had the custody schedule in his coat and did not take it out, and after Elias had finished he said one thing.

"Eleven," Joon Park said. "The form has eleven names on it and the health center has eleven names on the back of a list three years old, and they are the same eleven, and two good people made them separately in two buildings and neither was asked for the other's, and there is no form in this city that says one of them had to be. I am not calling that anything. I am saying the number is eleven on two pieces of paper and I have put it in a room."

Nothing in the room resolved anything, because there was nothing to resolve. A tower had gone over on Rennard Street at twenty past eleven in the morning and eleven people were hurt, and the district was still standing, and the program was still right about the ground under all of it, and a hardback book on a shelf in two rooms above a shop on Cutbank Row said who had handed over the piece of paper, and a book on a bench in a yard with a rubber band round the corner of it said what a foreman had been told at five to nine, and a list on the back of a discharge sheet at a health center on Marden Row had the same eleven names on it and nothing in the world between them.
