# Chapter 111 — The Issue Slips

Cliff Marner was sixty-four days out of the ground. Della Marner was ninety-four days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was ninety-four days in, and on the far bank of the river at ten to six that morning the depot had a hose on the wall with a pressure gauge on it and a board with two columns and R. Dunleavy, who is fifty-nine and does not do the talking part of it.

Elias Rook was there at ten to six, which is what he does, and the needle went to nineteen hundred and twenty and stayed there, and the gauge on the wall settled at one hundred and two, and Dunleavy wrote two figures in a book in his own hand and turned the book round so that the page faced the man who had brought the sheet, and said nothing at all.

Twenty minutes. The depot runs Tuesdays and Fridays at half past six and this was the Tuesday half and the Friday half is an inventory half and is on the notice and has been empty in his week since October for reasons that belong to a Tuesday in October and not to Friday.

He had been up since a quarter to five. The Tuesday costs him an hour of sleep a week for the rest of his life and he had done the arithmetic on it in October and got the right answer and had not looked at it since.

He got the 11 at ten past eight in the light with the folder under his right arm and a cardboard tube he had stopped needing to think about.

---

Number six was a shop that sold door furniture and had a counter at the back of it, and above the shop there were two rooms with a stair on the outside of the building, and the stair shook under him.

"You are on the list," the woman at the counter said, before he had got the door closed. "For an observer. It is not me that assigns them and there is not one free this week, and if there was one it would not be me that gives it to you, and when it comes it will come to the counter and you will be told."

"That is fine."

"I know it is fine. I am telling you the shape of the door." She had a name card and a card that said what her role was and her role was *enquiries*. "You asked for a standing one on the first of December and it is a standing one and it is in the queue, and I will not tell you when, because if I tell you when, then on the day it does not come, you will have been told something."

That was the second time in a fortnight a woman behind a counter had declined to make a date smaller. He wrote nothing down about it and went upstairs.

---

Selwyn Anum was forty-one and had a laptop open and a kettle going and did not look up, and then did.

"Rook. You have got one hand."

"I have."

"Good. Do not sign anything. It saves a step." He put his hand out towards the rail against the far wall. "Which one do you want."

"I want to know when they were taken."

Anum stopped with his hand on the rail.

"Say more than four words to a man on a Monday."

"The two rolls in this room have got no date on them anywhere. They are a soft line in three colors of ink and a number on the corner of the sheet. I have got the eleventh of September and the twenty-ninth of November off the mouth of a man in a church hall and I have got them off your mouth and I have not got them off any paper. I have got a footnote on a schedule that says which reading it was built on and nothing that says when the other one was taken, and I want the other one on a piece of paper with a day on it."

"That," Anum said, "is a better question than the one you asked on Monday and it has taken you eight days to get to it, and I am not going to be unpleasant about that because it is a Monday and it is raining."

He went to a drawer and came back with two yellow slips, rubber-banded to each other, the way receipts are, and put them on the trestle without straightening them out.

---

> **SECURING BAND — OCCASION 1 · Ref. BWP/TG/4SD/2042**
> **TAKEN 11 SEP 42 · 06:40**
> **WORKS CLEARANCE 05:50 – 07:05**
> **ISSUED 11 SEP 42 · 08:10**

> **SECURING BAND — OCCASION 2 · Ref. BWP/TG/4SD/2042**
> **TAKEN 29 NOV 42 · 06:15**
> **WORKS CLEARANCE 05:50 – 07:30**
> **ISSUED 1 DEC 42 · 09:40**

And at the foot of each one, under a ruled line, a signature in ink: **N. PEART, YARD**.

"The reader is on a vehicle," Anum said. "It is not a thing you carry up a street with a handbag. It goes down the middle of the alignment at a walking pace with the mast out and it wants the street to itself, and the street is a works street, and the works shut the street. That is the clearance line and that is the foreman who shut it, and his name goes on because the sheet is evidence that he shut a street that two thousand nine hundred people use to get to work."

"You have never told me that."

"You have never asked me that." Anum said it without any weight, which was how he said everything. "You have asked me about the method twice and about a schedule twice and about a man in a corridor once, and every one of those was the right question and none of them was that one."

Elias Rook pressed his right forearm to the yellow slip to stop it curling and looked at 05:50 twice.

Six twenty in the morning, on the eleventh of September, on a Thursday.

He knew what day the sixth of September was, because it was the day everything in the eleven weeks behind him had happened on, and it was a Saturday, and a man who is thirty-five does not have to do arithmetic to know that a Thursday is five days after a Saturday. He had a notebook in his coat with a date on the inside back cover and a margin in it where he had tried twice to write a sentence and crossed it out both times.

He did not write it down.

The reason he did not write it down was that a man with four dead people's names in pencil on the back of somebody else's hand has no way in the world of knowing whether he has got one of them right, and a man who has just started keeping dates about the ground is the same man, and two dates in a notebook is a pattern and three is a theory, and he does not have a theory and has not earned one.

---

Then he looked at the two clearance times side by side for longer than the two taken times.

"Five fifty," he said. "Both of them. Same minute."

"Same minute both times, and a different minute at the other end, and that is not us." Anum shut the drawer. "That is the crew. They come on at ten to six because the day starts at ten to six, and they go back when the reading is finished and they have put the road back. Ninety-five minutes the first time, one hundred minutes the second. I did not choose either of those numbers and neither did he, and if you want to know which end of the alignment took the extra five minutes then you are asking the foreman and not me."

Elias Rook thought about asking a man in a corridor about the extra five minutes, and did not say it out loud, and the not-saying took about half a second and cost him nothing, which was the cheapest refusal he ever made and the one he was going to be charged for later.

---

Nev Peart was fifty-one and had been the foreman of that yard for nineteen years and kept a shift book in his own hand on the tail of the bench by the kettle with a rubber band round it, and the rubber band was where it had been on the sixth of December when a stranger had put a hand on it and taken the hand off.

Elias Rook did not put a hand on it on the ninth.

"You were on my street at ten to six on Saturday the twenty-ninth," he said. "I have got a piece of paper that says so and I have not come to show you the paper. I have come to ask you one question and it is a bad one because I do not know why I am asking it."

Peart put the rubber band back on a book that had not moved.

"Go on."

"You shut the alignment to take a reading. What were you clearing it for."

"Boats," said Peart. "Not boats. Traffic." He said it the way a man corrects his own sentence, and then went on properly. "The works has three tippers and a lorry on that street in the morning and if you want a street quiet at ten to six you have to move your own metal off it first, so you move it at ten to six, and then it stands on the low road until the reading is done, and then it comes back. I sign for the road because if a bus goes through at half six and puts a hole in somebody's wall I do not want to be the man who said the reading was more important than the bus."

"That is the whole of it?"

"That is the whole of it and it is the only thing I know how to answer about a slip." He looked at the man. "You have been in this district since before the twenty-ninth and you have not asked me anything, and there are two men from the Council on this site every Monday, and one of them asked me in October whether the Saturday shift was costing overtime and I told him what it cost, and that is the last conversation I have had about a piece of paper with my name on it."

He did not know that a regional director had said his name out loud in a church hall on the Monday before last, in front of about two hundred people, as the reason a schedule had not been rebuilt. He had been told nothing, and he was not owed anything, and his name was on two yellow slips in a drawer above a shop on Cutbank Row.

"Thank you," Elias Rook said.

"You are not welcome. It is half past four."

---

The two streets where the two lines were four inches apart were Marlow Street and Sallow Court, and he had known that since the Thursday before, the fourth of December, when he had counted forty-four addresses off an overlay with a finger in a room above number six, and he had not said it to anybody, and on the way to the bus he worked out the thing he had been walking toward for two days.

The line had moved *off* Sallow Court and *onto* Marlow Street, and that meant the two streets were on opposite sides of the same move, and that meant the forty-four addresses were not all the same kind of wrong.

On one side, forty-four addresses were being cleared for a hazard that, as of the twenty-ninth of November, was not under them, and they had a date on a piece of paper in a drawer and nobody had been told anything.

On the other side, there were addresses the schedule called `RETAINED`, which is a promise, and as of the twenty-ninth of November the ground under some of them was inside the band, and they were not on the forty-four, and they were not on any list of anybody's, and the wall was going to be built along the board's alignment through whatever was there.

He did not know how many. There was no number in the room, and there was no number in the two yellow slips, and there was no number in a schedule that could not show him anything that had happened after the second of December.

He got the 11 at ten past six and stood on Marden Row for a minute with the folder under his arm, and there is a man in this city who could tell him in about four minutes, on any street he stood on, whether the ground under a wall was the ground he thought it was, and that man is sixty-four and he is not available as a structural authority on a designated site, in the past tense, in a document, forever, and he said so himself in a room in November, and nobody in this city is going to ask him about any designated site by anybody, in writing or out of it, including Elias Rook, who rode past his street on the way to the bridge without getting off the bus.
