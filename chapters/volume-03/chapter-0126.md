# Chapter 126 — Nobody Was Hurt That Night

Cliff Marner was seventy-nine days out of the ground. Della Marner was one hundred and nine days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was one hundred and nine days in, and Elias Rook did not sleep much on the night of the twenty-third of December, and the front room on Ash Street was full at nine in the morning on Wednesday the twenty-fourth, which was the last working day before the yard closed for the year.

Twenty-four days, and that was the last one. Twenty-four fares and a hundred and thirty-two pounds, and the run had two Sundays out of it and one Tuesday that cost him an hour of sleep a week for the rest of his life.

---

The woman from the regional office was called Elin and her name card said *Oversight, Pilot Program* and she had come out on the 11:40 and she had a one-sided form on the table with a heading and eleven ruled lines and boxes at the end of each of them.

"I am not here to take a statement about anybody," Elin said. "I am here to write down what happened in a room, because a regional pilot has to be able to show what it did on the day, and there is no part of this form that asks me what I think about it."

She filled the boxes in while she talked, and it took her about ninety seconds, and the answers were already in her hand.

Line four: *A loan was offered and the terms were read aloud to five people present.*

Line five: *No loan was accepted.*

Line six: *The borrower asked one question of the lender.*

Line seven: *The lender declined, and gave a reason, and the reason was written down as it was said.*

"Those four are in my voice," Elin said. "They are in the voice of the program and they are the correct four and I have been doing this for six years."

"Then what is the problem."

"The problem is the third one is a box and it wants a word in it," Elin said, "and there are six words that would go in that box and every one of them is wrong, and I sat in a room in the Meridian on Friday and let three of them be written down and scratched out in front of me by a man who does not have a technical term for the situation either, and I would like to know whether the answer is no, or whether the answer is that the program does not have a way of writing it down."

The room did not help her. Petar Lisk put his pencil behind his ear the other way round and said the wall was full, and Inez Sato said nothing, and Cliff Marner was in the chair by the window with the blanket and said nothing at all, and Elin waited about twenty seconds and then filled the box herself, in ink, and read it back.

"Declined, with a reason, in the room, before any use," she said. "That is five words and I have been told to say why I chose them, so here it is: because the reason is part of the answer and I will not have a form that lets a program take a decision out of a woman's mouth and leave the reason behind."

Elias Rook said nothing, because there was nothing he could add to that which would not have been a man in a front room managing a woman's answer, and he was not going to do that in front of eleven people.

"The statement has to say where the room is," Elin said, and put her pen down. "That is the one thing I cannot do in my own voice, because the room is in a program's building and the answer came out of it, and if I write the address then a copy of this goes to an office a long way from here with a street in it, and a street is the only thing in this that anybody could act on at eight o'clock in the morning."

Elin wrote it in the margin, in ink, in a hand that was not a clerk's.

"Are you going to do it again," she said, with the pen still down.

Elias Rook looked at the eleven lines of the form.

"I do not know," he said. "And I am not going to tell you that I do not know in a way that sounds like a man being careful with a regional office, because you have written down a woman's reason in full and you have come out on the 11:40 on Christmas Eve to do it, and you have earned the true version. I do not know, and the reason I do not know is that I asked a woman in a room about eleven people instead of about myself, and I did it in a room with your officer in it, and I read the terms out afterwards like a man performing a step. If it is offered again I will not know anything different until somebody stands in that room and tells me what I did wrong, and she is not going to, because she said she was not going to explain it to me and she was not lying."

"That is a worse answer than I expected," Elin said, "and it is the first one anybody has given me about any of these."

Joon Park looked at the margin and then did not write anything, and did not take the custody schedule out of his coat.

"That is one place," he said. "It is now in one hand and a program file, and I am not copying it, and it is not going near the file in the next room, and a woman on that row has kept a list of doors in her own hand since 2031 out of nothing but being asked about it once, and nobody asked her again, so that is her street and not mine, and I have said no."

"Thank you," Elin said, and did not argue, and closed the form, and left at ten past ten with it.

---

The yard at half past two had a generator on and the lorries had gone and the office was warm for the first time since November, and the closure card behind the glass on the gate had a new line typed along the bottom of it in the same face as the rest of it.

`YARD CLOSED 25, 26, 27, 28 DEC · REOPENS 29 DEC`

Nerys Hobbs was in the survey office with her coat on and her helmet under her arm and a photograph in a plastic wallet, and she put the door to, and did not sit down.

"Say it and go," she said.

"Eleven cards went out on the wallet at nine o'clock on Wednesday morning," Elias Rook said. "That is the whole of it and I am not going to be thanked for it, because I brought a list out of a clinic and the foreman turned it into eleven pieces of card with his own initials on them, and that is his and not mine."

"He told me," Hobbs said. "He came and told me on the yard side of the gate at ten past seven this morning with his coat open, before he had said a good morning to anybody, and he told me, because a clerk of mine had heard from a crew on the sixteenth that a woman at number nine was going to want two people and a torch on her stair, and had asked him the question, and he had gone and found out. He has not asked me to change anything and he is not going to, and I am going to keep him for another nineteen years if he will stay."

She took the plastic wallet off the shelf above the terminal and did not open it.

"Ninth of November. Twenty to seven in the morning. Sallow Court, from the north corner, about four photographs' worth of men in a doorway and a torch beam on a stair."

"How many hurt."

"Four dead," Hobbs said, "and that is the part that has been sitting in me for six weeks, and it is not about the four. If you put a torch in a man's hand and you give him four minutes a door, there is not a great deal that can go wrong on a stair, and that is the whole of what the works knows and the whole of what the program does not contain, and a torch is not a document and four minutes is not a date and neither of them can be put in a box on a piece of paper, and I have written schedules for twenty years and there is not one line in any of them for the man."

She opened the wallet and turned four photographs round on the table, and did not point at any of them, and Elias Rook did not lean over them, and after a few seconds Hobbs closed the wallet and put it back on the shelf above a terminal that had been down since Thursday and had been working again since Monday.

"Now the thing I actually came down here to say," she said. "The works' booking is twenty working days. It starts on Monday the fifth of January and it finishes on Thursday the fifth of February, and I sign a sheet for every night in it, and there are four nights left in it, and two of them are Saturdays, which I did not choose and have not questioned, and one of them is a block of stairs on Murrow that nobody has ever walked in daylight, and I know all of that because I put it in the booking myself in October and I have not moved it since."

"Twenty working days," Elias Rook said.

"And after the fifth of February the board has to come back for money," Nerys Hobbs said, "and in my experience the board does not come back for money in the same year that it has already spent it, and when it does not come back the works' booking ends and the ground sits there and everybody in this district keeps on being moved by a piece of paper with a date on it from the last time anybody looked. I am telling you that now, in December, in a room, because in January I will not have the time to tell you and because you are the only man in this district who has been writing dates down in his own hand for three weeks and has not once been asked to stop."

She put her helmet under her arm and looked at him.

"I am not going to tell you what to do with it," she said. "I have been an engineer for twenty-two years and I have never once told a man in a coat what to do, and the reason is that the four minutes are his and not mine, and so is the torch, and so is the ladder, and so is the forty minutes a body takes to get down a stair when it is being done properly by two people who have done it before."

Elias Rook stood in a warm office in a closed yard with his left hand hanging at his side and a plastic wallet of four photographs on a shelf above a terminal that was working again, and the last of the working year was over at four o'clock, and nothing whatever had happened on the previous night, and there were four nights in January and two of them were Saturdays and one of them was a Monday, and it was about eleven minutes and two pounds seventy-five away from the room he was standing in.
