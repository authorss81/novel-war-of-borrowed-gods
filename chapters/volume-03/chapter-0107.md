# Chapter 107 — The Request

Cliff Marner was sixty days out of the ground. Della Marner was ninety days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was ninety days in. Elias Rook had been in Tidegate for five days and had come back on the 11 with a folder and two rolls of paper and the beginnings of an understanding, and none of it was evidence, and on Friday morning the fifth of December, the ninetieth day, the first thing in the building that made any sense was a man of twenty-four at a table with a form.

Joon Park had a public records request form. He had brought it from the county library on the bus, along with a pen, and a folded piece of paper with what he wanted written on it in his own handwriting in case they would not let him keep the form, and he had got up at five to do it.

"Subject," he said, without looking up. "Series. Span. And no year, and I want to explain the no year to somebody in about four minutes because it is the only interesting thing on this page."

---

The counter was on the ground floor of a building with a stone step and a queue of two people, and it was not the cell's counter and it was not the board's, and the woman behind it had a card that said what her role was and it was not *enquiries*.

The form wanted four things.

**Who is holding it.** Regional, so the regional office, which is not in Tidegate and is on the far side of the city, and the form has to be handed in at a counter and not posted, because a posted form does not get a date.

**Which series.** The form had one box for a series and no room for two, and it said in eleven words on the back that a request for more than one series is two requests.

"Verification log," Joon Park said. "Not the clearance record, not the shift book. The verification log."

"If you want to know what we decided and when, that is the log." She said it without any weight at all, which he noticed, because he had now heard that sentence in two buildings. "If you want to know what happened on a site, that is the works, and they are not us, and I would not put that in this form because you will get the wrong answer and it will be a correct wrong answer and you will not be able to tell the difference for a month."

"That is the second time this week somebody has told me the correct answer was the wrong one," Elias Rook said, from behind him.

"I am not going to be able to tell which one you mean."

"Neither can I. That is why we are here."

**The span.** Free text, and it takes any range you write in it, and he wrote the first of September 2042 to the date on the form and would have gone back further if the box had been bigger.

And the fourth box was the year, and it was not free text. It was a menu, and if you filled it in it overrode the span, and Joon Park put his pen on it and left it there for a while.

"I need two years," he said. "The regional program goes back further than this district does. The clearance history for this alignment starts in 2039, and there is a model year in 2039 on a board at the gate in Tidegate, and everything I actually want is in December. If I put 2042 in that box they will search 2042 and they will send me 2042, and 2042 is the year where the answer is *we did not do anything until December*. And it will be true, and I will not be able to do one thing with it."

"And if you leave it blank?"

"Then they search the whole span, which is longer, and they hold it longer, because a long request is a request that has to be looked at by a person who is not the person who took it."

"How long is the whole span?"

"Four to six weeks from the date it goes in." She said it kindly, the way you say a thing you have said a lot. "It is not us. We do not set it and we cannot shorten it and I am not going to tell you it can be shortened, because it can be shortened by a person and that is a favor and favors do not come back in the same form."

Joon Park said, "Understood."

"And I want to say the other half of it, because nobody says the other half of it. Four to six weeks is not the service refusing you. It is the service being able to tell you what it did. Anything that came back sooner than that and you are asking a person to remember, and a person who has to remember is a person who is guessing, and you do not want a guess in a file. If somebody in this building told you it could be done in ten days they have either broken a rule for you or they have told you a thing that is not true, and I would rather you knew that now than in January."

Elias Rook looked at the back of the form while she said it. There were eleven words on the back about series and nothing anywhere on it about the ten days, and that was the correct way round.

Joon Park put the pen down on the table beside the form rather than in the year box, and signed it, and did not put a year in.

---

**NEAR END: 2 JANUARY 2043.**
**FAR END: 16 JANUARY 2043.**

He wrote the two of them on the back of his own folded paper in his own hand, and put the paper in his coat, and then said the two dates out loud in the front room on Ash Street that evening in the middle of a conversation about a light fitting, to about thirty people, and did not put them on the pad.

"I want everybody to hear this once, in case anybody wants to know," he said. "On Friday I filed a request for a document that is held in another building, and the service says four to six weeks, which means the second of January or the sixteenth. That is what I have. I cannot make it come sooner, I have not asked anybody to make it come sooner, and anybody who wants to know the date can have the date and anybody who wants me to say the date is sooner is going to be told no by me in a room rather than in a corridor.

"I am not the records keeper of this building. Petar is not the records keeper of this building. I am a man of twenty-four who went to a counter on Friday with a form and a pen, and that is the entire authority I have, and I would rather everybody knew that now than in January when it does not come."

Petar Lisk said, "Put it on the board."

"No."

"That is the board for."

"That board has got three questions on it and a clerk's column and nineteen hands, and there is no room, and we are all going to have to build another one, and it is not going to be me." He was not being dramatic about it. "And I am not going to stand up in this room every Friday and read out a date, because the day I do that this is a room that waits for dates. Ask me on a Tuesday. I will tell you any Tuesday."

Nobody argued with him.

That was the part Elias Rook did not understand for about a week afterward. In thirteen weeks that building had never once been told a thing it could not check, and it had built a habit out of that, and the habit was so complete that a man of twenty-four could stand in the middle of a Friday evening and say *here is a date six weeks away, I have no way to move it* and thirty people would hear that as information and not as an excuse. It is a strange thing to have built by accident out of being right, and it had not been built by accident, and he had built most of it.

---

Tidegate's church hall is off Marden Row and it was booked for the hour by a group with no name on the booking sheet and a kettle in the corner, and about forty people came, which was more than the twelve who had come to the first one in November and fewer than anybody had hoped for.

Elias Rook had the folder. He had the two rolls in a cardboard tube because a man of forty-one had told him on a Monday that a man on a bus is the reason things get damaged.

He got the date out first, before he said anything at all about the schedule, which was step four of a method in eight pages that a man of sixty-four had put on a table in a records room, and it was the fourth time anybody on that team had used it, and the first time it had been used on a promise instead of a plan.

"The first thing is a date and I am going to give you the date before I tell you anything else, and then I am going to tell you the rest." He said it in about nine seconds and then he waited, because step four was not only the saying. "There is a document held in another building that would tell you what this program decided and when, and I asked for it on Friday, and it will arrive between the second of January and the sixteenth. Four to six weeks, that is the service and not anybody in this hall, and I cannot make it sooner, and I am not going to stand here and tell you I am working on it, because I am not. That is the first thing. Now the rest."

He gave them the rest for about fifty minutes. The reading is right. Nobody is lying. The band moved on the twenty-ninth of November and the schedule is dated the second of December and did not move, and he could not explain why that was not a scandal, and he did not pretend to.

A man at the front, about fifty, with a works jacket on, said, "Right."

"You have a question."

"I have got a question and it is the only one I have got." He did not stand up. "Is six weeks worth it. Because I have got a house and I have got until the end of January, and there is a woman in this hall who has got a ground-floor flat and there is a notice on that flat. Six weeks is six weeks to me, and I want to know whether the man who came on the bus knows that."

Elias Rook stood in the middle of a hall of about forty people and did not have an answer that was worth anything, and he said so.

"No," he said. "I do not know whether it is worth it. I know that the six weeks are real and I know that the thing I am going to do with them is this." He did not put his hand up and gesture at anybody, and did not use the word *document*, and did not say the word the man was about to use. "If it was shorter I would be a man who had asked for something and got it quickly, and you would be a room full of people who had been told a thing six weeks early. I would rather be the second one for six weeks than the first one for six days, and I have not thought that out very well and I have thought about it a great deal."

The man said, "Right," again, and did not say anything else, and somebody behind him said *he has got no answer*, in the flat voice of a person making an observation about the furniture, and the woman in front of him said *he said so himself*, and that was the end of the row and it lasted about eleven seconds.

---

He walked back to the stop at half past nine with the tube under his arm and wrote two dates in the notebook on the inside back cover, because inside back covers are for things that will still matter in February.

**NEAR END: 2 JANUARY. FAR END: 16 JANUARY.**

And then he stood at a bus stop on Marden Row and understood that for four days he had been asking a district to be angry at a schedule, and that on Friday afternoon he had handed the only thing he had actually got to a woman behind a stone counter and she had put it in a queue, and the queue was six weeks long, and there was no part of that he was able to do on a Tuesday.

Two miles and eleven minutes, two pounds seventy-five each way, and a back that hurt on the Tuesday.

Joon Park had a form with a year box on it that he had deliberately not filled in, and a man of twenty-four had stood in a room of thirty people and told the truth about how little he had, in a voice with nothing in it to keep, and nobody in that room had needed to check whether it was true, and that was the first useful thing anybody in this business had done in five days.
