# Chapter 162 — Whoever Is Holding The Check Box

Cliff Marner was one hundred and fifteen days out of the ground. Della Marner was one hundred and forty-five days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was one hundred and forty-five days in, and Elias Rook got the 11 at ten past eight on Thursday morning, the twenty-ninth of January 2043, and paid the fare, and there were six working days to the fifth of February, and the room above the counter at number six was free from ten until one and had been free from ten until one since the middle of December.

Selwyn Anum had a sheet of paper on the table with a date on it that everybody in the room had seen on Tuesday, and he had a second sheet, which was not the same sheet, and he put the second sheet face down for about a minute and a half and then turned it over, which he did not have to do and which everybody in that room noticed.

"I want to say what this is before I say what I want," Anum said, "because I have been in this building nine weeks and I have learned what happens if I do not. It is a request and it is the first one I have made of anybody in this room since the fourteenth of January, and if the answer is no I am not going to ask you a second time, and I am not going to mention it again, and I will not put it in a minute in a way that makes the asking the fact."

"That is all of it," Anum said. "I want a line in a book that says the checker was named in advance by the person who is going to hold it, and I do not want a line in a book that says anybody refused anything, and I have drafted it that way on purpose and I would like somebody to check the drafting."

Joon Park had the custody schedule in his coat and did not take it out.

"You have been awake since the twenty-ninth of November," he said.

"Six days over fifty-eight," Anum said. "It is in the program\'s own medical line and it is the only line in that file about me and it is the only true one."

---

"What I want," Anum said, "is for you to be the one who stands on the ground on the tenth."

He said it the way a man says a thing he has rehearsed and then decided not to rehearse any more.

Elias Rook did not answer for about eleven seconds, which is long enough that everybody in the room can see him deciding not to say the first thing.

"No," he said.

"Before you say anything else," Anum said, "I have got a paragraph ready for the case where you say yes and I am not going to use it, and I have got a worse paragraph for the case where you say no, and I am not going to use that one either, and I would like you to hear the shape of the second one and then I am going to stop talking."

"Say the shape of it."

"The shape of it is that the checker is the program," Anum said. "It has been the program on every occasion since September and it is going to be the program on the tenth, and the register that the name goes into is a register the program keeps, and the man whose name goes in it is a program officer, and I have never once in nine weeks been able to think of that as a fault and I have not been able to think of it as anything else either. I have not slept properly since the twenty-ninth of November and it is partly that and it is not only that."

"Then why me."

"Because you are the only person in this district who is not the program and is not a resident of it either," Anum said. "You are a fifth man on a road. You have said that sentence yourself, in a street, to a man with a dog, and I read it in a minute in somebody else\'s book and I have thought about it every day since. If your name is in that field then the field contains a person from outside the thing being measured, and that is worth something to me, and I am telling you that it is worth something to me and not that it is worth something to the register, because the register does not care and has never cared."

"It is worth something to a room in about four years," Elias Rook said. "That is the trouble with it. If my name is in that box then there is a document in a regional office that says the ground under this district was stood on by a man who is not employed here, who had been in a church hall two miles away on the Thursday of the fifteenth of January, and who carried a wardrobe down a stair on Rennard Street on the sixth of January with one arm. And none of that is a lie. And in about four years there will be a man with a clipboard who reads that line and does not read any of the rest of it, and he will not be a liar either, and that is the exact shape of the thing I have been standing in the middle of for two volumes."

"You have not carried a wardrobe in this district," Anum said.

"I have carried a door frame," Elias Rook said. "In January, on Rennard Street, for two hours, and it went on a shift book that nobody outside this building will ever see, and the man who wrote carried-throughout on it wrote it against his own name and not mine, because that was what he could attest to and not the other thing. I have thought about that line every day for a fortnight and if you put my name in a check box on Tuesday morning that line becomes the interesting part of my year and it is not."

---

"Who else," he said. "Not as a criticism. As a list."

He wrote nothing down, and he had the notebook in his coat and did not take it out, which two people in that room noticed.

"Here is the whole of it," Anum said. "There is a foreman in this district with nineteen years in that yard and a booking that ends on the fifth. There is a woman of forty-seven who is the only person here that has read that ground with the works\' own instruments and got the same answer as the program. There is a woman behind a counter who has run it for twenty-nine years and who is, in her own words, nobody's district officer. There is a man of about forty-one at number nine Rennard who does not want the paper on the wall. There is a woman of nineteen who is on that sheet and is not asking to be. And there is me."

"Read that list back to me slowly," Elias Rook said, "and notice what it is."

"It is a list of people in a district who have each said no to a thing of this exact shape in the last three weeks."

"Yes," Elias Rook said. "It is. You have gone round a district asking four people for a second name and been told no four times, and you came to me because I was the fifth person you thought of, and I have said no to the same request twice in January and one of those refusals cost a working man his Sunday afternoons and one of them cost a woman with a rota four hours of sleep. I am not going to give you a fifth."

"I did not think you would."

"Then why did you ask."

"Because the fourth of January is the fourth of January and it is only January now," Anum said.

---

"Then say what you are going to do, and say it in this room while all of us are in it, and do not make me ask you for it."

Anum turned the second sheet over and wrote three lines on the bottom of it with a pen he took out of his coat, and then turned it round and pushed it across the table.

The bottom of it said that the checker for the reading of Tuesday the tenth of February would be Selwyn Anum, method officer, that he would hold the check box himself and fill in the name field himself at about seven o'clock in the morning, that he had said it out loud in a room in January on purpose and not because anybody had made him, and that no form existed in which one person in a district could decline to hold a check box and that he had looked.

"That is mine and not yours," Elias Rook said.

"That is mine and not yours," Anum said, "and I am not going to pretend I chose it. I did not choose it. I asked, and the answer was no, and I am the last one on the list, and the program is not withdrawn and the authorization of August is not withdrawn and the reading is not withdrawn and nobody has caused any of that, and a method officer who says he does not want the box is a program decision, and I am not the program. I have to hold it. I have to hold it because I am the one who is left."

---

Joon Park said one thing and he said it at about half past twelve with the schedule still in his coat.

"There have been three mornings," he said, "on which that box has been signed, and the register has three lines in it, and on each of the three the name against the line was the only other name anywhere in the world that had anything to do with the morning. That is not a fault. I want to say that in this room while I am in it, because in about four years somebody is going to find that out and think it is one, and it is not one, and I would rather it were written down by me than worked out by them. Twenty-two items have not become twenty-three in twenty-one weeks and I have checked this morning."

Nobody wrote that down either.

---

The yard at about ten past three had a gate on it with two documents and four biro words, and there would be one document and four biro words on it on Friday, and nothing at all in the way of a person working in that yard after Friday afternoon until the board came back with money or did not.

Peart had his hands in his pockets and the string of the accident form in his pocket with it.

"You have named the man who is going to stand on that street on the tenth," Peart said, when he had been told, in about nine seconds, and then he said the other thing, which is the whole of what he said.

"That gate is going to be bare," he said. "On the tenth of February, at about a quarter past seven in the morning, there is going to be a man in a good coat standing in a road in this district holding a box, and behind him there is going to be a gate with a notice in a frame and four biro words under it and nothing else, and that is going to be the first morning since the twenty-ninth of November that anybody can look at that gate and see that there was no booking, and he is going to see it, because he is going to be standing in the road looking at it, and he already knows, because he told us in January in a room while he was in it. So naming him has not stopped that. It has only made sure that a second man knows about it, and that second man is me, and I have got nothing to put on either nail."

He got the 11 at about five o'clock and sat on the far side of the cut for an hour.

Six working days to the fifth of February, and a yard with a booking and no work, and a foreman who had decided on Friday that a piece of paper about eleven injuries was coming off his own gate and had not told anybody except a locked one, and a man who had stood up in a room on Thursday morning and named himself as the only person left on a list, and not one of those four things was on a form, and not one of them was going to be settled by anybody writing it down, and the reading was in five days after the booking and in four days after that, and a gate with nothing on it was going to be in the background of a photograph somebody was going to take out of a vehicle at twenty to seven in the morning on the tenth of February, and two men in this district would know that it was there, and the man holding the box would know it, and nobody else on earth would ever find out, and that had been true since the first of January and was going to be true on the tenth.
