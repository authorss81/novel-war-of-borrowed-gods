# Chapter 153 — The Paper Ran Out On Cutbank Row

Cliff Marner was one hundred and six days out of the ground. Della Marner was one hundred and thirty-six days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was one hundred and thirty-six days in, and Elias Rook got the 11 at ten past eight on Tuesday morning, the twentieth of January, and paid the fare, and he had not been in this district at half past six in the morning and had not asked to be.

All four of her ruled lines were signed.

He saw them at about ten, on the trestle in the survey office, with the terminal off and Hobbs's coat still on, because she had come in early on a day she was not open and had done the thing she does in a morning and had gone back out.

Her name was at the foot of all four and the hours were hers and not the crew's and the last one was written about two minutes after the barriers came down, and the sheet had nothing on it after the fourth line and there was no fifth line on it and no room for one, and it was not going to be a sheet about a fifth night, because there were not going to be a fifth night in that booking.

Nev Peart had written four words under his own notice on the gate, in biro, under the line that said it was printed by the yard and was not a program document, and had not told anybody he was going to.

"That is not a correction," Peart said, when he was asked about it, which was nobody, because the yard was working and there were three lorries in it. "That notice said four nights. It was four nights. In about four years somebody is going to want to know whether a foreman's notice is worth the pin it is on, and the answer to that is it is worth the pin if the man who printed it says afterwards what happened, and I have never once done it in nineteen years and I am doing it now and I would like nobody to make a thing of it."

The rest of the booking was day work and the make safe and it ran to the fifth of February, and after the fifth of February the board had to come back for money, and in Nerys Hobbs's experience it does not come back in the same year it has already spent it, and this year it had already spent a great deal of it on four nights and eleven men and a scaffold that came down in a street at half past one in the afternoon on the eighth of January.

---

The lower half of the east side of Cutbank Row was empty at about eleven and had been since about four in the morning, and the skip was full and the tip was up and there was nothing to do with it until Monday, because a skip is a private firm's skip and a private firm does not come back on a Tuesday, and the man of about seventy who was painting a door at the top of the boatyard wall was still painting it.

"You have been on that wall three days," Elias Rook said.

"I have, and I will be on it next week, and I have been on it four times since November," the man said, "and I am being paid in paint for it out of a shop that is shut tonight and tomorrow night because the man who owns it is in a bed at number nine. I have not had a day's work since the twenty-second of November and I am not going to be the one who says anything about that in a street, and you are the fourth person this week who has wanted me to say it."

---

Mrs Postridge had the shop open and the sheet on the nail above the counter and there was no paper left on it.

The last of the four feet had gone on Monday night about two o'clock, in the fourth hand of about two hundred and forty, on a line about eleven Rennard, and the paper had stopped about two inches short of the end of the board it was pinned to and there was nowhere for line two hundred and forty-one to go.

"I have not got a roll of anything," Mrs Postridge said, before she was asked, from behind her counter, with the pencil still behind her ear. "I have got leaflets, which are not the right size, and I have got the back of a shelf, and I am not going to buy a roll of paper for a sheet of paper. The other nail above that door is for leaflets and that is what it is for, and I have had a nail in this shop for leaflets since 2014 and I am not changing what a nail is for at sixty-two because a woman from a housing office has an idea."

"Then it goes on nothing," Elias Rook said.

"It goes on the back of a shelf," Mrs Postridge said, "and if you do it, you do it wrong on purpose, because if you do it right it will be another four foot sheet and there is a foot and a half between that and anybody's hand and I have been thinking about that since Saturday."

Winifred Alagoa had the shopkeeper's pencil and counted for about eleven minutes at the counter, standing up, at half past one on a Tuesday, and she did not let anybody help her and she did not read anything out loud.

"A hundred and ninety-one in the fourth column on Friday night at about half past ten," she said, "and I told a pavement on Saturday that it would be wrong by nine by Tuesday, and it is wrong by more than nine by Tuesday, and I am not going to tell you by how much, and I have put the pencil back."

Nobody in that shop asked her to say it again and nobody asked her to say it at all, which was the correct arrangement, and the reason it was the correct arrangement was that she was the only person in the district who had been reading doors out loud on a Saturday for eleven years and the second one in that shop on a Tuesday in January was a shopkeeper with a counter and a queue.

Then she did the thing she had come in for, and it took under a minute.

She tore a strip about nine inches wide off the back of a shelf, held it along the bottom of the sheet so that the line of the third column carried across it, and put four strips of tape on it herself, and squared it with her thumbnail.

The columns did not line up. The first column on the brown paper started about an inch and a half to the left of the first column above it and the fourth was worse than that, and the heading over the fourth column, in capitals, underlined twice, stayed on the white paper at the top and did not come with the tape, and everybody in the shop looked at that for a second and nobody said anything.

"There is a line in the first column of that sheet that is not in anybody's hand on this row," Alagoa said, with the pencil not back behind her ear yet. "It has been there since the sheet was about two foot long. It says *cannot be reached at this address — see sheet*, and there is no address against it, and it is the only sentence on four foot of paper that was not written by somebody who lives on this row, and I am not going to tell you who wrote it and I have known about it since the Monday after the parents' meeting."

"Who wrote it."

"That is exactly the question and I am not going to be the one who asks it," Alagoa said, "and neither are you, and neither is she either, and the three of us are the only people in this district that anybody would tell."

The third column carried across the tape and onto the back of a shelf, and the sheet still had four columns on it, and the fourth heading was where it had always been, and the pencil was the shop's pencil, and it was the only part of that sheet anybody had ever extended, and there was nobody in that shop who was going to say a word about it.

---

He got the 11 at about ten past three and sat on the far side of the cut for about an hour with his right forearm across a page and did the arithmetic of a district in his own head and did not say any of it out loud.

There were six pieces of paper about the four nights in this district. A foreman's notice on a gate with four rows on it and four biro words under it. A woman's four ruled lines on a trestle, all four signed, with the last one signed half an hour after the barriers came down. A hardback shift book on a bench with a rubber band round the corner of it. An accident form with eleven names on it and a line under those in a different pen. A works' day sheet in a tray. And a length of unruled paper on a nail above a counter in a shop, four columns and a strip of shelf taped across the bottom of it.

Not one of those six is a form and not one of them is in a system, and there is no index for any of them and no clerk and no office and no reference on any of them, and every one of them was written by somebody who was standing in the street at the time.

He had been in this district for seven weeks trying to find a document that a person living in it could pick up and argue with, and there were six of them, and they were on a gate, in a shop, on a bench and in a tray, and there was no way at all of asking a question about any of them, and that was not a defect anybody in this district was going to fix in January.
