# Chapter 160 — Nobody In That Room Knows Who Signs The Check Box

Cliff Marner was one hundred and thirteen days out of the ground. Della Marner was one hundred and forty-three days at a family-reception desk in Cordell. The man behind the wall was one hundred and forty-three days in, and Elias Rook got the 11 at ten past eight on Tuesday morning, the twenty-seventh of January, and paid the fare, and there were eight working days to the fifth of February, and the yard gate had two documents and four biro words on it, and one of the documents was going to come down on the fifth, and the other nail was still empty.

Nerys Hobbs had signed a piece of paper that said DAY at the top of it in capitals, and the date, and the crew, and the hour, and she had put it in the tray with the rest of the works' papers and had told nobody she was going to and had told the room on Monday.

---

The slip came out of a tube in the survey office at about eleven on the Tuesday, and it was a works' slip and it was not a document anybody could argue with, and it had a date on it.

```
READER ON ALIGNMENT — alignment slip
Date: Tuesday 10 February 2043
Street shut from: 06:20
Reader on alignment: 07:15
Check box: [ signed by checker ]   Name:
```

"The securing band stands and the reading does not stop," Selwyn Anum said. "That came out of the region on Friday with four other things in it, and the date on it is not a date anybody chose to be kind to us with. It is five days after the works' booking ends, and I am telling you that now, in January, in a room, while I am in it, because I am not going to have anybody find out in February that the district was measured on a Tuesday with a bare gate and no booking and a foreman with nothing in his hands."

Nobody contradicted him and nobody thanked him.

"Is the checker named on it."

"There is a name field and it is empty," Anum said, "and it has been empty on every one of the three, and it gets filled in the morning by whoever is holding the check box, and that is the arrangement, and in September and in November and on the first of January it worked," and the man who held it wrote his own name and the time on it, and it went into a register in a regional office and it was in a file in a cabinet in the Meridian on the fourteenth of January with a copy of a document in it, and I have been told to keep going and I am going to keep going."

Elias Rook had been looking at the slip for about a minute without touching it.

"You said in this room on the fourteenth of January that the man on the gate that morning had a book and that it was his," he said.

"It is his," Anum said. "I said it was his and I said I was not going to read his book out in a room because it is his, and I have not read it out in a room, and I have not read it, and I want to say the next part myself and not have it drawn out of me. That book is the only independent record anybody in this city has that the first of January was stood on by a person and not by a machine. It is not indexed. It goes nowhere. Nobody has asked him to keep it. On the first of January the check box got signed and the regional register got a line in it, and the only other thing in the world that knows the sign-off happened is a man in a yard writing in a book after a shift."

"You could ask him."

"I could ask him whether I could ask him," Anum said, "and that is the whole of the difficulty and it has been the difficulty since the fourteenth of January and I have not got a way round it and I have thought about it every day for thirteen days, including last night, and I am not going to and I would like it said here today that I am not going to, and why."

Nobody said why.

"Because a man who writes a line in his own book at the end of a shift is a man who is keeping his own record, and there is a version of asking him where that record ends and mine begins," Anum said, "and if he says yes, then in about four years there will be two documents about one morning and a man with a clipboard will pick the one with a reference on it, and the one without will be the truth, and there is nothing in this city that would let anybody prove which is which. And if he says no, then I have spent a working man's goodwill in January on my own paperwork, and he will not have got anything out of it, and he will know that, and so will I."

"When the ninth of February is the Monday before," Elias Rook said, "he will have been in that yard nineteen years, and there is not one form in this city that asks a man whether the thing he wrote down at the end of his shift on the first of January may be read out in a room. And I have watched that exact card go up a set of stairs in this district in January, and I have watched it get refused on a doorstep by a woman who has not slept, and I am not going to be the one who asks a foreman's man to surrender a book of his own so that a regional register has a second witness in it. You keep going with the method. I am not asking him."

Selwyn Anum looked at him for about four seconds and then said, "That is the correct decision and I would like it minuted, and I am not going to say who made it, because if I say who made it then it is a man's judgment in a program's file, and in about four years that is the sentence somebody will use, and it will be true and it will be useless."

And there was one more thing in that room about it, and the woman of forty-seven had already worked it out and had been working it out since the eighth of January.

"The four lines are in a drawer in this office," Nerys Hobbs said, "and the drawer is in a building, and the building is a yard, and the yard has a booking until the fifth of February and after the fifth of February it has not got a booking, and on the morning of the tenth of February at a quarter past seven a vehicle is going to come out of a depot and go down an alignment and the only piece of paper in this district that says anything about the four nights of January is going to be in a locked drawer in a yard with nothing booked in it."

"Then it comes out of the drawer," Anum said.

"It does not come out of the drawer," Hobbs said. "It comes out of the drawer and it goes to a table at the counter at number six and it lives on a table with the counter log and the arrival book and it is on a table and not in a drawer, and anybody can walk in on any working day of the week and look at four ruled lines with four dates and four hours and a woman's name at the bottom of them, and nobody signs for it and nobody takes it away, and that is the only arrangement available and I have thought about it for a day and a half and I have not told anybody until now."

"Every working day," Elias Rook said.

"Every working day," Hobbs said, "because that is when the counter is open, Monday to Friday from nine till half past four, and I am not opening a counter on a Sunday to keep a piece of paper dry."

---

Cutbank Row at about two on the Tuesday was cold and bright and there was nothing on the east side of it but skip boards and one light on the third floor, and the sheet was on the nail in the shop, four foot and a strip of shelf, two hundred and forty-one lines and more than two hundred names in the fourth column and nobody counting, and it was nine inches lower than it had been a week before.

Alagoa had a shopkeeper's pencil and put two lines at the top of it, above the first address and under the heading, in her own hand, and she had done it at about half past eleven and had not told anybody.

```
SECOND SATURDAY · 31 JAN · THE CORNER · HALF PAST TEN
nobody in this district is answerable for it. say so at the corner.
```

"That is what I have done and it is the last thing I am going to write on anybody's paper," she said, before he could get to it, "and you are not going to tell me it is too much, because I have been saying for two weeks that I am not a chair and I am not an office and I do not take votes, and then a man came on Monday and told me that three surfaces in this district had all said no to me, and he was right, and I have not got a fourth one, so I have used my own."

"You do not have to be answerable for it."

"I have just made myself answerable for it in the only handwriting on that paper that is not a resident's," Alagoa said. "That is the cost and I know what it is and I did it at about half past eleven this morning and I have not slept. On the fourth Saturday it will be somebody else's turn, because on the fourth Saturday somebody in this row is going to stand in the cold and say it is not fair, and they are right, and then it is going to be a notice with a woman's shopkeeper's pencil at the top of it and a man's coat on the wall, and after that it is a thing with an owner, and there is no form in this city for giving one back. That is what I have bought and I did it on purpose and I would like it left alone until Saturday."

---

He got the 11 at about ten past three and sat on the far side of the cut for two hours in the cold with his right forearm across his knee.

In four days it would be the second Saturday, and in nine days a foreman was going to take eleven names off his own gate with a piece of string, and on the tenth of February at a quarter past seven in the morning a vehicle was going to come out of a depot and go down an alignment with the street shut at twenty to six, and a man was going to sign a check box with a date and a time on it, and the only other record in the world that it had happened would be a book on a bench in a yard that nobody had standing to ask about, and he had spent a day and a half deciding not to ask, and there was no part of any of it that was on a form, and the two lines of pencil at the top of a sheet of paper in a shop said in a woman's own handwriting that nobody in that district was answerable for it, which is a thing about two hundred and forty people can read and exactly one of them wrote.
