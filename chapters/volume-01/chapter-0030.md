# Chapter 30 — The Ward Holds a Shift

It started at seven in the morning on Sunday because the hall wakes up at seven and there was no version of a meeting that started later, and it went to four in the afternoon, and it was a shift rather than a session because nobody sat down for more than nine minutes at a time.

Petar Lisk read the board first, because that was the job, and then the four domains said what they would refuse, in the order they had been written on the wall a week ago, and nobody was allowed to argue with the list until it was finished.

**Mara:** no medic on the line; no predicted outcome, no number and no category, in any document about that wall; and nobody moves a person for their own benefit, including for a stretcher, including for a hospital, including for me.

**Tomas:** no door. Not the door at the far end, not a door we find, not a door we are offered. And my number is written down before anybody goes and I do not get to argue with the instrument, and I want it minuted that the reason I am saying the second half is that four people in this hall are now allowed to say the word *enough* and I am not the only one of them.

**Joon:** no record in one building. Nothing consolidated. No list of the twenty-three anywhere that a person who is not in this room could edit. And I do not publish what a person has told me unless they have told me I may.

**Inez:** no load without a card; no blackouts that were not announced in advance; and the power can be cut by any resident at any hour for any reason or none, including during a survey, including mine, including while I am standing on the ramp.

Then it was Elias's, and he stood up, and it took him a while.

“I do not go below the west apron alone at any hour for any reason. I do not go at all unless four of five of us have said yes in the same week, and if one of the five says no, I do not go, and I do not go and look for somebody to overrule her, and I do not go and come back and mention that I went.” He put his hand on the table. “And one more, which is new, and I do not know how to do it and I am going to say it anyway. I am not going to be the last person to speak on any of this. If there is an objection in this room, it gets said after me, not before, and if I have had the last word then the record says the record is wrong, because I always have the last word. Joon. Change it. Put in *the last word does not close the item*.”

Joon wrote it, and Petar Lisk initialled it, and the hall read it off the board and nobody asked for a vote because it was not the hall's to vote on and everybody understood why.

---

The survey is what the board wanted and what nobody in the building was willing to hand over.

Here is the argument, and it took most of the morning.

Tomas put the case first, because he had a right to after Saturday. If they held, the joint-inspection condition lapsed on the eleventh day, and with it went the resident observer and the copy of every measurement in the residents' file, and the 2031 pour, the brick lining at seven meters, and a buried paired node that had moved the ground twice in eight years went back to being a line in somebody's report about *legacy equipment of unknown configuration*.

“If we hold, the node is the only version of that ground,” he said, “for another cycle. Which is a year, possibly two. And in a year the thing that will have changed is not the ground, it is us. Two of us will not be here. Three of the five will have a different job. There will be a document in a building saying that a community once asked, and then a period of not asking, and a person in ten years will read that period of not asking as a decision, because that is what a period of not asking is.”

“Holding is a decision,” Mara said. “We write it as one. We write the reasons. We file it Tuesday with the reasons attached, and the reasons are the run and the man and the four words, and a board cannot read a period of not asking if the period of not asking says in its own hand why.”

“It cannot read it if the reasons are *we are frightened*,” Tomas said.

“Then the reasons are the reasons,” Mara said, “and one of them is a man behind a wall who told us on Sunday that a body in the run closes the run, and that we are not allowed to decide that is the wrong answer. The 2031 pour is under the apron. The run is under the apron. Going to look at a pour means going to look along a run, and looking along a run on the first day after a road goes missing is a rescue, and we do not do rescues, and it is not a moral position, it is a schedule.”

“That is the whole of the argument and I do not think it is wrong,” Tomas said. “I want it on the minutes in those words, and I want my name on it as the man who said the thing I did not want to hear, and then I am going to go and check the batteries, which are wrong chemistry and which Ru Sabar has already told us about once.”

---

**THE SITE IS HELD.** Eleven days, Tuesday, a written statement of refusal with the reasons on the page, in Inez's hand and five signatures above it and forty-two people's cards on the wall, of which twenty-two say go and fourteen say do not, and the fourteen are on the same sheet with their names.

Cost, named in the same hand as the gain: the joint-inspection condition lapses; the standing designation is unchallenged for a cycle; the 2031 node is not examined; the Office's sealed node becomes the only version of that ground; and the man behind the wall has no way in.

What they prepared instead was the thing that makes the refusal cost something. A method, not an operation: a five-name stop page with an expiry on it, drawn by somebody who is not Elias. A standing refusal from every domain that survives whoever signs the next one. A per-circuit power plan for four draws a day, written in sentences with no box ticked by default, because four people who had never been surveyed could write sentences a rescuer could not. And a rule for what happens if the ground changes: any of the five may re-propose, the hall may re-propose, and a proposal that comes from somebody who was not in the room counts the same as one that comes from the man who was.

“It is a slower plan and it is a plan that has no date on it,” Inez said, “and both of those are the reason it is not a way for one of us to get comfortable.”

---

The promise was the last thing and it took forty minutes and it was the only part anybody argued about.

“If the run comes back,” Elias said, “and a conversation is possible, then I do not go. I do not send anybody. I do not put a plan in front of the hall about going down to a man who has told us he is standing there.”

“That is not enough,” Ansel Duarte said, from the mat by the radiator. “That is you deciding now that somebody else goes, which is the same thing with a longer corridor in it. What stops you putting a plan in front of the room in a month when everybody in here is frightened and you are the only person in this room who has been down that run at all?”

“Ansel.”

“Answer the question,” Ansel said. “You are good at promises that are technically correct. Say the one that has a person in it who can break it.”

Elias stood there for a long time with his notebook closed on the table in front of him and his left hand lying open beside it, and the room waited, and nobody helped him.

“Ansel Duarte may break this promise,” he said, “by saying *that is a rescue*, in this hall, in front of anybody. And if he says it, I do not go, and I do not put a plan, and I do not seek an overrule from the Office, and I do not go looking for somebody with more standing than a nineteen-year-old on a mat. And he does not have to give a reason, and he does not have to be right.”

“I will not always be right,” Ansel said.

“I know. That is why it is you and not me.”

“Write it exactly like that,” Ansel said, “with the *and he does not have to be right* in it, because in five weeks there will be a version of this where that clause has been dropped for being inconvenient, and I want the inconvenient one on the paper.”

Petar Lisk wrote it out in full, and read it back, and made Elias initial it, and then made Ansel initial it, and then made Inez initial it as the person who holds the copy, which is a thing that had never happened in fifteen days and cost nobody anything and took two minutes.

---

Joon found the last thing at four in the afternoon, when he was closing the node query for the week, and he did it in front of the room because he did not do anything alone any more.

“The audio index,” he said. “The eleven seconds from the first week. The one a crew voice said into, the one that is in five buildings. The copy in the sealed node is zero point four seconds longer than ours.”

“Longer how,” Elias said.

“Not longer at the end. Longer in the middle. There is a gap of zero point four seconds in the node copy at a point where ours has nothing, and where the gap is, the waveform is flat, and flat is what a carrier sounds like when nothing is being said on it.”

The room went quiet.

“I am not writing a word under that,” Joon said, and he put his pencil down and picked it up again and put it down. “A file that is longer in a copy than in the original is a transmission. It is a compression artifact. It is a clock. It is a person with a different scanner. It is a second copy made from a different original, which we cannot rule out because we have never seen the node's original. Or it is nothing. I will not put a name to it and I will not put a fear to it, and I am asking this room not to, and I am asking Director Arden not to, when she sees it, because the second somebody who builds a meaning on a quarter of a second is the one who decides what it meant.”

“Record it,” Elias said. “The fact and not the meaning.”

“The fact and not the meaning,” Joon said, “and nobody reads the fact out loud in a room without me in it.”

---

At seven Inez read the board, all of it, in order, and the hall listened, and the last line was the one Petar had put there at his own initiative without being asked, which he had started doing on Thursday and which was now, in the estimation of a nine-year-old with a clipboard, the most important job in the building.
