# Chapter 37 — Saturday

Saturday was a day of antibiotics and small repairs and one argument about a number, and the argument was better than the repairs and the repairs were the reason the argument had a floor to stand on.

Lena Mir did the ward round at nine with two nurse-assistants and no physician in the building, because that is now the arrangement and the arrangement has not been changed by anybody in a hurry. She did the twenty-three — twenty-two, from eleven o'clock — in fifty-one minutes and she wrote it on one page in plain sentences, and the page is the only clinical document in the building that anybody can read without a key.

Oren Pike walked to the end of the hall and back and was not winded. Anja Ro did the same carrying a full water can. Ruta Amsel had the brace off for the first time in nine days and cried about it and then stopped. Tarin Voss asked, once, whether anybody had put his name back, and was told no, and said good, and went back to reading.

Emil Baros came down the ramp at ten with his clipboard and did not sign anything, because the keeper's line is eleven meters away and he could not reach it, and he wrote instead at the bottom of his own sheet: *Keeper present. Nothing changed since Monday.* Nine days of that, every morning, in his own hand.

The dark hours came at two, the way they come every night, and Petar Lisk walked them with a hand lamp and a door to open, and on Saturday he had a second person with him because the nine-year-old had asked and Inez had said yes and Ru Sabar had volunteered and had not been thanked for volunteering, and Inez was not going to start now.

Perry Dunne came at eleven with his toolbox and did not knock, because he has never once knocked, and he put the box down and said, “I want to say a thing and then I am going to fix the closer on the records room, which has been off since Friday because of the lamp and because it is a door that people are now using at night.”

He waited until Inez looked at him, because Perry Dunne has learned in four weeks that a thing said at a woman who is busy is a thing that gets recorded and not heard.

“My clean week was never delivered,” he said. “That is not anybody's fault. The crew thinned and it went on the second Friday and I got the second date and I put both of them on the board with my name on them and I asked for nobody to call it a favor, and I meant all of that, and I am not raising it as a grievance. I am raising it because the Service took two men off you and this building has spent three weeks rearranging itself around not needing them, and now there are two technicians from a regional pool in your street under an Office chain doing in a morning what three of your own people were doing, and that is the best news I have had since I got here and I do not trust it.”

Nobody said anything, because there was nothing to say that was not either false or worse.

“So I am asking again, in the same words, for a week where none of you are on this street, and I am asking it of a hall that has one rope ticket, a nine-year-old with a clipboard and a woman whose powered frame is on somebody's arithmetic. Take the time you have got,” Perry Dunne said, and picked up the toolbox, “and do not tell me it is a favor, and when it is real I will be here on the Friday, and if it never comes I am not going to ask you twice, because I have watched a man in this hall be asked twice and I did not like it.”

At four the hall came up in three stages, and Petar read the board's first line in the dark with the lamp on his own shoes, which nobody has ever let him forget.

---

The form came at half past ten in the morning, and it was the only thing in three weeks that came by courier with a signature block on it, and it was four lines long.

*Population of record — designated site, daily return. Submit count and date. Return is mandatory under the classification. A count of persons present is sufficient. A count of persons present is the only return this office will accept.*

“I have read it four times,” Sable's letter underneath said, which was four lines longer, “and I know exactly what it looks like, and I know who it will look like to, and I am sending it anyway, because I am not permitted to send you anything else and because a Director who edits a return to make it palatable has stopped being a check on anything. I have asked for an exception and been refused an exception, and the person who refused it is right, and you should know that he is right before you decide to be angry.”

Elias put the form on the table.

Twenty-three. That was the number. It had been the number since the second day, and it was the number in four buildings, and it was on every return the Office had filed for three weeks.

Nadi Bell read it standing up, which she does now, and then she sat down and read it again.

“No,” she said.

“Nadi —”

“No, and I want the whole of it on the page, and I want you all to hear the shape of it before I say the rest, because in two weeks one of you is going to argue that I was being clever.” She turned the form around so it faced the room. “This is not an insult. This is a form with four lines and a signature block and it asks for one thing, and the one thing it asks for is the thing that took my sister. A count of persons present. That is it. That is the whole of the instrument. It does not ask who they are, it does not ask when they arrived, it does not ask whether they are leaving, and it cannot be amended afterward by anybody, because you cannot amend a number, and therefore nobody ever has to decide anything, and therefore a person can be subtracted from a page by an arithmetic error and the page will still be correct.”

She put her hand flat on it.

“My mother used to say that a number cannot lose anybody, and she said it in nineteen forty-nine about a hospital list, and it is still true, and it is the most dangerous sentence anybody has ever said to me.” Nadi Bell straightened up. “I am not going to supply it. I am not going to supply a count of persons present, today or any day, and I will put my refusal in writing to the Director by this evening with my name on it, and I will not put anybody else's name on it, and the hall can decide separately and I will not stop the hall.”

“Which leaves what,” Elias said.

“Which leaves a different return.” She had it ready, and she had had it ready since the day before yesterday, which is the thing about her that nobody in the hall has ever managed to be surprised by. “Not a count. A **movement record**. Dated. One line per person. In, out, or here. If out: where to, with whom, by what ordinary road, and whether they chose it. If here: why they are staying, in their own words, in whatever length of sentence they want, including one word and including no words at all and a mark. And nobody's name goes to the Office without the person telling me it can, and if a person does not want their line anywhere but in this building then their line stays in this building and the Office gets *a person is here* and nothing else.”

“That is not a return the Office will accept,” Joon said, reading it.

“No,” Nadi said. “It is not a return the Office will accept, and that is the second reason I like it, and the second reason is the one I am going to say out loud so that nobody in this room can pretend I only had the first one. It is not acceptable, which means the Office keeps a number and we keep a record, and the number is wrong, and the record is right, and in four years somebody is going to pull both and find out which one lost a person. I am not being brave. I am putting two versions of the truth in two buildings so that the one that erases people is outvoted by the one that does not.”

Joon wrote the refusal for her and read it back and she signed it, and then she read the movement record aloud to the room in the flat voice she uses for things she has decided, and there were twenty-two lines on it and nineteen of them had something after *here* that was longer than one word.

---

Niko Senn said it at four in the afternoon, in the dry end, by the window, to four people and no audience, which is how he wanted it.

He had asked Lena Mir on Wednesday. He had not asked Elias, and when Elias found out he sat down without saying anything about it, because the rule on that wall is that Joon is the only person who asks anybody anything about a person who has not offered it.

“I am not leaving because I am better,” Niko said. “I want that said first because I know what it looks like. I have been in this hall for twenty-one days and I got the second one, the one where somebody writes down what I said in my own words, and that is the only time in three weeks that anything in this building has belonged to me, and it is because a document has a field with my name in it.”

He put his hands flat on his knees.

“EVERYTHING in here is about a road that used to be there,” he said. “That is not a complaint about the road. That is a complaint about me. My sister is on the east bank and I have a bed there and I am going to walk to her on Sunday morning on an ordinary road, on a bus, with a ticket, and it is going to take two hours, and nobody is going to write anything down about it, and I have decided that I would like to be a person who has done one ordinary thing.”

“You do not have to explain it,” Elias said.

“I know. I have explained it because I have been in a building for three weeks where every single thing that happens to me gets written down by somebody, and I am not refusing to be written down, and I am saying that I would like one decision that is mine and does not go in a file, and I would like it to be *I am going to my sister's house*, and that is the whole of it.”

“All right,” Elias said. “Sunday. On the bus.”

Joon updated the record at ten past five, at the table, with Niko Senn standing there holding his coat, which is how it is done, because the number and the person are supposed to be in the same room at the same time and everybody watching.

**PAPER COUNT: TWENTY-TWO. One movement out, tomorrow morning, ordinary road, own choice, own words, unaccompanied by any instrument. Line removed from the population line. Line added to the movement record. The person is not a case any more. He is a person who left.**

Petar Lisk read it out at seven, along with everything else, and the second line of the board was a new one that Inez had written in chalk.

**WE ARE TWENTY-TWO. WE ARE NOT A NUMBER. HERE IS WHO IS HERE, WHO IS GOING, WHERE THEY ARE GOING, AND WHO SAID SO.**
