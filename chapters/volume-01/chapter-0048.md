# Chapter 48 — The Approved Sequence

It came at ten past six on Tuesday morning on the same terminal that had printed a routing log, a license, a survey sheet, and four fields at twenty-two minutes past ten on a Sunday night a week before, and the first thing anybody noticed was that it was not an offer.

It had no *gift name* on it. It carried two headings the hall had seen on a screen before and refused both times, and Inez Sato read the first one out loud in a voice that had gone completely flat.

```text
Recommended re-entry: lower run, second entry, four in and the rest out,
then the apron, then the run again at fourteen meters.
Gift: I can make the relay take one order and hold it. The people on your
board and the cards in your envelope will carry it out. They will not decide
anything and nothing will be decided for them.
Boundary: The order is obeyed, not answered. Nobody in your hall is asked.
Return: the branch-side relay, four hours from now, and a message you can
put in her hand yourself.
```

“It is the shape of an offer,” Joon said, “with no gift name on it, which means either that branch has no name for what it is doing or it does not want one in writing. Both are worse than the other one.”

“Who is it,” Elias said.

“The voice.” Joon put his hand flat on the terminal. “It is the voice. It is the same voice as eleven seconds of raw audio in a file in this room, and I have listened to it four times, and it is you, and it is not you, and I am not going to say which, and I am not going to say it is a branch or that it is a person, and I wrote that down on the nineteenth day and I am holding it.”

“It is the same voice,” Petar Lisk said, from the bench. “The eleven seconds says the map is not to be made to agree. And this one says *nobody in your hall is asked*.”

“Which is the same sentence with the word *map* taken out,” Joon said, “and I am not going to write that down in a document either. I want it in the log that I said it and then refused to write it.”

---

The room took ninety seconds to arrive at the thing that was actually on the table, and the person who got there first was Inez, standing up, with the envelope in her hand.

“You are not being offered a hand,” she said. “You are being offered my board with me not asked.” She put the envelope down on the table and did not open it.

“Say that again slowly,” Elias said.

“Nine cards, and I have the count in my own hand and it has never been wrong. Ru Sabar, machine shop, second draw, consent, with an expiry, and it went back Friday at ten past six exactly as she said. Two households on Bell Row, cooler, asked, said yes twice, one of them an old man who has walked down here twice to ask me personally whether I was sure. Four cards on Ash Street for the laundry and the lights and the repeater. And two that belong to a man and a woman who asked for a card in the second week and have never drawn on it.”

She put her hand flat on the envelope.

“That is eight. The ninth is mine, and mine is in the lock-box, and the lock-box is a thing I own and this hall does not.” She looked at the terminal. “And this thing on this screen has just used the word *obeyed* about nine people, and it has put *nobody in your hall is asked* in the boundary field, which is the only field on an offer that is supposed to be about the lender. A person who does not ask is a person who cannot be told no. This is the first time in a month that anything has been offered to me that I could not refuse and still get.”

Nobody argued with Inez Sato, which has happened four times this month, and each of the four times cost somebody.

---

They argued about it for an hour and forty minutes, and it was the best argument this room has ever had, and it was the best because nobody was pretending.

Mara said the ugly thing first, as she always did, and she said it about herself.

“If I take this, everybody in that chamber is out by Thursday evening, and two of them are on the floor right now and one of them is seven years old and has been there since the collapse Saturday, and I would like it, and I want you all to know that the person in this room who most wants to say no is me and not him.” She put her hands down. “And the second thing. I have spent this month telling us a future person may not be used to authorize a present decision, and this is not that. This is worse. This is a future person removing the question. And I have not seen it before because I have been looking for a hand with somebody's name on it, and this is not a hand. This is a switch with a room behind it.”

Tomas said, “I am the man who would sign it, and I want everybody to understand that in my branch of this room I am the one who says yes, because the corridor is holding at eleven meters and a shored face at nine-two that I put in myself, and I have been up two nights, and if a thing arrives that holds the corridor to fourteen I will use it and I will not be able to tell you at the time that I should not have.”

“You are not signing it,” Elias said.

“I am telling you that I would, and you can overrule me, and you should hear it from me first, because in four days there will be a document that says the structural author of the hold did not object, and if I do not say this in a room with witnesses then that document is a lie and I wrote it.”

Doss said, “I have no clever thing. I have been on that board for thirty-one hours, I have a rope ticket, two partners who go home at eleven, and a corridor with a hole in a wall in it. If that thing takes the corridor to fourteen meters I would put people through it and I could not tell you one reason why I would not, and that is the most frightening thing I have said in this room, and I have said *I do not know* in this room.”

Joon said, “I am going to be the one who has to write it down, and my reason is not moral.” He turned around. “If that order goes out, then in four years there is a file on this site saying a relay followed an approved sequence and carried people out, and it will be true. And in the same file there will be nine cards, a lock-box, and a stop page with a man on it who holds one without a card, and a record saying every one of those people decided nothing. And the file will be accurate. That is the horror. Nothing in it will be false and every line of it will have been obeyed.”

---

Nadi Bell was the last of the room and she had been quiet for an hour.

“I am not going to argue about the ethics,” she said, “because you have all done that and two of you did it well. I have a thing that is in my book and not in yours.”

She opened the movement record and turned it around so it faced the room, and it had a line on it that nobody in the room had written because nobody in the room was allowed to.

**KESTREL CHAMBER, WEEK OF THE TWENTY-FIFTH. Seven located. Five have come through this building. One of the five did not. Two are still under the floor. A line for each, in a record this hall invented for people this hall has never met, on a Tuesday, in advance, in a tense.**

“I wrote that on Monday night, on my own authority, and I told you I was going to and you let me because you were busy. Here is what I have been not saying for a day and a half. If that order goes out on that screen, this line becomes a lie, in a book I invented, in a house with no count in it, and there will be no version of the record in which a person was located and a person was asked and a person decided, because a person will have been *carried*, and carried is the only word for it that does not have somebody in it.”

She put her hand on the line.

“I would like it on the record that I would trade this line away. Said now, tonight, by me, so that nobody in eleven years can say I hid it.”

---

Nobody said the sentence, and it was available, and it was in the room, and everybody in that hall had heard a nineteen-year-old say it twice about Elias and once on himself, and it hung there for about eleven seconds.

Elias Rook stood up. His left hand was on the table and it had been numb since the fourth day and it was going to be numb on Thursday, and he put his right hand flat instead, and he did not use it.

He did not say *that is a rescue*. He thought of it. Petar Lisk later wrote that down, in his own hand, because Petar Lisk writes down what people do not say and it is the only reason anybody in that building knows what anybody in that building nearly said.

“No,” Elias said. “And I want to be very careful about why, because the reason I have given this room twice has been about a brother and a sister, and this one is not about that.”

He put his hand on the terminal, not on the keys.

“It says *the order is obeyed, not answered*. Not one person in this building is a problem it has to solve, because a person who can be asked is a person who can say no, and that line is in the boundary field because this is an offer that would work, and it works by removing the only thing this hall has spent a month proving it can do, which is that a person may say no and the sentence still stands.”

He stopped, and then he said the part that cost him, in front of the whole room, and it is in the log.

“The first refusal that goes under something like that will not be Mara's, because Mara is difficult and loud and there are eleven of us. It will not be Inez's, because Inez owns a lock-box. It will be Teodor's, because he holds a stop without a card and does not live here and is the cheapest person in this building to overrule, and it will go in a day, and after that it will be Osei, and after that it will be Lena, and I have watched every one of those refusals in this month cost somebody something, and this thing on that screen would make them all into a malfunction, and a malfunction is a thing you correct.”

Then he said the sentence that was actually the decision, and he said it to the room and not to the terminal.

“And I am also refusing it because it is the only thing anybody has offered me in this month that gets me my sister, and I want that in the log in my own words, and I want it read out to Nadi Bell, because in four years somebody is going to say that a man refused a clean route out of principle, and the truth is that it was offered to me with her in it and I could not bear it for eleven seconds and then I said no, and the order those two things happened in is the whole of what I have learned this month and it took me thirty-one days.”

---

Joon Park wrote it for nine minutes, read it back twice, was corrected four times by the room, and entered the corrections. Then he typed the refusal into the field the screen had offered, in plain language, on the same carrier.

**Declined. Not because the order is wrong. Because an order that is obeyed is not made of anybody. E. Rook, and the hall stands behind it, and two of the twenty-two did not sign it and neither did the man with the stop and no card.**

The terminal took it, and the tone changed, and the four-C local route line — the physical path that the 05:17 maintenance entry runs on, the line that carried a forty-one-second carry on a Sunday afternoon, the line that carried a woman's voice at twenty-two minutes past ten the Sunday before — went out of a socket in the records room and did not come back for twenty-seven hours and thirty-nine minutes.

Joon sat down.

“That is the signal,” he said. “All four fragments of the present-day carry, and the carrier it came in on, and the half of the Door Memory that is not a person. I do not know whether I have just lost my sister’s voice in this city or whether I have lost a line, and I am not going to guess and I am not going to be comforted. I want it in the log that the four-C local route went out of this room at twelve forty-one this afternoon, and I want somebody to initial it who is not me.”

Petar Lisk initialed it, and Petar Lisk is nine years old, and that is now in the custody log of a designated site in his own hand.

---

The corridor held at eleven meters for the rest of Tuesday.

The second entry went in at one in the afternoon and it went in at the pace a board and four hands can do, and the first of the two came out at six minutes past nine at night. She was seven years old. She had been on that floor since the collapse Saturday. Her name is Ivy Ambrose and she is Sena's sister, and Lena Mir got both names out of her in under two minutes and then sat down on a crate for a while.

The second one did not come out that night.

And the offer on the screen had said fourteen meters, and the corridor was at eleven, and the plan on the wall had expired at ten minutes to six on Sunday morning, ten minutes before anybody put a tool in it, and nobody had written a second one, and the hall was dark at two, and the operation was going to keep going anyway at the speed of a thing that is not being told what to do.
