# Chapter 43 — The Argument About the Number

The form came on Thursday morning by courier, and it was four lines, and one of them was new, and the new one had been added overnight by a clerk in a building the team cannot enter.

*Daily return, designated site. Persons present. Persons recovered from site. Estimated survival window for persons not yet recovered.*

The first two lines were the return Nadi Bell had refused three weeks ago and the last line was a box with nothing in it, and the middle line was new, and the middle line was the one nobody in the hall had ever seen before.

“They added it Tuesday night,” Joon said. He had the routing slip in his hand. “It has a form number and it has a revision letter, which means somebody signed a change to a return, and the change is in the middle of the form, not at the end, where changes go. I am not saying that on the page. I am saying it to you and I am writing *revision in the middle of the form* in the custody log in my own hand, because if a form gets edited again I want it in the log that I noticed the first one.”

“What happens to the middle line if we answer it,” Elias said.

“That is the question,” Joon said, “and the answer is that we have exactly one name for those people and it is a letter. **Group D** is on our own sheet from the fourth day, in a clerk's hand, because the form wanted a group and the clerk wanted a letter. Four people in four buildings have read that letter and I am one of them. The only reason we know it is seven is that a technician from a regional pool would not sign a number she had not counted herself, and what she signed is six, and the difference between her six and the machine's eight is a stove and a man who will not lie still. I am not objecting to the letter. I am putting it next to everything else that is in a document and is not a person, and I am writing down that a stove in a record is a thing that later becomes a person.”

“That is the question, and Nadi Bell asked it out loud in this room eleven minutes before you did, and she asked it to the courier, who was still in the doorway.”

---

Nadi Bell was standing, which she does now.

“I refused this form three weeks ago and I refused it in my own name and I did not put anybody else's name on it, and everything I said then is still true, and I am going to say the part where my refusal does not cover what is in that box.” She put her finger on the last line without touching the paper. “I said a count of persons present cannot lose anybody. I said it about this building. I said it about people who are *here*, who have a line in a book with their own words against the word *here*, and who can read the book. That is a real protection and I still believe every word of it.”

“Then it does not cover them,” Elias said.

“It does not cover them at all, and I want that said in the first minute and not the fortieth, because I have watched this room be very good at a rule and terrible at the edge of it.” She turned around. “There is no line in my book for a man sitting up against a far wall nine meters past a brick lining that no drawing in four buildings holds. There is no line in my book for a person whose name nobody in this building has ever heard, and I do not have a rule for that person, and I have spent three weeks telling this room that a rule you cannot point at is not a protection.”

Mara said, “Then the second line does not get answered either, and we write *not applicable* and we go and open a wall.”

“No,” Nadi said. “And I have thought about it for a day and a night and I am going to say the sentence I came in to say, and I am going to say it before anybody agrees with me, because if I say it after somebody agrees then it is a thing I was handed.”

She put her hand flat on the table, next to the courier's slip, without touching it.

---

“A count is a number of bodies,” Nadi Bell said. “A movement record is a set of sentences about people. That is the whole difference and I have been saying it for three weeks as though it were about counting and it is not, it is about the tense.”

“The tense,” Joon said.

“The tense. *A person is here* is present. *We brought four people out on the second day* is past. *We knew on the nineteenth day that there were people in a chamber and we decided on the twenty-sixth* is past, and there is no way to write that line in the present tense, and you cannot write it in the present tense because you do not know it yet, and everybody in this room knows you do not know it yet, so the only honest tense for that box is the one you are all frightened of.” She tapped the last line. “*Estimated survival window.* Future. Nobody in this hall knows one. Not one of us. And I will not write a number in a box that a courier will carry to a building in which my sister is a subtraction.”

Somebody at the back said, “Then leave it blank.”

“Yes,” Nadi said. “Leave it blank. And leave it blank *in our copy* and not only on the courier's, because a blank field is not a refusal, and I have been told by an archivist in this city that a field which can be blank can be filled by somebody who was not there and be entirely accurate about their own afternoon, and I am not going to leave a blank in a form that four agencies reconcile.”

She picked up a pen, and put it down again.

“But I am not finished, and this is the part I came in to say.”

---

“Extend the record,” Nadi Bell said. “Not the count. The record. To the people we have never met.”

Nobody said anything, and she went on, because she always went on.

“Here is my argument and I am going to lay it out in order and I am not going to be interrupted, and if it is wrong somebody can take it apart after, not during. One. I have refused a number because a number cannot lose anybody and a name can. Two. For seven people in a chamber we have no names, and we are going to have them by Thursday evening, and a name is only a name in a document that says *person*, not *body*. Three.” She turned to face the room properly. “There is a form coming back to a building on the Meridian with a box in it that says *persons recovered from site*, and whoever fills that box fills it with a number, and that number is going to be in the incident file forever, and it is going to be the only sentence anybody reads about seven people, and it is going to read either *four* or *seven*, and both of those are wrong and neither of them is wrong in a way anybody can complain about. Four.” Her voice did not rise. “I cannot make them a name yet. So I am going to make them a **line**, and the line is going to be about what this hall did, in a tense, with a date, and it is going to be true whatever the number turns out to be, and it is going to be checkable by a person who was not in this room, which is the only kind of record there has ever been worth.”

She turned back to the table.

“A movement record is not a list of people. It is a list of what people were allowed to do, in their own words, with a date on it. You do not need to know somebody's name to write down that they were nine meters under a street for twenty-five days and that we chose on Tuesday not to look. That sentence needs no name and it is the sentence that matters.”

---

Joon wrote it for four minutes and read it back twice, and then he did the thing he does, which is the part that makes him necessary.

“I am going to say the cost out loud before I put the pen down,” he said. “I learned it from a man at a wall, and he learned it in a building where nobody wrote anything down.”

Everybody waited.

“If that line goes in, then on the day the seal goes on this site there will be a document in this hall that says we knew. And Sable Arden said something in the second week that I have written down and I am going to read it back, because it is the sentence this row will be used against.” He found the page. “*I would rather look and be blamed than not look and be thanked.* And: *when it eventually happens, the question in front of every inquiry in this city will be why nobody looked, and the answer will be because the neighborhood preferred not to have anybody look, and that answer will be true.*”

He put the page down.

“That answer is going to be more true on Friday than it was on Wednesday, because we looked on Wednesday and then stopped at four minutes to eight because of a condition she wrote in her own hand, and one day somebody is going to hold those two facts next to each other and the second one will do the work.” He squared the paper. “I am not arguing against the line. I am arguing that we write it knowing which way it will be pointed, because a record that has only ever been pointed one way is a weapon, and I would like ours to still be readable in ten years by a person who hates us.”

“Write it anyway,” Nadi said.

“Write it anyway,” Joon agreed, and did.

---

Mara filled in the middle line and it took her ninety seconds, and she read it out twice.

*Persons recovered from site to date: four, carried out by this building's crew on the second day. Persons located under this site on the twenty-fifth day: four confirmed, one probable, one unknown, and a heat source of undetermined origin. The machine says nine. The difference is four people.*

“Read the second sentence again,” Joon said, and she did.

Then she took the courier's form and wrote in the last box, in one line, and turned it around so the whole room could read it upside down.

*No estimate can be made by this building. Nothing in this room knows one. The question was not answered because nobody here is qualified to answer it, and this office has been told so in writing.*

“I am not writing *unknown*,” Mara said, “because *unknown* is a technical term and a technical term in a box reads as a finding. I am writing a sentence, and the sentence is true, and a sentence can be argued with.”

“What happens to the classification,” Elias said, “when the return carries a sentence.”

“It carries a sentence and a number,” Joon said, “and on Thursday the number is four, and if we go through that wall on Friday or Saturday or Sunday and come out with six, then there is a document in this hall that says four and a chamber that says six, and somebody in an archive in four years is going to sit down with those two numbers and ask which one the hall was told and which one the hall was holding, and I would like it to be on the page that we were told the other one first and wrote it down anyway.”

He wrote that down too.

---

The plan that came out of the room at four in the afternoon was not a rescue, and Petar Lisk wrote the difference on the board at seven in his own capitals because nobody else had said it out loud.

**A PLAN HAS A TIME ON IT. A RESCUE HAS A PERSON ON IT.**

Five domains, and each of them said its own piece and nobody else's, and the pieces did not fit and they wrote it anyway.

Tomas took the ground: the brick lining at nine-two is a wall somebody built, and it is a wall and not a fault, and the only way seven people come out of that chamber is a hole in it, and the hole has to be a person wide, and the chain says the survey stops at nine and a half and a hole is not a survey. **And the ground above the chamber is the ground with the 2038 pour in it at thirty-eight centimeters, and his number stands, and the 2031 contractor survey puts two hundred and ten meters of migrating void somewhere under this street, and nobody has ever been told where.**

Mara took the person: she will not write a protocol, she will not write a survival window, and she will be the physician at the mouth, and what happens to a person between the chamber and the street she decides in the corridor, out loud, one at a time, and it is not written down first.

Inez took the power: the instruments are a carded draw until eleven, the hall is dark at two, and if the operation is still running at two then the hall is dark at two, and she is not moving the dark hours, and the hall has four carded draws a day and a carded draw is somebody's lamp.

Joon took the record: two copies, one in the hall and one that goes out of the hall on Sunday on a bus by an ordinary road, and the second copy is not in this building and he has not said where and is not going to.

Elias took the rope.

The specific obstacle was on the board at four in the afternoon and it was six words and Petar wrote them and they were the only six words nobody in the room could argue with.

**THE WALL IS THE ONLY WAY IN.**
