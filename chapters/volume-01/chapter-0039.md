# Chapter 39 — The Old Relay

Sunday started with a sound and ended with a road, and the six hours between them were the longest so far.

The sound came at four in the afternoon on a carrier Joon had a name for, and he had the room inside ninety seconds, which was a personal record for a Sunday.

“It is the four C local route line,” he said. “It is the same physical path as the five seventeen maintenance entry, which runs through the twenty meters of the lower service run that were there on Monday and were not there on Tuesday morning, and which I have twice refused to interpret next to anything. There is now a third thing on it.”

He put two prints on the table.

“One. A carry, at four this afternoon, forty-one seconds, with nothing on it. No voice, no words, no compression. It is a tone, and a tone is somebody holding a channel open, and a channel held open for forty-one seconds at four in the afternoon on a Sunday is a person, or a machine that a person has set going.”

“And the second.”

“The second arrived at ten past two this morning, and I missed it, and I have been sick about that since four o'clock, and I am telling the room I missed it.” Joon's hand was flat on the print. “It came up on a licensed broker's branch circuit. Site four C. It is in the routing log with three words against it and a broker of record, and the broker of record did not put it there, because the broker of record was asleep, and the routing log does not care who was asleep.”

“Meaning what,” Elias said.

“Meaning the branch route that Adare is licensed to sell is carrying a voice that his circuit cannot account for, and the only way that happens is if somebody on the far end of a branch route has a channel that behaves like a relay handoff.” Joon looked up. “And it is the same kind of handoff as the nine characters on Saturday night, and I am not putting those two things next to each other in a document, and I am putting them next to each other in my head, and I have done that before and it cost me a week, so somebody please say something.”

“Direction,” Elias said.

“Direction,” Joon agreed. “Not a place. A channel held open for forty-one seconds from the far side of a branch route that runs under an apron, on a path that goes through a corridor that is eleven meters long and used to be thirty-one. And I want to say the thing about it out loud, because Elias is going to need to hear it in front of witnesses: **I cannot tell you where she is, and I cannot tell you how to get to her, and the difference between those two sentences is the entire subject of this month.**”

---

Elias built a plan by half past five and it took him twenty minutes and it was, by any reasonable measure, good.

He wrote it on a fresh sheet and he wrote it in the hall with everybody in it, and it had three stages and a timing and a name at the top, and it got as far as the second stage before Nadi Bell put her hand on the paper.

“Say out loud what stage two is,” she said.

“Access. We go to the old relay, through the pour, from the west side, with the chain and the technicians and a shoring plan, and we get to the relay and we see what the far end of a channel looks like when it is being held open.”

“By a shoring plan, you mean by moving ground that has a pour in it from twenty thirty-eight with a crack in it that Tomas has said nothing is to touch.”

“By a shoring plan that obeys Tomas's number.”

“Tomas's number is *nothing touches it until we know what is under it*,” Nadi said, “and *we know what is under it* is the last clause, and you are putting it in a document with a date on it.” She did not raise her voice, and the hall went quiet in a way that had nothing to do with authority. “Elias. You are not writing a survey. I have read four surveys. This is a retrieval with three stages and a name at the top, and you are calling it access because I would throw it out if you called it what it is.”

He looked at the paper for a while.

“Yes,” he said.

“Then what is the honest sentence,” Mara said.

Elias put the pen down. His left hand was lying open on the table beside the sheet, and it had been doing that for twenty minutes, and nobody had said anything about it and Petar Lisk had written the time on the corner of the sheet without interrupting anybody.

“The honest sentence,” Elias said, “is that there is a channel held open from somewhere my sister is, and that the shortest way to learn anything is to put a pair of technicians and a shoring plan over a pour nobody has examined, and that every part of that is a thing I would be telling a board was justified. And I want it in my own hand that I did not get talked out of it. I got caught out of it by a woman who prices people.”

Mara said, “The medical part, since you will forget it in the morning.” She had the ledger and did not open it. “There is no protocol for this and there is not going to be one, and nobody in this room is authorized to describe in advance what a route is for, because the second that is written down it becomes the instruction, and the instruction is what the first loan was. If we get to a relay and there is somebody at the other end of a held-open channel, then what we do is not a plan. It is whatever that person says, in their own air, at their own pace, and I will not be told in advance.”

“Seconded,” Tomas said, “and I want it in the minutes that I seconded a medical refusal, because in 2029 I signed a rule that made one man's reroute legal and I have spent eight years telling people that the rule was about structure.”

Ansel Duarte, from the mat, said the thing that the room needed and nobody had asked for.

“Use the sentence,” he said. “My sentence. Because right now the reason you are not going to do that plan is that a woman with a school coat put her hand on it, and in three weeks the reason is going to be that everybody is frightened and there is a channel and a chair is empty on Tuesday, and I am not going to be consulted in a room I am not in.” He closed the book. “You do not need me for this one. I am saying that out loud so that you cannot pretend later that you did.”

Elias said, “I know.”

“And I am going to hold on to having offered,” Ansel said. “That is all. Put it in the log.”

Joon put it in the log.

---

At fourteen minutes past ten, on Sunday night, on the same terminal that had printed the routing log that morning, a notice came up, and it came up on the broker's circuit, and Adare was asleep, and nobody in that room had asked for it.

```text
Loan offered: Nell Rook, nineteen years ahead
Gift name: Door Memory
Gift: I know a way out of here. I have walked it. It is there this minute.
Boundary: I will not open it for one person and let it close on the rest.
Return: When you have written down that you did not use it.
```

Nobody spoke for a long time.

“That is her,” Elias said, and his voice was not steady and he did not try to make it.

“That is a possible version of your sister, nineteen years ahead, on a channel we cannot trace, offering a memory of a route in a branch that is not this one,” Joon said, and it was the single most important sentence he said in three weeks, and he said it in exactly the voice he used for a count. “Which is a person. And I am not going to be the man who says *don't answer her*, because I have spent a fortnight refusing to speak for people on the other side of this wall and this is the first time one of them has addressed me directly.”

“It came up on the broker's line,” Mara said.

“Yes,” Joon said. “Which means the only wire in this city that can carry a message to her is a wire a man is licensed to sell, and he is asleep, and he will be awake in the morning, and he will be owed a fee.”

Nobody in that room said the next sentence for about a minute and then Elias said it, because it was the true one and it was the worst one.

“I could send the envelope,” he said. “Tonight. On the only line in the world that can carry it.”

“Say the rest of it,” Joon said.

“The rest of it is that the road is there, and it costs money, and the money buys a person in a ring on Calder Street standing up all night, and I have been in that room and I have seen what it is made of, and I have spent three weeks telling this hall that I would not put a price on a person who is not on any list, and a message from me to my sister costs a night of somebody's feet.” He put both hands flat on the table. “So the envelope is still unsent, and the reason has changed, and I am not going to pretend the old reason was the good one. The old reason was that I was afraid of a survey. This one is better and I do not like it better.”

Mara said, “That is the first thing you have said all night that I believe you will still be able to say in a month.”

---

She spoke twice more, at ten forty and again at eleven, and both times it was very short, and both times three people wrote down exactly what was said and read it back in the morning, because a person on a channel that is worth money gets one chance to say the true thing.

The first time, the want.

“Keep the exit on my side open. Not for me. I am not asking about me. I am asking about the people standing near it, and about the ones your man at the wall is feeding on Tuesdays, and about the ones no list has. When it shuts, it shuts for them and not for you, because you are on the other side of the wall and you have always been on the other side of the wall.”

The second time, the boundary, which came with a cost attached and which made the room go quiet for the second time in an hour.

“If you come through, it comes through. That is not a warning and it is not a condition, it is the shape of the thing. There is a door here that is held by people, and it is held by people because it is nobody's business, and the only reason a person holds a door is that nobody has made it official. The moment it is official it is closed, and the moment it is closed they are standing in a place that no list has and that no list will ever have and that is not a branch anybody has a name for.” A pause. “Do not make my road into an Office document. That is the whole of what I am saying. Do not make it into a document and I will keep answering, and make it into a document and I will stop, and you will not know why, and I will not be able to tell you why.”

The third time was the reason, and she gave it in nine words, and Elias wrote it down himself because he could not trust anybody else with the pen.

“You published your own objection,” she said. “That is the only reason it is safe to say anything from here. You published what you did not know and then you did not pretend you had known it later, and I have been reading that board in a place with no board, and it is the only document I have found that is not lying to me.”

Then she gave them the clue, and she gave it fast, and she said the words were expensive.

“Three things and then I go. It is not the door you have been walking. It is not the road that is eleven meters long, though the eleven is the same eleven. It is on the other side of the wall that is not a wall, and it is under the water, and it is under the water because the water is the only thing anybody on my side is not allowed to touch.” A pause. “And when you get near it, do not count. I know what you want to ask and I am telling you that a count is the last thing that should come down that road, and I am telling you that as a person who has watched a count take a person off a list in a building with no list, which I am not going to describe because describing it would make it findable.”

Joon looked at Elias.

“Under the water,” he said. “The relay is under the water table. The 2031 pour terminates six meters short of where the cameras point, and there is a pour from 2038 with a crack in it that nobody is allowed to touch, and the buried paired node is a *node*, and a node needs the other end of a pair to be somewhere that is not a machine room.” He wrote one line and read it back. *The relay is not under the apron. The relay is on the far side of the wall the corridor ends at, and it is below the water line, and the man behind that wall has been standing on top of it for twenty-two days and has never once mentioned it, and I am not going to ask him about it, because he told us on the first day that he will answer one thing and he has not been asked.*

“He does not know,” Elias said.

“He knows where he is standing,” Joon said. “Those are different and you know they are different, because you have spent a fortnight being taught the difference by a sixty-eight-year-old man with a bar of soap in his coat.”

At eleven, the channel closed. The notice stayed on the terminal, patient, with its four fields, and nobody in the hall touched it, and Petar Lisk wrote the whole of it on the board in his own hand because that is what happens to documents in that building, and the last thing he wrote underneath it was not part of the offer and was not part of the record and was nine years old and true.

**SHE TOLD US NOT TO MAKE IT A DOCUMENT. THERE ARE FOUR THINGS WRITTEN DOWN IN THIS ROOM TONIGHT AND SHE SAID THAT OUT LOUD TO ALL OF US.**
