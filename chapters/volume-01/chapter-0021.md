# Chapter 21 — The Finding

Hollis Vane came back at nine on Friday morning with a folder under his arm and a second cup of coffee he did not drink, and he set the folder on the long table face down, which told Elias more about the document than anything inside it was going to.

“The hold says I write my findings in the room and not in the Office,” he said. “I wrote it Thursday afternoon in a room with a borrowed chair and a radiator that knocks. This is four pages. It is not nine. I tried to make it nine and I could not, and I regard the four as the honest ones.”

He did not sit down, and nobody asked him to. Elias had spent two days learning what it cost a man to write down something about his own office, and the cost was the ability to be comfortable in a room while he did it.

“Three things about it,” Vane said. “It is a finding and not a decision, and the board can accept all four, reject all four, or thank everybody. I have not softened the Office's position. And these pages are about the file, and the file is mine, and if you disagree with the fourth finding I would rather you disagreed loudly in front of these people than politely in a corridor.”

The hall had filled while he talked, because a working day in a hall where twenty-three people sleep is not a day when anybody can be quiet for eleven minutes.

Vane turned the folder over.

---

**Finding 1.** The classification of the Kestrel event as structural equipment failure is not supported by the evidence the Office holds. The form carrying that cause was opened at 05:24 from a standing pack in which the cause box arrives pre-ticked to the site class. The Office's own patrol sheet of 04:50 establishes a west signal cabinet faulting on intermittent power. No field observation recorded on the incident morning overwrote the pre-ticked box, and no superseding cause was filed before the rescue report at 06:27. The Office therefore holds a cause it did not observe and has not retracted. I am not saying the site class is wrong. I am saying the Office cannot presently show the moment at which anybody decided.

Joon wrote that down without expression, which was how Joon wrote down everything that hurt.

“Read the last sentence again,” Elias said, “and put it on the wall in that wording.”

“It is already in that wording.”

“Good. Then it is in your hand, and mine does not matter.”

---

**Finding 2.** The Kestrel file cannot be revised in any respect that depends on credential attribution, because the sector roster for west apron 4C does not carry a holder field. The renumbering of 2035 carried the credential forward and did not carry the holder. I have checked whether a later correction exists. It does not. I have checked whether a paper source in the depot could be indexed against the 2035 sequence. It can, and it has not been, in seven years, and the reason it has not been is that nobody has asked with a date on it.

A hole in a file is normally a nuisance. A hole that is the only thing a board can act on is a different animal.

“So the classification is weak,” Elias said, “and the weakness cannot be repaired until somebody produces a name, and the name was thrown away by the institution in 2035, and the board will tomorrow have to choose between doing nothing and ordering somebody to look.”

“Yes.” Vane wrote on the back of his own finding. “You understood that in nine seconds, which is eight seconds faster than I did, and I am not grateful about it.”

---

**Finding 3** was four lines long, and it was the one that put Tarin Voss on his feet.

It read: that the medical page in this file names an individual as the body to which a lent capacity was applied; that a named subject in a medical record keeps the right to read, limit, or withdraw the named field of that record without withdrawing the underlying event; and that a withdrawal so made is itself a record and must be entered as one, in this file, in the same hand, and not as an absence.

The page it referred to was the one Mara had dictated in the first week: the offer, the boundary, the return, the refusal of an extension, the one the Office had countersigned, the one Elias had spent five days treating as the strongest document in the building. It named Tarin Voss as the body.

“Right,” Tarin said. He was already at the table, in the shoe he had wanted back. “No.”

The room went quiet in the way rooms do when something has been decided before anybody had a chance to argue.

“That page is the strongest thing we have got,” Elias said. “It is the only document in here that says a person was kept alive by a thing that cannot be asked for twice, and tomorrow a board sits in this hall and reads it. If you withdraw it, what is on the table is a summary saying a capacity was used on a person, and I will not be able to stop them reading that as equipment.”

“I did not say I wanted it off the table.” Tarin spread his hands flat on it, the way a man holds a table when he has been told he may stand whenever he wants. “I said no. I want to know what I am saying no to, because I have been in this building a week and nobody has said my name out loud in front of a piece of paper that matters.”

Elias had no answer, which was the correct answer and cost him something to let stand.

Hollis Vane turned around. “What is the subject withdrawing? I need the scope in the subject's words and I am going to use the subject's words and not improve them.”

Tarin took a while.

“The name in it,” he said. “Not the event. I am not withdrawing the event. There is a wheel and there is a hand and there is a man on a trolley and I was the man on the trolley, and I want that written down everywhere it needs to be written down for as long as it needs to be. What I withdraw is the field with my name in it. I did not know it was going to be in a field. Nobody asked me. I am nineteen weeks off an operation a woman with a bad chest in this room performed because I asked her to, and I did not know I was going to be *the body*. That is the word for equipment. That is the word for the thing a loan gets used on, and I am not a thing the loan got used on — I am the reason we can talk about the loan at all.”

Nobody made an event of the silence.

“All right,” Vane said, and wrote for half a minute, and then read it back in full without altering a word. Tarin said, “Yes, that is what I said. Initial it. Give it to him.” He pointed at Joon without looking, which was a thing he had learned from Nadi Bell and should not have been able to.

“Can you do that,” Vane said, quieter than anything he had said all morning. “Record a withdrawn subject as a finding rather than as an absence.”

“I can record it as a finding. An absence would require me to say the material does not exist. The material exists. It is in the custody of the medical lead and I am not asking her for it. I am recording that its owner has declined to have a named field read aloud in a board setting, on the stated ground that the field makes him an object, and that he has been told the consequence, which is that the page is withdrawn from the reading and the withdrawal sits in the same file, in the same hand.”

Joon wrote. He read it back, Tarin initialed it, and then he sat down, and Ru Sabar brought him soup he had not asked for and he took it without thanking her, which was correct.

That was when Niko Senn stood up.

“Mine too,” he said. “The note on my page says I asked not to be published, and I dictated that wording, and I have read it more times than I have read anything in six days. There is a second page from Tuesday where the same thing happened and the note says *declined publication*. I told you to change it. You changed the first one.”

“I changed the first one.”

“Then there are two notes and one of them is better than the other, and the better one is on a page I have not seen since Tuesday, and it goes into a board's hands tomorrow with a sentence in it that I did not say.” He put his hand flat on the table. “I am withdrawing that page. Same as him. Bring it back to whoever holds it and put a line on it in front of me.”

It took a while to do properly, and Joon did it in the room with the door open, and at no point in any of it did anybody suggest that a note about a man's name was a small thing.

By the end of it the strongest document in the building had two clean holes in it, both made by the people the document was about, and Elias understood for the first time what the word consent weighs when it is not his to give away.

---

**Finding 4** was the one Vane wanted on a page of its own, and he read it standing, which told Elias he had learned the text in the last hour and did not want to be accused of reading it off paper.

“The classification stays provisional until the board either accepts a cause or states that it cannot establish one. Either way the hold expires and the site reverts to the standing designation. I want that on the minutes in those words, because the standing designation is what put the cause box pre-ticked in the first place, and if the board lets the hold lapse without a cause, the pre-ticked box becomes the file's answer by default and the four findings I have just read you become a complaint about a document nobody is contesting.”

He looked up.

“That is not an accusation of anybody in this room. That is what an archivist does when the file is his and the clock is his and the findings he wrote will be filed by people who did not write them.”

“Sit down,” Inez said, from the board. “You have been standing up long enough and this is not a building that thanks people for standing.”

Vane sat down.

---

The finding went up on the wall at half past ten with the Office's 1800 digest answer pinned directly underneath, close enough to touch, contradicting each other in the same building on the same board.

Joon read Finding 1 aloud, then the digest's paragraph about one approved outcome, and then said, “Both of these are now eleven inches apart on a wall in a neighborhood with twenty-nine doors in it, and anybody who comes to that corner to find out what is happening gets both at once. Neither of us is in a position to argue with a paper for having a second sheet printed.”

“Whose idea was the digest underneath it?” Anja Ro asked.

“Mine. Ours. I asked Inez and she said yes before I had finished the sentence, and Elias said no for about four minutes and then stopped, because the answer to that digest is not a paragraph of ours. It is his document sitting above it.”

Elias found he had nothing to add to that, which was becoming a pattern.

---

Then they did the part they had gone in to do.

“We are not asking the board to exonerate us,” Elias said. “The board is not competent to exonerate us and I would be insulting it. We are asking for the depot box.”

Vane looked up. “Say that again with the date on it.”

“The 05:02 dispatch sheet. The original is in a box at the Spine depot and it has a filled second signature that the Office copy does not have. The board can order it produced under a review. That is inside a review's power, it takes one line, and it costs the Office nothing it can name.”

“It costs the Office a document it has been keeping from a team that has been keeping documents from the Office for six days, which is a different sentence and the true one.” He wrote it on the back of his own finding. “I will carry the request. I will not sponsor it, because I do not have standing, and because if I sponsor it they will know it came from the room. Lodge it yourselves, in your own words, before the board sits this afternoon, and if it is lodged after, the board has no obligation to it at all.”

“Lodge it this morning.”

“Lodge it this morning,” Vane said, and closed the folder, and then said, unexpectedly, “Mr. Rook. I have read four hundred incident files and I have never read one where a subject took his own name out of the record in the middle of the process. I want to be exact. I am not saying you did that well. I am saying it is in the file, in the file's own hand, dated, and it cannot be quietly gone, and whoever sits in the chair over this in ten years will be able to see it. That is all an archivist is for. It is not a judgment and it is not a friend.”

“Understood,” Elias said.

At the door, Vane stopped and looked at the board — the finding above, the digest below — and said, “Both of those are now on a public wall four hundred meters from where I live. That is not a remark about the case.”

---

The rest of the day belonged to Inez, and she did not share it.

She cleared the board at eleven and put up one line under the tally that had been there since Wednesday.

**HOLD EXPIRES AT MIDNIGHT TOMORROW NIGHT. REVIEW BOARD TOMORROW AFTERNOON. PERRY DUNNE ASKED FOR THE CREW TO BE GONE TODAY.**

Nobody argued with her about the arithmetic, because the arithmetic was true. Ninety-six hours from Wednesday afternoon took them to Saturday afternoon, and at midnight that night the standing designation took the site back. The board sat Saturday afternoon. The team had a seat at that board, which was the entire reason the hold had been worth ninety-six hours instead of a refusal — and a team that had walked off the Quarter on Friday morning in order to be absent on Saturday afternoon would have broken a term five people had signed.

“I am not going to move a man’s date because a clock says so,” Inez said. “Perry. Come here.”

Perry Dunne had been standing by the door with his arms folded for an hour, and he had done the sum himself before anybody handed it to him.

“Saturday,” he said.

“Saturday the board sits. Three of you have to speak. If you are not in the building there is no seat for you, and the review happens in an office with a form on it, which is the thing we have spent six days refusing to allow.”

“Fine. Then it is next Friday.” Perry did not soften it. “And I want it said that I did not move it to be helpful. I moved nothing. You moved it. I am giving you a week from this evening because the week has a board in it and a crew gone in it, and that is arithmetic, and it is also the only reason I have got.”

“I will say that in those words, on the board, in your mouth, with your name against it,” Inez said. “And the day I first put it on the board, so that in six weeks nobody comes and tells you that you agreed to it.”

“Put my name against it,” Perry said. “And put my first date against it too.”

Inez wrote it in her own hand and read it back to the room, and the room laughed, and Elias understood that a man had just described this hall more precisely than he had, and that it was the third day in a row on which having nothing to contribute was the correct amount for him to contribute.
