# Chapter 38 — Saturday Night

The records room was on a lamp all day Saturday because the instruments had taken the clinic's generator margin on Friday, so at half past eleven on Saturday night the four people who were going to sit and listen to a file were sitting in a room the size of a closet with one bulb in it and a bulb is not a thing you design a listening exercise around.

Joon brought his own speaker, not the hall's, and he brought it for a reason he explained before he plugged it in.

“In a room this size, a room tone is a fact. I want the room in the file and I want it in the same file as the two copies, and if the two copies were made in different rooms then the room is the only thing in this exercise that is not supposed, and I would like everybody in here to hear the room before they hear the file.”

He played four seconds of nothing. It sounded like a closet with a heater in it and a road outside and a door two floors down.

Then he played the team's copy. Eleven seconds, a room that was not that room, and a man's voice in it that said the thing it has said twice in public since the second week, which is that the map is not to be made to agree.

Then he played the node's copy, and stopped it, and played it again, and stopped it again, and the four people in the closet did not say anything for a while.

“It is the same eleven seconds,” Elias said.

“It is the same eleven seconds, byte for byte, and I have checked it twice and I am going to check it a third time on Monday because I do not trust a thing that says what I want,” Joon said. “The voice is the voice. The words are the words. The room in both is the same room. And the file is four tenths of a second longer, and the four tenths are in the middle, and they are not silence.”

He put a finger on the table without touching anything.

“They are a handoff.”

---

Nobody overrules Joon for about ninety seconds, which is a record, and the reason is that Mara gets there first and gets there for the wrong reason and therefore stops the room from being tempted.

“If that is a hand,” she said, “then it is my hand, or it is the hand of a person in a branch where a person like me is a surgeon, and I have had three weeks to decide how I feel about that, and I am telling this room that I would like it very much to be a machine.” She had her arms folded and she was looking at the speaker, not at anybody. “I know that is not a scientific position. I do not care. I have a nineteen-year-old on a frame I gave away this morning and I am not spending tonight being talked into a feeling about my own hands by a quarter of a second in a file in a building I have never been in.”

“That is not what anybody is suggesting,” Elias said, and then stopped, because that was not quite true, and everybody heard it, and he did not try to unsay it.

Elias was the one who was closest to it. He had the notebook open on his knee and the notebook has a bulge in the front where an envelope is, and he had been listening to a voice that sounded like his own for three weeks, and at one in the morning on Saturday he said, in a voice nobody in the room had heard from him before:

“Give me the terminal. Not tonight's reading — I am not asking Joon to break his rule, I am asking to be the second pair of eyes. Give me the node terminal and the prefix registry and ten minutes and I will not write a meaning down. I will write down what the prefix is.”

“No,” Joon said, without heat.

“Elias —”

“No, and here is the whole reason in one sentence, and then I am done.” Joon took his hands off the speaker. “I said on Sunday that a quarter of a second gets a meaning the moment somebody is the second person to give it one. I did not say I would protect the fact. I said I would protect the fact. A prefix is a fact. A prefix is nine characters and a letter and it is sitting in a gap in a file and if I write it down, then in a year the person who reads the log will not read a gap, they will read *Rook wanted to know* — and that is how a file stops being a record and starts being a story, and the story gets better every time it is retold, and the quarter of a second will be the part everybody remembers.”

He put the speaker down.

“You can have it Monday. Not tonight.”

Petar Lisk, who had been sent to bed an hour ago and had come back and been allowed to stay because it was a Saturday and because he had asked, put his hand up.

“Joon.”

“Yes.”

“You told us not to say what it means. So we will not say what it means.” Petar was looking at the sheet in Joon's hand. “Can we still write down the number?”

The room went quiet in a way that has happened about nine times in twenty-one days.

“Yes,” Joon said. “That is the difference and nobody here has ever seen it before, so say it slowly. We are not refusing a meaning. We are refusing to be the second person who builds one. The fact is not a meaning. The fact has a name and a length and a shape and it can be checked on Monday by a second person who does not care what it means. So yes. Write the number down.”

“Can I do it,” Petar said.

“Do it in your own hand and I will initial it and it goes in the log at the top and not at the bottom.”

---

The number was nine characters and a letter.

**K-7Q . then four digits.**

Joon wrote it and initialled it and read it back, and the reading-back is the part that matters, because a thing that is read out loud in a room of six people in a closet at one in the morning on a battery bulb cannot afterwards be something that happened to one man.

“The form of it,” Joon said, “is a Civic Spine relay stamp. Every segment a relay carries gets stamped, and the stamp tells you which relay handed it on. Ours is *H-4C* and it is Havenport four C, and it is on the front of both copies because both copies came in through Havenport four C. The second stamp is in the gap in the node's copy, which means the node's copy was handed on once more somewhere, by a relay that is not four C.”

“Which is where,” Elias said.

“Which is *a prefix and four digits*, and I cannot tell you where, and I am going to be very exact about why, because this is the sentence I made everybody promise.” Joon held the sheet up. “A relay is not a place. A place is not a route. A route is not a person. Every one of those is a jump, and the jumps are where a quarter of a second becomes a conspiracy, and I have read the log we wrote on Saturday and I know what the third line of it says, and the third line of it says that in all three cases the name is not present, and that sentence is going to keep working for us for about forty years if we let it.”

“Then it is useless,” Elias said, not complaining, just checking.

“It is *not yet* useless. It is unusable on Saturday night at one in the morning by six people in a closet, and there is a difference and the difference is that on Monday there is daylight and a registry and a second person who does not care.” He put the sheet down. “And I am writing one more line under the number, in my own hand, and it is the line that stops this from being a story in six weeks: *prefix format not in the Havenport stamp set indexed to this file. Seen once before. Not placed.*”

“You have seen it before,” Mara said.

Joon did not answer for a moment.

“Yes,” he said. “I have seen a stamp in that format once, in the last three weeks, and I have not put the two next to each other and I am not going to tonight, and I would like it noted in the log that I have said that out loud in front of witnesses instead of sitting on it, which is a thing I have been doing badly for three weeks and have only just noticed.”

---

They did the last thing at two in the morning, before the dark hours, and it took forty seconds and Elias asked for it himself, which nobody had expected and nobody applauded.

“Write this down,” he said. “*Elias Rook has no intention of asking his own future for anything, ever, and if a voice that sounds like him turns up offering, the answer is the same one the man behind the wall gave: it is not a road, it is a schedule.*”

“That is not what that sentence means,” Joon said, “and you know it is not, and I am writing down that you know it is not, because in four weeks somebody is going to read a line on a wall and believe that a man has decided something and he has decided exactly one thing, which is that he is not the person who asks.”

“That is a better line than the one I said,” Elias said.

“It is the line you said,” Joon said, “with the truth in it. That is the only difference and it is the entire job and I would like somebody else to have to do it one day.”

Petar Lisk read the board at seven on Sunday morning, as he read it every morning, and the last line of it was not Elias's, because Petar had put a line under it himself and Inez had let him.

**THE VOICE IS STILL UNKNOWN. THE COPY IS FOUR TENTHS LONGER. WE HAVE ONE NUMBER AND IT IS NOT A PLACE, AND ON MONDAY WE WILL BE BORED ABOUT IT, WHICH IS WHAT I HAVE BEEN TOLD TO WRITE AND NOT WHAT I THINK.**
