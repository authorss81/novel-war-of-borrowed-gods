# Chapter 27 — Friday

The Service sent a form at ten past seven on Friday morning, and Rafi read it standing up and then handed it to Kellan without saying anything, and Kellan read it and sat down on the end of a cot and put his face in his hands for about four seconds and then got up.

“There is a gas main on the Meridian,” Rafi said. “It is not ours. It has been theirs for a week and they have been running it with a borrowed crew out of the Third Ward, and this morning a supervisor I have never met in my life drove down Ash Street to tell me that they have four certified confined-space rope technicians in the city and three of them are in this hall.”

“Three of them,” Elias said.

“Rafi, Kellan, and a man called Doss who has been doing the Friday nights on the batteries and who is asleep upstairs at the moment and does not know.” Rafi said it without any self-pity, which made it worse. “Elias, there is a pump station on the west bank that has been running since the eleventh with nobody in it and two hundred people downstream of it. I have to go back to that. I have been putting it off since Wednesday and I did not tell anybody, and I am telling you now because I am not going to be the man who stays in a warm building while somebody else covers my rotation.”

Nobody in the hall said anything, and the hall had forty-one people in it, and the silence had a different texture from the ones that came before, and Elias understood that he was hearing the sound of four people deciding whether to be angry with him for letting it go on.

“I should have had this conversation on Wednesday,” he said.

“Yes,” Tomas said. “You should have. And you did not, because you are the person who decides who hears about a thing like this, and today the Service decided it for you, and that is a lesson you are going to be allowed to keep.”

---

Perry Dunne came at eleven, which was not the hour anybody had expected him to use, and he came with a toolbox and stayed two hours.

He did not thank them. That was the thing Elias had braced for and did not get.

“A week ago I asked you to be gone by Friday,” Perry said, “and then you told me the hold ran to Saturday and the board sat on Saturday, and I moved to this Friday, and I have had a week to think about what I got.”

“Yes,” Elias said.

“I have been thinking about it in the shop, with the freezer on my neighbor's and no power and my whole life reorganized around a hole,” Perry said. “And here is what I have. I did not get a week where nobody from outside tells me what my quarter needs. I got a week where nobody from outside tells me, and four people from inside tell me, and one of them is a boy of nine who has more authority in this building than I do, and he is right about it about nine times in ten.”

He put a wrench down on the table.

“Do not thank me for this,” he said. “I want it very clear that I am not being generous and I am not moved. If I say thank you then a year from now somebody in this building says the crew left when we asked, and that is a story about a favor, and it is not what happened. What happened is that a hold ran to Saturday and five people signed a thing and you would not break it. That is all. If I ever need to be angry at you about the Friday I have moved twice, I will be, and you will not be able to stop me, and that is also fine.”

Then he fixed the door closer on the dry end that four people had installed on Monday without a plan, and before he left he put his hand flat on the block wall for a second, the way a man does with a wall in his own building.

---

Joon put two dates on the board under Perry's, in his own hand, because nobody had asked him to and because a week of nobody counting was how clocks got lost.

**BOARD: SURVEY OR WRITTEN REFUSAL DUE ON THE ELEVENTH DAY, WHICH IS TUESDAY.**

**DISPATCH CONFIRMATION, SHUTTLE SIX, ROUTINE, NO INCIDENT REFERENCE: DUE TUESDAY. NOT HERE. YOLANDA BELL'S ROW IS STILL NOT ON THE DIGITAL LIST.**

“It is not coming early and it is not coming with a name in it,” Joon said. “Bex Amadi told us on the second day that he could not put an incident reference anywhere near it, and if one gets close the inquiry locks and we get a letter instead of a confirmation. So what arrives on Tuesday is a smaller question, and the answer to a smaller question is allowed to be nothing.”

“Write *allowed to be nothing* on the board too,” Inez said, and he did.

---

By the end of the day the hall was running itself and running it badly, and everybody agreed that both of those things had been true since Wednesday.

Anja Ro found the error. She was reconciling the witness statements against the custody log because somebody had to and because she had been doing it unpaid since the second day, and on Friday afternoon she came to the long table with a page and said, “Your Monday entry for the depot says the box came out at eleven oh four and back at eleven forty-one, and it says you photographed the aisle log at eleven oh four as well, and the camera stamp in the corner of that frame is eleven oh two, and the two are two minutes apart, and one of them is wrong and it is not the photograph.”

“I read it off the camera,” Joon said.

“Then you read it off the camera badly, and you wrote it down badly, and nobody in this building can check you because we cannot see your camera.” Anja put the page down. “I can check you. I have been checking you since the second day. Fix it and initial the fix.”

Joon fixed it and initialled the fix and said thank you, and Elias, standing four feet away with a hand cart, understood that he had just watched a man get corrected by a survivor and be glad about it.

Petar Lisk ran the exit walk at four o'clock on Friday and found the block wall had been built four inches off square, which meant the dry end's door did not open all the way, which meant a man carrying a filing tray could not get through it sideways, which Petar demonstrated by trying, in front of everybody, until Elias took the tray.

Ru Sabar found the batteries. She had been charging them on her own schedule because she owned the schedule, and on Friday she opened the second bank and said, “This is the wrong chemistry. It has been running warm for a week and it is going to be warm in a way that matters on a night when somebody is on oxygen. I have been charging them at the wrong current for six days because I was copying the card off a bank that died in 2038.” She looked at Elias. “You have not noticed, and you would not have, and I am not being brave, I am being an electrician.”

Lena Mir took the ward round of the twenty-three that evening with two nurse-assistants from the clinic and no physician in the building at all for ninety minutes, and did it, and was extremely good at it, and told Mara on the way out that she had been doing that for eleven years and that the only thing Mara had been adding was a signature.

And the thing the residents did better than anybody who had been in the tunnel: the consent cards got rewritten into plain sentences with no box ticked by default and no question that could be read two ways, which four people who had never been surveyed in their lives could do and one rescuer with a clipboard could not, because he could not stop writing questions that sounded like permission.

Nobody wanted a plan that day. That was the other thing. On Friday the room argued about the hall, about the two hours of dark, about whether the block wall should have a window, and about Ru Sabar's machine shop, and not once about the run at eleven meters or the man behind the wall, and the reason was not forgetting. The reason was that the hall had become the thing they were doing, and the tunnel had become a fact on a board, and everybody in the room knew which of those two things they were more frightened of and had stopped arguing about it because you cannot be frightened out loud for eleven hours a day.

---

Elias was on the loading lip at nine that night with the laser case, because it was his job and because he had nothing else to do, when Ansel Duarte came out to the doorway in a coat and a pair of borrowed boots and stood there until Elias put the case down.

“You are not going down there,” Ansel said.

“I am not going down there.”

“I know. I came out here to say a thing and I want to say it once.” He leaned on the frame the way he leaned on everything. “You are not the reason this hall works.”

“Okay.”

“I want to be very clear that this is not a compliment,” Ansel said. “If you were gone tomorrow, the hall would be worse at two things and better at one, and I know which two and I know which one, and you do not get to be the reason. You get to be one of the reasons, in a room with about eleven other reasons in it, most of whom are not trained, and one of whom is a man of sixty-four who is a safety instructor and is a great deal more comfortable being the last voice in a room than he has any business being.”

“Ansel.”

“If you are the reason, then the day somebody comes to this hall and says *Rook says* is the day that everybody in here agrees to whatever you say,” he said, “and it will not be because you are right. It will be because you are the only name they recognize in a sentence. And I have been in a hole for six hours with six people, and I know what it is like when one person says the thing out loud and then everybody just does it.”

He turned to go, and then stopped, and said the last part to the door instead of to Elias.

“You were good in the tunnel. I am not saying you were not. I am saying the tunnel is not the building, and this building does not need a hero in it, and you are not going to be one, and I would rather somebody told you on a Friday than let you find out in a year.”

Inside, Ru Sabar was writing the corrected consent cards out in her own hand, and Petar Lisk was re-measuring the dry-end door with a ruler, and Joon was initialling a correction a survivor had caught, and none of them looked up when the two of them came in, and Elias understood that they had not looked up on purpose.
